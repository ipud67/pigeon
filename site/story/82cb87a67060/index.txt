1b:T47e,Trump signed the sanctions bill into law on September 19. The measure gives the administration tools to hit countries that buy large volumes of Russian oil with import tariffs as high as 100 percent. It also tightens rules on senior Russian officials and on the tanker fleet that moves Russian crude outside normal shipping channels.

This sits on the ladder of earlier US steps that first froze Russian assets, then targeted banks and energy exports, then pressed buyers to cut volumes. The new law adds a direct price on continued purchases by China and India.

The next procedural step is for the administration to publish the list of countries and volumes that trigger the tariffs and the list of Russian individuals hit by fresh travel and asset bans. How long the pressure lasts depends on whether those buyers keep taking Russian barrels at current rates or switch suppliers. If China and India reroute purchases or negotiate carve-outs, the sanctions lose bite; if they absorb the tariff cost, the law stays in force until Congress or the next president changes it.
