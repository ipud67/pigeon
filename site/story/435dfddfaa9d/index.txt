This comes after he spent the summer campaign trail calling for Netanyahu’s arrest, then telling reporters in late July that protest is a bedrock right in the city and that he would leave any decision to New Yorkers themselves.

The shift lines up with steady NYPD warnings that mayoral encouragement of large protests could stretch police resources during the high-security UN event. Mamdani now says he never urged protests, only defended the First Amendment right to them, and that he has stayed in regular contact with the police department. No specific threats have been reported.

The situation stays stable unless Mamdani reverses his stance or a court action forces a change in security posture.
