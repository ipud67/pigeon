FAA proposes permanent flight restrictions over Mar-a-Lago

The FAA issued a notice of proposed rulemaking on September 3 2026 to set permanent flight restrictions from the surface to 2,000 feet above mean sea level inside a one-nautical-mile radius of Mar-a-Lago in Florida. The rule would replace earlier temporary flight restrictions that the agency put in place around the property. Those temporary measures began when Donald Trump served as president from 2017 to 2021, were renewed during his post-presidency period, and continued after he took office again in 2025. The proposal gives the FAA authority to limit which aircraft may enter the airspace and under what conditions. A 30-day public comment period follows the notice.

Thinking ahead
The FAA put out a notice Wednesday for a permanent flight ban from the ground up to 2,000 feet inside a one-mile circle around Mar-a-Lago in Florida. This builds on the temporary restrictions the agency has kept in place there since 2017. The proposal now sits in the federal register for a 30-day public comment period that ends in early October. Next step is review of those comments by FAA staff. After that the agency can issue a final rule, tweak the language, or drop the plan. The restriction stays in place only if the final rule gets published; any shift in White House occupancy or a court order blocking the rule would force a rewrite or cancellation. This setup looks likely to run through the rest of 2026 while comments come in and get sorted. It moves to final rule or gets shelved sometime in 2027 once the review finishes. Horizon estimate: Final rule decision by mid-2027.
