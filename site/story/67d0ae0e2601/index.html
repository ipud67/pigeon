Sam Altman will brief the UN Security Council next week

The United Nations Security Council has met on questions of emerging technology since at least the 2010s, when members began holding periodic sessions on how digital tools affect international peace and security. Those sessions have covered everything from cyber operations between states to the use of drones in armed conflict, with the council issuing statements that call on member nations to observe existing international law.

On July 22, 2025, the council held its first formal discussion devoted to artificial intelligence systems, hearing from governments and outside experts on how such systems could alter decision-making in crises and the conduct of warfare. That meeting produced no binding resolution but set a pattern of inviting private-sector leaders to provide information on fast-moving capabilities.

Sam Altman, chief executive of OpenAI, will appear before the council on September 25, 2026, to brief members on the current state of artificial-intelligence development and its direct implications for global stability. The session was arranged at the request of the council's current president and will be closed to the public.

The briefing places the council's attention on the pace of commercial AI progress inside the existing framework of international security discussions that began more than a decade ago.

Thinking ahead
Sam Altman is set to brief the UN Security Council on artificial intelligence next week. This marks the latest step in a series of high-level government meetings on AI rules that began with his 2023 congressional testimony and continued through White House executive orders and EU AI Act talks. The briefing itself is a closed-door procedural session with council members. How long this continues depends on whether the council follows up with a formal resolution or working group. That would require at least nine affirmative votes and no veto from a permanent member. Without those votes the process stays at the talking stage. Next move is the council vote on any draft text that emerges from the Altman session. If none is tabled the file moves to the General Assembly or stays with national regulators. Horizon: 3 to 6 months until any binding UN measure is on the table or the track goes quiet.
