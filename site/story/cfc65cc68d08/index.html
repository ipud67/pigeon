KYIV
September 28, 2026 · 16:40 UTC

One dead, dozens injured after Russian daytime strikes on Kyiv facilities

Russian strikes on Ukrainian civilian and economic sites have continued for more than three years since Moscow launched its full-scale invasion in February 2022. Kyiv has come under repeated drone and missile attacks, especially as winter nears and after last year’s campaign that damaged power and heating systems across the country. Russian forces have widened their targeting in recent days to data centers, telecoms facilities, and other economic beyond the earlier focus on warehouses, ports, and energy plants.

On September 28 a Russian drone struck the National Academy of Sciences in central Kyiv during working hours. The high-speed drone hit at a sharp angle, exploded, and started a fire that sent smoke over the city. One person died, the assistant to former national security official Volodymyr Gorbulin, who was wounded in the same building. Mayor Vitali Klitschko reported 19 people injured in the capital, six of them hospitalized. Additional strikes hit a pharmaceutical company, petrol stations, a medical facility, and other sites in Kyiv. In Dnipro four people were killed and at least 19 wounded at a business center. One more death occurred in a logistics office in Synelnykove. Strikes also damaged an ice cream factory in Zhytomyr region, residential buildings in Sumy, Rivne, and Odesa, and wounded 30 civilians in Zaporizhzhia, three of them seriously. An unspecified number of fatalities were reported in a residential area of Kharkiv. Poland scrambled jets to protect its airspace during the assault.

The attacks leave Ukraine facing another winter with damaged infrastructure while European officials discuss new coordination measures against Russian sabotage and interference.
