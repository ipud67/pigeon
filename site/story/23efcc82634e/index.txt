19:T48d,Putin’s rename of the pipeline from “Power of Siberia 2” to “Power of Baikal” is the latest procedural tweak in a project that has been on the books since the mid-2010s. The change was announced by Energy Minister Tsivilev on Wednesday in Vladivostok and does not alter the route, capacity targets or delivery timetable already agreed with China. Construction contracts, land permits and cross-border engineering studies remain the next concrete steps; those have moved slowly for years and still sit at the design-and-approval stage.

The rename itself is a branding move that can be reversed with another decree. Observable indicators that the project is advancing would be signed engineering-procurement-construction contracts or visible pipe-laying starts inside Russia. Indicators it is stalling would be further postponements of those contracts or public statements from either side citing financing or technical problems.

The name change adds no new momentum. The pipeline’s physical progress is likely to stay on its existing slow track for at least another 12–24 months.
