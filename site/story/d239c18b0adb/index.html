ARGENTINA
September 22, 2026 · 16:54 UTC

7 Iranians, Hezbollah official to stand trial for 1994 Jewish community center bombing that killed 85

An Argentine judge ordered eight men to stand trial in absentia for the 1994 bombing of the AMIA Jewish community center in Buenos Aires that killed 85 people and injured hundreds more.

The case began with the July 18 1994 attack on the seven-story building that housed the main Jewish mutual aid society in Argentina. Investigators long accused Iranian officials and members of the Lebanese group Hezbollah of directing the truck-bomb operation through local contacts. Argentine prosecutors issued international arrest warrants for several Iranian diplomats and a Hezbollah operative in the years after the attack but obtained no extraditions. The suspects remained at large while the investigation moved through repeated delays and separate probes into possible local accomplices.

On September 22 2026 the court ruled that seven Iranians and one Hezbollah official will face trial on charges connected to the bombing. The eight will be tried together in their absence under Argentine law that permits such proceedings when defendants cannot be brought into custody. The case now turns on the presentation of evidence gathered over three decades and whether Argentine courts can deliver a verdict without the physical presence of the accused.

Thinking ahead
An Argentine court set a trial date for seven Iranians and one Hezbollah official to appear in absentia over the 1994 bombing of the AMIA Jewish community center that killed 85. The case sits on the ladder that began with the attack itself, moved through years of Argentine probes that repeatedly named Iranian officials and Hezbollah operatives, and reached formal indictments that Iran has always rejected. The suspects remain outside Argentine custody, so the trial will run without them present. The procedural next step is formal notification to the defendants through diplomatic channels, followed by presentation of evidence gathered since the original warrants. Argentina would need physical custody or a major shift in Iranian policy to move the case beyond a symbolic proceeding. Hezbollah has shown no sign of handing over its member, and Iran continues to treat the charges as politically driven. This legal step adds one more layer to the standing accusations but does not change force posture in the Strait of Hormuz or the pattern of nightly strikes and tanker incidents. The war stays anchored to naval control and missile exchanges; the Buenos Aires courtroom sits apart from that operational line. Trial opens in early 2027.
