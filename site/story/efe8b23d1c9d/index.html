Bossier, Caddo tax renewals head to Dec. 12 ballot

Bossier and Caddo Parish voters in northwest Louisiana will face two separate tax renewal measures on the Dec. 12 ballot. A regional hotel occupancy tax first imposed in 1995 and renewed in later cycles now comes up for another vote; if approved it continues to levy a percentage on hotel rooms across both parishes and directs the revenue to a convention and visitors bureau. Bossier Parish voters will also decide on renewing a property tax millage that has been in place since 1980 and last renewed in 2016; that tax generates more than $4 million a year for parish operations. The measures follow the standard expiration schedule written into the original ordinances.

Thinking ahead
Voters in Bossier and Caddo parishes will see the hotel tax renewal and the bigger Bossier property tax renewal on the Dec. 12 ballot. That date is the next procedural step after local governments put the measures up for approval. Turnout, how the two measures interact on the same ballot, and whether parish officials campaign hard for them are the clear indicators that decide passage or failure. If both pass, the taxes stay in place and keep feeding the expected $1.7 million a year from hotels plus the $4 million-plus from Bossier property levies. If either fails, that revenue stream ends on schedule and forces the parishes to cut spending or find replacement money. No court action or legislative change is on the table right now, so the ballot outcome is the sole trigger that shifts the picture. This runs until the Dec. 12 vote and the final certified results a few days later. Horizon: ends December 2026.
