That lineup puts Nigeria eighth on the speaker list, two places higher than last year, with its national statement set for Sept. 23. The schedule already lists 168 side events, roughly 1,000 bilateral meetings between member states, and another batch of meetings involving the secretary-general, plus 3,225 press passes issued so far.

These numbers line up with the pattern of past assemblies where attendance holds steady once the provisional speaker list drops. The count can still shift if more delegations confirm or drop out in the next 48 hours. The debate itself runs through Sept. 26 and picks up again on the 28th, after which the session moves into committee work that stretches into next year.

The gathering will stay focused on the listed agenda items — development financing, climate, sea-level rise, pandemic rules, and racial justice — until the gavel comes down on the 28th. No procedural shift is visible yet that would shorten or extend the week.
