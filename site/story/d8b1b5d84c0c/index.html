More than 40% of candidates for governors and mayors have not yet filed campaign accounts

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlineMás del 40 % de candidatos a gobernadores y alcaldes aún no rinden cuentas de campañas

Peru requires every candidate for governor, vice governor or mayor to file campaign finance reports with the National Office of Electoral Processes before the October local and regional elections. The rule has been in place for prior election cycles as well. Candidates must hand over the accounts that show who gave them money and how they spent it.

As of September 16, only 5,595 of the 10,623 candidates had turned in their reports. That left 6,028, or 56.74 percent, still missing the deadline.

The gap now forces ONPE to decide how it will handle the late filings and whether any candidates will face disqualification or fines before ballots are printed.

Thinking ahead
ONPE data shows 6,028 of 10,623 required candidates for governor, vice governor, and mayor posts have filed their campaign accounts. That leaves 4,595 who have not. The filing deadline has passed, so the next procedural step is for ONPE to list the non-filers and apply the sanctions spelled out in election law. This pattern of late or missing filings has shown up in prior Peruvian local and regional races. The share of holdouts typically shrinks once ONPE starts publishing the names and local election boards begin enforcement. How far the list drops will track the speed of those public notices and any appeals that reach the National Jury of Elections. The situation changes only when ONPE releases the final compliance count and the boards rule on disqualification requests. Until those two steps finish, the 43 percent non-filing figure stays live. Horizon: 4 to 6 weeks until first official compliance list.
