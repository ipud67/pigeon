1a:T5ea,The State Department cleared a proposed $24.3 billion arms package to Saudi Arabia that includes F-35 jets and related equipment. Congress must still review and approve the deal before it can proceed. The clearance follows a pattern of past U.S.-Saudi arms notifications that drew opposition over technology-transfer risks with China and earlier disputes tied to the 2018 killing of Jamal Khashoggi.

Congress has 30 days from formal notification to pass a joint resolution of disapproval. Without that resolution the sale can move forward. Opposition has already surfaced from lawmakers citing intelligence warnings about Chinese access to the jet’s systems through Saudi partnerships or espionage.

Renewed Saudi-Houthi exchanges this week, including the pipeline shutdown that cut East-West oil flow, add pressure on Riyadh for stronger air defenses. That operational need lines up with the package’s stated goal of improving Saudi homeland defense and coordination with U.S. and NATO forces.

The next visible step is the formal notification packet reaching Capitol Hill and the start of the 30-day clock. If Congress stays divided along party lines the resolution of disapproval is likely to fail. The deal then advances to contract stage with Lockheed Martin and Pratt & Whitney as lead suppliers.

If new intelligence disclosures surface or a bipartisan bloc forms around technology-security objections the timeline could stretch or stall.
