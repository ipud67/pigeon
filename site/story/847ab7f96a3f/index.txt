1a:T631,A Texas system error left tens of thousands of voter registration applications sitting unprocessed for months. The secretary of state’s office flagged the glitch with the Department of Public Safety’s data feed to county election offices last week. County clerks now face a compressed timeline to clear the backlog before the voter rolls close ahead of the midterms.

The backlog stems from a routine data handoff between the Department of Public Safety and the secretary of state that stopped updating. Officials have not said exactly when the feed broke or how many applications sit in each county queue. Processing volume in the final weeks before a deadline has historically spiked in Texas when similar data mismatches surfaced in prior cycles.

County offices will keep entering applications by hand until the feed is fixed or the registration window ends. The secretary of state can extend the window only through a formal order, which has not been issued. Turnaround depends on how fast technicians isolate the error and whether counties get extra staff or software patches.

This episode sits on the ladder of past Texas registration snags tied to Department of Public Safety–secretary of state data transfers that surfaced weeks before earlier elections and were cleared through manual entry.

The situation is likely to run until the registration deadline or until the data feed is restored, whichever comes first. Next step is either a patch that resumes automatic uploads or counties finishing the manual work under tighter deadlines.
