25:T755,The release of 25 Belarusian political prisoners into Lithuania marks the latest step in a pattern of prisoner swaps tied to US envoy visits. John Coale, the US special envoy, met Lithuanian officials before heading to Minsk this week, following the same route he took before the March release of 250 prisoners. In exchange, the US agreed to lift sanctions on two Belarusian state firms, Lakokraska paint maker and Bellesbumprom forestry group. Lithuanian border crews handled the crossing, supplied temporary papers for those without IDs, and set up medical checks at arrival points. Viasna, the Belarusian rights group, lists 912 political prisoners still inside the country, a figure that has stayed near that level even as small groups leave and new arrests continue.

This round came after months of renewed US contact with Minsk that began when the current US administration took office. The sequence runs from the 2020 post-election crackdown that filled the jails, through the March 2026 batch of 250, to this smaller September group that includes journalists from Tut.by and BelaPAN plus the founder of the Symbal.by souvenir chain. Each release has followed a Coale trip and a specific sanction carve-out. No new large-scale prisoner transfer has been announced since the March event.

The next visible moves depend on whether Coale returns to Minsk with another offer or whether Minsk signals it wants more sanctions relief before it lets anyone else out. Lithuania will keep processing arrivals as long as the US channel stays open. The number of releases has varied sharply between visits, so the pace cannot be read from any fixed quota.

The situation stays on this track until either the US ends the envoy trips or Belarus stops linking releases to specific company sanctions.
