Mother of Renee Good says she voted for Trump, calls for ICE accountability

Donna Ganger's daughter Renee Good died from a gunshot fired by a federal immigration officer during an encounter in Minneapolis earlier this year.

Ganger spoke on Tuesday and said she had voted for President Trump. She told reporters she had expected federal immigration officers to protect Americans. She described the loss of her daughter as torture and said that as a mother and family they had been unable to protect Good.

The case now centers on what steps Immigration and Customs Enforcement will take to review the officer's actions and whether any changes follow in how agents operate inside the United States.

Thinking ahead
The mother of Renee Good, who was shot dead by a federal immigration officer in Minneapolis earlier this year, spoke out Tuesday and said she voted for Trump because she expected immigration officers to protect Americans. She called the death torture for the family and demanded accountability from ICE. This marks the latest public statement from the victim's family in a case that began with the shooting in early 2026. No charges have been filed against the officer as of Tuesday. The situation is likely to stay in the courts and internal review process for at least the next 12 to 18 months. It moves forward only if the Department of Justice or ICE inspector general releases its findings, or if the family files a civil suit that forces public records. Without one of those procedural steps, the public statements stay one-sided and the file stays closed. Next rung depends on whether federal investigators hand the family a report or the family lawyer gets a courtroom subpoena. Horizon: 14 months until first likely public filing or closure.
