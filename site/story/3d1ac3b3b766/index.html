Ukraine's Defence Intelligence strikes Russian Novatek-Ust-Luga oil refinery supporting war against Ukraine – photo, videos

Ukraine's Defence Intelligence carried out a strike on the Novatek-Ust-Luga oil refinery complex in Russia's Leningrad Oblast on 1 September 2026. The strike damaged key equipment and started a large fire at the facility. Novatek-Ust-Luga has supplied fuel components used by Russian forces since the start of the full-scale war in Ukraine in February 2022. The refinery sits roughly 100 km from the Estonian border and processes condensate into products that feed Russian military logistics. This is the latest in a series of Ukrainian strikes on Russian energy sites that support the war effort.

Thinking ahead
Ukraine hit the Novatek-Ust-Luga refinery in Leningrad Oblast on September 1. The strike damaged key equipment and started a big fire. Ukrainian Defence Intelligence claimed the plant feeds Russia’s war machine. This is the latest in a string of Ukrainian long-range attacks on Russian oil and energy sites that started in 2024. Each hit has followed the same pattern: drone or missile strike, visible fire, Russian repair crews move in. The pattern shows these strikes keep coming every few weeks as long as Ukraine has weapons that can reach the targets and Russia keeps using the plants for fuel. Next step is almost always the same: Russian officials say the damage is minor, firefighters put out the flames, then crews start patching or replacing the broken units. The only thing that would change the pace is if Ukraine runs out of strike assets or Russia adds enough air defenses and decoys to block most follow-on attacks. Horizon: strikes at this pace likely continue through winter unless one side loses the ability to hit or defend the sites.
