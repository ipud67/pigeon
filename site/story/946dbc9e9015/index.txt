Trump said Netanyahu told him the story and that investigators still do not know if the co-pilot meant to crash the aircraft.

This kind of lone-actor incident inside commercial traffic near the Gulf theater has happened a handful of times in past flare-ups but rarely changes the naval posture on its own. The next procedural step is the joint US-Saudi investigation into the cockpit recording and passenger statements, which will either tie the co-pilot to Iranian orders or mark him as unaffiliated. Until that link is shown or ruled out, the operational focus stays on tanker escorts through the strait and nightly strikes on Iranian maritime assets.

The incident adds one more data point to the pattern of irregular attacks on civilian routes that began after the 12 July IRGC push to close Hormuz. If the probe finds state direction, expect tighter screening of Gulf-origin crews and possibly expanded no-fly buffers; if it stays isolated, the story fades and the Hormuz contest continues on its current track of weekly strikes and tanker counts.
