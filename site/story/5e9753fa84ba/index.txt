It cites full sediment reduction targets met, 90 percent of phosphorus targets, and 57 percent of nitrogen targets across the seven watershed jurisdictions as of 2025. The order points to the smallest projected dead zone since 1985, at 31 percent below the long-term average, as evidence of gains under the 2025 revised watershed agreement. It directs federal agencies to shift from the old mandate to new practices that avoid local stormwater fees not tied to measurable bay improvements.

This approach will likely run through the current administration and into any follow-on term that keeps the same agency leadership. Next steps sit with the named departments and the Chesapeake Bay Program partners, who must file updated implementation plans under the 2025 agreement. The order stands until a future president issues a replacement or a court blocks its revocation of the 2009 measure.
