Venezuelan interim president offers details on US oil deal

Venezuelan interim President Delcy Rodríguez announced details of a new 25-year oil agreement with the United States on August 30, 2026. The deal covers development of 17 strategic oilfields and sets a production target above 1.5 million barrels per day. It follows President Trump’s statement that a large share of Venezuela’s oil reserves will come under majority U.S. control. The pact marks the latest step in efforts to restart and expand Venezuelan oil output after years of and production collapse.

Thinking ahead
The deal builds on years of back-channel talks between Caracas and Washington that picked up after the 2024 election reset. Delcy Rodríguez laid out the 25-year framework for 17 fields aiming at 1.5 million barrels a day, matching Trump’s public claim of majority U.S. operational control. Next procedural step is ratification by Venezuela’s National Assembly followed by joint operating agreements between PDVSA and U.S. firms. How much longer this runs depends on whether the Maduro government stays in power and whether Congress in both countries signs off. If either side walks, the whole structure collapses. Observable indicators will be the first set of field-by-field contracts filed and the first tanker loadings under the new terms. Barring a major political rupture in Caracas or Washington, the project is likely to grind forward for at least the next 18–24 months while the first wells are drilled. Horizon: into 2028.
