19:T4c4,Three women who studied at the Autonomous University of the State of Morelos have been killed in separate attacks since February. The latest, Claudia Tacoronte, 21, was found dead on September 19. Local prosecutors have opened homicide files on each case but have not announced arrests or named suspects.

Police in Morelos usually handle these killings through standard criminal investigation steps: gather witness statements, review campus security footage, and check for links between the victims. The pattern of three students from the same school killed in seven months would normally push state officials to assign a special task force or ask for federal help from the attorney general’s office. So far that shift has not happened.

The killings stop when either the perpetrator or perpetrators are caught and charged, or when university and state authorities install visible security changes that alter daily routines on and near campus. Without one of those two moves the file count at UAEM keeps rising under regular homicide procedures.

Next likely step is a joint announcement from the university rector and state prosecutor on whether they will treat the three deaths as connected.
