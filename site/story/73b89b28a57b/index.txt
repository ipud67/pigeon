19:T49a,The Federal Railroad Administration just finalized a rule that scraps a handful of outdated roadway worker safety mandates and sets up a formal process for railroads to pitch and get approval for bridge-worker safety alternatives that match or beat current standards. It also spells out extra training points for crane operators on roadway machines, like keeping proper vertical clearance. This sits at the end of a years-long review ladder where FRA has steadily updated or dropped old Part 214 rules that no longer match how track work gets done.

The rule takes effect August 31, 2026. After that, railroads can start filing special-approval requests, which must go through public notice and FRA sign-off before any alternative can be used. Watch for the first batch of those applications and whether FRA grants or denies them; each decision will test how wide the new pathway really is.

If lawsuits or a new administration direct FRA to reconsider the repeal, the timeline stretches or reverses. Absent that, the new approval pipeline and clarified training language become standard operating procedure.
