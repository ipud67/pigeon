New French ambassador to US cleared to take up post after diplomatic spat

France and the United States have kept ambassadors in each other's capitals without interruption since they restored full diplomatic ties in 1944. Presidents in Paris name the , the host government grants approval known as agrément, and the appointee then presents in Washington to begin work.

On September 16 the State Department confirmed that agrément had been granted to Aurélien Lechevallier, the career diplomat chosen by Emmanuel Macron to replace Laurent Bili. The approval came after Paris issued an apology for public criticism it had directed at Washington over a recent vote in the United Nations.

Lechevallier is now expected to travel to Washington in the coming weeks, take up the post, and resume normal embassy operations between the two governments.
