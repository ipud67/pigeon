The experiment holds 134 medicines and molecules plus matched controls that will fly to the ISS in December on Northrop Grumman’s NG-25 resupply flight. Samples go into two spots on the station with different radiation levels, get pulled back to Earth in three batches across three years, and run alongside heavy-ion tests at the GSI facility in Germany to build a dataset of more than 1,500 points.

The work sits on the ladder of earlier ISS drug-stability checks that showed faster potency loss but could not pin down radiation as the driver. This run isolates radiation by comparing orbit samples, ground controls, and ground-simulated cosmic-ray hits while testing new protective formulas at the same time.

The next clear step is the December launch, then station operations and the first sample return. The project keeps moving as long as the hardware passes NASA safety checks and the Northrop Grumman flight schedule holds. It shifts only if a launch scrub, station anomaly, or sample-return delay forces a reset of the three-year timeline.
