19:T5fa,Cuba’s power grid went down nationwide on September 19 after a transmission fault hit central parts of the island during bad weather. The energy ministry called it a total disconnection. Restoration started that evening in priority zones around hospitals and water stations in Havana and continued into Saturday. 

This marks the sixth major outage so far in 2026. The country’s aging plants and fuel imports have been squeezed tighter since the start of the year when the US began seizing oil shipments headed for Cuba and threatened tariffs on any nation that kept sending them. Venezuela used to supply about 35,000 barrels a day until its own flows stopped after the US took Maduro in January. 

Blackouts now run for 24 hours or more in many neighborhoods, cutting water, cooking gas, hospital equipment, and forcing schools and offices to shut. Residents report the gaps have grown from once a month to nearly constant. The pattern has already triggered small unauthorized protests that carry jail time under Cuban law. Havana keeps blaming the fuel shortage on US sanctions; Washington keeps saying the problem is mismanagement inside Cuba.

The next visible steps are continued piecemeal restoration and another cycle of repairs on the same worn equipment. Without new fuel arrivals or major grid upgrades the frequency stays high. The only recorded change that has ever paused the cycle in the past was a temporary lift in oil deliveries.
