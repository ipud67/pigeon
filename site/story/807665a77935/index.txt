21:T5f4,Ukraine's 3rd Army Corps dropped kamikaze unmanned ground vehicles more than 10 kilometers behind Russian lines in northern Donetsk Oblast using heavy bomber drones. The move formed part of the Vivaldi offensive that started on 15 September and has so far taken the villages of Serednie, Shandryholove, Derylove plus cleared ground around Korovii Yar, Oleksandrivka, Novoselivka, Yarova and Drobysheve for a total of 125 square kilometers regained. Electronic warfare and air defense assets blinded Russian reconnaissance drones during the operation.

This marks the first recorded use of air-dropped kamikaze UGVs in this theater. The Corps states the robots advance first to limit infantry exposure. Similar drone-delivered ground systems appeared once before on the Kinburn Spit in July but traveled by water, not air.

The tactic depends on bomber drone range, EW coverage, and the ability to keep follow-on infantry in contact with the robots. Russian forces hold prepared positions further north and retain drone and artillery reach that can target both the drop zones and the advancing Ukrainian units. Without sustained air and electronic dominance the drops stay one-off events.

Next moves hinge on whether the Corps repeats the air drops at scale or shifts to ground-launched UGVs as the front line firms up. Russian adjustment of drone orbits and jamming would limit the window for such strikes.
