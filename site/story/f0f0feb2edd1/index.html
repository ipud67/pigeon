Trump honors 9/11 ‘man in the red bandana’ Welles Crowther with posthumous Medal of Freedom

Welles Crowther worked as an equities trader on the 104th floor of the South Tower of the World Trade Center. On the morning of September 11 2001 he carried a red bandana he had received from his father as a child. United Airlines Flight 175 hit the building at 9:03 a.m. and smoke and fire filled the upper floors.

Crowther made his way down to the 78th-floor sky lobby where people were trapped. He organized groups to follow him through the smoke to a stairwell that was still passable and carried an injured woman on his back. He returned twice more to bring others out before the tower collapsed at 9:59 a.m. Officials later credited him with helping 18 people reach safety. His body was recovered in March 2002.

On September 8 2026 President Trump awarded Crowther the Presidential Medal of Freedom posthumously. The honor placed Crowther among other civilians recognized for actions taken on that day and gave formal national recognition to the account that survivors had told since the first weeks after the attacks. The medal now rests with his family in New York.
