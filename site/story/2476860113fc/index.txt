1b:T549,Four Muslim doctors filed a federal lawsuit in Chicago on September 17 against Illinois officials. They say the End-of-Life Options for Terminally Ill Patients Act forces them to give patients details on medical aid in dying and transfer records in some cases. The law took effect five days earlier and already lets terminally ill adults with six months or less to live request self-administered drugs while stating doctors do not have to deliver the drugs themselves.

The suit follows the same pattern as earlier religious-objector cases against similar laws in other states. It names the Becket Fund as the legal group handling the complaint in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois. The doctors want the court to block the information and referral rules or rule them unconstitutional.

This type of case usually moves through motions to dismiss, discovery on the burden to religious practice, then a preliminary-injunction hearing. The timeline stretches into 2027 unless the state settles or the judge narrows the issues early. Next step is the state's answer or motion to dismiss within weeks.

The suit lasts at least 12 to 18 months before any final ruling or appeal. It heads toward a hearing on whether the law's requirements count as a substantial burden under federal religious-freedom rules.
