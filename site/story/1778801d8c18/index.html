Óscar López says AI developers' warnings support Spain's push to regulate

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlineÓscar López sostiene que las alertas de desarrolladores de IA avalan la apuesta de regular

Spain began shaping rules for artificial companies in 2023 when the European Union started work on its AI Act. Madrid pushed for stricter oversight on high-risk systems than some member states wanted. The Spanish government kept that line through two years of negotiations even as developers in the United States warned that unchecked models could produce harmful results.

On September 17 2026 Óscar López stated that those same developers' public cautions now confirm the Spanish position. The minister for digital transformation told reporters in Madrid that the industry's own risk assessments match the case his government made during EU talks for binding requirements on transparency, data quality and human oversight.

The record now shows alignment between parts of the industry and the regulators who wrote the final text. What happens next turns on how those rules are enforced once the AI Act takes full effect across the bloc.

Thinking ahead
Spain's government has backed tighter AI rules for years. On September 17 2026, Digital Transformation Minister Óscar López pointed to recent public warnings from AI developers on safety risks as fresh backing for that position. The comments tie directly to ongoing EU-level talks on the AI Act and Spain's own national plans to set up oversight bodies. The push stays alive as long as EU member states keep negotiating specifics on high-risk systems and enforcement timelines. Next step is the scheduled review meetings in Brussels later this fall where Spain plans to table its enforcement blueprint. The situation shifts only if a blocking coalition of member states forms or if the Commission delays the Act's rollout past the current 2027 target. Horizon: 12 to 18 months of steady procedural talks.
