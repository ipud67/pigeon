TBILISI
September 17, 2026 · 10:40 UTC

Trump Tower Tbilisi project kicks off in Georgia, backed by Archi Group, other partners

Construction of luxury towers and districts in central Tbilisi has operated as a main channel for foreign real estate investment since the early 2000s. Archi Group, founded in 2006 by Meni Benish, Ilia Tsulaia and Irakli Kapianidze, became one of the largest developers in the country. Last year Israeli buyers put roughly $200 million into about 2,000 apartments across Georgia, 500 of them sold by Archi. In recent weeks Israel's ambassador to Georgia joined meetings with senior officials that stressed further Israeli capital flows into real estate and protections for those investments.

On September 17 the Trump Organization, Archi Group and other partners broke ground on the first Trump-branded tower in the Caucasus. The 280-meter, 65-floor Trump Tower will anchor a 600,000-square-meter district that includes five more towers, hotels, a business center and retail space. Prime Minister Irakli Kobakhidze attended the launch event for roughly 1,000 guests under heavy security. The ceremony ended with fireworks and drones that formed the US flag and Statue of Liberty above the city skyline at a reported cost of $1 million. The full project carries an estimated $2 billion price tag.

The district is now set to open in stages, with the Trump Tower scheduled for completion in 2031 and the entire site in 2033. Officials stated the work will add thousands of positions in construction, tourism and services while altering the capital skyline.

Thinking ahead
The Trump Tower Tbilisi launch on September 17 2026 marks the latest step in a 20-year run for Archi Group, which started with its founding in 2006 and grew through last year’s sale of 500 apartments to Israeli buyers out of 2,000 total Israeli purchases in Georgia. The $2 billion project with the Trump Organization and partners will put up six towers on a 600,000-square-meter site in central Tbilisi, anchored by a 280-meter, 65-floor Trump Tower slated to open in 2031 and the full district in 2033. Prime Minister Kobakhidze tied the build to thousands of new jobs in construction, tourism, and services, while Israeli Ambassador Walid Abu Haya’s recent meetings with Georgian officials on investment protections and real-estate ties supplied the visible diplomatic backing.
