The latest rung on this ladder is a JPL-led study published September 16 2026 in Geophysical Journal International that combines laser ranging from LAGEOS satellites with GPS data from low-Earth-orbit satellites and accounts for crustal deformation under changing surface loads. Prior estimates from 2017 and 2023 differed by 7 millimeters, roughly the size of the annual swing itself. The new method aims to tighten that uncertainty by using more evenly distributed tracking targets and loading corrections.

This work will continue as long as satellite navigation and elevation data require a stable reference point. The next step is peer review of the full dataset and incorporation of the technique into ongoing international reference-frame updates. The oscillation itself will keep occurring every year with the seasons; only a major unmodeled change in global water distribution or a new class of tracking hardware would alter the measurement cadence.
