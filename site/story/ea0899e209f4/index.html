Two Democrats cross aisle to help tee up final vote on Russia bill

The House has used targeted powers on nations that buy Russian oil and gas since lawmakers first wrote them into law in 2017. Those measures sat alongside a broader set of Iran sanctions that Congress renewed on a regular schedule. Both packages required fresh procedural steps each time lawmakers wanted to update or expand them.

On September 15, 2026, two Democrats joined Republicans to pass the latest version of the Russia Sanctions Act through a key committee vote. The bill hands the president new authority to place tariffs on any country that keeps purchasing Russian energy. It also extends the existing Iran sanctions for another period without change.

The measure now moves to the House floor for a final vote. Its passage there would send the updated sanctions language to the Senate, where it would need approval before it could reach the president’s desk.

Constitutional analysis
The Constitution gives Congress the power to regulate commerce with foreign nations and to lay duties under Article I, Section 8, Clause 3 and Clause 1. The House advanced the Russia Sanctions Act on September 15, 2026, with two Democrats joining to clear a procedural hurdle for a final vote. The bill grants the president tariff authority on countries that buy Russian energy and extends existing Iran sanctions. The founders designed tariffs as a tool of national policy, as seen in the Tariff Act of 1789 passed by the First Congress to protect American interests and raise revenue. Washington used economic pressure and militia force in the Whiskey Rebellion of 1794 to enforce federal law at home, showing the priority of securing peace through clear authority. This measure puts pressure on adversaries through commerce power rather than standing armies, consistent with the Anti-Federalist warnings and the Militia Acts of 1792 that kept defense close to the people. The vote keeps the decision in elected hands instead of agency overreach.
Thinking ahead

It sits as the latest step in a long-running sanctions ladder that has added layers against Russia since the 2022 invasion. The measure now heads to a final House vote, then the Senate. Passage odds look solid in the current Congress, but implementation rests on how the executive branch uses the new tariff tool. Treasury yields sit at 4.11% for the 3-month bill, 4.67% for the 2-year note, 5% for the 10-year, 5.36% for the 30-year, and the 2s10s spread at 33 basis points; none of those figures tie directly to this bill's path. This thing moves forward in weeks if the Senate takes it up before adjournment. Next clear marker is the final House roll call and any White House statement on enforcement. Horizon: 4-8 weeks to enactment or stall.
