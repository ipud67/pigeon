Government must create enabling environment for the macadamia sector

South Africa’s macadamia growers have operated inside a national export agriculture policy that pairs government trade negotiations with private production and processing. That framework dates to the end of apartheid-era controls in the 1990s when the sector expanded from a handful of farms in Limpopo and Mpumalanga into a commercial crop reaching KwaZulu-Natal.

On 18 September 2026 Deputy Minister of Trade Industry and Competition John Steenhuisen told the Macadamias South Africa regional meeting in Umhlanga that government must remove specific blocks on market access logistics investment and skills. He listed those blocks as the remaining barriers that keep South African nuts from competing on price and volume in established and new markets.

The speech sets the next test for the partnership between the department and the growers association. It turns on whether the listed constraints are eased through joint work on sanitary rules port operations and tariff talks so that existing farms can hold share while new and small-scale producers enter the supply chain.

Thinking ahead
Government told the macadamia growers at the SAMAC MacDay on the Road event in Umhlanga on September 18 that it would work on market access, tariffs, port efficiency, logistics, rules, investment, and skills to let the sector compete evenly. This fits the pattern of four regional grower meetings SAMAC is running through September to bring its platform closer to producers in key areas. The deputy minister pointed to ongoing talks on sanitary rules and tariffs with partners like China for cherries and flagged India as a place where tariff walls still cut into South African nut pricing power. The talk stays at the level of stated priorities with no new signed deals or changed rules reported. Port partnerships at Durban are listed as one concrete lever already in motion. No fresh data on actual export volumes, new market openings, or tariff cuts came out of the session. Indicators are too thin to forecast how long the push will run or what exact policy changes will land next. The event is one stop on a known four-event schedule that ends this month. Horizon: too few concrete markers to set a date.
