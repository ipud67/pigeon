Juan Velit on Americas Shield: I do not see an explicit threat to our sovereignty

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlineJuan Velit sobre Escudo de las Américas: No veo explícitamente amenaza a nuestra soberanía

Peru has joined a string of Latin American countries in security agreements with the United States that focus on shared military and police training. Those deals stretch back more than two decades and have centered on joint operations against organized crime networks that move cocaine through the Andes and into North America.

On September 12 Juan Velit Granda, an international-relations analyst who has advised Peruvian governments on regional , said the newest agreement known as Americas Shield carries no explicit language that would let foreign forces override Peruvian command inside the country. He described the as one built to pool intelligence, patrol river borders, and run missions against drug labs and trafficking routes that cross multiple nations.

The statement comes as Congress debates of the pact and as opposition lawmakers argue that any permanent U.S. presence on Peruvian soil would limit the armed forces’ freedom of action. The government has not yet released the full text of the accord, so lawmakers continue to examine whether the operational clauses allow joint command structures or only liaison teams.
