Honduras: capital's water reserves below 26%

Translated from Italian · read the original →
The original headlineHonduras, riserve d'acqua della capitale sotto il 26%

Tegucigalpa draws its municipal water from two main reservoirs that have supplied the capital for decades. Those reservoirs dropped into a declared critical-risk phase this week as stored volume fell below 26 percent of capacity.

The latest measurement on September 16 showed the combined level at 25.8 percent. Officials cut daily distribution hours across neighborhoods and ordered tanker trucks to priority sites after the reading crossed the emergency threshold set by the national water authority.

The city now rations supply to roughly four hours per zone on alternate days while crews search for leaks in the aging pipe network. Further decline in the reservoirs will force deeper cuts until seasonal rains arrive.

Thinking ahead
The two main reservoirs that feed Tegucigalpa sit below 26 percent capacity right now. City crews have already cut pressure and hours in the distribution network to stretch what is left. Officials say the next procedural step is to truck in water from outlying wells and rivers while they wait for the rainy season to recharge the lakes. Rainfall totals would have to stay above seasonal averages for several weeks straight to lift levels back into the safe operating range. Without that, rationing tightens further and more neighborhoods lose piped supply for days at a time. This runs at least until sustained rain arrives or emergency wells come online. Horizon: 4–8 weeks.
