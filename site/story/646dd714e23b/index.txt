18:T5fc,The Constitution grants Congress the power to lay and collect taxes, duties, imposts, and excises under Article I, Section 8, Clause 1, and to regulate commerce with foreign nations under the same section. The founders designed these powers to raise revenue for the common defense and general welfare while protecting domestic industry from sudden foreign floods that could harm American producers. George Washington signed the Tariff Act of 1789, the first major law passed by the new Congress, which set duties on imports to encourage home manufacturing and farming without starving the treasury. In this case the president used existing trade statutes to expand beef import quotas from Argentina by tens of thousands of metric tons, citing high consumer prices after a natural disaster and herd contraction. The order rests on the claim that domestic lean beef supply falls short of demand at reasonable prices, a judgment monitored by executive officials under 19 U.S.C. 3601(c). Ranchers who raise cattle on American soil supply the food chain, hold private property in land and livestock, and form part of the independent yeoman class the founders saw as the backbone of self-government. Expanding foreign quotas to lower ground-beef prices shifts supply away from those domestic producers and risks further pressure on an industry already at its smallest herd size in decades. 1b:T40e,The executive order on September 4 continues the ladder that began with Proclamation 11010 in February, which raised the tariff-rate quota on lean beef trimmings from Argentina by 80,000 metric tons for 2026 to ease ground-beef prices after a natural disaster and market disruption. Monitoring under 19 U.S.C. 3601(c) since then showed the extra imports helped but did not offset herd contraction to a 75-year low and ongoing limits on live cattle from Mexico due to New World Screwworm controls. The new order directs further steps to support ranchers and domestic supply.

This action is likely to run at least through the end of 2026 while the phased reopening of southern cattle ports continues and herd rebuilding takes multiple seasons. It points next toward possible additional quota adjustments or import rule changes if the senior official’s quarterly supply checks show prices staying elevated. No Treasury yield figures from September 14 bear on the beef supply chain.
