Lonnie Bunch, Smithsonian leader, to retire amid Trump criticism

The Smithsonian Institution has operated as a network of museums and research centers in Washington since 1846 under a board of regents that includes the vice president, chief justice, and members of Congress. Lonnie Bunch, a historian who began his career at the institution in the 1980s, became its secretary in 2019 after directing the National Museum of African American History and Culture from its planning through its 2016 opening.

Bunch told the board on Tuesday that he will step down at the end of 2026. The announcement came weeks after President Trump and several Republican lawmakers publicly criticized the Smithsonian for what they described as exhibits and programming that presented biased views of American history.

The departure leaves the regents to select a new secretary while the institution continues to manage 21 museums, a zoo, and multiple research facilities on an annual federal of roughly one billion dollars.
