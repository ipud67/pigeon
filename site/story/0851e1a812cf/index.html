SRI LANKA
August 31, 2026 · 12:20 UTC

Sri Lanka’s ex-finance minister arrested over alleged corruption

Overview
Ravi Karunanayake allegedly used state funds to pay his private employees.
Short history
Sri Lanka arrested its former finance minister Ravi Karunanayake on 31 August 2026. Police say he used state funds to pay salaries of his private employees. The arrest follows years of probes into financial dealings by officials who served during the 2015-2019 government. Karunanayake held the finance post from 2015 to 2017 before moving to another cabinet role. He has faced repeated investigations since the change of government in 2019.
Predictive model
Ravi Karunanayake, who served as finance minister years back, was arrested Monday on claims he funneled state money to cover salaries for his own private staff. The arrest sits on the long ladder of Sri Lankan probes into former officials from the post-2015 governments, where similar graft cases have dragged through police filings, court summons, and occasional bail hearings without quick endings. Next concrete step is formal charges and magistrate hearings; those usually stretch months while evidence is argued over and lawyers file motions. How long it runs depends on whether the attorney general’s office keeps pushing fresh documents or if witnesses start to waver. If the case stays narrow to payroll transfers it could stay stuck in pretrial arguments through 2027. A shift would need either a clear plea deal or a judge tossing the file for lack of direct proof. It stays alive as long as the current government keeps the file active. Horizon: likely 12–18 months before any verdict or quiet fade.
