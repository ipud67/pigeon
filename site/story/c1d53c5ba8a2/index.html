The European Union says Statement by President von der Leyen on the derogation for Ukraine to purchase crucial products for Patriot air defence systems

The United States and Israel began strikes on Iran in late February 2026. The war settled into weekly exchanges that spread to Lebanon and naval clashes in the Strait of Hormuz. A June memorandum lifted the dual blockades and opened a 60-day negotiating window but collapsed on 8 July and the US resumed sustained strikes.

On 8 September 2026 European Union member states agreed to a that lets Ukraine buy critical components for air defence systems from suppliers inside the bloc. The European Commission announced the decision in Brussels alongside NATO Secretary General Rutte.

The derogation gives Ukraine direct access to European-made parts that had been restricted under standard rules. It sits inside the ongoing US-led pressure on Iran and the realignment of Gulf states toward the US alliance that followed Iranian missile launches and the closure attempts on Hormuz.

Thinking ahead
EU member states agreed to let Ukraine buy key parts for Patriot air defense systems under a new derogation. Von der Leyen and NATO chief Rutte both backed the move in a joint statement from Brussels. This fits the pattern of steady Western military aid that has run since the first shipments in 2022. The derogation removes one regulatory block on spare parts and components. Next step is for European suppliers to fill the orders and ship them. The flow continues as long as member states keep approving these carve-outs and Ukraine holds positions that need the systems. It stays in place until either the war ends or a new EU vote reverses the exemption. No visible signs yet of that reversal. Horizon: another 4 to 6 months of similar aid packages.
