18:T453,The Constitution gives Congress the power of the purse in Article I, Section 9, Clause 7: “No Money shall be drawn from the Treasury, but in Consequence of Appropriations made by Law.” The founders designed this to keep the executive on a short leash and force regular accountability to the people’s representatives. The First Congress passed the Appropriations Act of 1789 to fund the new government for a single year, setting the pattern of annual budgets rather than open-ended spending. Washington and the early Congresses treated each funding cycle as a fresh chance to review what the government was actually doing. H.R. 6500 keeps every federal agency running on autopilot through December 11 without new debate over priorities or waste. This continuing resolution hands the executive branch months of blank-check authority that the framers never intended. The founders expected lawmakers to weigh each dollar against the enumerated powers and refuse the rest.
