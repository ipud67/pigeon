ISLAMABAD
September 10, 2026 · 12:34 UTC

'Is judiciary so weak that you wish to strengthen it?' IHC CJ asks as KP counsel justifies PTI’s Sept 27 march

The Islamabad High Court has heard petitions challenging the federal government's orders that restricted public gatherings and imposed section 144 in the capital ahead of a planned protest. Those orders took effect after the PTI called for a nationwide demonstration set for September 27. The party framed the event as a demand for the release of its founder Imran Khan and for the supremacy of the Constitution.

On September 10 the chief justice of the Islamabad High Court asked the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa advocate general why the provincial government was supporting the march if its stated purpose was to strengthen the judiciary. The remark came after the advocate general told the bench that the protest would back the courts. The chief justice questioned whether the judiciary required such outside support and warned that the argument risked undermining the institution it claimed to defend.

The court continued hearing arguments on the validity of the gathering ban. The PTI protest call remains in place while the bench weighs the petitions against the restrictions. The outcome of the case will determine whether the September 27 demonstration can proceed in Islamabad under the current orders.

Thinking ahead
The Islamabad High Court chief justice pushed back on the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa advocate general's claim that the PTI's Sept 27 march backs the judiciary. The exchange happened during arguments over the protest call, which PTI framed as a push for Imran Khan's release and constitutional supremacy. The court has not issued any order halting the march or citing contempt so far. This kind of courtroom friction between lawyers and judges has flared before when political parties tie street action to judicial independence. The next visible step is further hearings or written replies from the KP government on how the march will be managed. The situation shifts only if the court sets clear limits on protest language or if PTI leaders file fresh applications citing this exchange. The back-and-forth stays inside the courtroom for now. No street action indicators have moved yet. Horizon: Another 2–3 weeks of hearings before any march-related order.
