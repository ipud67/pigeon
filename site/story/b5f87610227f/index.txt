The pair used stolen identities, listed dead people as riders, and claimed unlicensed people as drivers to pull in more than $13 million from DOT and HUD pass-through grants between July 2019 and October 2025. Watts started the scheme alone; Pino-Copete joined after their 2023 marriage in Colombia, entered the US on a visa in January 2025, and kept filing false claims even after her July 2025 wire-fraud arrest.

Sentencing is the next scheduled step. Both now face up to 20 years each on the main counts. The case sits inside the standing Trump Administration Task Force to Eliminate Fraud, which has produced similar convictions in other states this year. No appeals have been filed yet. The only visible change would come from a successful appeal or a sentence reduction deal.

This one likely runs another 4 to 8 months until final prison terms are set and any immediate appeals are docketed. After that the story ends unless one of them cuts a cooperation deal that opens new cases.
