The UN human-rights office counts at least 17,257 civilians killed and 53,693 injured across the conflict since February 2022, with children making up 835 of the dead and 3,247 of the injured; those numbers are acknowledged to be undercounts. Strikes have hit Kyiv and other rear cities almost daily in recent weeks, along with rail lines, shopping centers, gas stations, warehouses, food companies, and energy plants.

The pattern shows no sign of easing as winter nears and both sides target each other’s power grid. The UN call for an immediate moratorium on attacks on populated areas and energy infrastructure sets a procedural marker: either side could declare a pause, but none has. Continued high civilian casualty counts in the September data would confirm the trend holds; a sustained drop would mark a shift in targeting.

This latest rung sits on the established sequence of rear-area strikes that began after the initial 2022 invasion phase and have grown more frequent as front lines stabilized.

The situation is likely to run at least through the coming winter unless one side halts strikes on cities and power infrastructure first. Next indicator is the October casualty update from the UN human-rights office.
