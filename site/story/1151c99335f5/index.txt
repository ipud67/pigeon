1b:T540,Trump stated on September 21 that he wants to buy potash from Belarus at a price substantially lower than what Canada charges. Saskatchewan Premier Scott Moe called the idea blood potash because Belarus faces sanctions from Canada and dozens of other countries for backing Russia's actions in Ukraine. The statement fits a pattern of Trump pressing for cheaper input costs for US farmers after years of tariffs and supply chain fights.

This approach runs into logistics that add cost and time. Potash would move from Belarus through Russia, load onto ships using fuel now priced high, then cross to US ports before reaching Midwest farms. Canadian supply already flows by rail and lake freighter with established contracts and no sanctions layer.

The situation stays in place until either side changes its posture. Belarus keeps output steady under existing sanctions. Canada holds its price and supply terms. US buyers stick with contracts unless Trump directs federal agencies to loosen import rules or offer new subsidies that offset the added shipping. Without those steps the gap between talk and delivered tons stays wide.

Next rung comes from any formal US move on sanctions relief or a Canadian cut in export volume to test demand. No such step has surfaced yet.
