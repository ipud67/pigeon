Six Israeli cities ban live chickens for Yom Kippur kapparot, animal rights org. urges more to join

Six Israeli cities now prohibit live chickens in the kapparot ceremony that many Jews perform in the days before Yom Kippur. The practice requires a person to swing a live chicken overhead while reciting prayers that symbolically transfer sins, after which the bird is slaughtered for food or charity. Veterinary Services rules have long let each local authority decide whether to grant permits for ceremonies that use live animals inside its .

On 14 September 2026 Let the Animals Live said Tel Mond, Yeruham, Mevaseret Zion and Yavne had joined the ban this year while Kiryat Bialik and Rishon Lezion renewed the bans they put in place last year. The six add to seven cities that have barred the practice for several years: Kfar Saba, Ra'anana, Tel Aviv-Jaffa, Beersheba, Ashkelon, Ramle and Petah Tikva. The group first wrote to dozens of leaders in 2025 and repeated the appeal ahead of this Yom Kippur.

The bans mean organizers in those jurisdictions cannot obtain the required veterinary-department authorization and cannot use public space for the live-chicken rite. The organization continues to press remaining authorities to refuse permits and to require charity donations in place of the ceremony.
