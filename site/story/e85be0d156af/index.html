DETROIT
November 19, 2026

Ruby Scott got 4 years in prison for 1.6 million dollar Medicare fraud in Michigan

Ruby Scott owned and operated Delta Home Health Care LLC in Michigan. From 2018 through 2021 the nurse paid a discharge nurse at a Detroit hospital between $270 and $300 for each Medicare patient the hospital worker identified. The discharge nurse faxed the patients' confidential records to Delta without their knowledge. Scott then used those records to submit bills to Medicare for home health services.

On May 2026 a jury in Detroit convicted Scott of five counts of health care , one count of to the United States and pay illegal health care kickbacks, and four counts of paying illegal health care kickbacks. The evidence showed she paid more than $130,000 in kickbacks through CashApp, PayPal, checks and cash. She billed for patients who never received services, forged doctor certifications for patients who were never evaluated, and used real doctors' names on claims without their knowledge.

The court ordered her to pay Medicare $1,652,130.67 in restitution and to forfeit the same amount. The case was investigated by the FBI Detroit Field Office and the Department of Health and Human Services Office of Inspector General.

Ruby Scott got four years in federal prison Wednesday in Detroit after a jury convicted her in May on five counts of health care fraud, one count of conspiracy to defraud the United States and pay illegal kickbacks, and four counts of paying those kickbacks. She ran Delta Home Health Care LLC, paid a hospital discharge nurse $270 to $300 per patient for stolen Medicare records between 2018 and 2021, billed for services that were never delivered, forged doctor certifications, and kept no files for more than a third of the claims that brought in over $1.2 million. The judge ordered her to pay $1,652,130.67 in restitution and the same amount in forfeiture. The case now moves to the Bureau of Prisons for designation to a facility. The restitution and forfeiture orders stay in place regardless; collection proceeds through the U.S. Attorney’s Office and will continue after she is released. This particular prosecution ends here unless Scott files a successful appeal that overturns the verdict.
