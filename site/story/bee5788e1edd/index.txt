The visit runs through Saturday. Cho said he will spell out what Seoul can deliver while holding the line on steps that clash with its own interests or procedures. No joint statement or signed addendum has been announced. The next visible rung is whatever readout the two sides issue after the Friday meeting and whether Seoul names a specific investment or sets a Hormuz timeline in the days that follow.

This round of talks is likely to run its three-day schedule and wrap without a final Hormuz deployment order or a signed investment contract. Progress shows up only if Seoul announces a named project or the ministry confirms a revised 123 framework. Without those concrete steps the pattern of repeated consultations continues into the next scheduled security meeting.
