1c:T633,The family of Renee Good filed a civil lawsuit on October 1 naming the United States as defendant along with named officials including the ICE agent who fired the shots, Stephen Miller, and former Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem. The suit claims the January 7 shooting during protests against Operation Metro Surge in Minneapolis amounted to unconstitutional force against people exercising First Amendment rights. It follows the established pattern of civil actions after high-profile encounters between federal agents and observers in that operation.

These cases typically move through federal district court with motions to dismiss on qualified immunity or sovereign immunity grounds. Discovery would require the government to turn over internal memos, training materials, and body-camera records from the Minneapolis deployment. Settlement talks often start once depositions of line agents and supervisors are scheduled, which usually happens 12 to 24 months after filing.

The suit stays alive as long as the plaintiffs keep clearing procedural hurdles and the named officials remain in office or retain personal exposure. If the court narrows the claims to only the individual shooter, the case shrinks to a single excessive-force claim. If it survives summary judgment, trial is likely three to four years out.

Next likely step is the government's answer or motion to dismiss within 60 days, followed by arguments over whether the complaint states a plausible claim under Bivens or the Federal Tort Claims Act.
