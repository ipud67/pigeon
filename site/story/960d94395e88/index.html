Some 20 Israelis trapped in northern Ethiopia as combat in country intensifies - report

Fighting between Ethiopian government forces and armed groups in the Amhara region has grown since the summer and now blocks movement in and out of the northern city of Lalibela.

On 27 September 2026 about 20 Israelis who had been staying in Lalibela found themselves unable to leave after all local flights were grounded and the roads toward Addis Ababa were closed by the intensified combat. The group, which includes elderly people, told KAN News they still move freely inside the city itself, have food and shelter, but hear gunfire and explosions from the outskirts, have lost electricity, and are running low on cash while charging phones with generators. One said the main worry is that any of the older members who need medical care cannot get it if the cutoff lasts.

The Israeli Foreign Ministry said it is tracking the Israelis in the affected northern areas, remains in contact with them, and is looking for ways to get them out as security conditions allow. Ethiopian-Israelis in Israel reported similar isolation for relatives still living in the Amhara city of Gondar, where houses have been hit by gunfire and movement is sharply restricted. On Friday the ministry had already warned Israelis against travel to the neighboring Tigray region and told those already there to avoid nonessential movement and gatherings. Ethiopian Airlines has suspended all flights to the three main airports in the north.

Thinking ahead
About 20 Israelis remain stuck in Lalibela, Amhara region, after Ethiopian army clashes with armed groups cut off flights and roads out. The group reports occasional gunfire and explosions from the city edge, steady food and shelter inside, but no electricity and shrinking cash. One said the elderly in the party face the biggest risk if medical needs arise and the standoff drags on. Israeli Foreign Ministry says it tracks the situation, stays in touch, and checks exit routes with local sides as conditions shift. Ethiopian-Israeli contacts in Gondar describe fear, limited movement, homes damaged by fire, and one neighbor killed in that city. The fighting in northern Ethiopia has flared again in the past week, closing the main routes and grounding planes in the area. Ethiopian Airlines halted flights to Mekelle, Axum, and Shire. The Foreign Ministry issued a travel warning for Tigray on Friday and told those already there to avoid nonessential moves and gatherings. This flare-up sits apart from the US-Iran armed conflict that began with strikes in late February 2026, narrowed to the Strait of Hormuz, saw a June memorandum open a 60-day negotiating window that collapsed on 8 July, and brought resumed US strikes after Iran hit commercial tankers and tried to close the strait. The Israelis will stay trapped until either the army regains control of the roads and airport or a third party arranges safe passage. The situation could widen if the armed groups push deeper into Amhara or if the government answers with larger operations that cut more routes. It could ease if local talks produce a pause that reopens the Lalibela-Addis road. Next likely step is continued monitoring and contact by Israeli officials while the Ethiopian army tries to clear the immediate combat zone around the city. Horizon estimate: trapped at least another 7-14 days unless roads reopen sooner.
