22:T62f,The last US and UK troops pulled out of Irbil on Wednesday after a deal signed in 2024 set a firm end-of-September deadline for the coalition mission that started in 2014 to help Iraqi forces roll back Islamic State. Iraqi security forces and Kurdish Peshmerga took over training and counter-terror duties years ago, and a UN count now puts remaining Islamic State fighters at a few hundred who can only pull off small attacks. Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi declared four national Sovereignty Days to mark the exit and said the government will now handle all security, including plans to bring Iranian-backed militias under state control.

The withdrawal follows the 2017 Iraqi declaration that Islamic State lost its territory, after which the coalition stayed on to stop any comeback. Pentagon and UK Ministry of Defence statements both list the mission as complete and shift primary responsibility to Baghdad. Al-Zaidi took office in May with a public pledge to disarm those militias, but several factions have pushed back.

Iraqi forces keep up daily counter-terror sweeps in former Islamic State pockets. No new coalition bases or large foreign troop numbers are on the table right now. Any shift would need either a surge in Islamic State attacks that Iraqi units cannot contain or a formal request from Baghdad for fresh outside help.

This setup looks set to hold for at least the next 12 to 18 months unless militia resistance blocks al-Zaidi's weapons-control moves or Islamic State stages coordinated operations beyond current low-level hits.
