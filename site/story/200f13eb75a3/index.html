Last UK and US troops leave Iraq as anti-Islamic State mission ends

The US-led first entered Iraq in 2014 to conduct air strikes and train local forces against Islamic State fighters who had seized territory across the north and west that year. British and American troops stayed on after Iraqi forces retook the last Islamic State-held city in 2017, shifting to advisory roles and joint operations under the name Operation Inherent Resolve to keep the group from regaining ground.

On September 30 2026 the final contingent of about 100 British personnel left the airbase at Irbil in the Kurdistan region, departing on the same day as the last US troops. The exit followed a bilateral agreement signed with the Iraqi government two years earlier that set an end-of-month deadline for the complete withdrawal of coalition combat and training forces.

Iraqi security forces and Kurdish Peshmerga units now hold sole responsibility for countering the several hundred Islamic State fighters assessed by the United Nations as capable only of small-scale attacks. The Iraqi government declared four national Days to mark the end of the foreign military mission and stated that all weapons inside the country must come under state control.
