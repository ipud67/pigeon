24:T4f3,The UN Security Council voted down a resolution to keep the Panel of Experts monitoring Iran sanctions in place. The vote split 11 in favor, two against from permanent members China and Russia, and two abstentions from Pakistan and Somalia — the same pattern seen in procedural votes on this file in March, June, July, and earlier this month. That leaves the 1737 Committee without its independent monitoring arm after the snapback process reinstated the underlying sanctions last September.

The divide over whether the snapback is legally in force has blocked any Council agreement since then. The US, France, UK, and others treat the sanctions as active and want reporting on Iranian compliance. Russia and China maintain the mechanism was never triggered because the US left the 2015 nuclear deal in 2018.

Without a new consensus or a procedural workaround that gains nine votes and avoids two permanent-member no votes, the monitoring gap stays open. The next formal Council discussion is already on the calendar for late October, where the same split is the baseline indicator unless one side shifts its legal stance or a new external event forces a different vote count.
