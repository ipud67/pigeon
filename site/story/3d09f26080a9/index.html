Security Council Fails to Renew Iran Sanctions Panel amid Dispute over ‘Snapback’

The United Nations Security Council endorsed the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action in 2015 under resolution 2231. The agreement gave Iran sanctions relief in return for limits on its nuclear program and a ten-year monitoring system. It also set a snapback process that would automatically restore the earlier sanctions if Iran failed to meet the terms. The United States left the agreement in 2018. Iran then scaled back its own commitments. In September 2025, France, Germany and the United Kingdom declared that Iran's actions had triggered the snapback. The UN Secretariat informed the Council that the lifted sanctions had returned on 27 September 2025 and that the sanctions committee created by resolution 1737 in 2006 was again active.

Russia and China maintained that the snapback had not taken effect because the United States was no longer part of the original agreement. The Council held repeated meetings on the question in March, June, July and early September 2026. Each time a procedural vote to hold the session produced the same split: eleven members in favor, two against from the permanent members China and Russia, and two abstentions from Pakistan and Somalia. On 17 September 2026 the Council held the same procedural vote with identical results. It then voted on a draft resolution to extend the mandate of the Panel of Experts that supports the 1737 Committee. The measure failed because China and Russia voted against it.

The Panel of Experts therefore ends its work. The Council now operates without its main independent source of reporting on sanctions compliance. The 1737 Committee remains formally restored under the snapback, but the permanent members remain divided on whether the underlying sanctions carry any force.

Thinking ahead
The UN Security Council voted down a resolution to keep the Panel of Experts monitoring Iran sanctions in place. The vote split 11 in favor, two against from permanent members China and Russia, and two abstentions from Pakistan and Somalia — the same pattern seen in procedural votes on this file in March, June, July, and earlier this month. That leaves the 1737 Committee without its independent monitoring arm after the snapback process reinstated the underlying sanctions last September. The divide over whether the snapback is legally in force has blocked any Council agreement since then. The US, France, UK, and others treat the sanctions as active and want reporting on Iranian compliance. Russia and China maintain the mechanism was never triggered because the US left the 2015 nuclear deal in 2018. Without a new consensus or a procedural workaround that gains nine votes and avoids two permanent-member no votes, the monitoring gap stays open. The next formal Council discussion is already on the calendar for late October, where the same split is the baseline indicator unless one side shifts its legal stance or a new external event forces a different vote count. This pattern holds at least through the current Council rotation. Horizon: into 2027.
