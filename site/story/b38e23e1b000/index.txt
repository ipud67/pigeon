26:T486,New Zealand parliament voted 93-29 on September 16 to pass the legislation that puts the free trade deal with India into domestic law. The agreement, first signed in April, cuts or removes tariffs on 95 percent of New Zealand exports to India with more than half going duty-free on day one. Two-way trade stood at NZ$3.99 billion in the year to June 2026, and the pact also opens duty-free entry for all Indian goods into New Zealand while locking in a $20 billion New Zealand investment commitment over 15 years.

The deal now sits on the ratification ladder that began with the April signing and moves to the final step once India finishes its own procedures. Trade Minister Todd McClay tied the vote to a promise to deliver the agreement this parliamentary term. No court action or other observable indicator has surfaced that would delay the timetable.

This is likely to wrap up in the next few months once India ratifies. Next rung is formal entry into force later this year, followed by the first tariff cuts and the start of the $20 billion investment schedule. Gap exists on exact Indian ratification date.
