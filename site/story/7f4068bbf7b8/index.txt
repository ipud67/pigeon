17:T596,Ukrainian drone strikes hit the Syzran and Saratov refineries on September 8 and again days later. The Syzran plant's main CDU-6 unit, which handles 17,100 tonnes per day and 71 percent of its capacity, took fresh damage on top of an earlier July strike that already had the backup CDU-5 offline. Saratov shut down after the first hit, tried to restart around September 10-11, and got struck again before it could stabilize. Both sites remain out of normal crude processing as of September 16. Russia faced a one-third petrol shortfall by late August after months of similar strikes, with domestic output covering only about 70 percent of summer needs before imports.

These two plants together process 15.5 million tonnes a year. Repair timelines on the damaged distillation units point to at least another month of reduced runs at Syzran, while Saratov has already logged weeks of downtime in August and now more in September. The pattern of repeated hits on the same sites shows Ukrainian forces can reach deep into Russian territory on a regular schedule. No new indicators yet on whether Russia will add meaningful air defenses or if Ukraine will keep the pace.

The attacks are likely to keep Russian refinery output squeezed for the next four to six weeks at minimum. Next visible rung will be either official restart dates or fresh strikes on the same or nearby plants.
