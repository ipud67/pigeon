United States ordered changes to strengthen the H-1B visa program in Washington

The H-1B nonimmigrant visa program has operated since 1990 under the Immigration and Nationality Act to admit foreign workers for temporary specialty occupations that require theoretical and practical application of a body of highly specialized knowledge. The sets an annual cap on new visas, requires employers to that they will pay the foreign worker at least the actual wage paid to similar workers or the prevailing wage in the occupation and area, and bars the displacement of American workers. Federal agencies including the Department of Labor, the Department of Homeland Security, and the Department of State share responsibility for approving labor condition applications, petitions, and visa issuance.

On September 18 2026 President Trump signed an executive order that directs those agencies to strengthen program integrity and interagency coordination. The order requires the Department of Homeland Security to revise its petition standards to prevent the approval of visas for positions that do not meet the definition of specialty occupation, to increase site visits and audits of employers, and to improve information sharing with the Department of Labor on wage compliance and worker displacement. It also instructs the agencies to tighten oversight of third-party placement firms and outsourcing entities that use H-1B workers at client sites and to expedite investigations into fraud patterns identified in prior probes.

The order now sets new administrative requirements for how the three departments review, investigate, and enforce H-1B petitions and labor condition applications. Agencies must report back within 90 days on the regulatory changes and operational steps they will take to carry out the directives.
