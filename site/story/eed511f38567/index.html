Peru Interior Ministry prepares specialized police brigades to respond to El Niño phenomenon

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlineMininter prepara brigadas policiales especializadas para responder ante fenómeno El Niño

The National Police of Peru have operated under the Ministry of the Interior since the 1990s to handle public order and disaster response across the country. The Ministry maintains an Office of Security and National Defense that coordinates police units with other government agencies during emergencies. Peru sits in a zone where the El Niño climate pattern has triggered heavy rains, flooding and landslides every few years since records began in the 16th century, with the most recent major events in 2017 and 2023.

On September 20 2026 the Interior Ministry announced it is forming specialized police brigades inside that office to move into affected zones as soon as intense rains or related emergencies begin. The brigades receive targeted training and equipment so they can reach isolated communities, secure routes and support rescue operations without waiting for general mobilization orders.

The new units now stand ready inside the existing police command structure, with their deployment tied directly to forecasts of the coming rainy season. The Ministry continues to expand their training and logistics to match the scale of any El Niño-driven events that develop.

Thinking ahead
The Interior Ministry set up specialized police brigades inside the National Police to handle floods, landslides and rescue calls tied to the coming heavy rains from the El Niño pattern. The move builds on routine disaster drills the Office of Security and National Defense runs each year before the rainy season. Training and equipment checks are already under way so the units can deploy within hours of the first alerts. This preparation phase runs through the end of the southern summer. The brigades stay on call as long as the weather service keeps El Niño alerts active, which usually peaks between December and March. Next step is joint drills with regional emergency offices to test radio links and supply routes before the rains hit hard. If satellite readings show the Pacific warming faster than forecast, the brigades will expand shifts and pull in more reserve officers. If the pattern fades early, the units stand down and return to regular patrol duty. Horizon: through March 2027 unless the El Niño signal drops.
