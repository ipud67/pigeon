17:T4e5,Greenland’s government will stand up a Truth and Reconciliation Commission in 2026 to examine the Danish-led forced contraception program that ran from the 1960s into the 1990s. The move follows years of pressure from Inuit groups and comes after a panel of experts could not reach consensus on whether the campaign met the legal definition of genocide. The commission’s mandate is to gather testimony, review records, and produce a public report; no prosecutions or compensation decisions are built into its current charter.

How long it runs will depend on how quickly it can secure funding, hire staff, and get access to sealed Danish archives. Past Greenlandic inquiries on similar historical abuses have taken 18 to 36 months once launched. The next clear procedural step is the formal appointment of commissioners and the publication of their exact terms of reference, expected before the end of 2026.

The situation changes only if the commission’s final report triggers new legislation in either Nuuk or Copenhagen, or if Denmark refuses cooperation on document access. Without those two triggers the process stays inside the commission’s report-writing phase.
