SRI LANKA
October 26, 2026 · 03:47 UTC

Sri Lanka treated and flew 40 Iranian sailors home after medical emergencies off its coast

Sri Lanka has stayed neutral in the US-Iran armed conflict that began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran in late February 2026. The fight narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz after a June memorandum that lifted dual blockades and opened a 60-day negotiating window collapsed on 8 July. Iranian ships have remained at sea in international waters near Sri Lanka since the renewed US naval took hold.

The foreign ministry coordinated with the Iranian Embassy in Colombo, local shipping companies and other agencies to bring 40 sailors ashore from different ships, treat them in Colombo, and fly them back to Iran that night.

The operation followed Sri Lanka's earlier responses to similar calls from Iranian vessels in the same waters, including aid after the March sinking of the Iris Dena. Authorities have drawn the line at supplying the stranded ships themselves, citing national interests when 19 vessels reported shortages earlier this month.

Sri Lanka got a distress call Saturday from Iranian ships in international waters off the southeast coast. Forty sailors from different vessels showed up with unspecified medical problems. Local authorities brought them ashore, treated them in Colombo, and flew them back to Iran the same night. This fits the pattern set since March when Sri Lanka first pulled survivors from the torpedoed Iris Dena and let another Iranian naval ship dock on humanitarian grounds. The country keeps saying it stays neutral, turns down military requests from both sides, and only answers distress calls under international law. Earlier this month it refused food, water, and fuel aid to 19 stranded Iranian ships to protect its own interests. The situation shifts only if the US–Iran fight produces another direct attack near Sri Lankan waters or if Tehran and Washington reach a new agreement that moves the ships. Horizon: another medical extraction within weeks if ships remain stranded.
