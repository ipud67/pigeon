The scheme ran from at least 2021, with brokers like Geralda Adrien steering customers to accredited schools that issued the bogus diplomas and transcripts without training. Undercover buys and a family tip kicked off the case; prosecutors have now charged 43 people, all convicted so far, with sentences running four to six years and forfeiture orders in the millions.

The operation relied on fake testing routed through New York boards while the sellers pocketed cash for luxury homes, cars, and travel. Buyers received paperwork that let them enter healthcare jobs; federal agents say many remain untracked inside hospitals and clinics. With convictions complete, the next procedural step is restitution collection and any remaining asset seizures.

How long this runs depends on how many of the 15,000 credential holders get identified through license audits and hospital record checks. If state boards start pulling licenses at scale, more arrests or civil suits could follow. Without those audits, the trail stays cold.
