23:T5f1,Gunmen crossed by boat from Taraba and Nasarawa into Ayilamo, a farming settlement in Benue’s Logo Local Government Area, on Sept. 10. They killed 11 people — seven civilians and four police officers — before a seven-hour gun battle with state police units forced them back into the surrounding forests. Police have since sent extra tactical teams to the zone. Community accounts describe the attackers speaking Fulani and using religious phrases during the raid, though identities remain unconfirmed by independent checks. Similar strikes hit three spots in Plateau state that same week, with at least 13 more killed on roads and in villages there.  

This pattern of boat crossings, night raids on Christian farm settlements, and quick retreats into border forests has run for years along the Benue–Taraba–Nasarawa line. State police responses have followed the same script: initial clash, reinforcement, then patrols that rarely hold ground once forces thin out. No shift in herder movement corridors or new checkpoints has appeared since the last round of attacks.  

The cycle is likely to keep going at the current pace until either sustained joint patrols block the river crossings or local farming groups form larger armed self-defense units that change the cost of entry. Next likely step is another hit on an exposed village or road in the same triangle before the dry season firms up the ground for wider movement.
