Canada’s government says Parliamentary Secretary Turnbull in Oshawa to highlight recent affordability measures for Canadians

The federal government has spent the past year rolling out a series of targeted spending and tax changes aimed at cutting household and business expenses on groceries, housing, fuel, and payroll. Those changes began with the 2024 fall economic statement and continued through the 2025 budget and several targeted orders that adjusted GST rebates, expanded the Canada Carbon Rebate, and trimmed the small-business tax rate.

On September 8, 2026, Parliamentary Secretary to the Minister of Finance Ryan Turnbull arrived in Oshawa to speak directly about the most recent round of those measures. He laid out how the latest adjustments to the carbon rebate, the grocery rebate top-up, and the employment insurance premium cut flow to families and employers in the region.

The visit marks the latest stop in a cross-country tour that uses local events to tie the new spending rules to everyday costs. The government’s next moves now rest on whether those rebates and rate cuts produce measurable drops in reported household expenses by the end of the year.
