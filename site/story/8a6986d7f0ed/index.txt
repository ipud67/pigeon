25:T66a,The Trump administration opened a joint probe by the Departments of Education and State into Duke University and the University of North Dakota for possible failures to file accurate, timely reports on foreign gifts and contracts over $250,000 under Section 117 of the Higher Education Act. The review cites late filings, omitted details, and at least one case where a Chinese government-linked partner was listed as nongovernmental. Duke’s ties run through its joint campus at Kunshan, governed in part by officials from Wuhan University and China’s defense-related science agencies. North Dakota’s aerospace programs have taken Chinese aviation money tied to military-linked firms while holding major U.S. federal grants in national-security fields.

Rogers said the gaps turned up in routine checks and that both schools are expected to turn over records and work with investigators. The procedural next step is formal document demands, possible on-site audits, and a written response deadline set by the two departments. The probe continues until the agencies close it with a compliance letter, a fine schedule, or a referral for further enforcement.

This kind of Section 117 review has run for years across multiple administrations; most end with corrected filings and no campus shutdown. It moves forward on paperwork and cooperation, not courtroom rulings. Without new legislation or a department policy shift, the same disclosure rules stay in place.

Next rung likely brings detailed data requests from both schools within weeks, followed by agency findings on what was missed.
