18:T464,Russian strikes hit the ArcelorMittal steel plant in Kryvyi Rih and forced it to shut down. The company now plans to record a one-billion-dollar charge for the damage and lost output. The plant made about 20 percent of Ukraine’s steel before the strikes.

This is the latest in a string of attacks on Ukrainian heavy industry that started in 2022 and picked up pace after mid-2024 when Russian forces shifted to hitting energy and metals sites deeper in the rear. Repair crews have fixed earlier strikes at the same site twice before, but each time the work took months and output stayed lower.

The charge will stay on the books until the plant restarts or the company writes off the asset. Restart depends on air-defense coverage around Kryvyi Rih holding and on steady power supply; both have been uneven since summer blackouts. If strikes continue at the current rate the plant stays idle and the charge grows. If Russian long-range strikes drop off, repair work can begin again.

The situation will stay frozen until either side alters the strike-and-defense balance over central Ukraine.
