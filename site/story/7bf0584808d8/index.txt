The unit held 19 patients at the time and sustained enough structural damage that it will stay offline for months while officials weigh repairs against demolition. All patients were moved to temporary wards, with beds now being lined up at private North West hospital, North West Regional, and Mersey as the health department maps out the next 12 to 18 months until the delayed 27-bed replacement facility starts construction.

The case sits at the first procedural step: the woman is due in Devonport Magistrates Court this evening. From there the charge moves through committal, then either plea or trial in the Supreme Court, a stretch that usually runs six to 18 months in Tasmania unless she pleads early. The hospital rebuild timeline shifts only if the damage assessment finishes fast and funding is locked in; right now both steps remain in the planning phase.

This situation is likely to run another 12 to 18 months on the hospital side while the court case runs in parallel on its own track. Next visible markers are the court appearance tonight, the damage report in coming weeks, and the start of any physical works on the new unit once contracts are let.
