Gloriavale teacher censured for serious misconduct after striking student

A Christian community school in New Zealand allowed teachers to use physical force on students for decades until parliament banned it outright in 2007. The rule change reached every registered teacher including those at Gloriavale where Malachi Faithful worked.

On a day in 2013 Faithful struck a 14-year-old student on the leg with a length of vacuum-cleaner pipe after the student made an obnoxious remark. The blow went unreported inside the community until the former student left nine years later and told police. In December 2022 Faithful was charged with assault with a weapon. He pleaded guilty and received a discharge without after paying the student $1250 in emotional-harm reparation.

The New Zealand Teachers Disciplinary published its decision on 29 September 2026. It ruled that hitting a student with an object met the legal definition of physical abuse and therefore serious misconduct. The tribunal placed a formal on Faithful’s record, ordered him to finish a one-year mentoring programme on self-regulation and classroom management, required him to tell any future employer about the decision for two years, and directed him to pay $3673.30 in tribunal and legal costs. It stopped short of cancelling his practising certificate after finding he had apologised, held a clean prior record, and showed low ongoing risk.
