17:T5af,The Curiosity rover has passed the 5000-sol mark and climbed past the 1-kilometer elevation line in Gale Crater. It is now working its way up Valle Grande through a series of 25-to-200-meter-wide bands marked by changes in sand cover and rocky outcrops. On the latest two planning days the team used APXS, MAHLI, and ChemCam LIBS on nodular bedrock at Cerro Armazones, Monte Melimoyu, Tuta Huallpas, and Acllahuasi, then drove 60 meters and targeted a flakey, laminated block at Yungay, Chiu Chiu, Puya Raimondii, and Liolaemus Tacnae while shooting Mastcam and ChemCam mosaics of nearby buttes and the Sullivan Field sand ripples.

This pattern of short drives, contact science on selected rocks, and remote imaging of the surrounding terrain has run since the rover cleared the erosional supersurface a few weeks ago. The schedule stays steady because the next fixed milestone is the 15th launch anniversary on Nov. 26. The work will keep the same rhythm as long as the banded unit continues and the rover’s instruments stay within reach of the nodular and laminated targets.

Next the rover will finish the current set of measurements on the flakey block, then likely drive farther up the valley to the next band contact where outcrop density or sand cover shifts again. That step depends on the team spotting a fresh contact within a single sol’s drive range.
