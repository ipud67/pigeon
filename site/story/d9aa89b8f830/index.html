CANADA
September 23, 2026 · 00:40 UTC

Catherine O'Connell-Cooper planned two days of work for the Curiosity rover on Mars during the week of September 11 2026

The Curiosity rover has spent the last four years climbing the lower slopes of Mount Sharp inside Gale crater. The mountain is made of layered rock that records changes in ancient Martian environments. In early September the rover passed the 5,000th Martian day on the surface and crossed an erosional supersurface that marks a gap in the rock record.

On the sols between 5010 and 5015 the rover moved farther up the valley the team calls Valle Grande. It entered a region of subtle bands 25 to 200 meters across where the amount of exposed rock and loose sand shifts from place to place. On the first planning day the arm instruments brushed and measured nodular bedrock at Cerro Armazones and Monte Melimoyu while the ChemCam laser struck dark nodules at Tuta Huallpas and a dark float rock at Acllahuasi. After a 60-meter drive the second planning day left the rover beside mostly sand and one small rough-textured outcrop. That block holds small flakes, chips and smoother laminated patches. The arm will place MAHLI and APXS on targets named Yungay and Chiu Chiu while ChemCam will analyze Chiu Chiu, the smoother spot Liolaemus Tacnae and the rougher spot Puya Raimondii. Mastcam and ChemCam also built mosaics of nearby buttes, the scuff called Chocolatal and the sand field named Sullivan Field in memory of the late rover-team sand expert Robert Sullivan.

The rover now sits in the banded terrain with contact-science targets identified for the next few sols. The schedule returns to its normal rhythm after the recent milestones and the North American Labor Day holiday.

Thinking ahead
The Curiosity rover has passed the 5000-sol mark and climbed past the 1-kilometer elevation line in Gale Crater. It is now working its way up Valle Grande through a series of 25-to-200-meter-wide bands marked by changes in sand cover and rocky outcrops. On the latest two planning days the team used APXS, MAHLI, and ChemCam LIBS on nodular bedrock at Cerro Armazones, Monte Melimoyu, Tuta Huallpas, and Acllahuasi, then drove 60 meters and targeted a flakey, laminated block at Yungay, Chiu Chiu, Puya Raimondii, and Liolaemus Tacnae while shooting Mastcam and ChemCam mosaics of nearby buttes and the Sullivan Field sand ripples. This pattern of short drives, contact science on selected rocks, and remote imaging of the surrounding terrain has run since the rover cleared the erosional supersurface a few weeks ago. The schedule stays steady because the next fixed milestone is the 15th launch anniversary on Nov. 26. The work will keep the same rhythm as long as the banded unit continues and the rover’s instruments stay within reach of the nodular and laminated targets. Next the rover will finish the current set of measurements on the flakey block, then likely drive farther up the valley to the next band contact where outcrop density or sand cover shifts again. That step depends on the team spotting a fresh contact within a single sol’s drive range. Horizon: at least through November barring a mechanical fault or major terrain change.
