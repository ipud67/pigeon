The founders treated military honor as essential to that common defense. Washington himself pinned the Purple Heart on deserving soldiers in 1782. The First Congress passed the Militia Acts of 1792 and funded frontier defense while the new government was still in New York.  

S. 858 directs a commemorative work on the National Mall to mark the valor of Medal of Honor recipients. The National Mall itself traces to Pierre L’Enfant’s 1791 plan for the federal city, which placed sites of national memory under federal authority. The act fits within the federal power to manage public grounds in the District of Columbia, a power exercised since the Residence Act of 1790.
