Germany: Protests follow AfD election victory

The Alternative for Germany first entered the Bundestag in 2013 as a eurosceptic outfit and grew into the largest opposition party by 2017 on a platform that called for tighter border controls. Over the next nine years it held seats in every state while mainstream parties refused deals with it. Last Sunday the party took first place in a state election after it campaigned to cut inflows and reopen direct talks with Moscow.

That result triggered street demonstrations in several cities on the following Monday and Tuesday. Crowds blocked roads outside party offices and chanted against the outcome while police kept rival groups apart. Local officials said the protests drew thousands in Berlin, Cologne and Leipzig.

The state government now faces the task of forming a coalition that excludes the largest vote-getter. National leaders from the governing parties have repeated their pledge not to work with the AfD, leaving the arithmetic for a new administration still unresolved as of Saturday.

Thinking ahead
Protests hit German cities after the AfD took first place in the state election on September 7. The party ran on cutting migration numbers and reopening lines to Moscow. Turnout and street counts now serve as the visible gauge of opposition strength. The protests will likely run for days or weeks as long as organizers keep pulling crowds and police logs show no drop in permits. Next step is the state parliament seating where the AfD must either form a coalition or sit in opposition. The situation shifts only when vote tallies in the next federal poll move or when coalition talks produce a working majority that sidelines the party. Horizon: 3 to 6 weeks of street action before winter weather and parliamentary routine take over.
