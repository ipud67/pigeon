1d:T5f9,Thousands of IRGC and Basij volunteers marched through central Tehran on 20 September in the largest public show of force since the US and Israel began strikes in late February. The event marked the formal launch of the Janfada Iran program, with state media reporting over 300,000 in the streets and more than 600,000 already registered for rifle training that starts soon. Officials displayed air defense systems, armed vehicles with female IRGC crews, and drones of the type used in recent Strait of Hormuz attacks while the regime simultaneously claimed credit for striking the Togo-flagged tanker Trend that same day.

The march fits the pattern that has held since July: Iran keeps the Hormuz fight alive with sporadic naval hits and now layers in mass volunteer recruitment as an extra defense ring around the regular IRGC and Basij forces that have run suppression and coastal operations for years. Similar rallies are scheduled for other cities in coming weeks. The next visible step will be the start of the announced assault-rifle courses and the expansion of these parades, unless direct talks resume or a new round of US strikes hits Iranian soil again.

This phase of volunteer mobilization and public drills is likely to run for months as long as the naval standoff in the strait continues without a fresh ceasefire agreement. The situation changes only if either side lands a decisive hit on the other's logistics in the strait or if one side accepts a new negotiating window.
