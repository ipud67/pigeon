Thousands of IRGC, Basij volunteers march through Tehran in show of defiance against US, Israel

The US and Israel began strikes on Iran on 28 February 2026. The fighting settled into weekly exchanges by 1 March and narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz after the Islamabad Memorandum collapsed on 8 July. Iran responded by striking tankers that bypassed its approved transit routes, the IRGC attempted to close the strait on 12 July, and US forces hit Iranian infrastructure, storage sites and power plants in repeated nightly operations through August.

On 20 September 2026, more than 300,000 people marched through central Tehran in the largest public mobilization since the opening strikes. They wore combat uniforms, carried flags, stepped on US and Israeli symbols and chanted against both countries. The event launched the “Janfada Iran” program under which volunteers receive assault-rifle training to form an additional defense layer with the IRGC and Basij . Tehran IRGC commander General Hassan Hassanzadeh said over 600,000 had already registered, with a target of more than one million. Basij head Hossein Taeb stated the recruits would conduct comprehensive defense.

At the same time, Iranian state television claimed responsibility for a projectile strike on the Togo-flagged oil tanker Trend in the Strait of Hormuz, citing an illegal transit attempt. The march and the tanker attack occurred while President Masoud Pezeshkian and senior commanders reviewed the display of air-defense systems, armed vehicles and drones of the type previously used against vessels in the strait. The situation now turns on how many volunteers complete training and whether the additional forces alter the balance of maritime operations in the strait.

Thousands of IRGC and Basij volunteers marched through central Tehran on 20 September in the largest public show of force since the US and Israel began strikes in late February. The event marked the formal launch of the Janfada Iran program, with state media reporting over 300,000 in the streets and more than 600,000 already registered for rifle training that starts soon. Officials displayed air defense systems, armed vehicles with female IRGC crews, and drones of the type used in recent Strait of Hormuz attacks while the regime simultaneously claimed credit for striking the Togo-flagged tanker Trend that same day. The march fits the pattern that has held since July: Iran keeps the Hormuz fight alive with sporadic naval hits and now layers in mass volunteer recruitment as an extra defense ring around the regular IRGC and Basij forces that have run suppression and coastal operations for years. Similar rallies are scheduled for other cities in coming weeks. The next visible step will be the start of the announced assault-rifle courses and the expansion of these parades, unless direct talks resume or a new round of US strikes hits Iranian soil again. This phase of volunteer mobilization and public drills is likely to run for months as long as the naval standoff in the strait continues without a fresh ceasefire agreement. The situation changes only if either side lands a decisive hit on the other's logistics in the strait or if one side accepts a new negotiating window. Horizon: through at least December.
