United Kingdom’s government says Lisa Nandy's statement on protecting children online

The Online Safety Act passed in 2023 and gave Ofcom the power to set binding codes of practice for platforms that host user-generated content. Those codes require companies to assess and risks of harm to children, including exposure to pornography, violent material and content that promotes self-harm or eating disorders. Platforms must verify ages where necessary and remove material judged to present a significant risk once it is reported.

On September 8 2026 Lisa Nandy told the House of Commons that the government had now laid secondary to tighten the age-verification rules and expand the list of priority harms that trigger mandatory intervention. The statement sets out deadlines for platforms to introduce the new checks and confirms Ofcom will gain fresh enforcement tools, including the ability to fine firms up to ten percent of global annual or block access to services that remain non-compliant.

The new rules take effect in stages over the next twelve months. Ofcom must publish updated codes by the end of 2026 and begin formal audits of the largest platforms in the first quarter of 2027. The department said it will report back to Parliament on compliance levels once the first round of audits is complete.

Thinking ahead
Lisa Nandy told Parliament the government is moving ahead with rules that force tech platforms to block child access to harmful material. The update sits on the ladder of the Online Safety Act passed in 2023 and the phased rollout that started with basic reporting duties in 2024. Next procedural step is Ofcom publishing its final codes of practice this autumn, followed by enforcement notices to the biggest platforms by early 2027. The pace holds steady unless a new parliamentary session rewrites the Act or a court blocks an enforcement order. Industry pushback has slowed some deadlines before but has not stopped the timetable. Without fresh legislation or a major compliance failure that triggers fines, the rules stay on track. This runs another 18 to 24 months before full enforcement lands. Horizon: Ofcom codes published by December 2026.
