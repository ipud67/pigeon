I'd like to vote: Cubans react to Spain's Supreme Court decision

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headline"Me gustaría votar": los cubanos reaccionan a la decisión del Supremo de España

Spanish law has let people born outside the country claim if their parents or grandparents lost it under the that ran from 1939 to 1975. Once they , those new citizens gained the right to vote in Spanish elections from abroad. Many Cubans traced their family lines back to Spaniards who left during or after the civil war and applied under that rule in recent years.

On September 8 the Supreme Court issued an order that stops those newly naturalized voters from casting ballots until Spanish consulates finish checking each case to confirm the exile link. The suspension came after complaints that the verification process had not kept up with the volume of applications.

The decision leaves thousands of Cubans who received their Spanish passports under the law unable to vote in the next national election while the consulates work through their files. The court set no deadline for the checks, so the final number of eligible voters among them remains unknown for now.
