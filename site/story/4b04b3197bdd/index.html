Activists plan to create 'human chain' to protest Trump's threat to demolish Kennedy Center

The Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts opened in 1971 on the Potomac River in Washington as a national memorial to President John F. Kennedy. Federal law placed it under a board of appointed by the president and confirmed by the . The building houses theaters, concert halls, and opera stages that host performances year-round. Earlier this year part of the ceiling collapsed, forcing repairs and a temporary closure ordered by the current board.

On September 18 activists from Hands Off the Arts will form a human chain around the building starting at 6:30 p.m. They plan musical performances, employee , and speeches on the steps. The action comes after President Trump replaced the entire board, which then voted to add his name to the building. Trump has stated the structure could face full demolition without his support for renovation. A federal judge ruled Thursday that the government must give 30 days notice before any demolition can begin and declined to fast-track review of the closure decision.

The protest now stands inside that 30-day window. The center remains closed for repairs while the new board oversees renovation plans. The human chain will test how far the proceeds before the notice period expires.
