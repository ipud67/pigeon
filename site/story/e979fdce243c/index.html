Two Michigan residents charged with making terror threats

Michigan law allows state prosecutors to charge individuals with making terror threats when they communicate intent to commit violence against people or places. The reaches phone calls to emergency dispatch, emails and other digital messages, and adds separate counts for using a computer to commit a crime or for hate-crime enhancements when the threat targets a group identified by national origin or religion. Charges of this kind against public officials and houses of worship have been filed in Michigan for years.

On September 17 a 33-year-old woman from Roseville, Alicia Choucair, was on two counts of making terror threats, one count of using a computer to commit a crime and one hate-crime count. Prosecutors say she called emergency dispatch and told the operator that violence would be used against Dearborn Mayor Abdullah Hammoud and a local mosque unless “your people” were kept out of town. A judge set bond at $20,000 and she remains in . Two days later, on September 19, a 61-year-old man from Lake Orion, Anthony Dekinderen, was arraigned on nine counts of making terror threats. He is accused of sending an email to a Michigan journalist that named Gov. Gretchen Whitmer, Secretary of State Jocelyn Benson and members of the state Supreme Court as targets to be killed. A judge set bond at $5 million and ordered GPS monitoring.

The two cases now sit with the state Attorney General’s office, which will present evidence to a jury on the content and intent of the specific communications. No trial dates have been set.
