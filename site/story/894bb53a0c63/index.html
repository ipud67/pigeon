UKRAINE
September 16, 2026 · 14:24 UTC

Zelenskyy says war cannot end in win-win outcome after so many deaths

Russia invaded Ukraine on February 24 2022. Ukrainian forces halted the initial advance on Kyiv within weeks and pushed Russian troops back from large parts of the north and northeast that same year. The front then settled into a grinding line of trenches and artillery fire across the east and south that has barely moved since early 2023. More than three years of fighting produced heavy casualties on both sides and left Russian troops in control of roughly one fifth of Ukrainian territory.

On September 16 2026 Zelenskyy told CBS that no outcome could be win-win after the scale of deaths. He said the possibility ended on the first day of the invasion. He added that the only victory that matters for Ukraine, Russia and the world is to stop the war. The remarks came after US envoy Jared Kushner told a forum in Kyiv four days earlier that negotiations should seek a formula that works for everyone. Zelenskyy repeated Ukraine’s earlier offer to freeze the current front line if Russia agrees to an unconditional followed by talks.

The statement sets the Ukrainian position ahead of any renewed talks. It makes clear that Kyiv will accept a ceasefire at the existing line without additional Russian conditions while rejecting any framing that treats the war as a balanced settlement.

Thinking ahead
Zelenskyy stated in a CBS interview that the war lost any win-win path on its first day due to the human losses. He repeated readiness to freeze the current front line if Russia agrees to an unconditional ceasefire followed by talks. This lines up with his position at the YES forum in Kyiv four days earlier when the US envoy floated a formula that works for everyone. The next observable step is whether Russian officials signal any interest in a no-conditions ceasefire. Without that signal the public back-and-forth stays frozen in place. This pattern has held since early 2022 statements on both sides. Zelenskyy keeps tying any pause to a ceasefire first; Moscow has not accepted that sequence in public. The gap stays open until one side changes its stated precondition. Horizon: at least several months with no visible shift in preconditions.
