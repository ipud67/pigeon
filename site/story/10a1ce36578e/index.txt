18:T49c,The ANZUS Treaty of 1951 rests on the founders’ core idea that free governments exist first to secure the right to life by providing for the common defense. The U.S. Constitution gives Congress power to raise armies, maintain a navy, and call forth the militia to repel invasions, while the president serves as commander in chief. The treaty binds Australia, New Zealand, and the United States to treat an armed attack on one as dangerous to the peace and safety of the others, echoing the mutual-defense logic that drove the 1778 Treaty of Alliance with France during the Revolutionary War. Washington himself led the militia to put down the Whiskey Rebellion in 1794, showing that the new government would use force when necessary to protect ordered liberty at home and abroad. The founders rejected standing armies in peacetime yet built a framework for alliances that deter threats without surrendering sovereignty. ANZUS has operated for 75 years as one such practical instrument, helping keep sea lanes open and deterring aggression in the Pacific. 1b:T43a,The ANZUS Treaty was signed in 1951 by Australia, New Zealand, and the United States after joint fighting in two world wars. Today marks the 75th anniversary with official statements from Canberra on its role in regional security. No new policy announcements or changes in participation have been recorded.

The treaty has run without interruption since its ratification. Australia continues joint exercises, intelligence sharing, and defense planning with the US under its terms. New Zealand's full involvement remains limited after the 1980s nuclear policy dispute, though practical cooperation with both partners has grown in recent years.

The arrangement holds steady as long as Australia and the US maintain current force posture and budget commitments. Any shift would require one side to formally review or withdraw from the treaty text. Officials on both sides have shown no moves in that direction.

This setup looks set to run at least another decade on present course. Next visible step is the annual AUSMIN talks where defense chiefs review priorities.
