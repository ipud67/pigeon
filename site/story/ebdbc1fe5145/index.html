Ethiopia: Thailand Backs Ethiopia's Push for Sea Access, Calls for Regional Understanding

Overview
[ENA] Addis Ababa -- Ethiopia's sea access is a justifiable quest that requires understanding from neighbors, Thailand's Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Foreign Affairs Sihasak Phuangketkeow said.
Short history
Thailand lost its only coastline in 1904 when France forced it to cede the western part of the Chao Phraya delta. Since then the country has had no direct sea access. On August 31 2026 Thailand’s Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Sihasak Phuangketkeow told Ethiopian officials in Addis Ababa that Ethiopia’s push for sea access is a justifiable quest and called on neighboring countries to show understanding. The statement marks the first time a Southeast Asian government has publicly backed Ethiopia’s position on the matter.
Predictive model
Thailand just threw public support behind Ethiopia’s long-running bid for sea access through talks with neighbors. The Thai deputy prime minister called the request “justifiable” and said the region needs to show understanding. This is the latest public backing Ethiopia has picked up since it lost its coastline in 1993 and began pressing for a deal with Eritrea, Djibouti, or Somalia. How much longer this runs depends on whether Ethiopia can turn statements like this into concrete talks or port leases. Right now the pattern is steady diplomatic endorsements without binding agreements. Next likely step is more bilateral meetings or a fresh round of Horn of Africa talks that test how far neighbors are willing to bend. If Ethiopia lines up one workable corridor deal, the push could quiet fast. If every neighbor keeps saying no, the statements pile up and the dispute drags on for years. Horizon: At least another 18–24 months of public diplomacy before any corridor opens or the effort visibly stalls.
