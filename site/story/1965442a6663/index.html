Russians attack public transport stop in Kherson region, one person injured

Russian forces have struck villages and districts in the Kherson region with drones since they withdrew from the city of Kherson in November 2022. The regional military administration tracks each strike and reports the injuries to local residents.

On September 16 a Russian drone hit a public transport stop in the village of Zelenivka at 13:35. The blast and shrapnel wounded a 93-year-old woman who lives there. Medical teams gave her treatment on the spot. Earlier the same day a 42-year-old woman was hurt in a separate drone strike on a car in Kherson's Central District around 10:00; she received care for concussion, blast injury, closed head trauma and acute stress reaction.

The administration continues to record every strike and move the injured to safety or treatment. The pattern of attacks on civilian sites in the region has held steady for nearly four years.

Thinking ahead
Russian forces hit a public transport stop in Zelenivka with a drone on September 16, wounding one 93-year-old woman with blast and shrapnel injuries. The Kherson City Military Administration logged the strike at 13:35 local time and reported medics treating her on site. This fits the pattern of repeated small drone attacks on civilian spots in the region, as seen the day before when one person died and seven were hurt across Kherson. These strikes have run at a steady pace for months with no let-up in reported incidents. The next likely moves are more of the same: isolated drone or artillery hits on roads, stops, and houses in frontline villages, followed by local administration updates on casualties and aid. A real change would require one side to pull forces back from the Dnipro line or reach a ceasefire deal, neither of which shows in current frontline reports. This pattern runs at least another six months.
