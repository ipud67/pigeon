Russia will not end war “without pressure”: Zelensky responds to attack on Kyiv during UNGA

Russian forces have struck Ukrainian cities with ballistic missiles and drones since the full-scale invasion began in February 2022. Kyiv has absorbed repeated attacks on its civilian districts, energy grid, and transport links while its government has pressed Western allies for more air-defense systems and tighter on Moscow.

On September 24, 2026, missiles hit the capital as President Volodymyr Zelensky met US officials in New York. The strikes killed at least two people, damaged residential buildings, a maternity hospital, energy sites, logistics facilities, and a convention center that houses his party’s headquarters. Rescue crews continued pulling people from rubble hours later.

Zelensky stated that Russia will not end the war without pressure and called on the United States to respond strongly with additional missile interceptors. He told the UN General Assembly the same day that Russian President Vladimir Putin grows more dangerous the more freedom he is given to act. The situation now turns on whether Ukraine receives expanded air defenses and whether sanctions are tightened further.

The Constitution gives Congress the power to declare war, grant letters of marque and reprisal, and make rules concerning captures on land and water under Article I, Section 8, Clause 11. The same article lets Congress regulate commerce with foreign nations and lay duties to fund defense. Founders who wrote the document had fresh memory of the Revolutionary War and the 1783 Treaty of Paris that ended it; they designed a system that required deliberate legislative action before the nation committed blood and treasure abroad. The Preamble lists providing for the common defence as one reason to form the government, right after domestic Tranquility. Zelensky stated that Russia will not end the war without pressure. A decisive congressional vote on the exact limits of U.S. aid and sanctions would settle how far that pressure extends under the enumerated powers.
Thinking ahead

Emergency crews are still pulling people from rubble. He called for total pressure on Russia, more missile interceptors from the US, and stronger sanctions during talks with American officials. This fits the pattern of Russian ballistic strikes on Ukrainian cities timed to diplomatic events. Zelensky has repeated the same demand for interceptors and sanctions at every major international meeting since the first wave of big missile barrages in late 2022. The next visible steps are more Ukrainian requests in closed-door sessions at the UN and with NATO members this week, followed by whatever new package of air-defense munitions Washington decides to release. The situation stays on the same track until either side changes force posture on the ground or one government visibly shifts its red lines on weapons supply. It runs at least until the next big supply decision in Washington or Brussels. Horizon: through winter 2026.
