NORTHERN CYPRUS
August 31, 2026 · 15:04 UTC

In Northern Cyprus, families wait for news of passengers from sunk ferry

“deeply saddened by the tragic accident”
Overview
Seven people have died after a passenger boat capsized in Northern Cyprus. 267 people were on board and 23 are still missing. The boat capsized just a few miles off Kyrenia Port in northern Cyprus - a territory occupied by Turkey but not internationally recognised. The ferry has now completely sunk - and the company that operates it says it is "deeply saddened by the tragic accident". It says a hotline has been set up to provide passengers' relatives with information.
Short history
In 1974, Turkey invaded Cyprus and took control of the northern third of the island. It declared the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus in 1983. Only Turkey recognizes the TRNC as a state; the rest of the world still treats the area as occupied Cypriot territory under Turkish military and control. On 31 August 2026, a passenger ferry carrying 267 people capsized a few miles off Kyrenia port in Northern Cyprus and later sank completely. Seven people died and 23 remain missing. The operating company set up a hotline for families waiting for information on the missing passengers.
Predictive model
Families in Northern Cyprus are still waiting for updates on the 23 missing after the passenger boat capsized and sank a few miles off Kyrenia Port. Rescue teams have pulled 237 survivors and recovered seven bodies so far from the 267 people who were on board. The operating company has set up a hotline for relatives, and Turkish Cypriot authorities are running the search operation in waters they control. The search will keep going as long as divers and sonar can work the wreck site and weather holds. Next step is full mapping of the sunken hull and recovery of any bodies still inside. The situation only shifts if the missing are declared dead or if political pressure from Ankara or local families forces a formal inquiry into why the boat went down so close to port. This runs at least another week on current effort.
