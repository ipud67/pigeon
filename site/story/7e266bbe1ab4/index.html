Man arrested for allegedly making Jew-hatred threats against Ben Shapiro

Florida law makes it a to send written or electronic threats to kill or injure another person or to use a two-way communications device in the commission of such a felony. Those have stood since the 1990s and reach any person who posts messages that a reasonable recipient would interpret as a genuine plan to commit violence.

On September 12 Palm Beach County sheriff’s arrested Korby Aaren Strube, 29, of Horton, Kansas, on 23 counts of making written or electronic threats to kill or cause bodily harm and two counts of using a communications device to facilitate a felony. The charges rest on three social-media posts dated July 31, August 1 and August 2 in which Strube named political commentator Ben Shapiro and declared he would kill him for being Jewish. The August 1 and 2 posts included countdowns of 52 and 51 days that pointed to September 21-22, the dates of Yom Kippur.

A private security firm alerted authorities on August 4. Investigators traced the account to Strube, obtained an affidavit from the Palm Beach County Clerk of the Circuit Court, and secured admissions from Strube that he controlled the accounts. Shapiro told investigators he believed the threats were based on his Jewish identity and wished to press charges. The case now rests on whether prosecutors can prove the posts constituted true threats under the statutes and whether Strube’s later claim that the accounts were hacked holds up against the digital evidence gathered from phone and internet providers.
