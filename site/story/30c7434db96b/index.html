Legislature passes slew of bills as end of session approaches

The California has a deadline: all bills must pass both houses by August 31 in even-numbered years. On August 30, 2026, lawmakers held a rare Sunday session to vote on the remaining measures before the cutoff. The final batch cleared the floor that day and now heads to Governor Newsom for action. This process repeats at the end of every two-year session.

Thinking ahead
The California Legislature jammed through a pile of bills in a rare Sunday session on August 31, 2026, one day before the constitutional deadline to finish work. This is the standard end-of-session rush that has happened every two-year cycle for decades: lawmakers hold the deadline over their own heads, then sprint to vote. Next procedural step is the governor’s desk, where he has 30 days to sign, veto, or let them become law without signature. How much longer this particular crunch lasts depends on two observable indicators: how many of the passed bills the governor actually vetoes and whether any vetoes trigger override attempts when the Legislature reconvenes in 2027. If the governor signs most of them, the story ends here. If he vetoes high-profile ones and lawmakers start collecting the two-thirds votes needed to override, the fight stretches into next year. Right now the indicators are too thin to forecast veto volume or override success. No vote tallies or governor statements yet. Horizon: likely wraps by late September 2026 unless veto fights flare.
