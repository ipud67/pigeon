November 14, 2026 · 02:24 UTC

SpaceX launched its Starship rocket into orbit but the booster exploded over the Gulf of Mexico

SpaceX has spent years testing its Starship system at the South Texas Starbase site with the aim of turning both the booster stage and the upper spacecraft into hardware that can fly again without major overhaul. The vehicle stands 124 meters tall with the spacecraft mounted atop the booster and both pieces built from the start to return to the launch pad under their own power.

On this launch the booster cleared the pad alongside the spacecraft and the pair separated cleanly. The upper stage reached orbit released 26 Starlink satellites and stayed on course for a planned return. The booster however steered away from the pad toward a spot over the Gulf of Mexico and exploded above the water before it could be caught by the mechanical arms waiting at the pad.

The upper stage remains in orbit with a recovery attempt set for Saturday using the same catch system on the spacecraft itself.

Thinking ahead
SpaceX fired its Starship from the South Texas Starbase pad with both stages lifting off cleanly. The booster then steered away from the planned catch by mechanical arms at the pad and exploded over the Gulf of Mexico instead. The upper stage reached orbit, released 26 Starlink satellites, and is set to attempt its own catch on return this Saturday. The company has caught boosters on earlier flights but this was the first paired attempt for both stages. Reasons for the booster loss remain unclear right now. This test marks the latest rung in a series that had already recovered boosters while upper stages kept ending in the ocean. The next observable step is whether the upper stage survives reentry and gets caught Saturday. If that works the program adds one reusable stage; repeated booster failures would keep the full reuse goal out of reach. The situation is likely to stay in the test-and-iterate loop for months until both catches become routine. Horizon: next catch attempt this Saturday.
