Prime Minister Mark Carney met European Parliament President Roberta Metsola in Strasbourg today

Canada and the European Union have operated under the Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement since 2017. That pact eliminated most on goods traded between the two sides and opened selected service sectors. It left in place separate national rules on investment screening, standards and strategic supply chains.

On September 16 2026 in Strasbourg Prime Minister Mark Carney met the President of the European Parliament Roberta Metsola. Carney thanked her for the invitation to address the full Parliament later. The two leaders reviewed the agenda laid out in European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen’s State of the Union speech which called for a deeper partnership that goes beyond the existing trade deal. They listed concrete areas where Canada and the EU would expand joint work: critical-minerals processing defence production artificial-intelligence energy supply space systems and cross-border financial payments. They also directed officials to widen mobility rules so citizens could more easily travel study work and sell services on either side of the Atlantic.

The meeting ended with an agreement to keep direct lines open between the two offices. The next step turns on how the European Parliament and the Canadian government translate von der Leyen’s call into specific negotiating mandates that both sides must ratify.
