19:T46d,China set up a 600-kilometer-wide restricted airspace zone around Beijing after a light sport aircraft hit CITIC Tower on June 26 and killed the pilot plus 13 people on the ground. The zone starts September 20 and covers an Italy-sized area that includes Beijing, Tianjin, much of Hebei and parts of five other provinces plus the Bohai Sea. Only commercial airlines, approved business flights and emergency services can fly there if they stick to pre-filed plans, carry tracking gear, report security status and stay in radio contact.

The move follows the June crash where the 66-year-old pilot left his approved route and lost contact. Aviation notices list the exact rules and equipment needed. State media announced a Capital No-Fly Zone on September 13 without the full size details that appeared later in the NOTAM.

This setup will stay in place until authorities change the NOTAM or issue a new notice that loosens or cancels it. Next step is full enforcement on September 20 with tighter tracking on every exempt flight. It would take another incident or a policy review to shrink the zone.
