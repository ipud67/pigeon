BEIJING
September 17, 2026 · 16:18 UTC

China creates Italy-sized no-fly zone around Beijing after rare crash

China has operated civilian and military air traffic over its northern provinces for decades under standard aviation rules that required pilots to file flight plans and stay in radio contact with controllers. General-aviation airports around Beijing handled small planes and light sport aircraft on approved routes that kept them clear of the city center and its high-rise buildings.

On June 26 a 66-year-old pilot took off from one of those suburban general-aviation fields, left his assigned area, and flew a light sport aircraft into the 108-storey CITIC Tower in central Beijing. The crash killed the pilot and injured 13 people on the ground. The pilot had written repeatedly in his diary about ending his life, according to the official investigation.

A new 600-kilometre-wide restricted zone centered on Beijing takes effect on September 20. The circle, covering more than 280,000 square kilometres and roughly the area of Italy, reaches from the ground to unlimited altitude and includes Beijing, Tianjin, much of Hebei, and parts of Inner Mongolia, Shanxi, Shandong, Liaoning and the Bohai Sea. Only commercial airlines, pre-approved business flights, and military, customs, police or fire-rescue aircraft may enter. Every flight must file its exact route in advance, report any change for approval, keep two-way radio contact at all times, carry electronic tracking equipment, and declare its security status before crossing the boundary.

Thinking ahead
China set up a 600-kilometer-wide restricted airspace zone around Beijing after a light sport aircraft hit CITIC Tower on June 26 and killed the pilot plus 13 people on the ground. The zone starts September 20 and covers an Italy-sized area that includes Beijing, Tianjin, much of Hebei and parts of five other provinces plus the Bohai Sea. Only commercial airlines, approved business flights and emergency services can fly there if they stick to pre-filed plans, carry tracking gear, report security status and stay in radio contact. The move follows the June crash where the 66-year-old pilot left his approved route and lost contact. Aviation notices list the exact rules and equipment needed. State media announced a Capital No-Fly Zone on September 13 without the full size details that appeared later in the NOTAM. This setup will stay in place until authorities change the NOTAM or issue a new notice that loosens or cancels it. Next step is full enforcement on September 20 with tighter tracking on every exempt flight. It would take another incident or a policy review to shrink the zone. Horizon: at least several years.
