The notice posted Thursday is the latest step in a long-running push to add tech tools to field operations that already use drones, sensors, and ground vehicles. Next visible rung is the formal solicitation and vendor bids; after that comes contract award and delivery, now eyed for 2026.

How much longer this runs depends on whether Congress funds the line item and whether ICE actually obligates the money. If the budget stays flat or oversight committees flag privacy and safety concerns, the program can stall at the bid stage. Observable indicators will be the next posted RFP date, any amendment to the $1-2 million ceiling, and public test reports once units reach the border.

Without those concrete steps the forecast stays thin; right now the only hard signal is the planning notice itself.
