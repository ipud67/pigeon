IDF fires commander filmed praying with West Bank Jewish extremists during Kusra siege

IDF fired a commander on September 2, 2026 after video surfaced of him praying with Jewish extremists during the siege of Kusra in the West Bank. The IDF had given him explicit orders to avoid any actions that could be read as identifying with the extremists or backing their illegal conduct. The dismissal followed those orders.

Thinking ahead
The IDF has now relieved the commander who was caught on video praying alongside Jewish extremists while they laid siege to the Palestinian village of Kusra. The army had already given him clear orders not to do anything that could look like he backed the settlers’ illegal moves. This firing is the latest step in the military’s push to keep its troops from being seen as part of the settler violence that has been climbing in the West Bank for years. How much longer this goes on depends on whether more videos or complaints surface about other officers crossing the same line. The next rung is likely an internal IDF probe into how widespread the problem is and whether the relieved commander faces any further discipline. The situation only shifts if the military starts handing out serious punishments that actually deter other troops or if political pressure from settler-friendly ministers forces the army to soften its stance. Without fresh incidents or public probes, the story fades in a few weeks. Horizon: 4-6 weeks unless another video drops.
