17:T5c0,Russian drones hit four spots in the Kyiv region early Sunday and killed four people there, a mother, her two three-year-old children, and one other. The same morning a Ukrainian drone reached an oil refinery outside Moscow and another struck an apartment, killing two. Moscow officials said more than 1,600 drones were intercepted nationwide since Saturday, 450 of them aimed at the capital, one of the biggest one-day counts since the full-scale invasion began in 2022. The refinery strike adds to a pattern of repeated Ukrainian hits on Russian oil-processing sites that have already cut fuel supply inside Russia.

Both sides keep trading long-range strikes on each other's rear areas while ground lines stay largely fixed. Ukrainian drone range and numbers have grown enough to reach Moscow on election day; Russian glide bombs and drones keep hitting Ukrainian logistics and energy targets weekly. No big shift in either side's air-defense posture or production rate has shown up in the last month.

The back-and-forth drone and missile exchanges will most likely run at this pace through the rest of the year unless one side loses a major supply hub or air-defense network collapses. Next moves stay tied to seasonal weather: mud in October and November slows ground resupply on both sides, which usually lifts drone and missile strikes as the cheaper way to keep pressure on.

Horizon: three to six months of continued reciprocal deep strikes at current
