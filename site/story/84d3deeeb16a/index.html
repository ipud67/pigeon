Archbishop of Canterbury withdraws interfaith harmony award for Muslim cleric who praised bin Laden

The Church of England has run an annual award since the 1990s that it gives to people who advance ties between faiths. The Hubert Walter Award for Reconciliation and Interfaith Cooperation takes its name from the 12th-century archbishop who negotiated with Muslims during the Third Crusade. Lambeth Palace, the archbishop’s official residence, selects recipients and presents the honor each year on or near the anniversary of the 9/11 attacks.

On 11 September 2026 Archbishop Sarah Mullally handed the award to Imam Hafiz Muhammad Tahir Mehmood Ashrafi, chairman of the Pakistani Ulema Council. The citation praised his work with Pakistan’s Christian minority and his role in the 2012 case of Rimsha Masih, the girl charged under the country’s blasphemy . Four days later, on 18 September, Lambeth Palace withdrew the award after reports surfaced that Ashrafi had said in 2007 that Osama bin Laden was serving Muslims by waging jihad against infidels and that he wanted to give bin Laden the title “Saifullah,” or Sword of God.

The residence stated that the newly examined 2007 remarks led it to reconsider the decision and that it had informed Ashrafi of the revocation. Ashrafi told the BBC the comments had been taken out of context. The award now stands rescinded, and the Church of England has made no further public comment on the criteria it will apply to future recipients.
