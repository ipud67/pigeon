The White House announced the partnership on Monday, and Venezuela’s National Assembly, controlled by the ruling party, voted to approve the deal on Tuesday. This marks the latest move in Trump’s effort to shift control of roughly one-fifth of the country’s oil reserves toward US interests.

The arrangement is likely to hold as long as the current Venezuelan government stays in power and the ruling party keeps its majority in the Assembly. Next steps will be the actual hand-over of field operations from the Chinese and Russian operators to Blue Energy Partners, plus any Pentagon oversight details that still need to be worked out on paper. The situation would change only if the Venezuelan opposition gains enough seats to block future votes, or if US policy shifts after the next presidential election.

It could drag on for years unless Maduro’s government loses its grip or Washington changes course.
