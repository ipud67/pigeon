TAIPEI
September 16, 2026 · 06:32 UTC

Presidential Office to hold forum on Taiwan's whole-of-society resilience

The Presidential Office in Taipei began organizing international forums on defense issues after China stepped up military drills and gray-zone pressure around the island in 2022. Those gatherings brought together government officials, private companies and foreign partners to map out how civilians, businesses and the military could coordinate on threats that cross traditional boundaries. The first Resilient Taiwan forum ran in 2024 and produced working groups on protection and information security that continued meeting through 2025.

On September 16 the Presidential Office released details for the second forum set for Saturday at the Grand Hyatt Taipei. About 500 defense specialists, officials and business representatives will attend sessions on civil-military integration, psychological defense, maritime security, cybersecurity, gray-zone response and critical-infrastructure safeguards. Palmer Luckey of Anduril Industries will open with a speech on adapting democratic systems to new forms of conflict. The American Institute in Taiwan director, the UK representative to Taiwan, Japan’s Hirofumi Takinami, Palau’s national security coordinator and other former officials from the Philippines, Lithuania and Latvia will speak. The program ends with a panel featuring National Security Council Secretary-General Joseph Wu and former U.S. deputy national security advisers Matt Pottinger and Anne Neuberger.

The gathering now serves as the public record of two years of concrete steps taken by government agencies, civil groups and companies after natural disasters and hybrid incidents. Those steps include updated continuity plans, joint training exercises and information-sharing protocols. The forum’s timing, one week before the Trump-Xi summit, places the question of Taiwan’s coordinated readiness at the center of allied discussions.
