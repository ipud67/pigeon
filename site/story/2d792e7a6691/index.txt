18:T7e5,Two sailors remain missing after an attack on the Panama-flagged tanker El Gaia in the Strait of Hormuz, 1.3 nautical miles north of Oman's Musandam peninsula. Oman reports the ship caught fire in its engine room and is now under tow to an Omani port with 23 crew rescued. Iran says the tanker hit naval mines on a restricted route, while the US says it was struck by an Iranian missile last month and a drone over the weekend. UK monitors recorded a projectile hit on a vessel in the same area early Sunday. This follows Iran's July strikes on three tankers using the southern Omani route, the collapse of the June Islamabad Memorandum, and the resumption of US strikes and Iranian attacks on shipping.

The pattern shows Iran using missiles, drones, and mines to hit vessels that avoid its approved northern channel. US forces keep striking Iranian maritime targets and recently destroyed two small boats that tried to seize a US sea drone near the strait. Oman and Saudi officials point to Iranian projectiles rather than mines in these incidents. Search-and-rescue continues for fishermen after reported drone strikes on two boats near Kargan, 80 km northeast of Musandam.

This round of tanker and fishing-boat hits is likely to run for weeks until one side alters its posture. Iran holds the ability to seed mines and launch from shore, while the US maintains naval presence and strike capacity from Gulf bases. The next visible step would be either confirmed US strikes on additional Iranian launch or radar sites, or Iran releasing video of mine damage that matches independent verification. The situation changes only if both sides return to a verified truce that reopens the full strait under agreed escort rules.

Next likely moves stay tied to force posture in the narrow waterway: continued projectile and mine attacks on southern-route traffic, matched by US interdiction of Iranian small boats and follow-on strikes on support infrastructure.
