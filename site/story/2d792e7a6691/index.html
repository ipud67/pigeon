STRAIT OF HORMUZ
September 15, 2026 · 16:13 UTC

Two tankers were hit by an unknown projectile or debris in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, a UK maritime agency said

The United States and Iran have fought over control of the Strait of Hormuz since late February 2026 when the US and Israel began strikes on Iran. A June 17 lifted the US naval and Iran's closure of the strait and set a 60-day negotiating window. That deal collapsed on July 8 after Iran hit three commercial tankers that used a route bypassing Iranian-approved transit. The US resumed nightly strikes and Iran resumed attacks on shipping that used the southern passage through Omani waters.

On September 14 an Iranian missile struck the Panama-flagged tanker El Gaia 1.3 nautical miles north of the Musandam peninsula. A drone hit the same vessel over the weekend. The engine room caught fire. Oman's Maritime Security Centre said two sailors are missing and 23 others were rescued. The tanker was under tow toward an Omani port on September 15. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said the ship hit naval mines on a restricted route. The US military said the Iranian account was false and that its forces destroyed two Iranian boats that tried to seize a US unmanned surface vessel in the same waters.

The strait remains closed to normal commercial traffic. Iran continues to target vessels that avoid its preferred northern route while the US maintains its blockade of Iranian ports. A separate overnight incident saw Iranian reports of enemy drones striking two fishing boats near Kargan with several fishermen missing.

Thinking ahead
Two sailors remain missing after an attack on the Panama-flagged tanker El Gaia in the Strait of Hormuz, 1.3 nautical miles north of Oman's Musandam peninsula. Oman reports the ship caught fire in its engine room and is now under tow to an Omani port with 23 crew rescued. Iran says the tanker hit naval mines on a restricted route, while the US says it was struck by an Iranian missile last month and a drone over the weekend. UK monitors recorded a projectile hit on a vessel in the same area early Sunday. This follows Iran's July strikes on three tankers using the southern Omani route, the collapse of the June Islamabad Memorandum, and the resumption of US strikes and Iranian attacks on shipping. The pattern shows Iran using missiles, drones, and mines to hit vessels that avoid its approved northern channel. US forces keep striking Iranian maritime targets and recently destroyed two small boats that tried to seize a US sea drone near the strait. Oman and Saudi officials point to Iranian projectiles rather than mines in these incidents. Search-and-rescue continues for fishermen after reported drone strikes on two boats near Kargan, 80 km northeast of Musandam. This round of tanker and fishing-boat hits is likely to run for weeks until one side alters its posture. Iran holds the ability to seed mines and launch from shore, while the US maintains naval presence and strike capacity from Gulf bases. The next visible step would be either confirmed US strikes on additional Iranian launch or radar sites, or Iran releasing video of mine damage that matches independent verification. The situation changes only if both sides return to a verified truce that reopens the full strait under agreed escort rules. Next likely moves stay tied to force posture in the narrow waterway: continued projectile and mine attacks on southern-route traffic, matched by US interdiction of Iranian small boats and follow-on strikes on support infrastructure. Horizon: at least four to six weeks of tit-for-tat incidents.
