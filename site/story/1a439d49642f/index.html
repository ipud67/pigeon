Tennessee tried twice to execute death row inmate Christa Pike with lethal injection but she lived

Tennessee has carried out executions by lethal injection since it reinstated the death penalty in 1976. The state sets the three-drug protocol that begins with a sedative followed by a paralytic and a heart-stopping agent, and it schedules the date, place, and witnesses once all appeals and requests are exhausted.

On October 1 the execution team at Riverbend Maximum Security Institution in Nashville began the process on Christa Pike, who has been on death row since 1996 for the murder of a fellow student. After the first drug was administered the medical staff could not locate a usable vein for the remaining injections, and the warden halted the procedure before the full sequence could be completed.

The attempt ended with Pike returned to her cell and the state required to decide whether to schedule a new date, pursue different execution methods now allowed under Tennessee , or face further court review of the failed procedure.
