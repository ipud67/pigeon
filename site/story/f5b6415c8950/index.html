Sweden expels Iranian diplomat for targeting Jews

Sweden has tracked Iranian operations against people inside its borders since at least the middle of the last decade. Those operations have used criminal networks to plan violence against Iranian dissidents and against Jewish and Israeli targets. Swedish authorities have documented recruitment of gang members for attacks on Israeli-linked sites in Gothenburg and Stockholm.

On 17 September 2026 the government expelled one employee of the Iranian embassy and summoned the ambassador. Justice Minister Gunnar Strommer said the move followed fresh evidence of continued Iranian direction of threats against Jewish locals, dissidents, and Israeli interests. The Foreign Ministry declared the employee's activities incompatible with the Vienna Convention on diplomatic relations. The Swedish Security Service confirmed that Iran keeps trying to use for violence inside the country.

The expulsion sits inside the wider US-Iran armed conflict that began with American and Israeli strikes in late February 2026. That fight has narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz after the collapse of the June ceasefire memorandum. Sweden now treats the embassy as a direct channel for the same networks it has watched for years.

Sweden has expelled one Iranian embassy employee and summoned the ambassador after its security service tied Tehran to plots against Jewish targets and regime opponents inside the country. Swedish officials say Iran has used criminal networks for these activities over a long period, with past incidents including explosives found at an Israeli tech firm in Gothenburg in June 2024, a teen shooting at the same site, and gunfire outside the Israeli embassy in May 2025. The justice minister cited intelligence work as the basis for the move, while the foreign minister pointed to actions that violate the Vienna Convention on diplomatic relations. Iran has denied similar accusations before and did not comment immediately on this one. This fits the pattern of European countries tightening diplomatic screws on Iran while the US-Iran armed conflict stays focused on the Strait of Hormuz. Expulsions like this usually trigger tit-for-tat responses from Tehran, often within days or weeks. The next observable step would be Iran declaring a Swedish diplomat persona non grata or limiting embassy operations in Stockholm. For the situation to ease, Sweden would need to see a clear drop in proxy activity reports from its security service. The diplomatic friction will likely drag on for months.
