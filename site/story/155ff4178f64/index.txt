1b:T8d7,The U.S. Supreme Court stayed orders from a federal district court and the Eighth Circuit that would have required Missouri to use its 2025 congressional redistricting map for the 2026 election. The 2025 map has not taken effect under the Missouri Constitution as interpreted by the Missouri Supreme Court, which ruled on September 3, 2026, that the map “is not the law and has never been the law” and requires voter approval by referendum before it can govern elections. As a result, the 2022 map drawn by the Missouri Legislature remains the one in force for the current election, which has already begun. The federal plaintiffs argued that the U.S. Constitution demands the 2025 map regardless of state law.

The Court acted under its appellate jurisdiction to review lower federal court injunctions that conflicted with its own September 10 stay order leaving the 2022 map in place. Article I, Section 4, Clause 1 of the Constitution gives state legislatures primary authority to prescribe the times, places, and manner of congressional elections, subject to Congress’s power to alter those regulations. The founding generation understood this clause to leave map-drawing and election mechanics to the states in the first instance. The First Congress passed the Apportionment Act of 1792 without dictating state district lines, leaving those choices to the legislatures or to the people through whatever state processes they adopted.

The Missouri Supreme Court’s September 3 decision rested on the state constitution’s referendum requirement for new laws when sufficient voter signatures are gathered. That process traces to the state’s own sovereign authority over its internal lawmaking, consistent with the federal structure the framers designed. The Federalist No. 43 explains that the clause protects against state legislatures ignoring popular will in election rules while preserving their core role.

This stay returns the case to the 2022 map for the 2026 election unless a further ruling changes it. 1c:T5ae,The Supreme Court issued a per curiam stay on September 25 blocking federal court orders that would have forced Missouri to use its 2025 congressional map for the ongoing 2026 election. The stay keeps the 2022 map in place after the Missouri Supreme Court ruled on September 3 that the 2025 map cannot become law without voter approval in a referendum. This marks the third time the Court has stepped in to preserve the 2022 map since early September, following denials by Justice Kavanaugh on September 8 and the full Court on September 10.

The case now returns to the lower federal courts for further proceedings on the underlying claim that the U.S. Constitution overrides the state referendum requirement. Any shift back to the 2025 map would require either a final merits ruling in the plaintiffs' favor or new action by the Missouri legislature and voters to change the state process before future elections. The referendum on the 2025 map is already set for the November 2026 ballot, which will produce a clear vote count that either ratifies the new boundaries or rejects them.

This round of litigation is likely to run through the post-election period as the district court resumes work on a permanent decision. The next observable step is the outcome of that referendum in November, which will either lock in the 2025 lines for 2028 or keep the fight alive in state and federal courts.
