FDA closes 'explosive diarrhea' case at Taylor Farms in Mexico; confirms outbreak origin

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlineFDA cierra caso de "diarrea explosiva" en Taylor Farms de México; confirma origen de brote

The Food and Drug Administration has tracked outbreaks of cyclosporiasis linked to fresh produce for years. In 2013 the agency began routine testing of imported foods for cyclospora after repeated clusters of illness tied to cilantro and other greens from Mexico. Since then inspectors have sampled water sources and growing fields at farms that ship to the United States.

On October 3 the FDA closed its investigation into a 2025 outbreak that sickened dozens of people across multiple states. Samples taken earlier this year from a Taylor Farms facility in Mexico showed cyclospora in a wastewater tank and in a drainage ditch that supplied irrigation water for lettuce fields on the same property.

The positive test results established the farm as the source of the parasite that had reached U.S. grocery shelves and dinner tables. Taylor Farms has since stopped shipments from that site and the agency has placed the operation on import alert until corrective steps are verified. The case now turns on whether the farm installs and maintains separate wastewater treatment before it can resume exports.
