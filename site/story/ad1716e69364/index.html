Pakistan detained Christian Amir Masih in Punjab since August over blasphemy claims tied to remarks and a video

Pakistan’s blasphemy law has stood since the 1980s. Section 295-C of the Code states that anyone who makes remarks about the Prophet Muhammad faces the death penalty. Sections 295-A and 298 reach deliberate acts and words that wound religious feelings. Police stations register first information reports on complaints, after which courts decide and trial.

On August 11, 2025, officers in Nowshera Virkan arrested Amir Masih of Dhala Sharif after a complaint filed the day before. The complainants said Masih entered a shop in Dera Chowk on August 10, made remarks they found offensive to their beliefs, and that a video containing Christian and Islamic imagery plus Punjabi-language content had circulated on social media under an account linked to him. The FIR listed the three sections above.

Masih was moved on judicial remand to Gujranwala District Prison by September 4. Human Rights Focus Pakistan and the Centre for Legal Aid Assistance & Settlement called for checks on the video’s origin and whether the account belonged to him or had been misused. Local Christians reported an angry gathering, threats to families and property, and some households that locked their doors and left the village until police stepped in. The case remains in pretrial .

The case sits at the pre-trial stage. Courts in these filings normally schedule bail hearings or charge-sheet filings within weeks to months once the investigation file reaches the magistrate. Human Rights Focus Pakistan and CLAAS have asked police to verify the video account ownership and full context before any further steps. Next visible rung is either a police report on the digital evidence or a defense application for bail. Change would require the investigation to clear the 295-C count or the court to drop charges on grounds the material was not his.
