WEST BANK
September 16, 2026 · 12:03 UTC

IDF arrests Iran's Press TV journalist in West Bank, hands her to police, source confirms to 'Post'

The US and Israel opened strikes on Iran in late February 2026. A June lifted the dual and opened a 60-day negotiating window; it collapsed on 8 July and sustained US strikes resumed. The fight has narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz.

On September 16 Israeli soldiers stopped a Press TV camera crew at a checkpoint near Bethlehem in the West Bank. They searched the crew, arrested correspondent Naqqa Hamed, and released the cameraman. The IDF transferred her to Israel Police custody rather than to the Shin Bet.

She remains in police hands. The arrest followed weeks of online questions about how a Palestinian-American journalist for the Iranian state outlet could operate from Area B without prior detention.

Constitutional analysis
The right to life stands first among the unalienable rights named in the Declaration of Independence, and the government it created exists to secure that right along with domestic tranquility and the common defense. Israeli forces arrested a journalist working for Iranian state television in the West Bank on September 16, 2026, and transferred her to civilian police custody after a checkpoint stop near Bethlehem. The IDF confirmed the transfer to Israel Police rather than security services. Iran’s Press TV identified the woman as Palestinian-American Naqqa Hamed and claimed the arrest followed an online campaign questioning her work for the outlet while living in Area B. The founders understood that a nation at war must guard against foreign influence and propaganda that aids its enemies. Federalist 8 warned that perpetual danger from abroad invites standing armies and erodes liberty at home; Federalist 41 added that the first object of government is protection against foreign danger. The Constitution grants Congress power to define and punish offenses against the law of nations in Article I, Section 8, Clause 10, and the First Congress passed the Alien Act of 1798 to control threats from foreign agents during the Quasi-War with France. Washington himself led militia to suppress the Whiskey Rebellion in 1794 to enforce federal law inside U.S. territory. Israel faces an enemy that uses media arms of its regime to support attacks on civilians. Arresting and handing the reporter to police for ordinary legal process follows the founding pattern of separating military necessity from civil authority. The transfer to police rather than intelligence services keeps the response within the rule of law the founders required even in wartime. The facts show Israel acted to protect its people while observing limits the Constitution places on raw power.
Thinking ahead
IDF soldiers stopped a PressTV crew at a checkpoint near Bethlehem on 16 September 2026 and took Palestinian-American correspondent Naqqa Hamed into custody. The military transferred her to Israel Police rather than security services. This fits the pattern of Israeli forces detaining reporters tied to Iranian outlets after online questions about her work in Area B of the West Bank and earlier clashes at Kalandiya. The arrest sits inside the running US-Iran armed conflict that began with strikes in late February 2026, narrowed to the Strait of Hormuz after the collapsed June memorandum, and now includes periodic ground actions in the West Bank. Iranian state media framed it as part of a smear campaign aimed at PressTV coverage. Police custody points to standard criminal or administrative processing rather than immediate intelligence interrogation. How long this specific detention lasts depends on police charging decisions and any court hearing schedule. It could end in release, deportation, or formal charges within days to weeks. The case may draw fresh Iranian statements or reciprocal moves against dual nationals, but the broader Hormuz naval focus shows no sign of shifting because of one journalist arrest. Next visible rung will be the first police or court filing on her status. Horizon: 2–6 weeks until resolution or fade from view.
