ALASKA
November 19, 2026

Trump administration dropped its Alaska lawsuit to block election certification

The Justice Department filed suit last month in court to stop Alaska from its election results if Republican Dan Sullivan lost. The suit asked a judge to order ballots separated for review over possible voter confusion from a second candidate who shared the incumbent's name. The state Supreme Court had already ruled that second candidacy legal and allowed the name on the ballot.

On November 19 the department told the 9th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals to overturn the lower court's dismissal of the case but then dismiss the litigation anyway on the ground that it is now moot.

The underlying order will be vacated and the matter remanded for dismissal on mootness.

Thinking ahead
The Justice Department filed to drop its appeal in the 9th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals on the Alaska Senate election case. The suit had sought to block certification over a second candidate named Dan Sullivan on the ballot and to set aside certain ballots for review. The state Supreme Court had already ruled the second Sullivan’s spot legal, and Mary Peltola won outright with over 50 percent after the incumbent took 45.8 percent, the second Sullivan 1.7 percent, and another Republican 2.1 percent. The filing came two days after Peltola’s victory was declared. This leaves certification on track with no active federal block. The next procedural step is for the appeals court to vacate the lower-court order and formally dismiss the matter. The election result stands. Horizon: certification proceeds without further delay from this suit.
