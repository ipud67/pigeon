DOJ opinion urges states to report migrants lacking permanent legal status

In 1996 Congress passed a law that requires states to report to federal immigration authorities any person who applies for certain welfare or low-income family programs and is not a U.S. citizen or lawful permanent resident. That same year the Clinton administration’s Office of Legal Counsel issued guidance that limited the reporting duty to only those applicants the state already knew were unlawfully present. On September 2 2026 the Justice Department’s Office of Legal Counsel issued a new opinion that rescinds the 1996 guidance and directs states to report every applicant lacking permanent legal status who seeks those benefits. The opinion gives the federal government the names and identifying information states collect during the application process.

Predictive model
The DOJ’s new opinion reverses the narrow Clinton-era reading of federal law and tells states they must flag migrants without permanent legal status when those migrants apply for welfare or low-income programs. The memo lands as one more step in a long-running tug-of-war between federal immigration rules and state-run benefit systems that dates back to the 1996 welfare reform law. Next move sits with the states: they either start filing the reports or challenge the opinion in court. If a federal judge blocks enforcement or Congress changes the underlying statute, the requirement could shrink again. Without those two things the reporting pressure stays in place. This runs until a court ruling or new legislation reverses it. Horizon: 12–24 months.
