Australia launched a campaign for a United Nations Security Council seat for 2029 to 2030

Australia has held a non-permanent seat on the United Nations Security Council five times since the body was created in 1945. The last term ran from 2013 to 2014. Elections for the 2029-2030 seats are scheduled for 2028 in the General Assembly, where 129 votes are needed to win one of the two spots reserved for the Western European and Others Group.

On 21 September 2026 Prime Minister Anthony Albanese formally launched Australia’s bid for one of those seats during a press conference in New York. He named the country’s record as a stable partner in the Pacific and its experience with cyber security, artificial rules, and climate policy as reasons it should serve. Albanese also released a campaign video and said diplomats would begin direct talks with other member states in the days ahead.

The bid now enters a two-year canvassing period that ends with the 2028 vote. Separately Albanese announced that Siobhan McKenna, chair of Australia Post and former head of the National Broadband Network, will become Australia’s next Consul General in New York when the current holder finishes at the end of 2026.

Thinking ahead
Australia launched its bid for a non-permanent seat on the UN Security Council for the 2029-2030 term at a New York press conference on September 21 2026. The move follows the standard election timeline set by the UN General Assembly where candidates campaign for two years before the vote in 2028. Prime Minister Albanese named the appointment of Siobhan McKenna as the next Consul General in New York as part of the diplomatic push. The campaign will run on Australia’s record as a steady contributor to UN peacekeeping and its focus on Pacific island issues such as sea-level rise. Competition for the two seats in the Western European and Others Group has not yet surfaced in public statements but typically sharpens in the final twelve months before balloting. The procedural next step is sustained bilateral meetings with other member states to lock in support ahead of the 2028 vote. This phase of open campaigning is likely to continue for the next eighteen to twenty-four months until the field of declared candidates becomes clear. It moves next to the formal vote in the General Assembly in 2028 where a two-thirds majority of members present and voting is required to win a seat. Horizon: Campaign builds through 2027 then enters final vote push in 2028.
