1b:T7e0,The Constitution starts with the Preamble’s promise to “insure domestic Tranquility” and “establish Justice.” Secure elections stand at the center of that promise because they let citizens choose the officers who will protect life, liberty, and property. The Trump administration asked the Supreme Court to lift a federal judge’s order that blocks the updated Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements database from checking citizenship for voters. The database draws on records already held by the Department of Homeland Security and the Social Security Administration under statutes passed by Congress.

Founders who wrote the Elections Clause in Article I, Section 4 gave Congress power to regulate the time, place, and manner of federal elections so that states could not undermine the national government. The First Congress used that power in the Election Act of 1792 to set uniform rules for choosing presidential electors. Later statutes, including the 1996 Illegal Immigration Reform and Immigrant Responsibility Act, directed agencies to share immigration and citizenship data with states for official purposes. The executive order of March 25, 2026, directed those same agencies to expand the SAVE system for bulk citizenship checks by state election officials.

A district judge ruled that the changes violate privacy provisions in the Social Security Act and the Administrative Procedure Act. The D.C. Circuit declined to stay that order. The administration argues the injunction stops states from removing non-citizens from voter rolls and therefore threatens the accuracy of the vote itself.

The Supreme Court now faces the narrow question whether to pause the injunction while the appeal proceeds. That decision will test whether the federal government may use records it already possesses to help states verify who may lawfully cast a ballot. The ladder runs from the March 25 executive order that told DHS and SSA to build bulk-check tools, through the database overhaul that added U.S.-born records and SSN access, to the League of Women Voters lawsuit, the judge’s injunction, and the appeals-court denial.

Next move sits with the justices. They can grant the stay, which would let states use the current SAVE version right away, or deny it and keep the block in place while the D.C.
