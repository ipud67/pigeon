Illinois Tax Preparer Sentenced for Stealing More than $11M from Taxpayers in COVID-19 Assistance Fraud Scheme

Hiam Hmaidan worked as a tax preparer in Orland Park, Illinois. In 2020 Congress created the Pandemic Unemployment Assistance Program under the CARES Act to pay benefits to people whose jobs were disrupted by the public-health emergency.

The claims triggered more than $11 million in benefits that were mailed to her or addresses she controlled.

The court ordered Hmaidan to pay $11,130,490 in and a $1,965,712 judgment. She and her co-conspirators withdrew about $2.8 million in cash from nearby ATMs after the cards arrived. She was convicted of one count of to commit mail and five counts of mail fraud. The case was investigated by the Labor Department inspector general and IRS Criminal Investigation.

An Illinois tax preparer got 36 months in prison Friday for a fraud scheme that ran from May 2020 through December 2022. Hiam Hmaidan and others filed about 700 fake unemployment claims under the Pandemic Unemployment Assistance Program set up in the 2020 CARES Act. They used client names from her tax business, routed debit cards to addresses they controlled, and pulled roughly 2.8 million dollars in cash from nearby ATMs. She was convicted on one conspiracy count and five mail fraud counts. No indicators point to wider program changes or copycat cases in the public record.
