Former Kremlin political prisoner Mykola Karpiuk killed in action

Mykola Karpiuk spent decades in Ukrainian opposition politics. He joined protests against President Leonid Kuchma in 2000, backed the Orange Revolution in 2004, and took part in the 2013-2014 Revolution of Dignity that removed Viktor Yanukovych. After that he served as head of the Right Sector political wing and co-founded the Ukrainian National Assembly – Ukrainian People's Self-Defence group. In 2014 Russian authorities arrested him and Stanislav Klykh on charges they had killed Russian citizens during the Chechen wars of the 1990s. Both men said their confessions came from torture and threats against their families. A Russian court sentenced Karpiuk to 22 years and six months in 2016. He served the term in a prison in Vladimir Oblast until a prisoner swap on 7 September 2019 returned him and 34 other Ukrainians, including 24 captured sailors, to Kyiv.

On 19 September 2026 Karpiuk died on the frontline in eastern Ukraine while fighting in the war that began with Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022. Former member of parliament Artur Palatnyi, a friend, confirmed the death the next day. Karpiuk had volunteered for combat after he turned 60.

The loss removes one more veteran of the 2014 Maidan events from the active defense of the country. The war continues to draw in former political prisoners who returned in the 2019 exchange.
