Ukrainian air defense downed one Kalibr, all eight Banderol, and 177 drones while recording impacts at 22 sites in Dnipropetrovsk, Kirovohrad, and Poltava regions, including an industrial plant, critical infrastructure, and an agricultural site. The pattern matches the ladder of prior large-scale mixed-missile-and-drone barrages that test Ukrainian intercept stocks and force dispersal of mobile fire teams.

This level of nightly drone-and-missile volume has run at similar scale for months, cycling between mass cheap-drone waves and occasional cruise-missile spikes when launch platforms are available. Ukrainian forces continue to report intercept rates above 80 percent on drones but lower on faster ballistic threats, with hits still getting through to infrastructure. No sign yet of changed Russian launch rates or Ukrainian stock depletion that would shift the exchange.

The attack is still listed as ongoing with drones remaining in Ukrainian airspace as of early September 22. Next likely moves are continued nightly waves aimed at wearing down air-defense munitions and repair crews in the same central regions, unless Russian missile inventories force a pause or Ukrainian electronic-warfare units raise the suppression count further.
