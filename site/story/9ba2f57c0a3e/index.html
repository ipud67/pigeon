Georgia execution delayed for hearing under new law that may reduce sentence

Georgia has carried out the death penalty since the U.S. Supreme Court allowed it again in 1976. The state sets execution dates once all direct appeals and state and federal habeas reviews run out. On September 16 a judge in the case of one condemned inmate stopped that process.

The judge issued a stay so the inmate can have a hearing under a law the passed this year. The gives courts power to reconsider a sentence when the person was a victim of abuse and that history was not fully presented at the original trial. The hearing will decide whether the new facts meet the statute's test for reducing the sentence from death to life.

The execution remains on hold while the hearing goes forward. The outcome turns on whether the inmate proves the abuse claim and whether the judge finds it changes the original judgment enough to trigger relief under the new law.
