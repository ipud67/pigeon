ActBlue board member becomes latest to plead the Fifth in House investigation

Overview
ActBlue board member Kimberly Peeler-Allen invoked the Fifth as lawmakers probed her in their investigation into how the company vets foreign donations and whether it weakened its fraud protections during the 2024 election.
Short history
ActBlue is a Democratic fundraising platform that processes online donations for candidates and committees. In 2024, House investigators began examining how the company screens foreign donations and whether it changed its fraud safeguards that year. On September 1, 2026, board member Kimberly Peeler-Allen appeared before the House panel, invoked her Fifth Amendment right against self-incrimination, and refused to answer questions. She is the latest ActBlue board member to do so in the probe.
Predictive model
The House investigation into ActBlue’s donation screening has now seen multiple board members plead the Fifth when called to testify. Kimberly Peeler-Allen’s invocation follows the same step taken by others before her in the same probe. Each refusal adds another documented instance that the committee can log as non-cooperation. Next the panel is likely to keep issuing subpoenas for remaining witnesses and compile a public report that lists every Fifth Amendment plea alongside the specific questions asked. The investigation continues until the committee either wraps its hearings or the next Congress changes the priorities. A shift would require either new voluntary testimony from ActBlue insiders, a court ruling that forces answers, or the House dropping the matter. This rung sits on the ladder of repeated witness refusals that have slowed the probe since it began. Horizon: at least through end of current Congress.
