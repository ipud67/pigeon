19:T5dc,The Takapau Health Centre has run as a nurse-led service in the old post office building since 1989. Four years ago the community trust set out to add GPs and shift into a purpose-built site after buying a transportable medical building from Kaweka Hospital for $1.2 million. On 5 October the new GP-led practice opens a few hundred metres down the road with a fresh team of doctors and nurse practitioners already hired.

The centre now has room to treat more patients in Central Hawke’s Bay where 1800 people remain unenrolled. Local barriers like distance, cost of fuel and lack of public transport have kept many away until they get very sick. The trust’s not-for-profit model and track record of community care were the stated reasons it could recruit in a tight national doctor market.

The service will keep running on the same charitable footing. Next measurable step is patient numbers and enrolment rates at the new site in the first three to six months. If those figures rise steadily and the current team stays in place the practice is likely to hold its current size and hours. A drop in staff or flat enrolment would signal the model needs more funding or different hiring tactics.

This rural GP setup will probably run at least two to three years before the next big review. It may add telehealth slots or satellite clinics if enrolment climbs faster than the building can handle.
