An 18-year-old Minnesota man was arrested for planning an ISIS-style attack on the Mall of America, the FBI says

A Minnesota man came under federal investigation earlier this year after he began distributing ISIS propaganda online and recorded a video pledging allegiance to the group. He told investigators he had considered traveling to Somalia to join al-Shabaab or ISIS but instead focused on carrying out an attack inside the United States. Court records show he had looked at past attacks including the 2016 Pulse nightclub shooting in Florida and talked about recruiting others to help strike crowded sites.

On October 6 the 18-year-old bought a Norinco AK-47-style rifle, four 30-round magazines and roughly 200 rounds of ammunition from an undercover FBI agent for $600. Federal agents arrested him at gunpoint as he left the vehicle with the weapons. Charging documents filed the next day in U.S. District Court for the District of Minnesota state that he had picked October 24 as the date to open fire inside the Mall of America in Bloomington because the mall is owned by the Ghermezian family through Triple Five Group.

He now faces one federal count of acquiring a firearm and ammunition with intent to use them in a felony or a terrorism crime. The arrest marks the eighth person taken into federal custody this year on ISIS-related charges, according to a researcher who tracks such cases.
