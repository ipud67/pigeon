Two arrests for human smuggling and exploitation at Indian restaurants

Translated from Dutch · read the original →
The original headlineTwee arrestaties om mensensmokkel en uitbuiting bij Indiase restaurants

Dutch labour law gives the Labour Inspectorate’s Investigation Service power to open criminal probes into employers who bring workers into the country without permits or force them to work under illegal conditions. That authority has stood since the service received expanded police powers in the early 2010s.

On 18 September 2026 the service arrested two men who own eight Indian restaurants spread across Utrecht, Amsterdam, The Hague and Hoofddorp. The restaurants operate as part of several hospitality chains. Europol officers took part in the raids. The agency states that more than seventy people were moved into the Netherlands and put to work in the restaurants under terms that meet the legal definition of human and exploitation.

The two owners remain in while investigators examine payroll records, housing arrangements and recruitment documents from the eight sites. The Labour Inspectorate has not released the names of the arrested men or the specific chains involved.
