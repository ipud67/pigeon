Police took the owners into custody as the first formal step after the joint operation gathered evidence on recruitment and working conditions.

Next steps center on the public prosecutor's review of the case file. Investigators will likely question the suspects, interview listed victims, and examine business records from the restaurants. The situation changes only if prosecutors drop charges for lack of evidence or if a court rules the arrests invalid on procedural grounds.

This type of labor case typically moves from arrest to indictment within weeks, then to trial if the file holds. Without a major gap in the evidence chain, the owners stay in the system until a judge decides on release or continued detention.
