US court records capture Gazan terrorist laughing during Oct. 7 massacre

The Hamas-led assault on southern Israel began at 6:30 a.m. on October 7, 2023. Israeli border towns came under coordinated attack that morning from fighters who crossed from Gaza.

On the same day prosecutors filed new exhibits in the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Louisiana, transcripts of two intercepted calls placed Mahmud Amin Ya’qub al-Muhtadi inside one of those towns. At 8:55 a.m. an interlocutor told al-Muhtadi he had rifles but no ammunition. Al-Muhtadi instructed him to bring the rifles and whatever he had, then laughed when the man repeated the request for ammunition. At 9:33 a.m. al-Muhtadi told another caller he had gone inside, directed him to meet at a place called Malaka’s, ordered those around him to turn off their phones and not mention Al-Qassam, and confirmed he had extra ammunition. He also told the caller to secure a motorcycle if possible. The same court records show that after al-Muhtadi entered the United States in September 2024 an associate warned him online against contacting resistance figures or posting images of Yahya Sinwar.

The transcripts now sit as government exhibits in a motion to compel the defense to turn over its own planned trial material. Al-Muhtadi remains in Louisiana on immigration-fraud charges.
