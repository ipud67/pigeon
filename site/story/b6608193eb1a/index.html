Immigrants held in small outdoor cages at 'Alligator Alcatraz' in Florida, investigators find

Florida opened a temporary immigration center in the Everglades in 2025 under an executive order from Governor Ron DeSantis. The state built the facility in eight days on protected wetland and called it Alligator Alcatraz. The federal government covered operational costs while Florida ran day-to-day operations. Between July 2025 and January 2026 the center held immigrants in housing units that gave them little room to move.

On an unannounced visit in January 2026 inspectors from the Department of Homeland Security's Office of the Inspector General found 79 immigrants had been placed in outdoor metal cages measuring 18 square feet. The cages were used for periods from a few minutes up to nearly two hours, once as discipline. The same inspection turned up showers with bugs, water that detainees said was not clean, plastic cups that could not be washed, showers allowed only three times a week, and medical care and food that fell short of required standards.

The report describing those conditions reached the public on September 14, 2026. By then the state had already closed the center in June after calling it a short-term site. The federal government told the inspector general that Florida held authority over daily operations at the facility.
