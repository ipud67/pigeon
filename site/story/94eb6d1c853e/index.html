August 26, 2026 · 15:49 UTC

US Senator Blumenthal hopes Graham bill on against Russia will pass in September

Overview
US Senator Richard Blumenthal said the bill has strong Republican support and would impose secondary sanctions and measures against Russia’s shadow fleet.
Short history
The US first imposed sanctions on Russia after its 2014 annexation of Crimea. Those measures targeted Russian banks, energy firms, and officials. In 2022, after Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine, the US, EU, and allies banned Russian oil shipments and froze central bank assets. Russia responded by building a shadow fleet of older tankers that do not carry Western insurance and use ship-to-ship transfers to evade the price cap. The fleet now moves most of Russia’s seaborne oil. On August 26 2026, Senator Richard Blumenthal said a bill backed by Senator Lindsey Graham would pass in September. The bill would impose secondary sanctions on foreign companies and ports that help operate or service that shadow fleet.
Predictive model
Senator Blumenthal is pushing a bill that would hit Russia’s shadow fleet with secondary sanctions. The measure already carries strong Republican backing in the Senate. Next procedural step is a floor vote, which Blumenthal expects could happen in September. The bill sits on a ladder of repeated attempts to tighten Russia sanctions since the Ukraine invasion began. Passage would need 60 votes to clear a filibuster and then House agreement. Without clear signs of House leadership scheduling or a veto threat from the White House, momentum could stall again. If September comes and goes with no vote, the bill likely joins earlier versions that never reached the president’s desk. Horizon: slim chance of passage by end of September; more likely drifts into fall without floor action.
