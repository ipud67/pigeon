SEOUL
September 14, 2026 · 07:31 UTC

(2nd LD) Seoul stocks dip over 3 pct on Mideast uncertainties, AI warnings

South Korea’s main stock index has traded near 7,000 for most of the past year after the central bank raised rates in 2022 and held them steady while the country’s biggest firms poured money into memory chips, batteries and defense electronics. Foreign and institutional investors have been the main buyers during that stretch, pushing the KOSPI from its post-rate-hike lows.

On Sept. 14 the index dropped 225.54 points, or 3.26 percent, to close at 6,684.37. Samsung Electronics lost 4.05 percent, SK hynix fell 6.35 percent, and most other tech and auto shares followed. Traders cited weekend statements by Anthropic’s co-founder and other U.S. AI executives calling for slower development, which hit chip stocks directly. At the same time, a planned meeting to reopen transit through the Hormuz Strait was postponed, keeping Brent crude above 108 dollars a barrel and adding to the sell-off. Institutions and foreigners sold a net 4.46 trillion won while retail investors bought 2.97 trillion won.

The won weakened to 1,347.3 against the dollar. Three-year Treasury yields rose 1.1 basis points to 4.025 percent and five-year yields added 1.2 basis points to 4.279 percent. Financial stocks gained on the view that both the Bank of Korea and the Federal Reserve will keep rates higher for longer.
