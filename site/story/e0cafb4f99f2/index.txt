Congress set this framework in Title I, Section 112(b)(3) of the Trade and Development Act of 2000, Public Law 106-200, as amended through the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027, Public Law 119-103. The statute caps the preference at seven percent of the aggregate square meter equivalents of all apparel imported into the United States in the preceding 12-month period for which data are available. These limits rest on Congress’s enumerated power to regulate commerce with foreign nations under Article I, Section 8, Clause 3 of the Constitution. The first Congress exercised the same power by passing the Tariff Act of 1789, which imposed duties on imports to protect domestic industry while directing revenue to the general welfare. The Trade and Development Act of 2000 extended similar preferences to sub-Saharan nations through 2028 under the African Growth and Opportunity Act program. The notice supplies the exact cap numbers that will govern entries during the next period. 1b:T41b,The Committee for the Implementation of Textile Agreements published a new annual cap on duty-free U.S. imports of apparel assembled in sub-Saharan beneficiary countries using fabric from the region or from third countries. This cap sits at 7 percent of total U.S. apparel imports over the prior 12 months, with a 3.5 percent sub-cap for goods from lesser-developed countries. The limit stems directly from Section 112(b)(3) of the Trade and Development Act of 2000 as amended through Public Law 119-103, which keeps the preference in force until the end of 2028. 

The notice sets the enforceable ceiling that customs will apply starting with the new trade year. Once the cap is reached, further qualifying shipments face normal tariffs. No immediate change in the law or preference schedule is scheduled. The cap will reset at the next 12-month look-back unless Congress alters the percentages or the program expires.

The arrangement continues at current scale until at least December 31, 2028.
