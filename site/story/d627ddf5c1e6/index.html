US forces destroy three IRGC oil tankers after coming under Iranian fire

US and Iranian forces have fought for control of the Strait of Hormuz since late February 2026. The US and Israel began strikes on Iran on 28 February. A 17 June lifted the US naval of Iran and Iran's closure of the strait and set a 60-day negotiating period. That window ended on 8 July when the US resumed sustained strikes after Iran hit three commercial tankers on 6 July. On 12 July the IRGC moved to shut the strait and Tehran suspended the memorandum. On 5 September 2026 US forces destroyed three IRGC oil tankers after Iranian fire.

Constitutional analysis
US forces sank three IRGC oil tankers after Iranian fire hit two American vessels in international waters. The right to life and the security of American sailors come first, as the Declaration states that governments are instituted to secure unalienable rights and the Preamble lists domestic Tranquility and common defense as core purposes. The founders backed this with force when needed. Washington led militia against the Whiskey Rebellion in 1794 to restore order and protect commerce. Congress passed the Militia Acts of 1792 and naval measures in the Quasi-War with France to guard shipping lanes. The Tariff Act of 1789 funded protection of trade routes from pirates and hostile powers. Article I, Section 8 gives Congress power to provide for the common defense, raise navies, and punish piracies on the high seas. The response here matches that enumerated authority without expanding beyond it. The action stopped immediate threats to American lives and imposed costs on the attacker in line with the founders' view of measured self-defense at sea.
Thinking ahead
US forces hit three IRGC oil tankers after Iranian fire in the Strait of Hormuz on September 5. This follows the July 12 IRGC push to shut the strait, the collapse of the June Islamabad Memorandum, and the pattern of tanker strikes that started with the three commercial vessels hit on July 6. The exchange keeps the fight locked on who moves oil through the 21-mile choke point. The tit-for-tat cycle of shots at ships and immediate replies has run since early July. Each side now matches or exceeds the last hit, as shown by the US move from two ships fired on to three destroyed. No new talks have started since the memorandum ended. Next steps hinge on whether Iran tries to close the strait again with more vessels or mines, or if the US adds escorts and alternate routes to keep tankers moving. Without one side backing off the waterway or both sides agreeing to a fresh ceasefire, the naval clashes stay centered here. This pattern looks set to run at least another month.
