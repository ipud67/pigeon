That gap is the latest rung in a ladder that began with the crash itself, moved through the recovery of wreckage and bodies, and then into the formal parliamentary probe that started earlier this year. Witnesses have so far described missing radar logs, incomplete maintenance records, and conflicting accounts from air-traffic control.

The inquiry still has several more weeks of public hearings scheduled. Next it will call the German investigators and senior Malawi Defence Force officers to explain the discrepancy on record. How far the story runs depends on whether those witnesses produce new documents or simply repeat earlier statements; if the gap stays unresolved, the parliamentary committee is expected to hand its findings to the president by year-end.

Without fresh records or a second technical review, the mismatch will sit in the final report as an open question. The procedural clock is now set by the remaining witness list and the committee’s deadline.
