Rep. Thomas Massie introduces articles of impeachment against Hegseth

Rep. Thomas Massie entered the House in 2013 as a Republican from Kentucky and served seven terms. The Constitution assigns Congress the power to declare war and limits the executive branch on committing forces to hostilities. Presidents have ordered military actions in multiple countries without that declaration since the 1970s.

On September 15, 2026, Massie read articles of impeachment on the House floor charging Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth with abuse of power. The articles cite Hegseth’s execution of orders that imposed hostilities against Iran, plus operations in Venezuela and Yemen conducted without approval. Massie stated that Hegseth placed American troops in danger and remains a threat to the Constitution if he stays in office.

The House now has two legislative days to consider the resolution or to table it. The move follows impeachment articles filed by House Democrats in April that centered on the same Iran operations and an accidental strike on a girls’ elementary school that killed as many as 175 people according to the preliminary count. Lawmakers continue to debate whether the secretary exceeded the legal authority required for those deployments.

Constitutional analysis
Rep. Thomas Massie introduced articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth on the House floor. Massie charged that Hegseth issued an unlawful order imposing hostilities against Iran, and that operations in Venezuela and Yemen lacked congressional approval. The Constitution places the power to declare war in Article I, Section 8, Clause 11 with Congress alone. The founders designed this to keep any single officer from dragging the nation into armed conflict. Federalist 69 explains that the president has far less warmaking power than the British king precisely because Congress must authorize hostilities. The right to life stands first among the unalienable rights the Declaration lists, and government exists to secure it by maintaining domestic tranquility and common defense under lawful authority. Massie stated Hegseth remains a threat to the Constitution if he stays in office. The House has two legislative days to consider the articles or table them. A preliminary count from the Iran strike lists as many as 175 people killed at a girls’ elementary school that U.S. forces accidentally targeted.
