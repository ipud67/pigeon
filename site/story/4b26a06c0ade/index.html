US and Iran hold first talks since June after Trump's 'annihilation' threat

The United States and Iran have been in an open military conflict since February. On the 28th of that month the US and Israel carried out air strikes inside Iran. Iran answered by firing on Israel, on US bases across the Gulf, and on the territory of US allies in the region. It also closed the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow sea passage that before the war carried about one-fifth of global oil and liquefied natural gas. That closure drove sharp swings in energy prices and kept pressure on both capitals. A reached in June broke down within weeks, after which the two sides traded fire at intervals but did not return to full-scale combat.

On 23 September senior officials from both governments met for three hours on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York. The session, arranged through Qatari mediators, was the first direct contact since the June ceasefire collapsed. Iran’s foreign minister Abbas Araghchi sat across from Steve Witkoff, the envoy named by President Trump. No agreement was announced. Iran restated its call for an end to the fighting on every front and for the United States to halt what it called acts of aggression. Witkoff described the talks as completed and said he hoped they would prove constructive.

The meeting took place on the same day Trump addressed the General Assembly. He told reporters afterward that the session had been productive. The two governments now return to their capitals with the next round of contacts still to be scheduled. The conflict continues to turn on whether the Hormuz route reopens and whether the intermittent exchanges of fire remain limited.

Constitutional analysis
US officials and Iranian representatives held their first direct talks since a June ceasefire broke down, meeting on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York on September 23, 2026. The three-hour session was mediated by Qatar and included Trump's envoy Steve Witkoff and Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi. Iran repeated its call for an end to US acts of aggression and the war on all fronts. No agreement was announced. Trump addressed the assembly the same day and stated the choice was either a deal that lets Iran rebuild or quick annihilation of the Islamic Republic to stop it from killing and destroying again. He said he expected a deal after the November congressional elections. The talks follow US and Israeli strikes on Iran in February and Iran's responses that included attacks on Israel, US bases in the Gulf, and blocking the Strait of Hormuz. The Constitution grants the president power as commander in chief to direct military operations and conduct diplomacy under Article II, Section 2. The founders expected the executive to protect the nation from foreign danger while Congress holds the power to declare war under Article I, Section 8. Washington himself led the militia to suppress the Whiskey Rebellion in 1794, showing the executive duty to restore domestic tranquility and secure the peace the Preamble names as the first object of government. The right to life stands first among the unalienable rights listed in the Declaration, the very reason government is instituted. Any settlement that prevents nuclear weapons from reaching a regime that has attacked civilians and closed a chokepoint for 20 percent of world oil traffic serves that first purpose. The November elections will test whether the people consent to the course the executive has set.
Thinking ahead
US and Iranian officials met in New York on September 23 for the first direct talks since the June ceasefire broke down. The session ran three hours through a Qatari go-between, with Iran's foreign minister repeating the call to stop all US and allied military action. Trump's envoy called the round constructive and gave a thumbs-up on the way out. Trump spoke the same day at the UN General Assembly, repeating the choice between a deal after November's congressional elections or quick overwhelming force. No agreement came out of the meeting and both sides stuck to their opening positions. The next visible step is another round through the same mediator or a public signal from either capital that the gap on ending strikes has narrowed. Without one side dropping its core demand the pattern of intermittent exchanges and stalled talks holds. Horizon: weeks at most before the next procedural contact or renewed public threats.
