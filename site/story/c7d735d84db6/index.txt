22:T4e1,The Treasury Department put out proposed rules on October 1 for the new nonrefundable federal tax credit that lets taxpayers get a break for donations to state-certified scholarship granting organizations. These groups hand out money for elementary and secondary school tuition. The rules spell out who qualifies as a taxpayer donor, which states can opt in by certifying the organizations, and what the organizations must do to stay certified. Written comments are due by December 1, with a public hearing set for December 15 unless no one signs up to speak by the deadline.

This sits on top of the credit's addition to the tax code earlier this year. The proposal mainly sets the mechanics for how states and organizations report and track the donations and scholarships.

The process will run at least until the December 1 comment cutoff and the December 15 hearing. After that the agency will review what comes in and decide whether to change the rules before they take final form. No clear indicators yet on how many comments or requests to speak will arrive, so the exact timing for final rules stays open. Next step is the volume and content of public input by early December.
