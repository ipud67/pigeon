1b:T564,Haiti sent 18 suspects to Miami on September 21 for trial in the 2021 killing of President Jovenel Moise. The group includes 17 former Colombian soldiers and Joseph Badio, a former senior official in Haiti's justice ministry. US prosecutors have indicted 30 people total so far, with four already convicted in Miami in May on charges of conspiracy to kill or kidnap the president and providing material support.

This is the latest step in a case that began with the shooting at Moise's home in Port-au-Prince. Haitian probes have repeatedly stalled under gang control and weak courts, shifting the load to US authorities who say the plot was planned and funded from Florida, the Dominican Republic, and Haiti. The extraditions follow earlier arrests and killings of some plotters at the scene.

The trials will move forward in federal court in the Southern District of Florida. More defendants could arrive if Haiti locates and hands them over, or if arrests happen in the Dominican Republic or elsewhere. Progress hinges on whether Haitian gangs keep blocking local investigations and whether US prosecutors secure convictions that withstand appeals from the Colombians, who say they thought they were part of a coup, not a murder.

The case stays active in US courts until the remaining indicted suspects are tried or the probe closes.
