Haiti sent 18 suspects to the US to face trial for the murder of president Jovenel Moise five years ago

Haiti has operated without a functioning president since gunmen killed Jovenel Moise in his bedroom in Port-au-Prince in July 2021. The attackers were mostly Colombian former soldiers hired through a Miami security firm. Haitian courts could not move the case forward because gangs control large parts of the country and the justice system has collapsed. US prosecutors took the lead after they determined that the plot was planned and paid for from South Florida as well as the Dominican Republic and Haiti.

On September 21 2026 authorities in Haiti put 18 of the accused on a plane to Miami. The group included 17 Colombian ex-soldiers and Joseph Badio, a former senior official in Haiti's justice ministry who had worked in an anti-corruption unit. They are the latest to reach the Southern District of Florida where 30 people have now been . Four others were convicted there in May on charges of to kill or kidnap the president and providing material support for the killing.

The extraditions shift the remaining defendants into US federal court where they face possible life sentences. Haitian investigators continue to operate inside a capital where more than 1,400 people were killed between April and June of this year. The country has set December 13 for its first presidential and elections since 2016 if security allows the vote to take place.

Haiti sent 18 suspects to Miami on September 21 for trial in the 2021 killing of President Jovenel Moise. The group includes 17 former Colombian soldiers and Joseph Badio, a former senior official in Haiti's justice ministry. US prosecutors have indicted 30 people total so far, with four already convicted in Miami in May on charges of conspiracy to kill or kidnap the president and providing material support. This is the latest step in a case that began with the shooting at Moise's home in Port-au-Prince. Haitian probes have repeatedly stalled under gang control and weak courts, shifting the load to US authorities who say the plot was planned and funded from Florida, the Dominican Republic, and Haiti. The extraditions follow earlier arrests and killings of some plotters at the scene. The trials will move forward in federal court in the Southern District of Florida. More defendants could arrive if Haiti locates and hands them over, or if arrests happen in the Dominican Republic or elsewhere. Progress hinges on whether Haitian gangs keep blocking local investigations and whether US prosecutors secure convictions that withstand appeals from the Colombians, who say they thought they were part of a coup, not a murder. The case stays active in US courts until the remaining indicted suspects are tried or the probe closes. Horizon: trials run at least into 2027.
