Brookfield to invest up to $600 million in India's ACME green fuels business

India began pushing large companies to build renewable energy projects after 2014 when the central government set targets for solar and wind capacity and later added rules that required some new industrial plants to run on green hydrogen made from those sources. ACME Group started one of the first big green ammonia plants in the country in 2021 at a port in Gujarat and signed supply deals with shipping lines that run on the carbon-free fuel.

On September 17 2026 Brookfield Asset Management agreed to put as much as 600 million dollars into ACME's green fuels unit. The Canadian firm will buy a stake that gives it a seat on the board and lets it help pick which new projects move forward inside India and in markets abroad.

The investment hands ACME cash to finish construction on two more green ammonia facilities already under way and to line up land and power purchase agreements for several others that have not yet broken ground. The unit now operates with a clear line of equity from a global owner while the Indian company keeps control of day-to-day decisions on technology and local permits.

Thinking ahead
Brookfield signed a deal to put as much as 600 million dollars into ACME's green fuels unit in India. The move fits a string of big-ticket foreign bets on Indian renewable projects that picked up speed after 2022 policy tweaks on land and power sales. Treasury yields sit at 4.14 percent on the three-month bill, 4.74 percent on the two-year note, 5.01 percent on the ten-year, and 5.35 percent on the thirty-year as of September 16, with the two-to-ten spread at 27 basis points; those rates make long-horizon clean-energy builds in Asia look competitive against safer paper. The partnership is likely to keep rolling for at least the next 18 to 24 months while ACME lines up offtake contracts and builds the first plants. Next visible steps are milestone cash draws tied to construction start and any follow-on equity from local banks or export-credit agencies. If fresh permits or power-purchase deals drop in the next two quarters the project stays on track; delays there would stretch the timeline. Horizon: through mid-2028.
