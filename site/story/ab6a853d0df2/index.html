Chikli turns down apology to Gaza border communities for Oct. 7, blames Oslo accords

Israeli security officials had warned for years that Hamas could stage a large-scale attack from Gaza. Those warnings dated back at least to the early 2000s, after the second intifada, and grew sharper after the 2005 Israeli withdrawal from the Gaza Strip and Hamas's takeover of the territory in 2007. Successive governments maintained a policy of containment that included border fences, periodic military operations, and diplomatic efforts to isolate the group.

On 10 September 2026, Heritage Minister Amichai Chikli refused a request to apologize to communities near the Gaza border for the government's failure to prevent the 7 October 2023 attack. He said the oversight occurred on the government's watch and that demanding an apology from him amounted to blaming officials as if they had acted intentionally. Chikli instead pointed to the Oslo accords of the 1990s as the root cause that set the conditions for later failures.

The refusal keeps the debate over for the October 2023 breach centered on disagreements about past peace agreements rather than on operational lapses in the months immediately before the attack. The communities that lost residents that day continue to press for direct acknowledgment from the ministers who held power at the time.
