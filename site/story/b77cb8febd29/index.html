RED SEA
September 11, 2026 · 12:13 UTC

Saudi crown prince urged Trump to strike Houthis amid Red Sea threat

Saudi Arabia has watched Iranian-backed forces operate along key shipping lanes in the Red Sea since the US-Iran conflict widened in early 2026. The fight began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran at the end of February. A June between the two presidents lifted a US naval and Iran's closure of the Strait of Hormuz for 60 days of talks. That deal fell apart on July 8 after Iran hit commercial tankers and moved to shut the strait again. US strikes resumed that night and have continued since.

On September 11 Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman called President Trump twice. He pressed for US strikes on the Houthis after the group advanced toward a critical Red Sea passage. Trump turned down the request. US officials said the administration has no plans for direct intervention against the Houthis at this time.

The war remains centered on who controls passage through the Strait of Hormuz. Oil prices and shipping volumes still swing with each new clash or threat along the route.
