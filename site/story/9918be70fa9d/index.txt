1d:T4f9,The US has imposed sanctions on the International Criminal Court as an institution, including bans on American companies supplying banking or software services, with a six-month grace period before full effect. The court and a group of member states that includes the UK, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, and the Netherlands have stated their disagreement and reaffirmed support for its operations. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has called on those allies to restrain the court and said the measures will stay in place until the ICC ends actions the US views as threats to its sovereignty.

The sanctions build on earlier US steps that targeted 17 specific judges and officials. They do not list withdrawal of existing arrest warrants as a condition for lifting the measures. The six-month delay gives member states time to keep funding the court while talks occur on US concerns.

How long this lasts depends on whether negotiations produce changes the US accepts or whether the court and its backers simply absorb the financial limits. Next observable step is the end of the grace period or any public adjustment by ICC member states in response to the US call to rein in the institution.
