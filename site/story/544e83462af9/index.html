Black woman found hanging from tree was dead before body was 'staged', police say

Tasia Fortune was a 29-year-old mother of four who lived in Jackson, Mississippi. Her body was discovered hanging from a tree on the grounds of an abandoned home there in August.

On September 26 a detective told a court that Fortune had already died before the body was placed in the tree and the scene arranged to look like a hanging. The medical examiner ruled the death a homicide. Police linked the killing to a dispute over drugs. Three men have been arrested in the case, all Black: 51-year-old Jarques Ratliff, who pleaded not guilty and remains in custody; 25-year-old Earnest Lloyd Jr., arrested this week; and 45-year-old Eric Clark, who turned himself in Friday and was charged with murder.

Fortune's mother, Christy Spivey, said authorities told her the death was not an actual hanging and that her daughter had fought for her life. Spivey has called for no innocent people to be jailed. The case is one of several hanging deaths of Black people in recent years that have led members of Congress to ask the Justice Department and FBI to review such incidents nationwide.

The founding generation treated murder as a direct breach of that peace, handled under state criminal law with no enumerated federal power to take over local homicide cases. The First Congress passed the Crimes Act of 1790, which defined federal crimes but left ordinary murder to the states.
