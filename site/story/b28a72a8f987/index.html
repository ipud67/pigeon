November 19, 2026 · 21:35 UTC

Trump administration withdrew its Supreme Court bid to block a ruling on Venezuelan protected status

In 2021 and again in 2023 the secretary of homeland security added Venezuela because of conditions there. The secretary extended that designation through October 2026. After the new ended both the designation and the extension the holders sued in court in San Francisco. The district judge set the termination aside and the Ninth Circuit upheld that ruling. The Supreme Court first stayed the judge’s order in 2025 so removals could proceed while the case continued. In June 2026 the court held in a pair of cases on Haitian and Syrian beneficiaries that judges generally cannot review the secretary’s decision to end a country’s designation.

On November 18 the Trump administration asked the justices to block the September 2025 ruling from taking effect once the Ninth Circuit’s stay expired on November 20. The solicitor general argued the order would force the department to restore status for hundreds of thousands of Venezuelans after a nineteen-month pause and to release about 1 800 from .

The administration withdrew that application on November 19. The lower-court order now stands and the case returns to the Ninth Circuit for further proceedings on the limits of judicial review after the Supreme Court’s June ruling.

Thinking ahead
The Trump administration pulled its emergency request to the Supreme Court that would have kept a lower-court order from restoring Temporary Protected Status for Venezuelan nationals. The request came after the 9th Circuit put an administrative stay in place that runs out on Nov. 20, following the Supreme Court's June 2026 ruling in the Haitian and Syrian cases that generally blocks court review of the homeland security secretary's decisions on TPS designations. That earlier stay from the Supreme Court had allowed deportations to proceed for 19 months while the Venezuelan case played out. The withdrawal leaves the 9th Circuit's administrative stay set to expire in one day. Without further action from the appeals court or another filing, the San Francisco federal judge's order would take effect and require the Department of Homeland Security to restart TPS for the group. The case now sits with the 9th Circuit for any next procedural step on the underlying challenge to the termination decision. The situation changes only if the government files a fresh request or the circuit court rules on the remand. Horizon: end of November.
