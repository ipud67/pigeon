CBN, finance ministry sign MoU for improved fiscal-monetary policy coordination

The Central Bank of Nigeria and the Federal Ministry of Finance have worked together for decades on inflation management, debt sustainability, budget financing, exchange rate stability, economic reform programmes, and responses to domestic and global shocks. That cooperation operated through ad-hoc meetings and shared work on specific crises without a single written framework that set out regular consultation, information exchange, and joint planning.

On Friday in Abuja the two institutions signed a of Understanding that sets out a structured process for those tasks. The document requires regular consultation, information exchange, and policy coordination on government cash management, debt issuance planning, liquidity forecasting, macroeconomic analysis, and periodic policy reviews. It also supplies the operational framework the central bank needs as it moves to an inflation-targeting system that depends on aligned fiscal settings for liquidity, interest rates, and government borrowing.

The agreement turns decades of informal practice into a formal institutional arrangement with clear processes and fixed engagement mechanisms. The economy now operates inside that written coordination framework.

Thinking ahead
The Central Bank of Nigeria and the Federal Ministry of Finance signed a formal memorandum of understanding on September 18 2026 in Abuja. The document sets up regular meetings, data sharing, and joint work on cash management, debt planning, liquidity forecasts, and macro analysis. It builds on decades of ad-hoc cooperation that already covered inflation control, budget financing, and responses to shocks, and it now locks in those habits with written procedures just as the central bank shifts to an inflation-targeting system that needs steady fiscal backing. The next concrete steps are the first scheduled consultation under the new framework and the drafting of the operational rules that turn the paper into day-to-day routines. How long the arrangement lasts will be visible in whether those meetings happen on time, whether joint forecasts start to line up, and whether fiscal borrowing stays inside the limits the central bank can sterilise. If either side misses deadlines or if government spending spikes without notice, the coordination track record will flatten quickly. This formal channel is likely to run at least through the current fiscal year while both institutions test the new processes. It could widen to cover joint contingency plans for external shocks or narrow again if one party treats the meetings as optional. Horizon: visible test by end of Q1 2027.
