The document sets up regular meetings, data sharing, and joint work on cash management, debt planning, liquidity forecasts, and macro analysis. It builds on decades of ad-hoc cooperation that already covered inflation control, budget financing, and responses to shocks, and it now locks in those habits with written procedures just as the central bank shifts to an inflation-targeting system that needs steady fiscal backing.

The next concrete steps are the first scheduled consultation under the new framework and the drafting of the operational rules that turn the paper into day-to-day routines. How long the arrangement lasts will be visible in whether those meetings happen on time, whether joint forecasts start to line up, and whether fiscal borrowing stays inside the limits the central bank can sterilise. If either side misses deadlines or if government spending spikes without notice, the coordination track record will flatten quickly.

This formal channel is likely to run at least through the current fiscal year while both institutions test the new processes. It could widen to cover joint contingency plans for external shocks or narrow again if one party treats the meetings as optional.
