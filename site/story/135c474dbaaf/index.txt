1b:T619,The Pentagon inspector general delivered its first mandated accounting of the Iran war on September 15. The report tallies $33.4 billion spent from February 28 through June 30, with $22.3 billion on munitions already used, plus damage to four F-15E fighters, one F-35A, one A-10, 12 KC-135 tankers, nine other aircraft, and at least 30 large drones. It also records strikes on the Navy logistics hub in Bahrain and the displacement of over 20,000 US personnel. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth rejected the findings while President Trump posted that production of high-end weapons is at record levels and deliveries continue daily. Trump said over the weekend the war could run past the November midterms.

The munitions shortfall and procurement bottlenecks are now on the congressional record. Pentagon teams are already adjusting purchase rules, shortening build times, and stockpiling parts to speed replacement. How long the gap lasts hinges on factory output rates and new appropriations. The next public marker is the follow-on inspector general report covering July through December, due early next year. If production data in that report shows sustained monthly increases matching Trump’s claim, the shortfall narrows; if it does not, the gap carries into 2027.

The war itself stays open-ended until one side accepts a ceasefire or a decisive battlefield shift occurs. Current indicators point to continued US flights from European bases and ongoing Iranian strikes on regional assets.
