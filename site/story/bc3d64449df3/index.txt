Rabbi Rachel Short on the Big Island shifted from large public events like a 275-person Passover Seder to one-on-one work and life-cycle ceremonies after she received social media threats and a phone message about "pig hunting" following October 7, 2023. She and Rabbi CJ Mays at Temple Emanu-El in Honolulu now respond by visiting schools, blowing the shofar alongside local conch shell traditions, and explaining Jewish holidays with apples and honey to build direct contact in tight-knit communities where most people have little prior exposure to Jewish practice.

This approach has held steady for the past three years since the post-October 2023 rise in incidents. It will likely continue as long as state hate-crime tallies stay elevated and rabbis keep receiving personal threats or reports of school swastikas. The next visible step is Short's planned classroom visit this Friday and similar outreach by other rabbis, which depends on schools and families staying open to invitations.

The pattern shifts only if official counts drop for two straight quarters or if public events resume without new reports of targeted messages.
