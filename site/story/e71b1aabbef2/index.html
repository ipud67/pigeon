US Customs and Border Protection set new rules for foreign importers of record

The Department of Homeland Security published an final rule that those under Section 2 of Executive Order 14411. The change adds new requirements that foreign importers of record must meet when they make entry and spells out matching duties for the brokers who serve them. It also lists the penalties that apply when either side does not comply. The rule takes effect on January 5 2027 though brokers may ask for a delay in one part of the broker rules until no later than May 6 2027. Comments on the rule are due by the effective date.

Full compliance rests on the text that CBP will enforce at the ports and on the outcome of any requests for delayed application that brokers file before the January deadline.

U.S. Customs and Border Protection issued an interim final rule that adds new entry requirements for foreign importers of record and spells out what licensed customs brokers must do when they work with those clients. The rule also lists penalties for noncompliance. It takes effect January 5 2027 but gives brokers until May 6 2027 to reach full compliance and lets them ask for a delay on one section before the start date. Comments on the rule stay open until January 5 2027 and will be reviewed through the Federal eRulemaking Portal under docket USCBP-2026-1387. This change sits on the ladder of Executive Order 14411 Section 2 which directed CBP to tighten enforcement on foreign importers of record. The situation shifts only if the agency alters the rule after review or Congress passes legislation that overrides it.
