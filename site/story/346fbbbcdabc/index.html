Malaysia's king postpones decision on ex-PM Najib Razak's pardon bid

Najib Razak served as Malaysia's prime minister from 2009 until 2018, when he lost power after a general election that turned on accusations of graft tied to the 1MDB state investment fund. Courts later convicted him on multiple charges in the first of several linked trials. He began a 12-year prison sentence in 2022 after the final appeal failed. Malaysian law lets a convicted person ask the king for a royal once all appeals end. The king holds the power to grant, reduce or reject such requests after advice from a pardons board.

On September 11 the king put off any ruling on Najib's latest application. The application had reached the palace after Najib's lawyers filed fresh grounds that had not been part of earlier bids. The board had reviewed the papers and passed its recommendation to the king, who then chose to delay instead of deciding at once.

The case now rests with the palace while the king weighs the board's input and the new material. Najib remains in serving the original sentence. No new date has been set for the next review.
