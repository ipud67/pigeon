19:T691,The U.S. Treasury’s regular auction of 8-week bills on September 3, 2026, is an ordinary exercise of the power to borrow on the credit of the United States. Article I, Section 8, Clause 2 gives Congress that authority so the federal government can meet its lawful expenses without printing money or seizing property. The founders expected the national debt to be paid from taxes laid by Congress, not rolled over forever; Hamilton in Federalist No. 30 warned that without reliable revenue the government would be “feeble and inefficient,” and Washington signed the Tariff Act of 1789 to fund the new government and begin retiring Revolutionary War debt.

Demand for these short-term bills shows investors still trust the full faith and credit of the United States, which rests on the people’s willingness to tax themselves. That consent of the governed is the foundation of legitimate borrowing. Yet every new auction also adds to the principal that must someday be repaid with interest. The Constitution never granted the federal government a blank check; the power to borrow was meant to be used sparingly and repaid, not used to fund endless deficits that mortgage the future.

The right to life and the security of property that government exists to protect are both threatened when debt grows faster than the economy can service it. Founders who fought a revolution against unconsented taxation would see today’s routine, massive short-term borrowing as a warning that we are drifting from the limited-government republic they built. 1c:T41e,The U.S. Treasury auctioned its 8-week bill on September 3, 2026, with settlement on the same day. This is the latest short-term debt sale in a long string of weekly bill auctions that keep the government funded between longer bond sales. Demand for these bills serves as a direct money-flow read on how much cash investors want to park at the short end right now.

High bidder demand in this auction would show continued strong appetite for safe, short-dated paper even as the 3-mo Treasury bill sits at 3.92% and the 2s10s spread holds at 40 bps. Weak demand would signal investors are pulling back from the front end. No results have been released yet, so the indicator stays dark for now.

The pattern of weekly bill auctions will keep rolling without break. Next rung is the regular 4-week and 8-week auction cycle next week, plus the monthly refunding announcement later this month that will set the tone for longer debt sales.
