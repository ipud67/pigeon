Nine Israelis arrested as Turkey probes $3 b. international fraud network

Turkish authorities had been investigating complaints about online trading platforms that promised high returns on foreign exchange and cryptocurrency trades but delivered none. The probes began earlier in 2026 as four separate criminal cases in Istanbul. Companies in Turkey posed as service, consulting and tourism centers. They drew in customers through ads on social media, search engines and websites, then routed personal details to call-center representatives who spoke the victims' languages and urged transfers of increasing sums. When complaints mounted against one platform, operators shifted to another name. The network used the brands Inefex, AI, Algo Education and Exentral-Int among others.

On 19 September 2026 Istanbul prosecutors announced the results of a coordinated operation by the Interior Ministry, National Intelligence Organization, cybercrime police and Interpol. The sweep detained 191 suspects inside Turkey. Nine of them were Israeli citizens. Prosecutors stated the overall network had taken more than three billion dollars from victims worldwide. They described it as a single large organization managed by Israeli citizens who appointed country-level managers, set up the local companies and service centers, and oversaw the fraud. Employees inside the centers worked under code names, supplied fake biographies that sometimes included attendance at Oxford, passed polygraph tests for law-enforcement ties, surrendered their phones at the door and were barred from speaking Hebrew on site.

The cases now sit with Istanbul prosecutors who are tracing ownership structures, ultimate beneficiaries and the full chain of command. The nine Israelis remain in while the wider investigation continues through Turkey's financial-crimes board and international partners. The operation has shut the physical centers that ran the call rooms and data systems used to control what investors saw.

Turkish police and intelligence picked up 191 people in Istanbul this week in a raid on what prosecutors call a single fraud ring run out of converted office blocks that posed as forex and crypto trading desks. Nine of those arrested hold Israeli passports. The ring used rotating brand names such as Inefex, Quantum AI, Algo Education and Exentral-Int, moved customer leads through call centers staffed by people given code names and backstories, ran polygraph checks on hires, banned cell phones and Hebrew in the rooms, and shifted platforms whenever complaints piled up. Prosecutors say the structure was not separate companies but one organization whose top layer appointed country managers and whose ultimate beneficial owners mostly trace to Israeli entities. The probe sits inside four separate Istanbul cases opened this year and leans on data from MASAK, Interpol and company registries. This case sits apart from the US-Iran fighting over the Strait of Hormuz. It is a law-enforcement action in a NATO country that has kept trade open with both sides of that conflict. The nine Israelis will next appear in court while Turkish authorities decide whether to seek extradition or try them locally. The file moves forward only when prosecutors file formal charges; until then the suspects stay in custody under standard Turkish pre-trial rules. No public link has been shown between the money trail and any party to the Hormuz blockade. The situation stays inside the Turkish justice system until either a Turkish court convicts or an extradition request is granted. If the money trail leads to accounts in third countries, those states would have to open their own probes for the case to widen. Next rung likely sits at the first indictment hearing in Istanbul.
