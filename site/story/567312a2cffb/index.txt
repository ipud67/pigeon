1c:T5ab,A federal judge issued a temporary restraining order this week blocking the Bureau of Land Management from removing horses off the Riddle Mountain range in Oregon after the Oregon Wild Horse Organization, Central Oregon Wild Horse Coalition, and Western Watersheds Project sued. The BLM had planned to start the gather on September 9 because wildfires burned 70 percent of the forage and water sources are drying up ahead of winter storms that could shut the roads. The same court move puts the roundup on hold at least until a hearing on the request for a longer injunction.

The groups named in the suit have a track record of filing similar actions that slow or stop gathers on other ranges. One of them now sits under a House committee probe that tracks how taxpayer-funded legal fees get turned into donor appeals. BLM district manager Don Rotell stated the delay risks leaving the horses without enough feed through the winter.

Next step is the preliminary-injunction hearing where the judge will weigh the scientific population targets cited by the groups against the BLM’s on-the-ground counts of forage loss. If the injunction is denied the gather can resume before snow closes access. If it is granted the case moves to full trial on whether the removal plan meets federal wild-horse law.

The situation stays frozen until that hearing produces a clear yes or no on the longer block.
