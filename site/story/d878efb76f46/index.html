NYC Council approves 26 new street co-namings honoring 9/11 victims, heroes

The city of New York has marked the anniversary of the September 11 attacks every year since 2001 by adding names of victims and first responders to street signs across the five boroughs. The process starts with a council member proposing a co-naming for a specific block where the person lived, worked or responded, then requires approval by the full council and the mayor before the signs go up.

On September 11, 2026, the City Council passed legislation that added 26 new co-namings at once. The list included firefighters, police officers, recovery workers and civilians who died in the attacks or during the cleanup at the World Trade Center site. Each designation attaches one person's name to an existing street in the borough where that person had a direct connection.

The new signs will appear on corners from Staten Island to the Bronx before the 25th anniversary observances conclude. The total number of 9/11-related co-namings now stands at several hundred, and each one remains in place once installed.

Thinking ahead
The New York City Council voted to add 26 street co-namings that mark people tied to the 2001 attacks as first responders, recovery workers, and others. This fits a pattern of similar local honors rolled out in the years after the attacks and stepped up around each five-year mark. The signs still need installation by the Department of Transportation, a step that usually follows within months once the resolution clears the council. The list covers all five boroughs and lines up with the 25-year date. No council member voted against it. Further names could reach the floor if new petitions come in from families or community boards, but nothing in the record signals that volume right now. The co-namings stay on the maps until the city changes the rule or a later council reverses them, both rare moves. Next visible rung is the physical signs going up. Horizon: through at least 2027.
