The next clear procedural step is for prosecutors to bring formal charges under the strengthened anti-arson rules he has demanded; courts would then have to decide whether evidence meets the threshold for capital punishment, which Algeria still carries on the books but rarely applies.

How much longer this runs depends on two observable indicators: whether investigators produce credible forensic links to deliberate ignition rather than lightning or accident, and whether the government actually follows through with executions or settles for long prison terms. If no arrests yield solid proof of foul play in the next few weeks, the death-penalty talk will likely fade into routine political rhetoric. If arrests and trials move forward quickly, the story shifts from fire response to a high-profile capital case that could drag through appeals for months or years.
