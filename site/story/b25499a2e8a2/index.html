DRC: first phase of $120 million in reparations launched for victims of Ugandan abuses

Translated from French · read the original →
The original headlineRDC : une première phase de réparations de 120 millions USD lancée en faveur des victimes des exactions de l’Ouganda

The International Court of Justice ruled in 2005 that Uganda's army violated Congo's during the 1998-2003 war in the east and ordered Uganda to pay compensation for the damage its forces caused. For years the two governments negotiated the amount. In 2022 they settled on $325 million total, with the first $120 million earmarked for collective reparations to be spent inside Congo on projects chosen by the victims.

On 9 September the Congolese Ministry of Justice released a communiqué that started the first phase of that spending. The $120 million will now move from the Ugandan treasury into a fund that the Congolese government will administer for public works, health centers, schools and other shared benefits in the affected provinces.

The money has begun to flow under the 2022 agreement, and the ministry must now decide which specific projects receive it first. The remaining $205 million in the settlement stays on the schedule the two states set for later payments.

Thinking ahead
The International Court of Justice ordered Uganda to pay damages to the DRC in 2005 for abuses during the 1998-2003 war. Kinshasa and Kampala settled on a $325 million package in 2022. Wednesday’s launch marks the first slice of collective reparations drawn from the initial $120 million tranche. The Ministry of Justice communiqué sets out the start of payments to victim groups in Ituri and other eastern provinces. Next steps hinge on verifiable lists of qualifying communities and on-ground distribution mechanisms that both governments accept. Any delay in those lists or disputes over who counts as a victim would stall the rollout. The process runs until the full $325 million is spent unless one side walks away from the 2022 deal. That break would show up first in a fresh joint communiqué or court filing. Horizon: payments continue into 2028 barring a public split between Kinshasa and Kampala.
