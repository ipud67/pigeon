1d:T5c9,The executive order signed on September 29 directs federal agencies to launch a campaign in Washington D.C. by 2028 that cuts populations of Asian tiger mosquitoes, Egyptian mosquitoes, and invasive ticks. It sets a 60-day deadline for the Interior, Agriculture, EPA, and General Services administrators, working with Health and Human Services, to spell out smart land management, natural predators, sterile insect methods, and biotech tools that avoid broad chemical sprays. The order ties the work to an earlier 2025 task force on D.C. parks and waterways, then calls for those same steps to roll out nationwide once tested.

This approach builds on past federal efforts that leaned on pesticides but now shifts the main levers to detection, screening, and non-chemical suppression. Agency plans must address specific diseases such as Lyme, Rocky Mountain spotted fever, West Nile, and Zika without harming native insects or plants. The measurable test will be whether mosquito and tick counts in D.C. drop by the 2028 target while beneficial species hold steady.

The order runs until the stated goals are met or a later president revokes it. Next concrete step is the inter-agency report due by late November that lays out timelines, budgets, and performance metrics for the D.C. pilot. Expansion beyond D.C. depends on that pilot delivering verifiable drops in pest density and disease cases.
