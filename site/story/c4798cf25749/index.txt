18:T5be,A 70-year-old Islamic scholar filed a petition in the Bombay High Court on September 11 against the Maharashtra Freedom of Religion Act that took effect this year as the state's version of laws already on the books in 12 other Indian states. The petition claims the act's definitions of allurement and its reversal of burden of proof go beyond blocking forced conversions and amount to state surveillance of religious practice. Maharashtra became the 13th state with such a statute amid documented reports of church demolitions in Satara district, property audits of Christian institutions, and churches in Mumbai collecting signed self-declaration forms from worshippers.

The case now sits at the admission stage in the high court. Similar challenges to parallel laws in other states have produced interim stays, full hearings that stretch 12 to 36 months, or referrals upward to the Supreme Court when benches split on privacy and autonomy grounds. No schedule has been set for the first hearing here.

The petition adds one more pending case to the existing pile of anti-conversion law challenges across multiple high courts. How long it runs will track the court's docket speed and whether the state files a reply within the usual 4-to-6-week window. Next likely step is either a notice to the Maharashtra government or a clubbing order if identical points surface in related matters already listed.
