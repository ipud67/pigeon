Scott Jennings asked a court to dismiss Hasan Piker's 72.5 million dollar defamation suit

Hasan Piker filed a suit against him on Oct. 27 in court in Washington after Jennings said on an Aug. 11 broadcast that Piker had advocated for the lynching of Black voters. Piker argued that the took clips from his October 2024 livestream out of and that he had been using sarcasm to mock a Black conservative commentator.

On Nov. 10 Jennings asked the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia to dismiss the $72.5 million claim. His filing contended that the statements amounted to a protected interpretation of Piker's own words and that Piker had not shown actual malice as required for a public figure to win such a case.

The filing seeks dismissal of the counts for defamation defamation by implication and false light.

Thinking ahead
Scott Jennings filed a motion to dismiss Hasan Piker’s $72.5 million defamation suit in US District Court for the District of Columbia on November 10. The case sits at the motion-to-dismiss stage. Courts decide these on the pleadings alone unless the plaintiff shows a clear path to evidence of knowing falsehood. If the judge denies the motion the parties move to discovery; if the judge grants it the suit ends unless Piker appeals. The outcome turns on whether the court treats the core statements as verifiable fact or as non-actionable interpretation. Next move likely stays inside the DC federal courthouse on the dismissal papers.
