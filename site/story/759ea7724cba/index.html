Spain's Constitutional Court addresses amnesty for procés leaders on embezzlement charges

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlineEl Tribunal Constitucional aborda la amnistía a los líderes del procés por malversación

Spain's 1978 constitution created a Constitutional Court with power to review laws passed by parliament and the regional assemblies. That court has sat as the final authority on whether comply with the text. In 2017 Catalan officials organized a referendum on that Spanish law barred. Leaders faced prosecution for rebellion, and misuse of public funds. Parliament passed an amnesty law in 2024 that covered offenses tied to the independence drive between 2011 and 2023. The law reached embezzlement charges but required courts to decide if specific acts fell inside its terms.

On 20 September 2026 the Constitutional Court heard arguments on whether the amnesty statute can extinguish embezzlement liability for the procés leaders. The session examined the statute's wording against the constitution's rules on equality before the law and the limits on parliament's authority to criminal conduct.

The court now holds the power to declare the amnesty valid, void, or narrowed on those charges. Its ruling will set the exact reach of the 2024 law over the remaining procés cases.

Thinking ahead
The Constitutional Court in Spain took up the question of whether the 2024 amnesty law covers embezzlement charges against procés leaders. This step follows the law’s passage last year and earlier court referrals on its scope for the 2017 events. The court’s ruling will set the exact boundary on which charges can be wiped out. Next move depends on how the judges read the law’s text against the criminal code definitions. If they narrow the amnesty, separate trials on those counts stay active. If they widen it, those cases close. The situation stays in play until the court issues its decision and any follow-up appeals run their course. Horizon: months.
