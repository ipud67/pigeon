Cop, civilian injured in blast near KP's Sra Khawra checkpost

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa has seen repeated bombings along its main highways in the years since security forces resumed large-scale operations against armed groups in the tribal districts after 2014. Checkposts like Sra Khawra sit on the Indus Highway south of Peshawar where the road links the capital to Kohat and the former FATA areas. The Mattani police station holds over that stretch of Frontier Road.

On September 17 2026 a vehicle-borne improvised explosive device hidden inside a loader rickshaw detonated near the Sra Khawra checkpost. The blast injured one policeman and one civilian and tore away part of the checkpost’s outer wall.

Officials have cordoned the site and begun collecting fragments from the rickshaw. The Mattani police station is leading the investigation into who placed the device and how it reached the link road.

Thinking ahead
A vehicle-borne bomb hit a police checkpost on the Indus Highway south of Peshawar on September 17, wounding one cop and one civilian. The device was fixed inside a loader rickshaw that exploded on the link road to Khyber district, blowing out the outer wall of the Sra Khawra post in Mattani jurisdiction. Police have started collecting fragments and questioning locals along the route. This kind of low-tech VBIED against fixed police points has run in the region for years. Each new blast usually triggers a few days of cordon searches, extra checkpoints, and claims of responsibility from local networks. The pattern holds until security forces roll up a specific cell or the groups shift to softer targets. Next step is standard: bomb-squad forensics on the rickshaw parts, sweeps for secondary devices, and a look at recent theft reports for similar vehicles. The cycle continues unless officials lock down loader traffic on that stretch or pick up the bomb-maker through tip-offs or raids. It will likely run at this pace for weeks unless a big arrest breaks the local supply chain.
