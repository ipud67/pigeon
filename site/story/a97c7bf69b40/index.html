2027: Amnesty trains micro-influencers to promote human rights, counter misinformation

International has run volunteer networks at the grassroots level in Nigeria for years to document rights violations and push for from government agencies. The organization began expanding its reach into online spaces after digital platforms became the main place where public debate about elections, policy and issues took place.

On Tuesday in Abuja, the group completed a training session for 60 micro-influencers drawn from Lagos, Enugu and the Federal Capital Territory. The program, funded by the German Embassy and run by Amnesty, selected the participants through an open application process based on set criteria. It taught them how to fact-check material, identify false claims, discuss human rights on their existing platforms and handle political topics without crossing into .

The effort forms one part of a six-state pilot that also includes volunteer cohorts in three other states. Program manager Barbara Magaji said the organization wants people who already have online audiences to carry human-rights topics into their regular content streams rather than rely only on dedicated advocates. Communications manager Dooshima Tsee added that the training gives content creators tools to operate in a space where every civic discussion now moves through social media. The work sits ahead of the 2027 general elections.
