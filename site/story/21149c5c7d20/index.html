SINGAPORE
September 20, 2026 · 14:07 UTC

First auction of seized luxury bags from S$3b money laundering case closes, bringing in more than S$1.1m

Singapore authorities seized luxury goods as part of a money laundering investigation that totalled S$3 billion. The probe began years earlier and led to convictions, asset forfeitures and orders to sell the items at public auction. Auction house Hotlotz took charge of the first batch of 337 handbags and accessories taken from that case.

Bidding opened on those pieces and closed on September 20. A limited edition Louis Vuitton Yayoi Kusama pumpkin bag drew a 30-minute war that ended at S$87,000 after 42 late bids, more than five times its pre-sale estimate of S$12,000 to S$16,000. All 337 lots sold for a combined total above S$1.1 million. Chrome Hearts necklaces, bracelets and rings also drew 30 bids or more and closed several times above their estimates, while a beige goatskin Chanel mini flap bag reached S$15,000 and a caviar leather Chanel classic flap sold for S$13,000.

The next Hotlotz auction of 286 pieces of fine jewellery from the same case remains open until September 27. Two weeks earlier, on September 17, no buyers appeared for any of the seized luxury properties offered at auction.

Thinking ahead
The first batch of 337 seized handbags and accessories from Singapore’s S$3 billion money laundering probe sold out at auction on September 20. Hotlotz recorded more than S$1.1 million in hammer prices, with a Louis Vuitton x Yayoi Kusama pumpkin bag drawing 42 bids and closing at S$87,000 after a 30-minute war. Chanel pieces and Chrome Hearts jewellery also pulled multiple bids each and cleared several times above their low estimates. This sits on the ladder that began with the 2023 seizures tied to the laundering ring and moved through the September 17 property auction that drew zero buyers. The next procedural rung is the second tranche of 286 pieces of fine jewellery, which stays open until September 27. The two sales together were projected to pull S$2.9 million to S$3.9 million before buyer’s premium. Strong bidding on small portable items points to continued interest in the remaining jewellery lots. If the September 27 close matches or beats the handbag totals, the full auction run will finish above the low end of the combined estimate. Thin data on bidder profiles and post-premium net proceeds leaves the exact final haul uncertain. The jewellery auction closes in one week.
