20:T5d4,The Pentagon inspector general put the bill for Operation Epic Fury at $33.4 billion as of late June. That breaks down to $7.4 billion in incremental costs, $22.3 billion in spent munitions, and $3.7 billion in lost equipment. The report lists damage to U.S. diplomatic sites in four countries, especially over 600 strikes on facilities in Iraq that hit the new Erbil consulate hardest. It also tallies four F-15s, one F-35, 12 KC-135s, six helicopters, and up to 30 MQ-9s damaged or destroyed between February 28 and June 30, plus 14 U.S. service members killed and 417 wounded in that combat window.

This latest rung lands after the July 8 collapse of the 60-day Islamabad Memorandum, the resumption of nightly U.S. strikes, Iran's attempt to close the Strait of Hormuz, and the UAE trade cutoff in August. The fight has stayed narrowed on Hormuz control since then. The report flags persistent shortfalls in U.S. munitions stocks and slow industrial resupply, which tie directly to the scale of targets and Iran's hardened, dispersed setup.

The cost and inventory pressure point to continued U.S. strikes at the current pace until either side shows a clear shift in operations or a new diplomatic opening appears. No fresh talks have surfaced since the memorandum failed. The next visible change would come from a measurable drop in strike frequency, a new cease-fire announcement, or documented replenishment of key munitions.
