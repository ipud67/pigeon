23:T5de,Zelenskyy brought together leaders from Romania, Serbia, Poland, Slovakia, the Czech Republic, Austria and Hungary in Bukovel on September 18 for the first Carpathian Eight summit. The group, which includes both steady suppliers of military gear to Ukraine and states that have withheld direct aid, pledged joint work on border security, energy lines, logistics routes, trade deals and local projects. Poland used the meeting to sign onto the July air-defense coalition that is building a cheaper alternative to the Patriot system and to flag a fresh aid package whose contents remain undisclosed. Nearly 550 business delegates attended and the sides inked agreements valued at one billion euros that cover missiles, drones and infrastructure. The format now sits as one more standing coordination channel among these neighbors.

The summit will keep going as an annual or twice-yearly meeting because all eight plus the EU have already set working groups on the listed topics and scheduled follow-up sessions. Next steps hinge on whether the pledged money actually flows into joint logistics hubs and air-defense prototypes and whether the hold-out states start sending any direct supplies. If concrete projects stay on paper or new governments in the region shift stance, the format will shrink to talk only. Without those delivery milestones the group adds little beyond existing bilateral ties.
