Cuban dictatorship jails dissent: Eight Cubans sentenced to prison for 'terrorism' over social media criticism

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlineLa dictadura encarcela el pensamiento: Penas de cárcel para ocho cubanos acusados de “terrorismo” por disentir en redes

Cuba has operated under one-party rule since the 1959 revolution that brought Fidel Castro to power. The government maintains a legal framework that treats public criticism of state institutions as a criminal offense under laws covering propaganda against the order and acts of terrorism. Independent monitors have tracked repeated use of pretrial that lasts years before cases reach court.

On September 10 a court handed prison terms to eight people who had spent more than two years in . The sentences followed a trial in which the were convicted for social-media posts that authorities classified as propaganda against the constitutional order. Rights organizations outside the country questioned the fairness of the proceedings and the evidence presented.

The convictions leave the eight serving sentences inside the same system that held them without trial for more than 24 months. The cases now sit inside Cuba's standing practice of using these statutes to reach online speech, with further appeals possible only inside the domestic courts that issued the rulings.
