Turkish watchdog files complaints, orders trading bans over share manipulation

Turkey's Capital Markets Board has policed trading on the Borsa Istanbul since the early 1980s under rules that bar anyone from spreading false information or executing trades to move share prices away from actual supply and demand. The board can investigate suspicious price swings, freeze accounts, and hand cases to prosecutors when it finds evidence of deliberate distortion.

On September 17 the board filed criminal complaints against several traders and imposed temporary bans that block them from buying or selling shares for periods ranging from 15 days to six months. The orders targeted accounts the board said had coordinated purchases and sales in specific stocks to create the appearance of heavy demand or supply that did not exist.

The bans take effect immediately and remain in place while prosecutors review the complaints. The board's next public move will depend on what evidence emerges from those files and whether any of the traders appeal the trading restrictions in court.
