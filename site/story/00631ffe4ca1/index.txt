The Mastcam-Z camera took the 818 frames over 12 sols in October 2023, from sol 933 to 944. The rover team at JPL in California released the natural-color and enhanced-color versions on September 21, 2026, as part of ongoing mapping of rock layers that record ancient lake and river deposits.

The rover has kept rolling and sampling along the edge of Jezero Crater since that 2023 imaging run. Each new panorama feeds directly into the next drive plan and target list for the rock drill and sample tubes. The next visible step is continued westward progress along the Margin Unit, where the team picks drill sites based on color and texture contrasts visible in these images.

No major hardware limits or new instrument failures have surfaced since the original shots, so the pace of imaging and driving stays tied to daily power, terrain slope, and communication windows with Earth. If the Margin Unit yields enough intact samples, the mission profile shifts toward caching them for a future return flight; otherwise the rover keeps extending the mapped strip.
