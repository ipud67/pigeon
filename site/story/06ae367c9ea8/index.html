Reform to ban dual nationality for presidential and gubernatorial candidates advances in San Lázaro

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlineAvanza en San Lázaro reforma para prohibir doble nacionalidad a candidaturas; "nadie elige donde nacer", dice oposición

Mexico requires its presidents, state governors and Mexico City head of government to hold only Mexican nationality under rules written into the constitution decades ago. Those rules have stood without change since the 1990s when Congress last amended the nationality articles.

On September 9 2026 the Constitutional Points Commission of the Chamber of voted 26 to 5 to advance a bill that tightens the existing language. The measure would bar anyone who also holds another country’s from running for those three offices. President Claudia Sheinbaum proposed the change. Morena, PT and PVEM supplied the majority votes. PRI, PAN and MC cast the five votes against.

The bill now moves to the full Chamber of Deputies for debate and a floor vote. If it passes both houses and state it the constitution will carry the new prohibition and candidates will have to renounce any second nationality before they can register.
