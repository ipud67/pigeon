ISLAMABAD
September 18, 2026 · 15:01 UTC

Lawyers’ body to boycott Islamabad court proceedings on Saturday in protest against Imaan, Hadi’s rearrest

The Islamabad Bar Association has represented lawyers in the capital's courts for years. It sets the schedule for when its members appear before judges and handles disputes between the bar and the bench.

On Thursday the Supreme Court suspended the sentences of lawyer Imaan Zainab Mazari-Hazir and her husband Hadi Ali Chattha in a case over their social media posts and ordered their release on bail. Police arrested the couple the same night on a new case.

The Islamabad Bar Association called for its members to skip all court work on Saturday September 19. The boycott protests the rearrest that came hours after the Supreme Court ruling. The association has not said when its members will return to the courts.

Thinking ahead
The Islamabad Bar Association called a one-day boycott of court work on Saturday after police rearrested lawyer Imaan Zainab Mazari-Hazir and her husband Hadi Ali Chattha on fresh charges late Thursday. The Supreme Court had just suspended their earlier sentences and directed their release on bail in a case tied to social-media posts. Lawyers say the new arrest looks like a move to keep them in custody despite the court order. The boycott is a standard procedural step used by bar associations when they see interference with court rulings. It puts direct pressure on the district courts and the police to explain the timing of the second case. How long it lasts will depend on whether the Supreme Court hears arguments on the rearrest early next week or sends the matter back to a lower court for review. If the court schedules an urgent hearing and repeats its earlier directions, the lawyers will likely end the boycott and return to work. Without that hearing the boycott could spread to other bar groups or stretch into next week. Next move hinges on the Supreme Court listing the case. Horizon: one week.
