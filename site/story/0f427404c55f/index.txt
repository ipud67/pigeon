20:T52e,Somalia’s prime minister sat down with Qatar’s deputy prime minister and defense minister in Doha on 31 August and agreed to step up security and defense ties. This meeting is the latest move in the Gulf states’ steady shift toward the US side that started after Iran’s missile and tanker actions in July. Qatar, which has kept channels open to Tehran but also hosts the biggest US air base in the region, is now tightening practical defense links with African partners like Somalia to lock in its own security perimeter while the Hormuz fight stays hot.

The cooperation will likely roll out first as training packages, equipment transfers, and intelligence sharing rather than big new bases. How long it runs depends on whether Somalia’s government stays stable enough to absorb the help and whether Qatar keeps its balancing act between Washington and Tehran. Next visible rung would be a formal agreement signed in Mogadishu or a Qatari military delegation arriving in Somalia.

If the US–Iran fighting stays bottled up in the Strait and no new tanker attacks hit Qatari-flagged ships, this security track stays narrow and steady. A big Iranian breakout or direct hit on Gulf infrastructure would accelerate it.
