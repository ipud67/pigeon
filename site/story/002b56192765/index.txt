18:T518,Russian strikes hit Berestyn railway station in Kharkiv Oblast early on September 17 and damaged track and buildings used by Kyiv commuter lines. No one was hurt because passengers and staff were already in shelters. Ukrzaliznytsia rerouted two long-distance trains — Odesa-Kharkiv and Dnipro-Odesa — adding up to six hours to their runs, while Kyiv’s circular service now loops around the damaged Mykilska Slobidka section with repairs slated for about one day.

This fits the pattern set on 27 August when similar strikes pushed some trains more than ten hours behind and on 13 September when evacuations and reroutes caused one-to-ten-hour delays across regions. The company says it will assess the damage before deciding on further changes and has ruled out canceling trains into the city for now.

The pattern holds as long as Russian forces keep targeting rail hubs and Ukrzaliznytsia keeps shifting traffic to backup lines. Next step is the damage report and repair timeline from the state operator; if that shows heavier track damage than expected, more routes could shift and delays could stretch past six hours. The situation shifts only if strikes on rail sites drop off or repair crews get ahead of the hits.
