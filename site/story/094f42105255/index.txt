This builds straight on the framework the administration laid out earlier this year when it first opened the door to renewed U.S. private investment in Venezuelan energy after years of sanctions.

How much longer this runs depends on two observable things: whether Caracas keeps issuing the operating licenses and work permits the company needs, and whether Congress leaves the Pentagon’s equity vehicle funded and untouched. Both have to hold for the deal to move from paper to barrels in the ground.

Next step is paperwork between the company, the Venezuelan government, and the U.S. side to lock in the equity terms and start field work. If either government pulls the licenses or the U.S. Congress blocks future funding for the Office of Strategic Capital, the whole thing stalls.

It keeps rolling as long as both capitals keep signing the next permit.
