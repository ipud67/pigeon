17:T479,Trump signed an executive order on September 5 that directs federal agencies to strip Endangered Species Act protections from gray wolves in several states. The order follows years of back-and-forth listings and delistings that started with the wolf's return to the northern Rockies and Great Lakes in the 1990s. Ranchers have pushed for removal citing livestock losses while states already manage some populations under their own rules.

The order takes effect after a 30-day review period unless blocked. Courts have overturned similar delistings before when plaintiffs show the science does not support a recovered population. Next measurable step is the formal rulemaking notice from the Fish and Wildlife Service and any immediate lawsuit filed by conservation groups.

If states report stable or growing packs and no surge in illegal kills shows up in annual counts the delisting will stand. If wolf numbers drop below state targets or a federal court finds the order skipped required population studies the protections return.

This runs until the first court ruling or the next administration rewrites the rule.
