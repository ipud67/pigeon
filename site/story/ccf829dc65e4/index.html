TAIWAN
September 19, 2026 · 11:19 UTC
This story is from September 19, 2026 and is no longer on the front page.

Palmer Luckey told Trump that delayed US arms sales to Taiwan hurt Anduril business on Sept 19 2025

US defense contractors have sold weapons to Taiwan under a 1979 law that requires the United States to provide the island with the means to defend itself. That flow of systems and parts continued through multiple while China maintained its claim over the democratically governed island. In May 2025 President Donald Trump met Chinese President Xi Jinping in Beijing and placed a $14 billion arms package for Taiwan in abeyance, calling it a negotiating chip.

On September 19 2026 Anduril founder Palmer Luckey told reporters in Taipei that the months-long hold had slowed his company's sales there. Anduril supplies the Altius drone already in Taiwanese service and jointly developed the Barracuda-500 cruise missile unveiled with Taiwan last year. Luckey said he raised the matter directly with Trump, Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio. He added that the delay halted not only the immediate package but also further progress on other defense programs with Taiwan.

The arms package remains unapproved. Trump faces competing briefings on the economic fallout from any approval, including possible Chinese restrictions on rare-earth exports used by US tech firms. Taiwan President Lai Ching-te has centered drone production in the island's military modernization.
