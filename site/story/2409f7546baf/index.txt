19:T457,JAMB set up a committee of education people and experts on Wednesday in Abuja. The group has four weeks to look at the idea of adding life skills and critical thinking questions to the UTME instead of just repeating O-Level subject tests. The registrar said the exam has stayed mostly the same for over thirty years and now needs to rank students on real-life abilities that predict success in university or polytechnic.

The committee will decide whether to add the new parts as a separate test or fold them into the current subjects. The education minister’s office told them to build a system that checks reasoning and logic beyond memorizing facts. After the four weeks the committee reports back with a framework; JAMB then decides whether to test the changes on a small scale or roll them out nationwide.

No public timeline exists past the initial four weeks, and no pilot date has been named. The next clear step is the committee’s report. If the report meets stakeholder buy-in and JAMB accepts it, a revised UTME format could appear in the 2028 or 2029 cycle.
