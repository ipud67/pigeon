19:T497,A federal appeals court just kicked the $744.6 million coastal-damage verdict against Chevron back to a federal judge in Louisiana. That move puts a temporary hold on anything moving forward in state court. The case now sits with the federal judge who must decide whether the verdict stands or gets tossed.

This is the latest step in a long-running fight that started years ago when Plaquemines Parish sued oil companies over coastal erosion. The parish won big in state court, but Chevron has kept pushing the case into federal territory on procedural grounds. The appeals court’s order is the newest rung that resets the fight to the federal level instead of letting the state judgment roll on.

How much longer it runs depends on how fast the federal judge rules and whether either side appeals again. If the judge sides with the parish the money verdict could be back on track; if he agrees with Chevron the award could disappear or shrink sharply. Right now the clearest signal is the pause button the appeals court just hit.

Next move is the federal judge’s ruling on jurisdiction and the verdict’s fate.
