Article II, Section 1 of the Constitution vests the executive power in the president, and Article II, Section 3 directs him to “take Care that the Laws be faithfully executed.” Congress in the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1952 gave the secretary of state authority to revoke visas when doing so serves the national interest. The 14th Amendment, ratified in 1868, states that “All persons born or naturalized in the United States, and subject to the jurisdiction thereof, are citizens.” The Civil Rights Act of 1866, passed by the same Congress, limited citizenship at birth to those “not subject to any foreign power.” In 1790 the first Congress limited naturalization to “free white persons” of good character who had resided two years, showing early lawmakers treated citizenship boundaries as a core sovereign power. The executive order and visa revocations rest on that tradition of defining who falls inside the jurisdiction clause.
