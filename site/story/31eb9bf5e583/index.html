Waltz: Trump to keep 'all options on the table' to prevent nuclear-armed Iran

The United States and Israel began strikes on Iran in late February 2026. The fight settled into weekly exchanges by March and narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz after a June memorandum lifted dual blockades and opened a 60-day negotiating window. That window collapsed on 8 July when Iran struck three commercial tankers bypassing its approved routes, Trump declared the ceasefire over, and sustained US strikes resumed.

On 28 September 2026 Mike Waltz, the US ambassador to the United Nations, rejected a seven-day ceasefire Iran proposed at the General Assembly. Tehran had demanded access to frozen assets, sanctions relief, and an end to the US . Waltz said Washington had little confidence in any new agreement after Iran violated the prior one by attacking commercial shipping in the strait. President Trump, speaking the same day, kept all options on the table to stop Iran from acquiring a nuclear weapon and said the US holds total control of the Hormuz Strait with oil still moving.

The conflict now turns on whether Iran can force a deal by threatening renewed closure of the strait or whether the US and Gulf states can keep alternate routes and escorts open while continuing strikes and economic pressure. Iran’s foreign minister said Tehran is prepared to resume fighting or return to diplomacy; its military spokesman warned that ships outside Tehran-designated routes will not be safe and that new military technologies await any fresh attack.
