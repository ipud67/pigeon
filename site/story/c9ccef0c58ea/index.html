Iranian American Group Files Suit Alleging Iran War Is Unconstitutional

The United States and Israel began strikes on Iran on February 28 2026 after President Trump authorized Operation Epic Fury the day before. The initial exchange of airstrikes missile barrages and cyberattacks settled into weekly rounds of attack and counterattack. By April the United States had imposed a naval after talks in Islamabad broke down. A June 17 between the two presidents lifted both the blockade and Iran's closure of the Strait of Hormuz and set a 60-day window for final talks. That window ended on July 8 when the president declared the pause over and ordered sustained nightly strikes that have continued since.

On September 24 an Iranian American advocacy group filed a federal lawsuit in district court. The suit asks the judge to rule that the seven-month conflict was never declared by Congress nor authorized under any that grants the president power to conduct it.

The case now rests on whether the court will accept the group's standing to challenge the operation and whether it will reach the question of which branch holds the authority to keep the current level of strikes and naval actions in the Strait of Hormuz going without further approval from Congress.

Thinking ahead
An Iranian American advocacy group filed a federal lawsuit Thursday asking a judge to rule that the US strikes on Iran, now in their seventh month, lack any declaration or authorization from Congress. The suit treats the sequence that began with US and Israeli strikes in late February 2026, moved through the failed Islamabad Memorandum in July, and settled into nightly US attacks focused on the Strait of Hormuz as an undeclared war. Federal courts have heard similar challenges to presidential military action before, but judges have mostly dismissed them on grounds that the plaintiffs lack standing or that the matter belongs to the political branches. The case will move next to whatever federal judge draws it in the district where it was filed. The government will file a motion to dismiss, citing precedent on war-powers disputes. The suit changes nothing on the ground in the Strait of Hormuz or in the pace of US strikes. This legal action is likely to continue at least through the end of 2026 while motions and possible appeals play out. It may reach the Supreme Court if a lower court lets any part of the claim survive dismissal. The only development that would shorten it is a quick dismissal with prejudice or a settlement that ends the suit. Horizon estimate: dismissal or appeal into 2027.
