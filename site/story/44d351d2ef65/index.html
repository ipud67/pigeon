CALIFORNIA
November 5, 2026 · 23:09 UTC

California Governor Gavin Newsom commuted the death sentences of all 548 people on death row to life in prison

The state has maintained the largest death row population in the country. In 2019 the governor imposed a moratorium on executions that has remained in place.

Governor Gavin Newsom used his power to commute the sentences of all 548 people on death row to life without the possibility of parole. The action replaces the death sentences that had stood since the 1978 reinstatement and ends the possibility of any further executions under state authority.

The sentences now stand as life without parole for every inmate who had been condemned to death. The state has not carried out an execution since 2006 and the commutations remove the legal basis for any future ones under current law.

Thinking ahead
Newsom already imposed a moratorium in 2019. This move ends the legal possibility of execution for every current case on the row. Appeals on old convictions can still run but they no longer carry a death outcome. The incoming governor takes office after this week’s elections. Any reversal would require that new administration to restart the machinery Newsom froze, including funding, protocols, and court fights that have sat idle for years. Without those steps the commutations stand. The change locks in place unless a future governor or legislature rebuilds the execution system from scratch. Horizon: years.
