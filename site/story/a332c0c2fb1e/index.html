September 17, 2026 · 07:21 UTC

Israeli money launderer gets record jail sentence despite 'heroic' Bondi act

Shabtay Yaacoby ran a licensed digital currency exchange called Blue Star Exchange in Australia. The business took deposits from customers who thought they were buying cryptocurrencies or placing money in high-interest term deposits. Instead the funds fed a larger scam operation that targeted at least 39 people in New South Wales.

On 17 September 2026 Justice Averre sentenced the 56-year-old Israeli to 14 years in prison in the NSW District Court with parole eligibility after nine years. The judge ruled that Yaacoby played a leading role in a criminal enterprise that knowingly moved more than $50 million in stolen money out of the country even though he took no part in the initial frauds themselves. One victim alone lost about $4 million from a shared superannuation account sent to Blue Star Exchange.

The court heard Yaacoby was arrested in Sydney in November 2023 while trying to unfreeze accounts tied to the business. It also heard he had shielded a child during the Bondi attack last year while on bail and carried diagnoses of post-traumatic stress disorder from earlier trauma plus cocaine-induced psychosis during the offending period. The judge found those conditions did not reduce the seriousness of the crimes because they left his planning and understanding intact and no reparations had been offered. A reparations order was added to the sentence.
