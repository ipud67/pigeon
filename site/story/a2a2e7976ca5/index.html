Trump orders removal of Canadian goods from US public contracts

Translated from Italian · read the original →
The original headlineTrump dispone la rimozione dei beni canadesi dagli appalti pubblici Usa

The United States has barred foreign companies from many public contracts since the 1930s under the Buy American Act. That law requires federal agencies to buy goods made in the United States unless the president grants an exception. Presidents have added and removed countries from those exceptions over the decades through executive orders that list which nations get equal access to the bidding process.

On September 8, 2026, President Trump signed an order that struck Canada from the list of countries whose goods can be used in federal contracts. The move came after the president stated that Ottawa applies rules that treat American firms less favorably than Canadian ones in its own system. The order directs agencies to treat Canadian-origin products as ineligible for future bids unless they meet narrow domestic-content thresholds already written into .

The federal contracting system now operates without Canadian suppliers for any goods covered by the Buy American rules. Agencies must rewrite documents to reflect the change and notify vendors already under contract. The exclusion stands until a future president issues a new order that restores Canada to the list or Congress passes legislation that overrides the presidential determination.
