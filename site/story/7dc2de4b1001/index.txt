18:T49d,Russia sent two Zircon anti-ship missiles from Kursk Oblast and 174 drones overnight into 18-19 September. Ukrainian air defence downed or jammed 141 of those aerial assets while hits landed at 15 sites and debris fell at nine others. Both Zircons missed their targets and their impact points are still being checked. The attack has not ended with several drones still inside Ukrainian airspace. 

This pattern of nightly mass drone and missile launches has run since the first weeks of the war. Each wave depends on Russian stockpiles of cheap decoys mixed with faster jet-powered models and the occasional high-value missile like Zircon. Ukrainian crews have steadily raised their intercept rate by pairing ground radars, mobile guns, and electronic jamming. 

The current attack will likely run another few hours until the last drones are downed or run out of fuel. Future waves will keep the same mix unless Russian factories cut output or Ukraine adds enough new air-defence batteries to push the nightly intercept count above 90 percent. No shift in that supply-and-defence balance has appeared yet.
