It takes effect next year as the latest step in a string of tweaks that have slowly widened what the treasurer can buy with public funds. The treasurer’s office will now update its investment policy manual and start due-diligence reviews on the new choices; nothing moves until those reviews finish and the treasurer signs off.

How long it runs depends on how fast the treasurer’s staff finishes the paperwork and whether unions push money into the pool right away. It keeps going until lawmakers pass another change or the treasurer decides the new options no longer fit the state’s risk rules. Next visible rung is the treasurer’s formal policy update, expected sometime in 2026 or early 2027.

If union inflows stay small or performance lags, the expansion could sit unused for years. If inflows grow and returns hold up, the treasurer will likely add similar labor-linked options later.
