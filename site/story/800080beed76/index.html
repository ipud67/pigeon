NGO receives NESREA certification to transform End-of-Life Vehicles

Nigeria set up the National Environmental Standards and Regulations Enforcement Agency years ago to set and enforce rules on waste handling across the country. The agency issues certificates that let organizations collect, process and recycle specific waste streams under its oversight. One stream it tracks is end-of-life vehicles that reach the end of their road use and become scrap.

On Thursday in Abuja the Recycling and Economic Development Initiative of Nigeria received one of those certificates. The document gives the non-governmental group legal standing to recover and recycle end-of-life vehicles.

REDIN can now operate inside the framework NESREA laid down for vehicle scrap. The certification places the group on the list of approved handlers, and the work it does from here will be checked against the agency's standards.

Thinking ahead
REDIN got the NESREA certificate on September 10 in Abuja. The paperwork lets the group move forward with collecting, dismantling, and recycling end-of-life vehicles that pile up on Nigerian roads. Next step is for REDIN to start licensed operations, which means signing contracts with workshops, setting up collection points, and reporting volumes back to NESREA inspectors. The program will keep running as long as REDIN files the required quarterly returns and NESREA keeps renewing the certificate each year. It spreads if state governments or big fleet owners hand over their old trucks and cars; it stalls if the money for transport and storage runs out or if inspectors find rule breaks. No clear volume targets or deadlines have been published yet, so the exact pace stays unknown. Horizon: at least 12 months unless NESREA pulls the certificate.
