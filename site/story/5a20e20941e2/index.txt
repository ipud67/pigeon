This is the latest push in a years-long ladder of U.S. pressure on trading partners to tighten rules on Chinese goods. The statement lands with Treasury yields at 4.73% on the 10-year and 5.22% on the 30-year as of August 28, levels that show no immediate flight-to-safety bid from fresh trade friction.

How much longer this runs depends on whether other G20 members echo the call or stay quiet at the meetings. Observable signs will be joint communiqués that name China’s surplus, new bilateral tariff moves, or fresh data showing China’s export volumes still climbing. If the G20 response is mostly talk with no policy follow-through, the pressure track stays noisy but contained.

Next rung is likely a round of bilateral U.S. talks with big European and Asian partners in the weeks after this G20 gathering.
