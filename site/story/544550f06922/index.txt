Search warrants turned up a semiautomatic rifle, five magazines including three 30-round ones, 190 rounds of ammunition, a scope, bipod, knives, pepper spray, an entrenchment tool, a prayer rug, and a handwritten note. The criminal complaint lists him as having used the online name Hamza Al Rashid, claimed ties to ISIS, offered explosive manuals, and said he needed only a weapon for his mission. This arrest sits on a ladder that began with a 2023 state case when he was a juvenile for planning a mass casualty attack and possessing child sexual abuse material, followed by his release from juvenile detention this March and a fresh tip that he had resumed contact with old associates and was receiving unexplained packages.

The case now moves to federal court where prosecutors must present evidence at a preliminary hearing and a grand jury must return an indictment for the charges of receiving a firearm for terrorism and providing material support to a designated foreign terrorist organization. Kramer remains in custody; his next concrete step is an initial appearance and possible detention hearing. The situation changes only if the court finds the evidence insufficient, if he reaches a plea deal, or if trial goes forward.

This pattern of monitored online activity followed by an observed handoff and purchase has held for similar cases since 2023. Without new indicators of wider network involvement or additional arrests, the matter stays a single-defendant prosecution.
