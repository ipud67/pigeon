The alerts follow Houthi seizures of Mokha and Perim Island, drone and missile strikes on Saudi oil sites last week, and their claim that the moves answer more than 450 Saudi air strikes on their ground in the past week plus a Saudi blockade of Houthi ports and airports.

This fits the pattern of tit-for-tat escalation between Saudi forces and the Houthis that has run since the 2015 intervention in Yemen's civil war. The Houthis now control both sides of the Bab al-Mandab Strait and have shifted from Red Sea shipping targets to direct hits on Saudi territory. Saudi Arabia has asked the US for direct military strikes on the Houthis and received only intelligence and targeting help so far.

The exchange is likely to run at least another two to three weeks on current pace. Next observable steps are either another Houthi drone-missile salvo aimed at Saudi oil or infrastructure or renewed Saudi air strikes on Houthi positions in northwest Yemen. The situation changes only if one side visibly pulls back its blockade or air campaign or if outside mediation produces a verifiable pause in both.
