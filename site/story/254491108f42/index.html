TAIWAN
September 17, 2026 · 09:54 UTC

Taiwan’s government says President Lai meets Michigan Governor Gretchen Whitmer

Taiwan and the United States have built growing trade ties for decades. The US is Taiwan’s largest export market and top destination for outbound investment. Last year Taiwan ranked as America’s fourth-largest trading partner. Michigan sits at the center of the global auto industry with strengths in smart mobility, automotive software, information technology and industrial automation. Taiwanese firms in semiconductors, electronic components and smart manufacturing have increased their investments and operations in the state. Michigan’s formed a bipartisan Taiwan Friendship Caucus and passed Taiwan-supporting resolutions each year for the past five years. Governor Gretchen Whitmer opened the Michigan Taiwan Office in Taipei two years ago.

On September 17 President Lai Ching-te received Whitmer and a Michigan delegation at the presidential office in Taipei. It was her third visit leading a group to Taiwan in three years. Lai thanked the governor and the state legislature for their record of backing Taiwan’s role in international organizations and for expanding economic and technological links. He pointed to the match between the two economies and called for more joint work on supply chains, technology security and the drone sector. He urged both sides to sign an agreement to avoid double taxation so companies could invest more easily.

The meeting deepened the existing partnership between Taiwan and Michigan. It added another public layer of state-level support inside the broader US-Taiwan economic relationship that has expanded steadily in recent years. Further cooperation now rests on concrete investments, joint industrial projects and the status of the proposed tax agreement.
