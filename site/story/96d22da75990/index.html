As judge gives him 90 days for attacking Jews in Times Square, man flashes ‘victory sign’

Yehia Amin first appeared in Manhattan Criminal Court after his arrest on Oct. 18, 2023. Prosecutors charged him with seven counts after he followed a group of five kippah-wearing men, including a 23-year-old Israeli tourist, near Times Square. He made repeated threats against Jews and then punched the Israeli in the back of the head.

On March 2025 he pleaded guilty to one count of third-degree assault as a hate crime under a deal with the Manhattan District Attorney’s Office. The case reached sentencing on Sept. 16, 2026. Amin arrived late that afternoon with his mother, required an Arabic interpreter, and stood before Judge Althea Drysdale. The judge gave him 90 days in jail.

Amin smiled during the sentence and raised two fingers in a sign that court observers called a victory gesture. When the judge asked why he was smiling, he said through the interpreter that he wanted to look good for photographs. Watchers who had attended his 22 prior court dates described the behavior as new for him and said the plea outcome left him appearing satisfied with the reduced charge.
