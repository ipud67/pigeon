House Dems block capping SCOTUS to nine justices

Overview
(The Center Square) – All but one Democrat in the U.S. House helped tank a proposed constitutional amendment Wednesday afternoon that would limit the U.S. Supreme Court to nine justices.
Short history
House Democrats blocked a proposed constitutional amendment on September 2, 2026, that would have set the size of the U.S. Supreme Court at nine justices. The amendment needed a two-thirds majority in the House to advance. All but one Democrat voted against it, and the measure failed. The Constitution gives Congress power to set the number of justices by law. Congress last changed the size of the Court in 1869, when it fixed the number at nine.
Constitutional analysis
The Constitution gives Congress the power under Article III, Section 1 to ordain and establish inferior courts but sets no fixed number for the Supreme Court. The First Congress set the size at six justices in the Judiciary Act of 1789. Congress changed the number six times in the next eighty years, always by statute, never by constitutional amendment. The proposal to lock the Court at nine justices by amendment would have removed that power from future majorities and placed the limit beyond ordinary legislation. The right to life stands first among the unalienable rights listed in the Declaration. Government exists to secure that right along with liberty and the pursuit of happiness. A Court kept at nine by constitutional text would limit the ability of any single party to expand the bench and shift rulings on life cases that reach the justices under the Fourteenth Amendment. Founders such as Hamilton in Federalist 78 described the judiciary as the least dangerous branch because it possesses neither force nor will, only judgment. They expected Congress to check the Court through structure and jurisdiction, not through raw addition of seats. The House vote kept open the statutory route the First Congress used and the one followed through the nineteenth century.
Predictive model
House Democrats voted down a proposed constitutional amendment that would lock the Supreme Court at nine justices. The measure needed two-thirds support in the House and fell short when all but one Democrat opposed it. This keeps the current path where any future change to the court's size would still require new legislation or another amendment push. The amendment now sits dead in the House. Sponsors could reintroduce it in the next Congress if they pick up more seats. For the number to change, backers would need broad bipartisan buy-in in both chambers plus ratification by three-fourths of the states, a high bar that has blocked similar fixes before. Next move likely stays inside committee talks or campaign speeches until the 2026 midterms shift the numbers. Horizon: months to years, tied to seat counts after elections.
