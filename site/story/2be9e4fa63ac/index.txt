1d:T5bb,The US added tariffs on certain Canadian motor vehicles and parts in July 2026 under section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 after finding Canada’s auto tariff scheme discriminated against US commerce. Canada agreed to fix the issue in mid-August, prompting a three-day suspension of the duties, but talks broke down and the tariffs took effect August 22. On September 14 the president issued a new proclamation that narrows the list of Canadian products hit with the extra duties, based on advice from senior officials that the change would still offset the burden while better fitting US interests.

This adjustment sits on the same ladder as the original finding of discrimination and the short-lived suspension. No new talks or Canadian moves have been reported since the duties kicked in last month. Treasury yields on September 14 sat at 4.11 percent for the 3-month bill, 4.65 percent for the 2-year note, 4.97 percent for the 10-year, 5.34 percent for the 30-year, and the 2s10s spread held at 32 basis points; none of those readings tie directly to the tariff tweak.

The narrower tariff list is likely to stay in place at least until Canada changes its motor vehicle rules or new talks produce a deal. Next step is either continued enforcement of the adjusted duties or a fresh suspension if Canada offers concrete removal of the discrimination. Gap exists on exact product list changes and any Canadian reaction.
