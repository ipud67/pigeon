Portugal backs use of frozen Russian assets for Ukraine

The European Union has held roughly 200 billion euros in Russian central bank assets since it froze them in 2022 after Moscow sent troops into Ukraine. Most of those assets sit in the Belgian central securities depository Euroclear, which earns profits on them. EU governments have spent two years debating whether to seize the windfall for Ukraine while leaving the principal untouched.

On September 11 Portugal told its EU partners it backs the plan to direct those profits to Kyiv. Lisbon added that it sees the logic in Belgium’s call for a joint EU guarantee that would shield Euroclear and any member state from Russian lawsuits or other financial claims.

The proposal now sits with finance ministers who must agree on the exact wording of the guarantee before the mechanism can move forward.

Thinking ahead
Portugal lined up behind using frozen Russian assets to fund Ukraine. Officials there back the plan but want the kind of legal shield Belgium is pushing so the EU as a whole absorbs any blowback if Russia hits back at European banks. The next concrete step sits with EU finance ministers who must hammer out the exact wording of those guarantees before any money moves. This round of talks has run since the first asset freeze in 2022. Portugal’s position matches the majority view inside the bloc and adds one more vote against holdouts worried about legal precedent. Progress stalls only if a single member state blocks the guarantee package in the next ministerial meeting. Without fresh objections from holdout capitals the mechanism clears by year-end. The assets stay locked until then and the first transfers follow the final green light. Likely to run another 3 to 6 months until guarantees are locked in; next visible move is the EU ministerial vote on the legal text.
