Trump says he secured ‘majority US control’ of large slice of Venezuela’s oil reserves

“majority U.S. control”

Short history
The US first imposed on Venezuela’s state oil company PDVSA in 2017. Those rules blocked PDVSA from selling oil to US buyers and froze its US assets. In 2019 the sanctions tightened further, cutting off most of PDVSA’s access to the global dollar system and stopping Chevron’s long-running joint ventures. On August 28 2026 President Trump announced that the United States had signed an agreement with Venezuela giving majority US control over a large portion of the country’s oil reserves. He called it the biggest oil deal in world history. The agreement marks the first time since the 2017 sanctions began that a US administration has directly secured operational control of Venezuelan oil fields.
Predictive model
Trump posted on Truth Social Friday night claiming the US just locked in the biggest oil deal in history with Venezuela and will hold majority control over a big chunk of its reserves. The claim sits on top of years of US sanctions that cut Venezuela off from oil markets, followed by repeated rounds of talks that never stuck. No Venezuelan government statement has confirmed any agreement yet, and no text of a signed deal has been released. This claim is likely to keep running for weeks as both sides issue statements, walk back, or double down. Next steps hinge on whether Caracas puts out matching language or documents, and whether the US side follows with any licensing change or enforcement pause on sanctions. The story shifts only if one government produces a verifiable contract or if the other flatly denies it happened. It could drag on through diplomatic channels or fade if no paperwork surfaces. Horizon: 4–6 weeks until a clear confirmation or denial appears.
