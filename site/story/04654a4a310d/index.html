UAE and Israel opened probes after a co-pilot tried to crash a Flydubai flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv in the United Arab Emirates

The United Arab Emirates and Israel have maintained normalized relations since the Abraham Accords signed in 2020. Flydubai, the Dubai-based low-cost carrier, operates regular passenger flights between the two countries under that framework. Those flights cross Saudi airspace on a route that became routine after the 2020 agreements opened direct commercial links.

On 1 October 2026 a Flydubai Boeing 737 operating flight F21073 from Dubai International Airport to Tel Aviv was diverted after an inside the cockpit. Crew members on board intervened to secure the aircraft. The plane landed safely at Tabuk Airport in Saudi Arabia. Saudi authorities cooperated with UAE requests and allowed the aircraft to proceed once the situation was contained.

The UAE Attorney General ordered a specialized team of public prosecutors to investigate the incident and determine its circumstances and causes. UAE law applies because the aircraft is registered in the Emirates and flies its flag, giving UAE courts over crimes committed aboard even outside national territory. The investigation will examine possible links to terrorist activity, specific motives, or prior planning. Israeli officials stated they will examine the matter independently and may join the UAE probe. Both governments urged the public to await official findings and avoid unverified speculation.

Next visible step is the prosecutors' interim report on technical findings and any charges. That will decide whether the file closes quickly or expands into a joint review.
