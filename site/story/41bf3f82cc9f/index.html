Supreme Court dismisses immigration case that was scheduled for argument in October

The Immigration and Nationality Act has required the federal government since 1996 to detain without bond certain noncitizens who have committed listed crimes while their removal cases proceed. The only release allowed under the is on narrow witness-protection grounds. In 2018 the Supreme Court ruled in Jennings v. Rodriguez that the text of the mandatory-detention provision does not itself require periodic bond hearings, but left open whether the Fifth Amendment’s due process clause eventually requires one after grows long.

On Friday the justices dismissed Genalo v. Black, a case they had accepted in June and scheduled for October argument on exactly that due-process question. Solicitor General D. John Sauer told the court the case had become moot after one left the country and the other, Keisy G.M., received a from New York Governor Kathy Hochul in late August that erased his qualifying conviction. G.M. had already been released from custody in 2022 after 21 months. The challenger agreed the case no longer presented a live controversy.

The government said it will return to the court soon with a new petition presenting the same constitutional issue in a case that still has a live detention. The dismissal leaves the lower-court rulings on the due-process claim in place for the two men but does not settle the broader question for other mandatory detainees.
