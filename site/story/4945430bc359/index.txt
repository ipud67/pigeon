19:T554,NASA and SpaceX have set the next cargo run to the space station for 6:33 a.m. Eastern on October 13 from Cape Canaveral’s pad 40. The Falcon 9 will carry a Dragon capsule with more than 6,300 pounds of gear that includes the last pair of roll-out solar arrays plus experiments on artificial retinas, 3D heart tissue, glass formation, and brain organoids. The capsule is scheduled to dock two days later on October 15 and stay until mid-November before it returns to a splashdown off California.

This marks the 35th flight in the commercial resupply contract that has run since 2012. NASA has already announced the October 8 teleconference that will cover both this launch and the just-landed Crew-12 mission, then it will switch to live countdown coverage starting at 6 a.m. on launch day.

The pattern holds steady: each Dragon flight follows the same sequence of load, launch, autonomous docking, multi-week stay, and controlled reentry. The only things that have ever shifted past launches are range weather, last-minute technical holds, or station traffic. Nothing in the current schedule points to a break in that cadence.

The flight will continue the established rhythm until the Dragon undocks in mid-November. Next visible step is the October 8 briefing; after that, the October 13 liftoff itself.
