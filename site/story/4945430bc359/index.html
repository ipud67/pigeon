NASA and SpaceX set a launch for the 35th station resupply on Oct 13 from Florida

NASA has run a commercial contract with SpaceX since 2012 to carry cargo to the International Space Station, which has operated in low Earth orbit since 1998. The station requires regular deliveries of food, fuel, equipment and science experiments to keep its crew of up to seven astronauts working and the laboratory functioning.

On Oct. 7 NASA announced the next flight under that contract, the 35th SpaceX commercial resupply mission, is scheduled to lift off at 6:33 a.m. Eastern on Oct. 13 from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida. A Falcon 9 rocket will carry a Dragon capsule loaded with more than 6,300 pounds of supplies, including the last set of roll-out solar arrays that will be installed on the station to increase its electrical power. The capsule will also carry hardware for manufacturing artificial retinas in weightlessness, 3D heart tissue models, materials to study glass formation in microgravity, and brain organoids for research on neurodegenerative conditions.

The Dragon is set to dock autonomously to the forward port of the station’s Harmony module around 11 a.m. on Oct. 15. It will stay attached until mid-November before undocking and splashing down off California with time-sensitive research samples. NASA will provide live coverage of the launch and docking on its website, NASA+, and other platforms, and will hold a media teleconference on Oct. 8 to brief on the cargo flight and the just-completed Crew-12 mission.

Thinking ahead
NASA and SpaceX have set the next cargo run to the space station for 6:33 a.m. Eastern on October 13 from Cape Canaveral’s pad 40. The Falcon 9 will carry a Dragon capsule with more than 6,300 pounds of gear that includes the last pair of roll-out solar arrays plus experiments on artificial retinas, 3D heart tissue, glass formation, and brain organoids. The capsule is scheduled to dock two days later on October 15 and stay until mid-November before it returns to a splashdown off California. This marks the 35th flight in the commercial resupply contract that has run since 2012. NASA has already announced the October 8 teleconference that will cover both this launch and the just-landed Crew-12 mission, then it will switch to live countdown coverage starting at 6 a.m. on launch day. The pattern holds steady: each Dragon flight follows the same sequence of load, launch, autonomous docking, multi-week stay, and controlled reentry. The only things that have ever shifted past launches are range weather, last-minute technical holds, or station traffic. Nothing in the current schedule points to a break in that cadence. The flight will continue the established rhythm until the Dragon undocks in mid-November. Next visible step is the October 8 briefing; after that, the October 13 liftoff itself. Horizon: through mid-November undock and splashdown.
