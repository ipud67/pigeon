17:T4a0,Foreign student applications for special admission spots at public high schools in Tokyo and two other prefectures now top the number of slots set aside. The count comes from the latest round of forms filed by kids with foreign backgrounds who use the quota route instead of the standard exam. Schools set these quotas years ago to handle a steady rise in such students; the gap shows the quotas have not kept pace with the numbers arriving.

Local education boards run the next review step. They can raise the quota numbers, add more English-language support classes, or shift some students into regular tracks. Any real change needs the prefectural assemblies to vote new budgets and adjust enrollment caps, a process that usually takes one or two school years once the data is tabled.

This pressure has sat in the same three prefectures for the past four admission cycles. The gap first appeared when foreign enrollment crossed 10 percent of the relevant age group in those districts.

The situation will likely stay tight for at least another full school year. It shifts only after boards table concrete quota hikes and those hikes clear the assemblies.
