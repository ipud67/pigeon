17:T45c,The coroner in Lancaster County, Pennsylvania, has now put an official name on one measles death from last month: a 6-week-old girl. State health officials already flagged two such deaths at an Aug. 25 briefing. Local records and the state count now line up on at least one case.

The procedural next step sits with the county and state health departments. They track contacts, check vaccination records in the area, and decide whether to widen the alert or add shots clinics. That work continues until case reports drop for two full incubation cycles, roughly 42 days with no new linked infections.

The death alone does not shift the outbreak clock. Measles spreads fast in pockets below 95 percent two-dose coverage; officials would need sustained zero new cases plus clear lab links closed out before they dial the response down. If another linked case shows up, the timeline resets.

Too few public indicators on current case count or local vaccination gaps to pin a firm end date. The situation stays active until the health department says otherwise.
