False Tamaki info reposted mostly by X users outside Okinawa

Okinawa held its gubernatorial election on Sunday. Denny Tamaki, who had served as governor, lost his bid for re-election. The contest drew heavy attention on social media, with 1.67 million X posts containing the words "Okinawa gubernatorial election" between August 27 and election day. Of the roughly 360,000 posts whose origin could be identified, 95 percent came from accounts outside the prefecture.

A false post appeared on X at 10 a.m. on July 26. It claimed Tamaki had decided to move U.S. Marine Air Station Futenma from Ginowan to the Henoko coastal area in Nago and that opposition to the move was a stunt he orchestrated. The post used a photo and wording that made it look as if Tamaki had called the relocation a brilliant decision. It received 54 reposts in the first hour, more than 1,300 in three hours, over 3,600 in six hours, and more than 9,000 within 24 hours. An analysis using Brandwatch identified locations for 1,927 of those reposts. Users outside Okinawa accounted for 98.8 percent of them, with those in Tokyo making up more than 60 percent. Only 24 reposts, or 1.2 percent, came from Okinawa.

The post spread before the Japan Fact-Check Center ruled it false in August. The remark labeled brilliant had no connection to any base relocation decision, and no record exists that Tamaki made such a choice. Posts that pointed out the errors the next day and later reached far smaller audiences, with the widest one gaining about 600 reposts in its first 24 hours. The same election also saw a separate false image circulate that showed winner Genta Koja beside text claiming he would create an Okinawa that serves the national interest; his campaign stated it had not produced the image. Koja takes office on September 30.
