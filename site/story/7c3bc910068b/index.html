THE HAGUE
September 16, 2026 · 11:09 UTC

Dutch court upholds Israeli Judea and Samaria goods ban

The Netherlands imposed a ban on goods made or marketed by Israelis in Judea and Samaria through a Decree announced in July 2026. The decree made intentional violations a criminal offense punishable by up to six years in prison or a fine of 103000 euros. The Israel Product Center, the country’s largest importer of such products, faced the loss of roughly 20000 wine bottles a year from Judea, Samaria and the Golan Heights that made up 3 to 5 percent of its overall imports.

On 16 September 2026 the Court of The Hague rejected the Israel Product Center’s petition for an against the ban. The court ruled the five grounds advanced by the importer, which included European Union trade rules and Dutch freedom of commerce, did not justify blocking the decree. It also declared the claims of the European Jewish Association inadmissible because the Belgian group lacked legal standing in the Netherlands.

The ban is set to take effect next week. The Israel Product Center said it will study the judgment and weigh its next steps. The ruling leaves the Sanctions Decree in place and the criminal penalties attached to it unchanged.

Thinking ahead
A Dutch court in The Hague rejected five legal grounds raised by the Israel Product Center against the government’s import ban on goods from Judea and Samaria and ruled the European Jewish Association had no standing in the case. The ban, announced in July, takes effect next week and treats violations as a criminal offense carrying up to six years in prison or a €103,000 fine. The importer, which brings in about 20,000 wine bottles a year from the area, said it will study the judgment and weigh next steps. The ruling leaves the decree in place unless the government reverses it or a higher court overturns it on appeal. The procedural path now runs through any appeal filed by the importer or fresh political action in the Dutch cabinet. This ban is likely to stay in force at least through the rest of 2026. It may widen to other EU states that adopt similar sanctions decrees or narrow if the Dutch government changes course after an election or bilateral talks with Israel. The key observable indicators are whether the importer files an appeal within the legal window and whether any EU member state follows the Dutch move with its own decree in the next three months. Horizon estimate: 4–9 months barring appeal success or cabinet reversal.
