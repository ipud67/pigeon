Hong Kong court rejects lawyer's request to use Evergrande founder's funds for legal costs

Overview
HONG KONG, Sept 2 - Hong Kong's High Court on Wednesday rejected a request from the lawyer for the founder of embattled Chinese property company Evergrande to use funds controlled by liquidators to pay HK$1.2 million ($153,039) in legal costs.
Short history
Hong Kong's High Court on September 2, 2026 rejected a request by lawyers for Evergrande founder Hui Ka Yan to release HK$1.2 million from company funds held by liquidators to cover his legal costs. The request came after liquidators took control of Evergrande's assets when the developer entered liquidation in Hong Kong in January 2024. Court-appointed liquidators now hold authority over the company's funds and must approve any spending from those accounts. The court ruled that Hui's legal bills fall outside the allowed uses of the controlled funds. This leaves Hui responsible for paying the costs from other sources.
Predictive model
The Hong Kong High Court turned down the lawyer's bid to tap liquidators' funds for HK$1.2 million in fees tied to representing Evergrande founder Hui Ka Yan. This keeps the freeze on those controlled assets in place for now. The ruling follows the company's long-running liquidation process and leaves the lawyer to cover costs out of pocket or find another source. Next steps hinge on whether the lawyer appeals the decision or shifts to other funding routes. The case moves forward only if new evidence on asset control surfaces or if liquidators agree to release money under stricter terms. Without that shift, the restriction sticks and legal proceedings for Hui stay slowed by funding limits. This runs until an appeal succeeds or alternative payment is arranged. Horizon: 3-6 months.
