Netanyahu confirms New York trip for UN General Assembly, to address UN on September 24

Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu last addressed the United Nations General Assembly in New York in September 2025. At that time the city had not yet elected Zohran Mamdani as mayor. Mamdani ran on a pledge to direct the New York Police Department to arrest Netanyahu on sight if the prime minister entered the city. He repeated the pledge through the first half of 2026 and called Netanyahu a war criminal in multiple interviews and public statements.

On July 22 Mamdani posted on social media that he lacked the legal authority to carry out an International Criminal Court arrest warrant. The prime minister’s office announced on September 14 that Netanyahu will fly to New York on September 23, stay through the 25th, and speak to the General Assembly on September 24.

The trip places Netanyahu inside the city limits four months after the mayor publicly conceded he cannot order the arrest. The visit now turns on whether any other local, state or federal authority attempts to act on the warrant while the prime minister is present for the annual assembly.

Constitutional analysis
Netanyahu will address the United Nations General Assembly in New York on September 24, 2026, after the mayor of that city admitted he lacks authority to enforce an International Criminal Court arrest warrant against the Israeli prime minister. The right to life stands first among the unalienable rights listed in the Declaration of Independence, the document that sets the purpose of any just government as securing life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness. The founders built the federal government with enumerated powers only, leaving police power and ordinary criminal law to the states under the Tenth Amendment. New York’s mayor holds no delegated federal authority to arrest a foreign head of government on an ICC warrant, an institution that did not exist in 1787 and rests on no power granted in the Constitution. The Jay Treaty of 1794 and the acts of the First Congress show the founding generation expected the president and Senate to manage relations with foreign nations, not city officials acting on foreign court orders. Washington himself led the militia to enforce federal law during the Whiskey Rebellion of 1794, making plain that the national government, not local mayors, speaks for the United States in matters touching war, peace, and foreign leaders. Netanyahu’s visit tests whether American soil remains open to an ally’s elected leader or yields to pressure from an international body the United States never joined. The mayor’s retracted threat and continued public statements change none of the constitutional lines drawn in 1787.
Thinking ahead
Netanyahu heads to New York on September 23 for a three-day stay that ends with his UN General Assembly speech on the 24th. The trip follows Mayor Mamdani's July 22 admission that he lacks authority to order an NYPD arrest under the ICC warrant, after months of public threats that began during the 2025 mayoral campaign. This marks the first such visit since last year's assembly address, when Netanyahu criticized moves toward Palestinian statehood. The sequence sits inside the larger pattern of Gulf states shifting toward the US alliance after Iran's missile and tanker actions in the Hormuz corridor. No court filing or new legal step has surfaced that would block entry. The visit itself tests whether Mamdani's retracted enforcement claim holds or prompts any procedural challenge at the border or in city government. It will likely run its scheduled three days with the speech delivered. Next step is the formal address on the 24th and any bilateral meetings attached to the assembly session. The situation changes only if a federal or state warrant is executed on arrival or if the mayor's office issues a new directive that survives legal review. Three-day visit ends September 25.
