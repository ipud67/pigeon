This fits the pattern of Turkey ramping up public criticism of Netanyahu since the Gaza war began, paired with Ankara’s push to line up Muslim-majority states under shared defense talks that started in Mecca earlier this year.

The rhetoric is likely to keep running as long as Israeli operations in Gaza and the West Bank continue at current levels and as long as Turkish officials see domestic and regional political gain in the attacks. The procedural next step is the working-group meetings now scheduled to draft the membership rules; those talks are set to report back to the next foreign ministers’ gathering. For the situation to shift, either the Gaza fighting would have to wind down sharply or a major new diplomatic track—such as a credible ceasefire deal accepted by both sides—would have to emerge and change Turkey’s calculus.

The agreement itself will stay general and non-targeted until enough states actually ratify the membership mechanism and decide to invite or exclude specific partners.
