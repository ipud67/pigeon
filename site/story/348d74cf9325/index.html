TEXAS
September 15, 2026

Texas AG announces arrest of seven for­eign nation­als who allegedly vot­ed ille­gal­ly in 2024

Texas law has barred non-citizens from registering to vote or casting a ballot in any election since statehood. Federal law has made it a crime for a non-citizen to vote in a federal election or to lie about on a voter registration form since 1996. The Texas attorney general’s office has run a unit that investigates election complaints and refers cases to federal prosecutors for the past decade.

Last week federal prosecutors in the Northern District of Texas seven foreign nationals on charges that they registered and voted in the 2024 presidential election while ineligible. On Tuesday, September 15, 2026, the state attorney general’s office announced that all seven had been taken into custody. The defendants are Carlos Salas Barrios of Mexico, Joshua Nmelichukwu Akpom of Nigeria, Helen Mayen Adams of Nigeria, Khalwinder Singh Bhengura of India, Moises Anwar Arellano-Alba of Mexico, Celestin Kuasa Katubadi of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and Rocio Thrasher of Mexico. Each faces charges of illegal voting by an alien or false statements of citizenship on registration forms, or both.

The cases now sit with the federal courts in northern Texas, where the indictments remain pending and the defendants are in custody.

Thinking ahead
Texas AG Ken Paxton announced arrests of seven foreign nationals already indicted last week by the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Northern District of Texas on charges tied to illegal voting in the 2024 election. The individuals — from Mexico, Nigeria, India, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo — face counts that include voting by an alien and false statements of citizenship to register. This fits the pattern of state-federal handoff on election cases that started with the 2024 vote audits and moved to indictments in the past week. The cases now sit with federal prosecutors in Northern Texas. Next step is arraignment and pretrial motions. Convictions would require the government to prove each defendant was not a citizen at the time of registration and casting the ballot. Any plea deals or dropped counts would show up first in court filings. The situation changes only if federal judges dismiss the charges on citizenship proof or if higher courts narrow the statutes used. Without those moves the prosecutions run their course through trial or settlement. This runs another 12 to 18 months in court.
