TENNESSEE
October 1, 2026 · 07:01 UTC

Tennessee put Christa Pike to death with two lethal injections but she lived and went to hospital

Christa Pike has sat on death row in Tennessee since a Knox County jury sentenced her to death in 1996 for the murder of 19-year-old Colleen Slemmer. Pike, then 18, and her boyfriend Tadaryl Shipp beat, tortured and killed Slemmer at a job training camp for troubled teens near Knoxville. Tennessee carries out capital sentences by lethal injection at Riverbend Maximum Security Institution in Nashville under a protocol that calls for one set of syringes followed by a backup set if the first fails to stop the heart. The state has used pentobarbital as the primary drug in that protocol for years.

On October 1, 2026, at about 8 p.m. local time, executioners at Riverbend gave Pike two full syringes of pentobarbital. The witness-room curtain closed twice, media were asked to leave the area, and Pike lost consciousness but kept a heartbeat and began snoring audibly. Her lawyers filed an emergency motion to stop the process while it was under way. She was taken from the prison to an off-site hospital where staff began life-saving measures.

The Department of Correction says it followed every step of the protocol approved by the attorney general's office and that the chemical has worked in prior executions. Governor Bill Lee ordered a third-party review of what happened and postponed the state's remaining execution scheduled for later in 2026. Pike's legal team says the state failed to carry out a lawful execution. Tennessee's corrections department has transported her to the medical facility.
