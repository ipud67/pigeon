The email followed the fatal shooting of ICU nurse Alex Pretti by Customs and Border Protection in Minneapolis, an incident still under civil rights review ordered in January.

The First Amendment protects even harsh political speech that does not cross into true threats. The founders wrote the Bill of Rights to guard against federal agents using vague warnings to chill criticism of government power, especially on matters of life and law enforcement. The judge found the notice created a continuing injury because officials never withdrew it, giving the man standing to sue in federal court.

The Constitution limits federal power to enumerated duties and rejects prior restraints on speech. The founding generation knew from English experience how officials could twist general statutes into tools to punish dissent. This ruling keeps the line where the First Amendment drew it: words alone, no matter how bitter, stay protected unless they meet the narrow test for threats.
