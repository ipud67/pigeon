1a:T49b,The Justice Department Civil Rights Division closed more than 50 school desegregation cases that had stayed open on federal dockets for over 50 years. The cases stretched from Waterbury, Connecticut, to Flagler County, Florida, and others in between. Officials said the districts had met their obligations under the original court orders.

These closures mark the procedural end of long-running consent decrees that required districts to file yearly reports on student assignment, faculty hiring, and facility use. Once a case is dismissed, the court no longer supervises those metrics. Any future claim of segregation would need a new lawsuit with fresh evidence.

The pattern of closing old cases has appeared in prior administrations when districts showed sustained compliance. No immediate appeals or new filings tied to these specific dismissals have surfaced. The next visible step would be a new complaint in one of the same districts that produces enough current proof to reopen supervision.

This round of closures is likely to stand unless fresh litigation starts in the affected districts.
