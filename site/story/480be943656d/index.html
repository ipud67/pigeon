President ordered inquiry into false statement claims against Federal Reserve governor Lisa DeNell Cook

The president holds authority under Article II, Section 3 of the Constitution to ensure faithful execution of the laws, which includes the power to remove certain executive officers for cause. That power reaches members of the Federal Reserve Board of Governors under 12 U.S.C. 242, as the Supreme Court construed the earlier this year in Trump v. Cook. Lisa DeNell Cook has served as one of those governors since her Senate confirmation in 2022.

The president issued a that establishes a Committee of Inquiry to examine specific that Governor Cook made false statements. The memorandum directs the committee to gather evidence, consult with the Department of Justice and other agencies as needed, and determine whether cause exists for her removal under the statute. It orders the committee to hold a closed four-hour hearing at the White House on November 5, provide Governor Cook the evidence in advance, allow her to submit a written position statement at least three days prior, require her appearance for examination by the committee and counsel, and permit her to present arguments, documents, and witness statements. The committee must transcribe the hearing, accept a post-hearing statement from her by November 10, and then deliver written findings of fact, conclusions of law, and a recommendation to the president as soon as practicable.

The memorandum gives the committee flexibility to adjust procedures with notice to Governor Cook and directs the Counsel to the President to transmit copies to her, the committee members, and the Attorney General. The Attorney General must publish the memorandum in the Federal Register. The process now turns on the committee's receipt of evidence, conduct of the hearing, and submission of its report.

Constitutional analysis
The president issued a memorandum directing the creation of a committee to examine whether Federal Reserve Governor Lisa DeNell Cook made false statements. Article II, Section 3 of the Constitution requires the president to take care that the laws be faithfully executed. The memorandum cites 12 U.S.C. 242, which sets removal for cause from the Federal Reserve Board of Governors, and references the Supreme Court decision in Trump v. Cook from 2026 that construed that statute. The committee must hold a closed hearing at the White House on November 5, 2026, lasting no more than four hours, provide Cook with the evidence beforehand, allow her to submit written statements before and after the hearing, and question her under oath with counsel present. It may consult the Department of Justice and other agencies, request documents from Cook, and must deliver written findings of fact, conclusions of law, and a recommendation on removal to the president as soon as practicable after November 10, 2026. The attorney general must publish the memorandum in the Federal Register. The counsel to the president must transmit copies to Cook, the committee members, and the attorney general.
Thinking ahead
The president set up a committee to look into claims that Lisa DeNell Cook gave false statements as a Federal Reserve governor. The committee gets until November 5 to hold a closed hearing at the White House where Cook must appear, answer questions, and hand over any documents they request. After she files a final written statement by November 10 the committee sends its findings and a recommendation on whether there is legal cause to remove her under the cited statute. This process runs on a fixed schedule that ends with the committee’s report shortly after November 10. The next clear step is the hearing itself, followed by the written recommendation to the president. The situation changes only if the committee finds no cause or if Cook’s evidence shifts the record enough to alter their recommendation. The committee will deliver its findings and recommendation in the second half of November.
