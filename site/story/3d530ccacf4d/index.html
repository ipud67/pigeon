Japan says progress made on $550 billion investment pact with U.S.

Japan and the U.S. began talks on a $550 billion investment in 2025. The agreement covers joint projects in semiconductors, artificial intelligence, and supply chain security. Trade Minister Ryosei Akazawa said on September 5, 2026 that the two sides made progress in the latest round, with AI and chips carrying very significant weight in the discussions. The pact remains under negotiation.

Thinking ahead
Japan and the U.S. have reached a point in talks where officials say measurable headway has been made on the $550 billion investment agreement. Trade minister Ryosei Akazawa singled out artificial intelligence and semiconductor rules as the heavy items still on the table. The next procedural step is continued working-level sessions to close the gaps on those two sectors before any final text is put forward. The deal stays on track as long as both sides keep the same negotiators in place and no major policy shift hits either capital. If one government changes its lead team or sets new red lines on tech export controls the timeline stretches. Right now the indicators point to steady forward motion rather than a stall. This round sits as the latest step in the ladder of bilateral economic talks that have run since the first framework was sketched years ago. Likely to wrap formal talks in the next three to six months barring a sudden break on AI rules.
