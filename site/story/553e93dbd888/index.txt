This follows a September 17 strike that wrecked the Berestyn station in Kharkiv Oblast and parts of Kyiv’s commuter loop, which already pushed some trains onto backup tracks and added six-hour lags.

Delays of this size have recurred whenever strikes cut power or damage track in the south and east. Without a drop in strike frequency or faster repair capacity on the southern routes, the same 10-to-14-hour backups will keep showing up on the delay board.

Next moves depend on whether Ukrzaliznytsia adds more buffer time to timetables or shifts more traffic to buses for the worst-hit stretches. The situation changes only if strike tempo falls or rail crews get enough warning to keep trains moving between alerts.
