CORAL SPRINGS
October 7, 2026

Jury convicts Joseph Rodriguez of Coral Springs in Medicare fraud scheme

A federal jury in the Southern District of Florida heard evidence about how Joseph Rodriguez, 58, of Coral Springs, ran a marketing company called Phoenix Health that set up drive-through testing events at nine residence country clubs in the West Palm Beach area. Those events ran for about four months in 2021, drawing almost two thousand elderly residents who wanted only nasal swab and blood antibody tests for COVID-19. Rodriguez also served as vice president of the laboratory that processed the specimens.

On October 6, 2026, the jury convicted Rodriguez of one count of conspiracy to commit health care fraud and six counts of health care fraud. Trial records showed that when patients signed up for the two tests they requested, staff drew extra blood for dozens or hundreds of additional panels, from hormone levels to heavy metals such as arsenic, mercury and cadmium. No physician had ordered those tests, yet Rodriguez caused a doctor's name to be placed on the Medicare claims as if the orders existed. The laboratory billed Medicare more than $15 million and received more than $500,000 in payments.

Rodriguez now faces a maximum of 10 years in prison on each count. A federal district judge will set a sentencing date in January 2027 after reviewing the U.S. Sentencing Guidelines and other factors. Two other convictions for similar health care fraud schemes were returned in the past two weeks, one in New Jersey on October 2 for a $13 million federal program fraud and one in Fort Worth on September 25 for a $26 million scheme against a military health benefits program.
