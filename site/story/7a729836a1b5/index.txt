22:T622,The Supreme Court on September 4 put a temporary hold on a Fourth Circuit ruling that had blocked political parties and joint fundraising committees from using the lower candidate ad rates on broadcast stations. The unsigned order rests on the procedural point that the candidates’ challenge reached the appeals court before the FCC finished its own review of the March public notice. That notice had extended the 45- and 60-day discount windows to party committees for the first time.

The committees now have a clear runway to file a full petition for review. The justices signaled they see the Fourth Circuit as having jumped ahead of the statutory sequence that requires an FCC order and public notice of that order before court review can begin. Broadcasters have already started pulling the lower rates in response to the Richmond decision, so the pause restores the status quo that existed after the FCC notice.

Jackson’s lone dissent flags that agency delay alone has not barred review in past cases, leaving the door open for the candidates to keep pressing both the jurisdictional and the merits side once the petition arrives. The FCC could still issue a final order on the original Democratic challenge, which would reset the timeline and give both sides a fresh shot at court.

The case stays alive at least until the committees file their petition and the justices decide whether to hear it. Next concrete rung is that filing, expected in the coming weeks while the stay remains in place.
