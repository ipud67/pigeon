US blocks Palestinian leader from UN meeting in New York for second year in row

The United States has hosted the United Nations General Assembly in New York since the world body moved into its permanent headquarters there in 1952. Under the 1947 UN Headquarters Agreement the host country must grant entry to representatives of all member states and recognized observers so they can take part in sessions. The Palestine Liberation Organization gained observer status in 1974 and the Palestinian Authority took over its representation after the 1993 Oslo accords created the Authority as the interim governing body for parts of the West Bank and Gaza in exchange for the PLO’s recognition of Israel and renunciation of violence.

On 17 September 2026 the State Department denied a visa to Palestinian President Mahmoud Abbas for the second year in a row. The department said it was extending first imposed in August 2025 against eighty unnamed Palestinian officials after determining that the Authority and the PLO had not met commitments under the peace process. Officials cited the groups’ support for what the statement called glorification of terrorism and efforts to bring the conflict before international bodies rather than resolve it through direct talks.

Abbas addressed last year’s assembly by video after the same denial. The 193-member General Assembly was scheduled to vote later on 17 September whether to allow him to speak by video again this year. The United States remains the only permanent member of the Security Council that has not recognized a Palestinian state. The visa decision came as UN officials reported that the situation in Gaza stayed severe after the October 2025 with continued Israeli strikes in populated areas.

Thinking ahead
The US has blocked Palestinian President Mahmoud Abbas from entering New York for the UN General Assembly for the second year running. Last August the State Department first denied visas to 80 Palestinian officials after citing the Palestinian Authority and Palestine Liberation Organization for glorifying terrorism and trying to internationalize the conflict instead of sticking to the Oslo process commitments. Abbas spoke by video link in 2025; the General Assembly is scheduled to vote Thursday on whether the same remote arrangement will be allowed again this year. The visa denial rests on the same stated grounds as 2025 with no public sign the Palestinian side has met the US conditions for lifting sanctions. The UN Headquarters Agreement requires the host country to grant access to diplomats yet Washington maintains exceptions for foreign policy and security reasons. Palestinian officials continue to reject the accusations and say the move blocks trust-building needed for any two-state talks. Without a shift in the Palestinian Authority's documented positions or a US policy change the block is likely to hold through this session and the next annual cycle. The immediate procedural step is the Thursday vote on video participation. The situation changes only if the US revokes the sanctions or the Assembly finds another workaround that does not require Abbas's physical presence. Horizon: through at least the 2027 General Assembly unless sanctions are lifted.
