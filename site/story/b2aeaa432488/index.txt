1a:T42d,The FRA just issued a final rule that gives railroads clear discretion on when to blow the locomotive horn at passenger stations. No federal requirement forces them to sound it just because a station is there. Railroads can now set their own operating rules for it, and if they do sound the horn, they do not have to meet the usual minimum loudness standards spelled out in locomotive safety regs.

This is the latest step in a long-running push to cut unnecessary noise at stations while keeping safety calls in the hands of the railroads. The rule takes effect August 31, 2026. From here, railroads will likely update their internal rule books and crew training over the next 12 to 18 months to match the new flexibility.

The situation stays stable unless Congress passes a law that reverses the discretion, a court blocks the rule on procedural grounds, or the FRA itself reopens the rulemaking after new accident data comes in. Without one of those three triggers, the policy stands.
