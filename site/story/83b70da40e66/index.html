Justice Department says federal magnet school aid must meet strict scrutiny on race

The and its rules direct the Department of Education to weigh racial enrollment data and set targets for racial balance when it decides which districts receive funds. Courts have required any such use of race by government to survive strict , the highest level of review.

Grants aimed only at diversity goals or racial balancing without that become ineligible for federal support. The opinion binds the Education Department’s future award decisions.

The program now turns on whether districts can supply evidence of identified unlawful discrimination that meets the strict-scrutiny test.

Thinking ahead
The opinion puts the program under strict scrutiny and bars awards aimed only at diversity, reduced isolation, or racial balance. The departments of Justice and Education will now review pending and future applications against this test. Districts that relied on the old racial criteria will have to rewrite their plans or lose funding. The change will hold until Congress alters the statute, a court strikes down the opinion, or a future administration withdraws it. Horizon: At least through the current administration; measurable shift only on new statutory language or successful court challenge.
