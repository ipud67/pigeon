Venezuela freed dozens of political prisoners days after its interim leader met the US president

Venezuela has held political prisoners since Nicolás Maduro took power in 2013. His government arrested opponents on charges that included plotting against him and tied them to violence. Those reached into the hundreds at their peak. President Delcy Rodriguez took office this year after US forces captured Maduro. Her began releasing prisoners as a measure and freed hundreds in the months that followed.

On Tuesday she met US President Donald Trump at the UN General Assembly in New York. Early Saturday morning Venezuelan authorities opened detention centers in several parts of the country and let out dozens of long-term prisoners. Among them was Wilder Vasquez, sentenced to five years in 2018 on charges of plotting to kill Maduro. Foro Penal, a Venezuelan rights group, also confirmed the release of Miguel Castillo Cedeño, held since June 2019, and Frank Cabañas, detained nine years after being linked to the death of a police officer.

The releases come as opposition figures press Rodriguez for new elections. The government has issued no statement on why the prisoners were freed or whether the Trump meeting played any part. Foro Penal said the releases marked the largest single-day movement of detainees in months after the pace had slowed sharply in April.
