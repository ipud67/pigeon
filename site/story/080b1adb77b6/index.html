South Africa’s government says President Ramaphosa booked off from public engagements

South Africa’s Public Service Commission has operated as an independent body since 1994. Section 196 of the Constitution set it up to promote high standards of professional ethics in public administration, to investigate complaints, and to advise on appointments and dismissals across national and provincial departments. For 29 years its powers and reach were defined by the Public Service Commission Act of 1997, which limited its formal role mainly to the national and provincial spheres of government.

On 16 September 2026 President Cyril Ramaphosa signed the Public Service Commission Bill into law. The new Act repeals the 1997 . It sets out the exact procedure the President must follow when appointing commissioners, creates a permanent secretariat to support the commission’s daily work, and gives the commission authority to require any executive official or person named in one of its decisions to file regular progress reports on implementation. The statute now reaches local government and public entities, and it makes it a criminal offence to obstruct the commission, punishable by a fine of up to R50 000, up to 12 months in prison, or both.

The commission now holds directive powers over a wider range of public bodies and can enforce compliance through binding reporting requirements and criminal . Its operational independence rests on the mechanisms written into the new Act and on the constitutional protections that have stood since 1994.
