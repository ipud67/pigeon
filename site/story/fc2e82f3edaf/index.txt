20:T528,Taiwan set up the World Alliance for Disaster Management on September 19 with 12 founding members and 21 observers or partners. The group will run joint training, share information, and set common standards for early warning and recovery work. President Lai tied the launch to two years of domestic resilience planning that produced a fire and rescue center now used as a model for cross-border coordination.

The alliance runs on voluntary participation from countries that already maintain ties with Taiwan. Next steps are the first set of joint exercises and the rollout of shared protocols, which depend on how many partners send teams and commit resources. Expansion would require more diplomatic allies or observers to sign formal membership papers and fund ongoing operations.

Without those concrete commitments the group stays at the level of a forum rather than an operational hub. The current membership list sets a narrow base that has held steady for years.

This setup is likely to continue at its present scale for at least two to three years. It may add a few more observers if existing partners bring in neighbors, but formal growth stays tied to the number of countries willing to maintain official links with Taipei.
