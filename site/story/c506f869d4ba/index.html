Police block opposition march against constitutional revision in Matadi

Translated from French · read the original →
The original headlineLa police empêche la marche de l’opposition contre la révision constitutionnelle à Matadi

Police in the Democratic Republic of Congo have stopped opposition marches before when they targeted government plans. The current push for change began months ago when President Félix Tshisekedi’s allies moved to alter term limits and expand presidential powers. Opposition groups formed the C64 to resist those changes through public demonstrations across several provinces.

On Tuesday police blocked the C64 march at its planned starting point in Matadi, the capital of Kongo-Central province. Officers set up barriers and turned back supporters before the group could leave the assembly area. No route was opened and the protest did not proceed.

The prevented gathering leaves the constitutional revision process on track with no immediate public pressure applied in that province. The C64 coalition has announced it will seek other ways to register its objections while security forces continue to control street activity in capitals.

Thinking ahead
Police in Matadi stopped the C64 opposition coalition from starting its march against the constitutional revision on September 15. The block happened at the assembly point in the Kongo-Central provincial capital. This fits a pattern of authorities halting opposition street actions before they gain momentum. The coalition has called for further protests in coming days. Police have shown they will deploy to block assembly points rather than allow crowds to form. The situation stays frozen until either the opposition shifts to smaller unannounced gatherings that evade detection or the government agrees to talks on the amendment text. Without a visible split inside the ruling bloc or a court order that forces police to stand down, the blocks will keep the marches off the streets. Horizon: at least two more weeks of repeated attempts and blocks.
