19:T5d0,The fire at the neonatal unit of Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences in Islamabad on 26 August killed 14 babies and left one survivor. An official inquiry headed by a retired senior civil servant has now pinned the scale of the loss on missing fire alarms and sprinklers plus locked or obstructed doors that slowed rescue crews. Firefighters had to break windows to reach the ward where melted equipment fell onto the infants. The report calls it systemic failure after an electrical spark from an air-conditioner cable ignited plastics and bins.

Hospital administrators have stayed silent on the findings. No public moves toward charges, funding fixes, or inspections of other neonatal wards have surfaced yet. The procedural next step is for the health ministry or provincial government to table the report in parliament or order follow-up audits; that has not happened. Until visible pressure from families, doctors, or outside donors builds, the pattern of inquiry-then-quiet seen in past Pakistan hospital fires points to months of delay.

This story is likely to stay in the public eye for weeks while the report circulates and families press for answers. It moves next to possible disciplinary notices against PIMS staff or a tender for safety upgrades, but only if the provincial assembly takes it up. Indicators remain thin beyond the report itself and official silence so far.
