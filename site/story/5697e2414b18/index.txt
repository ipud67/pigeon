1b:T53e,The Trump administration filed its third request with the Supreme Court on September 24 to lift a district court order that blocks third-country removals. The procedure lets officials send people with final deportation orders to a nation other than the one listed in the order after they check for torture risk. The filing came after a Massachusetts federal judge again ruled the process gave immigrants too little notice and chance to object.

This round follows a January 20 executive order directing faster removals of people with standing deportation orders, followed by February and March guidance from Homeland Security on screening and notifying before sending someone to a third country. The same judge issued the first block in March, the appeals court kept limits in place, and the administration returned to the high court twice before.

Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson ordered the immigrants’ side to file its response by 4 p.m. on September 28. The court can grant an immediate administrative stay that would let removals resume while it weighs the full request, or it can let the lower-court order stand and schedule regular briefing.

The dispute stays alive until the justices act on the stay or the underlying petition. Next step is the response deadline, then a possible order within days or weeks.
