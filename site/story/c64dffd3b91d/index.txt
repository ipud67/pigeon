The request is the latest step in a pattern of Republican complaints about non-citizen voting that dates back to the 2020 cycle and intensified after Trump took office again in January 2025.

Whether ICE shows up depends on two concrete things: whether the White House chooses to act on the ask, and whether Maine election law or federal court rulings block state or local officials from letting federal officers inside polling stations. Maine’s Secretary of State has already said local clerks run the polls and no outside law-enforcement presence is planned. No formal request has reached DHS yet, so the next observable rung is any written letter from Charles to Homan or a public response from the administration.

The situation stays quiet unless the White House directs ICE to move, or a lawsuit forces a judge to rule on federal authority at Maine polls. Right now the indicators point to talk staying talk through the fall.
