Abbott Agrees to Pay Over $384M to Settle Related to Contaminated Infant Formula

Abbott Laboratories has made and sold powder infant formula and nutritional therapy products for decades from plants in Sturgis, Michigan, and Casa Grande, Arizona. Federal and state programs, including the USDA-funded WIC program that supplies more than half the infant formula bought in the United States, bought those products under contracts that required the facilities to meet specific manufacturing standards set by , , and the purchase agreements themselves. Those standards governed everything from plant maintenance to cleaning cycles to testing for contamination.

On September 14, 2026, Abbott reached a civil settlement with the United States and participating states. The company agreed to pay $384,999,040 to resolve allegations that, from January 1, 2018, through December 31, 2022, it caused false claims to be submitted to those programs. The government’s complaint, filed in intervention on November 13, 2025, described repeated roof leaks that dripped onto processing equipment, cracks and pits inside spray dryers, extended production runs between cleanings, and instances where Abbott skipped bacterial testing or withheld positive results from FDA inspectors.

The payment closes the False Claims Act portion of the case. Of the total, $348.7 million goes to the United States and $36.3 million to the states that joined the settlement. The agreement leaves the underlying manufacturing practices and the government’s oversight of WIC purchases as the next point of dispute.

Thinking ahead
Abbott reached a $385 million civil settlement with the US government and several states over claims its Sturgis Michigan and Casa Grande Arizona plants shipped formula that failed federal quality rules from 2018 through 2022. The deal follows the government's November 2025 complaint that laid out roof leaks patched with temporary umbrellas, cracked spray dryers run past cleaning cycles, skipped bacterial tests, and withheld test results during FDA visits. Under the agreement Abbott pays without admitting fault, closes the False Claims Act case on these specific allegations, and faces no further payout tied to this complaint. The settlement ends the federal and state civil recovery on the 2018-2022 conduct described. No criminal charges have been filed. The next observable step would be any separate FDA enforcement action or new whistleblower suit on post-2022 production; without those indicators the matter stays closed. This chapter is over.
