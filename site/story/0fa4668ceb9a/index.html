NEW YORK
September 26, 2026 · 17:47 UTC

New York Governor Kathy Hochul and New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill declared states of emergency on Friday ahead of this weekend's nor'easter

A nor'easter is a low-pressure system that moves up the East Coast and pulls in strong winds from the northeast, often producing coastal surges, heavy rain and gusts that can top 50 miles per hour. Such storms have hit the Northeast for centuries, with effects worsened when they coincide with a full moon that raises the tide line.

On September 26, 2026, the current system reached peak strength across the region from Virginia to Maine. New York and New Jersey declared states of emergency as coastal waters flooded streets and homes in Surf City and Manasquan, New Jersey. Nearly 150,000 households lost power, more than 1,100 flights were delayed and another 400 were grounded, and officials cancelled the Red Sox game, a professional soccer match, Ed Sheeran's concert at Gillette Stadium and the Global Citizen Festival in Central Park.

The storm now turns on how long the surge and winds remain elevated against the Saturday full moon, with forecasters warning of roads underwater, downed trees and further outages through the weekend in low-lying shore areas. New York City advised against all non-essential travel while governors told residents to follow local orders.

Thinking ahead

The storm peaks Saturday with coastal flooding warnings from the National Weather Service for low-lying roads homes and businesses. Officials in both states tell people to stay off roads avoid floodwater and follow local orders. Rain and wind are forecast to push north through the weekend. This pattern usually lasts two to three days once it reaches peak strength. The flooding and outages will likely ease by Monday as the system moves out to sea unless a stalled front holds it in place. Next step is local crews clearing debris and restoring power once winds drop below 40 mph and tides recede. It runs through Monday.
