UKRAINE
November 3, 2026 · 16:57 UTC

Russian forces killed at least 38 people in Ukraine in strikes on an aid centre, hospice and hospital on Monday

Russian forces killed at least 38 people in Ukraine in strikes on an aid centre, hospice and hospital on Monday. 4 outlets report this from Ukraine, as of November 2.

Thinking ahead
Russian strikes hit an aid centre in Komyshuvakha village southeast of Zaporizhzhia, a hospice in Dnipro, and a hospital in Kharkiv within 24 hours. Separate strikes in Dnipro killed two and wounded 18 while destroying the hospice holding nearly 50 terminally ill patients up to age 90, and the Kharkiv hospital strike wounded 73 mostly elderly. Russia’s defense ministry said the Komyshuvakha target held Ukrainian military units and killed 25 servicemen, yet no major military facilities sit in the settlement and it forms part of Ukraine’s defensive and transport network approaching the regional capital. Energy infrastructure took hits across nine regions, cutting all water in Kyiv and halting the metro network. Zelensky called the Komyshuvakha strike one of the largest mass killings in the war so far. The high casualty count and trapped civilians in one location will keep local rescue operations active for days while regional officials move patients and restore basic utilities. Further strikes on similar targets remain likely until Ukrainian air defense improves coverage or Russian glide bomb stocks draw down.
