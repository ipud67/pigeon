1b:T5a2,ICE added more 287(g) partnerships with Tennessee sheriffs and police departments this week, pushing the total active agreements past the single jail-only model that operated before 2025. Since January 20 2025 those partners have booked over 1 300 people on immigration violations and spotted another 540 already in local lockups. As of September 15, 55 more applications sat pending, most of them for the warrant-service and task-force models that let officers serve ICE paperwork or act at traffic stops.

The program runs on training hours, signed agreements, and ICE oversight; each new sheriff’s office that finishes certification adds another crew that can flag, hold, or hand off removable cases without waiting for federal agents to arrive. Last month Lawrence County used its fresh partnership to grab a Honduran with two felony sex-battery convictions near the Alabama line, then turned him over for deportation. Similar handoffs have moved a Mexican aggravated felon and a Honduran wanted for a 2016 shooting.

Expansion keeps rolling as long as counties keep sending applications and ICE keeps approving and training them. The pace will slow only when pending applications drop to near zero or Congress changes the 1996 law that created the authority. Next visible step is the next batch of certifications and the arrests that follow in those new jurisdictions.
