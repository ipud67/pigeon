Shettima reaffirms Nigeria’s commitment to WTO

Nigeria joined the World Trade Organisation in 1995 as one of the founding members of its predecessor, the GATT, and has taken part in every major round of global trade talks since then. The country sends to ministerial conferences, sits on committees that set rules for tariffs and , and uses the dispute settlement system when its exporters face barriers abroad. Vice-President Kashim Shettima leads when the president cannot attend.

On Saturday in New Delhi, Shettima met WTO Director-General Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala on the sidelines of the BRICS leaders’ summit. He told her that Nigeria remains committed to the organisation and to the multilateral trading system it oversees.

The reaffirmation keeps Nigeria inside the current set of WTO agreements while the organisation itself faces pressure from members who want faster reform of its appellate body and clearer rules on digital trade and fisheries subsidies. The next test of that stance will come at the ministerial conference scheduled for 2026.
