Federal employees paid $9.5 billion not to work in 2025: GAO

Federal payroll rules have required agencies to keep paying certain employees their full salaries even when those workers sat idle for months at a time. The practice dates back decades through successive administrations and has been tracked by the Government Accountability Office in regular audits of executive-branch spending.

On September 17 2026 the GAO released its latest review showing that in calendar year 2025 federal agencies paid out $9.5 billion in salaries to employees who performed no work. The figure covered roughly 216000 fewer workers on the rolls than the year before yet the idle-pay total still climbed because more staff qualified under leave rules that bar them from their desks while investigations or reorganizations drag on.

The $9.5 billion payment now sits on the books as a line item in the fiscal 2025 outlays and has triggered fresh requests from committees for agency-by-agency breakdowns of who received the money and how long each person remained idle. The next GAO follow-up is scheduled for early 2027.
