DUBAI
October 4, 2026 · 06:19 UTC

Australia police and security agency investigated Flydubai co-pilot's links to the country after he tried to take control of a plane bound for Israel

Australian authorities began issuing student visas to foreign nationals in the early 2010s under rules that let approved applicants study at registered institutions and remain in the country for the length of their course. Hamam al-Hammami received one of those visas in 2014, enrolled in classes, and lived in Australia until 2017. His visa was cancelled in 2019 after he stopped attending classes and no longer met the enrollment requirement.

On 30 September 2026 the co-pilot attacked the captain with a crash axe two and a half hours into a Flydubai flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv that carried more than 170 passengers. The plane dropped 17,000 feet before passengers and crew forced open the cockpit door, restrained the co-pilot, and allowed two reserve pilots to land the aircraft safely in Saudi Arabia. The captain, an Indian national, was hospitalised and later airlifted to Abu Dhabi in stable condition. Saudi authorities took the co-pilot into custody.

Australian Federal Police, state police forces, and the national security agency opened a joint investigation on 4 October into the co-pilot’s past connections to Australia. The inquiry examines the years he held a student visa, any contacts he maintained after leaving, and whether those years played any part in the events that ended with the emergency landing and his arrest. UAE prosecutors are conducting their own parallel examination of the incident and any motives.

Thinking ahead
