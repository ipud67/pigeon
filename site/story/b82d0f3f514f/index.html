A federal judge blocked border wall work in Texas Big Bend National Park on Friday

A federal law gives the executive branch power to waive dozens of other when it declares a national-security need to build barriers along the U.S.-Mexico border. Presidents have used that waiver power since 2005. In 2017 the Trump began invoking it again to speed construction across stretches of federal land in Texas, including parts of Big Bend National Park, a 1,200-square-mile preserve along the Rio Grande that had stood without such barriers since its creation in 1944.

On October 2, 2026, U.S. District Judge Kathleen Cardone in El Paso issued a two-page . She barred the administration from building any border barrier, related infrastructure, or performing ground-disturbing work inside the Big Bend sector. The order rests on her finding that a river guide and a church preservation group who sued are likely to win at least one of their claims that the waivers exceeded the executive’s lawful authority.

The case now turns on whether the waivers can survive full review on the merits. Construction inside the park remains halted while the continues.

A federal judge in Texas issued an injunction Friday that stops border wall work in the Big Bend sector of the national park. The order bars the administration from using waivers or starting any ground-disturbing activity until the full case ends. Plaintiffs, including a river guide and a church group, argued the waivers exceeded authority and would permanently damage park land that cannot be fixed with money later. This is the latest step in a ladder of legal challenges that began when the administration moved to restart construction after earlier pauses. The judge found the plaintiffs showed a strong chance of winning on at least one claim, which triggers the block. The case now sits in discovery and briefing. It continues until a final ruling or until an appeals court lifts the injunction. The block holds unless the administration wins on the merits or negotiates a settlement that satisfies the core claims about waivers and park impact. Next likely move is motions to dismiss or for summary judgment once records are exchanged. Any shift would require the district court to rule for the government or the Fifth Circuit to overturn the injunction on appeal. Horizon: 12 to 18 months to final district court decision.
