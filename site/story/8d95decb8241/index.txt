This fits the pattern that started with the failed Islamabad Memorandum in July, when Iran tried to shut the strait, struck tankers, and drew nightly US attacks on its maritime assets. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards reported several dead and wounded and promised retaliation, which has been their standard move after every US hit since late February.

The fight stays locked on who controls the 21-mile strait. US strikes keep targeting launchers, boats, and support sites to keep the waterway open. Iran keeps trying to close it or punish ships that ignore its rules.

Next moves depend on whether Iran launches another missile or drone wave at US or Gulf targets, or tries to mine the strait again. If they do, US strikes will likely resume the nightly pace seen in July. If Iran holds off, the lull could stretch longer.

No clear indicator yet on which way it breaks.
