Trump admin. adds former Iraqi official to terrorist watchlist, revokes visa

“We don't allow suspected terrorists to reside in our country,”
Overview
"We don't allow suspected terrorists to reside in our country," the State Department wrote on X.
Short history
The practice began after the 9/11 attacks when the State Department and DHS gained power under the Immigration and Nationality Act and executive orders to designate individuals as terrorists or threats to national security. Those designations let officials block entry, revoke visas already issued, and bar people from residing in the US. In the years that followed, both Republican and Democratic administrations used the same authority on foreign officials, militants, and former government figures from Iraq, Syria, and other countries. On August 30, 2026, the Trump administration placed a former Iraqi official on the terrorist watchlist, revoked his visa, and removed him under that standing legal framework. The State Department stated the action followed the rule that bars suspected terrorists from living in the country.
Predictive model
The Trump administration added a former Iraqi official to the terrorist watchlist and yanked his visa on August 30 2026. The State Department posted that the US does not let suspected terrorists live here. This move sits on the ladder of repeated Trump-era tightening of entry rules for people flagged from Iraq and other high-risk spots. How much longer it runs depends on whether the ex-official or his lawyers file a court challenge to get the visa back. Without a lawsuit or new diplomatic push from Baghdad that produces fresh evidence clearing him, the ban stays locked in. Next visible step is either a formal appeal landing in federal court or quiet talks between US and Iraqi officials that could quietly reverse the flag. If no court filing shows up in the next few weeks and no Iraqi government statement pushes back hard, the restriction settles in as standard operating procedure. Horizon: likely holds for months unless a lawsuit forces a hearing.
