SpaceX crew reached ISS from Florida in record 7 hours 55 minutes

The International Space Station has orbited Earth since 1998 and hosted rotating crews from multiple nations under agreements that kept the platform running through Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. NASA extended operations of the station through 2030. Crews from the United States have reached it on SpaceX Dragon capsules since 2020, with flight times set by the relative positions of the station and the launch site at the moment of liftoff.

On October 2 2026 a Falcon 9 rocket lifted off from Cape Canaveral, Florida, at 11:10 a.m. local time carrying the four-member Crew-13. The Dragon capsule Grace docked with the station 7 hours and 55 minutes later. That beat the previous shortest US trip of more than 30 hours set by the prior crew. A Russian Soyuz holds the all-time record of just over three hours from October 2020.

The new crew of commander Jessica Watkins, Luke Delaney, Joshua Kutryk and Sergey Teteryatnikov will remain aboard for six months and take over station duties from Crew-12, which returns to Earth on Monday. Watkins is the first Black woman to command a crew flight to orbit. The mission had been delayed from mid-September by a leak on the Dragon.

Thinking ahead
SpaceX launched its Crew-13 mission from Cape Canaveral on a Falcon 9 at 11:10 a.m. local time. The Dragon capsule docked with the ISS in 7 hours 55 minutes, beating the previous US record of more than 30 hours. The faster time came from the relative positions of the station and the launch window. Crew-13, made up of commander Jessica Watkins, Luke Delaney, Joshua Kutryk and Sergey Teteryatnikov, will spend six months on station and swap places with Crew-12, who leave Earth on Monday. The mission follows a leak that pushed the original mid-September launch back to early October. Similar crew rotations have run on schedule once docking is complete, unless new hardware problems appear. The record itself does not change the six-month stay length or the planned 2030 end of ISS operations. This pattern holds until the next crew rotation or a major maintenance issue forces a schedule shift.
