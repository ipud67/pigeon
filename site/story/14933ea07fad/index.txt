19:T4b9,Member States hit the final deadline today for completing the reforms and investments written into their national recovery and resilience plans under the Recovery and Resilience Facility. The plans were drawn up after the COVID-19 pandemic and updated following Russia’s invasion of Ukraine, with the EU treating the two shocks as the reason for the joint borrowing that funded NextGenerationEU. 

The Commission will now shift to verifying what was delivered, triggering the last tranche of payments where milestones are met and starting the formal closure process for plans that fall short. No new money can be committed after this cutoff, so any unfinished items stay on the member state’s tab unless the Council grants an extension. 

The story is likely to continue for another 12–18 months as audits and dispute talks play out. It moves next to bilateral talks between Brussels and individual capitals over withheld funds, possible infringement cases, or revised timelines if political pressure builds. 

If several large states miss key targets, the Commission could propose a formal extension package to the Council; without that, the facility simply winds down on schedule.
