This fits the pattern of repeated Russian strikes on the grid that started in fall 2022 and picked up again after the summer lull. Ukrainian repair crews have patched lines from earlier hits, but each new wave adds damage that takes weeks or months to fix.

The next moves depend on how fast Ukraine restores the downed lines and whether Russia follows up with more strikes before the ground freezes. Repair speed hinges on parts, crews, and air defense cover. A drop in Russian attack volume or a big increase in Ukrainian intercepts would slow the power cuts.

The pattern runs at least through the cold months unless one side shifts its supply of missiles and drones or its air defenses in a measurable way.
