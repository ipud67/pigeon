ICE says it now has enough body cameras. And, Congress averts shutdown before midterms

ICE began requiring body cameras for its enforcement officers in 2016. The agency rolled out the program in phases because it lacked enough units and funding to equip everyone at once. By September 2026, ICE stated it had purchased and distributed sufficient cameras for all agents.

Separately, Congress passed a short-term spending bill on September 2, 2026. The measure kept federal funding in place and prevented a government shutdown days before the midterm elections.

Thinking ahead
ICE now says it has enough body cameras for every agent. Congress passed a funding bill that keeps the government open past the deadline, just weeks before midterms. Both moves sit on a ladder of repeated short-term spending patches and stepped-up border enforcement that have played out since 2023. The body-camera rollout had been slowed by procurement and training delays that stretched back two years; the new statement marks the point where supply finally caught up. The funding vote follows the same pattern of last-minute deals that have kept shutdown fights off the calendar since the 2024 cycle. No clear market signal ties the Treasury yields to these two items. The 3-mo bill at 3.92 percent, 2-yr at 4.39 percent, 10-yr at 4.79 percent, 30-yr at 5.27 percent, and 2s10s spread at 40 bps show steady borrowing costs with no fresh move tied to this week's votes. This pairing of operational readiness and fiscal patch looks likely to hold through the midterms. After that, the next spending deadline and any fresh immigration enforcement targets will set the next rung. Horizon: through midterms, next test in December 2026.
