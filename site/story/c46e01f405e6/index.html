Slink skin factory boss protests outside Southland council over charges

The Southland Regional Council and Southland District Council regulate discharges to land and water under the Resource Management Act. In August and September 2025 both councils issued notices to Eddie Zhi and his Tuturau slink skin business NZAgri Development after neighbours reported smells and piles of carcasses on the site.

On 9 September 2026 the Environment Court granted enforcement orders that halted all carcass deliveries and processing at the factory. The orders also required removal of all carcasses, minced-carcass containers, and contaminated liquid, with disposal at approved facilities, and barred any further unlawful discharges of blood, leachate or contaminants that could reach water. Both councils later laid a total of seven charges against Zhi and the company for earlier breaches and for failing to obey the abatement notices.

Zhi responded by carrying dead lambs to the regional council car park on 18 September and staging repeated protests outside both council offices. Council chair Jeremy McPhail and district chief executive Cameron McIntosh stated that staff would not tolerate the behaviour and that the orders and charges remain in force regardless of the protests. The business stays closed under the court orders.

Eddie Zhi carried dead lambs to the Southland Regional Council car park on Friday after the Environment Court issued interim enforcement orders on 9 September that halted his Tuturau slink skin business from receiving or processing carcasses. The orders followed seven charges against Zhi and NZAgri Development for discharges of blood, leachate and contaminants to land where they could reach water, plus failure to obey abatement notices from August and September 2025. Neighbour complaints about smells and carcass piles triggered the regional council action, with parallel charges and orders from Southland District Council. The protest is the latest step in a compliance dispute that began with the abatement notices last year. Council staff have locked the office door during visits and declined meetings. Zhi has said he will keep protesting peacefully as his democratic right. This pattern of public demonstrations and locked doors points to the dispute running at least until the next court appearance. It will continue until Zhi either removes the carcasses and liquids as ordered, disposes of them at approved sites, or the court varies the orders after a full hearing. Without one of those shifts the business stays shut and the charges proceed. Next likely rung is a scheduled Environment Court date where compliance evidence is tested. Horizon: at least three to six months.
