He held one-on-one meetings with the Indian prime minister, the Egyptian president, the Indonesian president, the Malaysian prime minister, and the head of the International Solar Alliance before delivering prepared remarks on the final day. Those remarks listed specific institutional targets for change: the Security Council, Bretton Woods bodies, and the wider financial setup, plus calls on AI rules, faster fossil-fuel shift, and mineral processing inside producer countries.

The pattern of these trips shows the Secretary-General uses each BRICS host rotation to press the same reform list. India’s turn at the helm supplied the platform; the next procedural rung is the hand-off to the following BRICS chair, which sets the agenda for the 2027 summit. Visible markers that the push has shifted will be any concrete follow-up language in BRICS communiqués or a documented increase in developing-country seats on the named institutions.

This cycle of speeches and bilateral meetings will run at least until the 2027 BRICS summit unless a major membership change or a documented reform breakthrough occurs first.
