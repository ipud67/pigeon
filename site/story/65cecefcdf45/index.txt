The threat hangs on whether the EU and Canada sign new joint standards by year end. If they move forward anyway, US agencies would need to publish formal sanction lists naming EU sectors. So far no lists have been drafted and no EU or Canadian agency has altered its timetable.

This plays out as one more round in the ladder of tariff threats that started with steel duties in 2018, paused during the 2020-2024 trade truce, and resumed after the latest election. The next visible rung is the EU-Canada summit readout due in late October. If that readout shows no change in pace, US follow-through paperwork would surface within weeks.
