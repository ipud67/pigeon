FinCEN proposed tighter checks on owners of companies at banks

The Bank Secrecy Act of 1970 gave the Treasury secretary power to write rules that require banks and other covered financial institutions to identify who really owns and controls legal entity customers.

FinCEN has now published a proposed to the Customer Due Diligence Rule to Executive Order 14406. The change would that covered institutions collect enough information to the identities of beneficial owners and would keep in place their existing authority to obtain additional information when it bears on risk assessment, including facts about lawful immigration status and employment authorization of those owners where such facts are relevant.

The proposal sits open for public comment until January 19, 2027. After that period closes, FinCEN will review what it receives and decide what text to adopt as the final rule.

Thinking ahead
FinCEN put out a proposed rule that tightens the Customer Due Diligence Rule for banks and other covered financial institutions. The change requires those institutions to gather enough data to verify the identities of beneficial owners of legal entity customers and keeps their ability to pull extra details when risk flags come up, including lawful immigration status and work authorization if those tie into the risk picture. This sits on the ladder of earlier Bank Secrecy Act updates that started in 1970 and have been adjusted multiple times to hit money laundering and illicit finance risks. The proposal follows a direct order in Executive Order 14406 to reshape the rules for clearer risk focus while cutting unnecessary burden under Executive Order 14192. The rule is still in draft form with no effective date set. Observable next steps are the volume and content of public comments landing on regulations.gov plus any schedule FinCEN sets for review or hearings. Horizon: mid-2027.
