Canada’s government says Statement by Prime Minister Carney on Rosh Hashanah

Canadian law has long required the federal government to protect the rights of citizens to practice their faith without interference. That duty sits inside the Criminal Code, which sets the rules for what conduct counts as a crime against public order and worship. The code reached places of worship, schools and community centres but left some forms of intimidation and obstruction outside clear criminal penalty until lawmakers changed it.

On September 11, 2026, Prime Minister Carney issued a statement marking the start of Rosh Hashanah at sundown. The statement recorded that antisemitism had risen for three years to levels not seen since the post-war era. It listed two concrete federal moves already taken: passage of the Combating Hate Act, which added a specific ban on intimidating or obstructing people at houses of worship, schools and community centres, and a fresh $75 million commitment to the Canada Community Security Program that supplies security resources to faith-based sites.

The new criminal prohibition now applies across Canada to anyone who blocks or threatens access at those locations. Funding for physical security at Jewish institutions and other faith sites has increased by the added amount. The situation now turns on whether the updated Criminal Code provision and the expanded security grants change the reported rate of incidents against Jewish Canadians in the year ahead.
