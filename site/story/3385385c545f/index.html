USTR, Commerce, and the UK Government Convene the 10th United States-United Kingdom Small and Medium Enterprise Dialogue in Cardiff, Wales

The United States and the United Kingdom began holding an annual Small and Medium Enterprise Dialogue in 2018 under the Trump Administration. The Office of the U.S. Trade Representative, the U.S. Department of Commerce, and the U.S. Small Business Administration run it on the American side together with the UK Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade and the UK Embassy on the British side. The two governments take turns hosting the meetings in each country so that small business owners, state export councils, and trade officials can meet in person to talk through problems that affect cross-border sales.

On September 9, 2026, the two sides held the tenth session in Cardiff, Wales, hosted by the UK government. Assistant U.S. Trade Representative Bryant Trick and UK Director Graham Floater opened the meeting. They were joined by remarks from Deputy Assistant Secretary Bradley McKinney of the U.S. Commercial Service, Deputy BIST Director in Wales Tom Taylor, and Minister Counselor Jonathan Heimer of the U.S. Embassy in London. Participants spent the day on four topics: trade barriers that hit small firms, the outlook for small manufacturers, ways to expand innovation in digital tools and artificial , and how to link supply chains and investment between the two countries.

The session left the standing U.S.-UK trade machinery unchanged. The dialogue continues as the regular channel where the two governments collect direct feedback from the roughly 24,000 American small businesses that exported $22.1 billion in goods to Britain in 2024, the year that made up ninety percent of all U.S. exporters to that market.

Thinking ahead
The 10th US-UK Small and Medium Enterprise Dialogue took place in Cardiff on September 9 2026. It is the latest meeting in a series that started in 2018 under the Trump Administration and has alternated between the two countries since then. Officials from USTR, Commerce, SBA, and their UK counterparts met with small business groups to talk through trade barriers, manufacturing shifts, digital and AI innovation, and supply chain links. The US-UK goods trade stood at $162 billion last year with $1.8 trillion in two-way investment, and roughly 90 percent of American exporters to Britain are small and medium firms that shipped $22.1 billion in 2024. This format has run twice a year on average since launch. With no new trade deal announced and the same topics recurring, the dialogue is likely to keep its current pace of one or two sessions per year. The next rung will probably be the 11th meeting in the US in early 2027 unless a concrete policy change such as tariff adjustment or regulatory alignment alters the schedule. It runs another 12 to 18 months at current frequency.
