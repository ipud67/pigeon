GUATEMALA
September 10, 2026 · 17:26 UTC

Road in Guatemala rise to seven on Thursday due to protests against fuel price increase

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlineLos bloqueos en carreteras de Guatemala subieron a siete este jueves por las protestas contra el alza de los combustibles

Protests against higher fuel prices spread through Guatemala this summer and produced road blockades that shut major routes. The blockades began after the government raised fuel taxes in July and grew in number through August as drivers and transport unions refused to move cargo or passengers until the increase was reversed.

On September 10 the number of active blockades reached seven. The Court issued a provisional amparo that same day after the business sector petitioned for it. The order requires security forces and local authorities to clear the roads and guarantees free circulation, with special attention to routes serving La Aurora International Airport and other strategic .

The ruling now stands as the legal authority for ending the blockades while the underlying fuel-price dispute remains unresolved.

Thinking ahead
Protests in Guatemala against a fuel price hike have now set up seven road blockades as of Thursday. The Constitutional Court issued a provisional amparo that orders free movement and protects key sites like La Aurora International Airport after the business sector filed for it. This is the latest push in a pattern where price complaints trigger blockades that courts then try to clear. The blockades will likely hold for days or weeks until police remove them or the government cuts the fuel price enough to pull protesters off the roads. The court ruling sets the next legal step where authorities must enforce open transit or face contempt proceedings. If the price stays high and enforcement stays light the number of blockades could climb past seven; a visible drop in fuel costs or arrests at the sites would shrink them fast. The situation stays local to Guatemala with no tie to the Hormuz conflict. Horizon estimate: 2–4 weeks until either price relief or sustained clearance operations.
