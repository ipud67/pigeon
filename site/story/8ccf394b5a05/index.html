Malaysia PM to offer to host Myanmar's Min Aung Hlaing in deal for Rohingya return

Malaysia has hosted more than 190,000 refugees from Myanmar for years. Most arrived after the military drove out hundreds of thousands of Rohingya in 2017. The refugees live in legal limbo because Malaysia never signed the UN refugee convention and does not grant them permanent status.

On September 16 2026 Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said he would invite Min Aung Hlaing, who seized power in the 2021 coup and became president in April after an army-backed election, for an official visit to Kuala Lumpur. In exchange the Myanmar government must take back thousands of the refugees. Anwar told a podcast that ASEAN has refused to recognise the new government and bars its leaders from summits, yet Malaysia still needs someone in authority to arrange returns. He said Malaysia would pay the repatriation costs and had already secured agreement for the first 5,000 to go home, with the first group of 1,500 due in September.

The offer marks the fifth time an ASEAN member has hosted Min Aung Hlaing since he began travelling to rebuild ties after the coup. Malaysia now treats him as the necessary for refugee talks even while withholding formal recognition. The arrangement turns on whether Myanmar carries out the staged returns and whether the refugees can be sent back without papers.
