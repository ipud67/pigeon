Trump administration asks justices to decide on its appointments of acting U.S. attorneys

The Federal Vacancies Reform Act sets the rules for filling a vacant U.S. attorney position that normally requires confirmation. It lets the attorney general name a first assistant who automatically steps in as acting U.S. attorney for up to 120 days. The law took its current form in 1998 and has governed acting appointments across since then. Jason Frierson held the post in Nevada after nomination by President Biden. He left office on Jan. 17 2025 just before President Trump took the oath for his second term.

On March 31 2025 Attorney General Pamela Jo Bondi named Sigal Chattah acting U.S. attorney for Nevada effective April 1. Two days before the 120-day clock ran out Chattah resigned the acting title and Bondi immediately designated her first assistant. The move gave Chattah the same powers as the U.S. attorney for cases in the district. Three men indicted on charges after the designation moved to dismiss their or at least bar Chattah from the cases because her name appeared on the charging documents. A federal district judge ruled she could not serve and the 9th Circuit agreed on the ground that the statute bars the executive from creating a new first assistant after a vacancy already exists.

The Trump administration filed a petition with the Supreme Court on Sept. 9 asking the justices to reverse the 9th Circuit decision. Solicitor General D. John Sauer argued the ruling threatens to disrupt five U.S. attorney offices across the circuit that together manage more than 8 000 civil and criminal cases each year. The petition states the appointments have operated without successful legal challenge for 25 years and that the 9th Circuit misread the statute’s succession and delegation provisions. The case now turns on whether the Supreme Court will hear the appeal and settle the meaning of the first-assistant rule.
