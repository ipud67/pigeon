Trump bans media outlets CNN, MSNOW and Politico from the White House

The White House has controlled access for reporters from news organizations since the 1920s when Calvin Coolidge first set aside a dedicated press room. Over the decades presidents granted and withdrew credentials based on conduct inside the building and on the grounds. The First bars laws that abridge press freedom but leaves the executive branch to decide who receives daily passes to the West Wing briefing room and Air Force One travel.

On September 18 2026 President Trump directed the press office to revoke the credentials of CNN MSNOW and Politico. He cited repeated stories from those outlets that he called inaccurate and hostile. The order bars their reporters from the daily briefings the workspace and the residence compound until further notice.

The three organizations now stand outside the controlled entry list while other outlets continue to operate inside the building under the same rules that have governed access for a century. The White House has not said whether the ban extends to pool rotations or only to individual reporters.

Constitutional analysis
President Donald Trump announced a ban on CNN, MSNBC and Politico from White House access over their coverage. The First Amendment says Congress shall make no law abridging the freedom of the press. The founders wrote that protection to stop government from punishing printers and speakers who criticized officials. In the founding generation the Sedition Act of 1798 sparked fierce debate precisely because it let the executive punish unfavorable newspapers. Washington himself faced harsh attacks in the press yet never barred outlets from federal property. The Constitution grants the president control over the executive branch and its premises, yet the power to exclude reporters has always been measured against the plain text that shields publication from federal penalty. This move tests how far that executive discretion reaches when it singles out specific news organizations.
Thinking ahead
Trump announced Friday he will bar CNN, MSNBC and Politico reporters from White House grounds after months of stories he called unfair. The move follows years of public clashes with the same outlets during his first term and echoes earlier steps like pulling press passes for individual reporters. Legal teams at the outlets have already signaled they will file suit in federal court citing First Amendment limits on government control of press access. Past cases show courts have blocked similar bans when outlets proved viewpoint discrimination, so the next concrete step is a lawsuit and a request for an injunction to restore credentials. The ban holds until a judge rules otherwise or the administration drops it. This will likely drag on for months in the courts. It ends when either a final ruling reinstates the outlets or the White House changes its policy.
