US judge dismisses X lawsuit over New York law requiring hate speech disclosures

Overview
Signed in December 2024 by Governor Kathy Hochul, the New York law required social media companies with at least $100 million of annual revenue to disclose how they eliminate hate on their platforms.
Short history
A New York law signed by Governor Kathy Hochul in December 2024 required social media platforms with at least $100 million in annual revenue to disclose their policies and practices for removing hate speech content from their sites. X sued the state in 2025, arguing the requirement violated the First . On August 27 2026 a federal judge dismissed the lawsuit.
Predictive model
A federal judge in New York tossed out X’s lawsuit against the state’s 2024 social media hate-speech disclosure law on August 27. The law, signed by Governor Kathy Hochul, forces platforms pulling in $100 million or more a year to post exactly how they handle hate content. X had argued the rule violated the First Amendment; the dismissal ends this round in court. This fight is likely to drag on for months or longer. X can file an appeal to the Second Circuit, which is the next clear procedural step. If the appeals court takes the case and reverses the dismissal, the lawsuit would go back to the district judge for more hearings. If the appeals court upholds the dismissal, X could ask the Supreme Court to step in, but that only happens if at least four justices agree to hear it. The law stays in force unless a higher court blocks it. No other immediate moves by the state or the company are on the table right now. Horizon estimate: Appeal filed by late 2026, outcome in 2027 or later.
