This story is from September 27, 2026 and is no longer on the front page.

China left Taiwan out of its fact sheet on Xi Jinping state visit to the United States this week in Taipei

U.S. policy on Taiwan has stood for decades on the formula that Washington acknowledges Beijing's claim to the island without accepting it, sells arms to Taipei under the Taiwan Relations Act, and states that it does not support a unilateral of by Taiwan. That position has been restated by every since the 1970s.

On September 25 President Trump met Chinese President Xi Jinping at the White House during Xi's state visit. Trump said afterward that the two leaders did not spend much time on Taiwan and that Xi understood his views. Beijing had pressed for explicit U.S. language opposing Taiwan independence.

On September 27 Taiwan's foreign minister Chen Ming-chi told reporters in Taipei that the U.S. had given repeated private assurances through multiple channels that its Taiwan policy remained unchanged. He cited U.S. Ambassador to China David Perdue's public statement the day before that there had been no change. Chen noted that the U.S. summary of the summit and Trump's own remarks contained no endorsement of the wording Beijing sought.

The arms package worth $14 billion that Taiwan has requested is still under review by the Trump administration, which weighs such sales against its own military requirements and industrial capacity. Taipei continues to press its defense needs through existing channels while Chinese military activity around the island persists.
