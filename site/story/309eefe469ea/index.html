Rep. Meeks puts hold on Trump’s reported $2.8B weapons sale to Israel

The US and Israel began strikes on Iran in late February 2026. The fighting settled into weekly exchanges by early March, with parallel combat in Lebanon and repeated naval clashes over the Strait of Hormuz. A June memorandum lifted the dual blockades and set a 60-day negotiating window, but that window collapsed on 8 July and US strikes resumed on a sustained nightly basis.

On 16 September 2026, Rep. Gregory Meeks, ranking member of the House Foreign Affairs Committee, placed a formal hold on a Trump proposal to sell Israel 40,000 one-ton bombs for $2.8 billion. The package contained 20,000 MK-84 bombs, 20,000 BLU-117 bombs, and 20,000 I2000 Penetrator warheads. Meeks stated that he would not clear the sale at this time.

The hold leaves the weapons transfer stalled inside the standard review process that governs arms sales. It now turns on whether the administration and Israeli government provide additional information that satisfies the ranking member’s stated requirement that US-funded weapons carry meaningful safeguards for human life.

Rep. Gregory Meeks placed a hold on the reported $2.8 billion sale of 40,000 one-ton bombs to Israel. The move follows the pattern set since the US and Israel began strikes on Iran in late February 2026, with the fight now centered on the Strait of Hormuz after the July collapse of the Islamabad Memorandum. Meeks cited risks to civilian lives from Israel's actions in Gaza and Lebanon as the reason he will not clear the package at this time. Holds like this usually trigger closed-door talks between the ranking member, the administration, and Israeli officials to address specific concerns before the hold lifts. The next visible step is either a revised proposal with added end-use monitoring or enough pressure from House leadership and the White House to force Meeks to stand down. Without a public compromise or procedural override, the hold stays in place and delays delivery. This pattern has repeated on past arms packages during the current round of fighting. The hold ends when the administration meets the stated conditions or when congressional support for the sale outweighs the objections. Horizon estimate: Hold likely lasts 4-8 weeks or until a procedural vote forces clearance.
