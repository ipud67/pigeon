Organizers obtained a police permit for up to 500 people and described the gathering as a show of presence, Jewish identity, support for Israel, and a call for greater safety after antisemitic incidents this summer. Mayor Mamdani responded at a Wednesday press conference that he welcomes the event but will be elsewhere in the city at the time.

This fits the pattern of Jewish groups in the city turning to visible public prayer and statements since Mamdani took office on January 1. The schools have framed the action as teaching students to show up in public rather than depending on a direct meeting with the mayor.

The event is set for one morning and carries no announced follow-on dates. It will likely end after the service unless additional schools join and organizers convert it into a weekly or monthly rhythm, which would require new permits and sustained turnout. Without a shift in city policy on hate-crime enforcement or a change in mayor, the underlying tension stays in place and similar one-off gatherings can recur when incidents spike.

Next rung depends on turnout numbers Friday and whether city officials log any policy response in the following week.
