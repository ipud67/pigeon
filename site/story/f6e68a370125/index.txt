18:T4e4,Rwanda has been building its nuclear infrastructure step by step. In March it hosted an IAEA review mission that looked at its plans for nuclear power and a research reactor. The government then put together an Integrated Work Plan to follow the mission's recommendations. This week at the IAEA General Conference in Vienna the Minister of State for Infrastructure said the country wants to join the Atoms4Food initiative to bring radiation and isotope methods into farming for better yields, pest control, food safety checks, and handling of climate shifts. Officials plan to work with IAEA and FAO teams to map national needs and write a specific program.

The push stays tied to the existing work plan and the March review outcomes. Next steps hinge on finishing the national report for a power decision, updating laws, and setting policies. The agricultural angle adds one more item to the Centre for Nuclear Science and Technology but does not change the timeline set by the Integrated Work Plan.

This effort will keep moving along the Milestones Approach until the government finishes the legislative review and adopts the full policy package. Without those two markers the program stays in planning.
