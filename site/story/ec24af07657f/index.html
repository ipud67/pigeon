Newsom signed a law that makes two Muslim holidays state days off in California

California state government has observed nine paid holidays for state employees since the 19th century, fixed by and listed in the Government Code. Those days include federal observances such as New Year’s Day, Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Presidents’ Day, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Day, Labor Day, Veterans Day, Thanksgiving and Christmas, plus Cesar Chavez Day. The list has remained unchanged in its core structure for decades.

On September 29, 2026, Governor Gavin Newsom signed Assembly Bill 765, which added Eid al-Fitr, the holiday that ends the month of Ramadan, and Eid al-Adha, the holiday that marks the end of the annual pilgrimage to Mecca, to the list of state holidays. The bill requires state offices to close and state employees to receive the days off with pay when the holidays fall on a weekday.

The two new holidays now sit inside the same legal framework that governs all other state holidays. Their addition changes the total to eleven paid observances and shifts the operational calendar for state agencies, courts and public schools that follow the state schedule.
