U.S. judge blocks Trump's move to limit duration of stay for foreign students, journalists: report

U.S. immigration rules have long granted foreign students on F visas and exchange visitors on J visas permission to remain in the country for the duration of their approved programs plus a grace period. Foreign journalists on I visas received an initial authorized stay of up to one year. Those arrangements stood until the Department of Homeland Security published a new regulation in July 2026.

On Sept. 14 2026 U.S. District Judge F. Dennis Saylor in Boston issued an order that prevents the department from putting the regulation into effect. The rule would have set a hard four-year limit on stays for F and J visa holders and capped the initial period for most I visa journalists at 240 days with extensions also limited to 240 days. The order arrived one day before the scheduled start date for enforcement.

The regulation remains blocked while the case continues in federal court. More than 13,000 South Koreans holding the affected visas can continue under the prior duration rules for now.

Thinking ahead
A federal judge in Boston blocked the Trump administration's rule that would have capped stays for foreign students on F visas and exchange visitors on J visas at four years and set an initial 240-day limit for most journalists on I visas with extensions of up to 240 days. The order came one day before the Department of Homeland Security planned to start enforcement in September 2026. The rule grew out of the administration's July announcement aimed at what it described as visa abuse among students, exchange visitors, and media. The block rests on a single court filing and will hold until the government either wins on appeal or the case settles. Next step is almost certainly a notice of appeal to the 1st Circuit, where briefing and oral arguments would stretch into 2027 unless the parties move faster. The administration could also rewrite the rule to address the judge's stated defects, which would restart the clock on public comment and legal challenges. Indicators are thin beyond the existence of the injunction and the prior July announcement. No public signals yet on whether the government will appeal or revise. This runs at least through early 2027.
