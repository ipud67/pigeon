The schedule builds on a steady ladder of steps since 2024: rising bilateral trade that hit $3.8 billion in merchandise and $1.3 billion in services last year, joint membership in the Maritime Security Partnership, and regular Arctic Council and NATO coordination.

Talks are likely to produce new working groups or MOUs on supply chains and Arctic patrol rather than sudden treaty changes.
