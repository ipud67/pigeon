DRC validates new protocol for severe malaria treatment based on injectable quinine

Translated from French · read the original →
The original headlineLa RDC valide un nouveau protocole de prise en charge du paludisme grave basé sur la quinine injectable

The Democratic Republic of Congo has run treatment protocols for severe malaria for years in a country that records some of the world's highest case loads each year. Health facilities there have relied on a mix of injectable drugs and follow-on tablets to bring patients through the acute phase when the parasite reaches the brain or causes organ failure.

On 17 September 2026 officials in Kinshasa presented a revised national protocol built around injectable quinine supplied at a fixed concentration of 500 mg per 2 ml vial. The document sets exact dosing schedules, infusion rates and transition steps from the injectable form to oral medication once the patient stabilizes.

The new text now governs how doctors and nurses across public and private facilities must manage severe cases. It replaces earlier guidance that allowed wider variation in drug choice and measurement. Implementation will begin once training reaches the level.
