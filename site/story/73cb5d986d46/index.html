Rescission of the Greenhouse Gas Findings for Fossil Fuel-Fired Power Plants and Repeal of Regulations for Power Plant Greenhouse Gas Emissions Under Clean Air Act Section 111

The Clean Air Act Section 111 has given the EPA authority since 1970 to set performance standards for new and existing stationary sources in listed industrial categories. The agency listed fossil fuel-fired electric generating units as a source category in 2015 and at the same time made an endangerment finding that their greenhouse gas emissions contribute to air pollution that endangers public health and welfare. That finding triggered the first national GHG emission standards for the power sector, which took effect in 2015 and were later revised in 2024.

On September 17 2026 the EPA published a supplemental proposal that would the 2015 endangerment finding and repeal every associated GHG standard for fossil fuel-fired EGUs. The agency now reads Section 111 as not authorizing regulation of power plant emissions to address global climate change and says this interpretation requires undoing the earlier determinations on multiple independent grounds.

The proposal stands open for public comment until November 2 with a virtual hearing set for October 1. A separate final rule already certain 2024 standards on different legal and technical bases and that rule remains closed to further comment. The new action turns on whether the reaches climate-related emissions from this source category at all.
