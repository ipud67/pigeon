The first duty of government is to secure the lives and property of the people so they can live in peace. The Preamble lists domestic Tranquility as a core reason to form the Union. The founding generation treated border security and law enforcement as basic functions of the sovereign states and the new federal government. The Militia Acts of 1792 placed the duty to repel invasions and suppress insurrections on the states and the people themselves when called. Washington rode with the militia in 1794 to put down the Whiskey Rebellion and restore order.

Article IV, Section 4 of the Constitution requires the United States to protect each state against invasion. Cross-border criminal flights fit that duty. The Tenth Amendment leaves to the states and the people all powers not given to the federal government. Cooperation between Mexican and American border agents follows the pattern of mutual aid the founders expected between sovereign governments facing common threats.

The incident shows why secure borders matter to the right to life and the safety of citizens on both sides. Criminal groups using drones to move contraband or conduct surveillance directly threaten that safety. 1a:T541,Mexican Army troops got a tip from US Customs and Border Protection, spotted a commercial drone crossing from the United States into Tijuana’s Nido de las Águilas neighborhood, took it out of action, and picked up the man believed to be flying it. The drone belonged to a criminal group moving product across the border. Sedena put out the notice on the intercept that happened early on September 18.

This kind of joint tip-and-intercept has happened a handful of times along the California-Baja line in the past two years whenever a group tests a new route or altitude. The detained operator will go through standard federal processing in Baja California, which usually ends in charges for illegal airspace use and whatever cargo was on the drone. The pattern holds steady unless the groups switch to smaller, harder-to-spot models or shift flight paths farther east where Mexican coverage thins out.

Next step is forensic work on the drone’s electronics and any video or payload it carried, plus whatever the operator says under questioning. If the pattern repeats with the same group, expect another drone launch within weeks; if the arrest yields usable leads, the group may pause operations on this stretch for a month or two while they regroup.
