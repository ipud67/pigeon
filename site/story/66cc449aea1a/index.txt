17:T648,The Constitution places the power to declare war in Article I, Section 8, Clause 11. The founders designed this to keep the decision to send Americans into sustained combat in the hands of the people’s elected representatives, not one man. George Washington, James Madison, and Alexander Hamilton all warned against letting presidents stretch executive authority into open-ended war. The War Powers Resolution of 1973 tried to restore that original balance by requiring presidents to pull forces out of unauthorized hostilities.  

House members voted 220-204 on September 16, 2026, to direct President Trump to end U.S. involvement in the Iran conflict under that law. Seven Republicans joined Democrats on the third try. The measure now heads to the Senate, which has taken no action on the earlier version passed in July.  

The right to life stands first among the unalienable rights listed in the Declaration. Government exists to secure it. Sending troops into months of combat without Congress’s consent risks American lives and drains the Treasury without the consent of the governed. The Pentagon’s own watchdog counted $33.4 billion spent through late June on Operation Epic Fury, with munitions shortfalls and supply bottlenecks. Disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz have raised energy prices for families at home.
