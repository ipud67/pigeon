1d:T467,Federal prosecutors indicted ten non-citizens in Minnesota on September 24 for illegal voting and for falsely claiming citizenship to register. The cases stem from a Homeland Security Investigations probe and cover offenses that include one vote cast in the August primary. All ten defendants have appeared in federal court on the charges.

Each case now sits with a single judge for pretrial motions and discovery. Plea talks or trial settings will follow standard federal timelines in the District of Minnesota. Conviction on the false-claim count carries up to five years in prison; the illegal-voting count is a misdemeanor with a one-year maximum.

The number of new indictments in similar voter-fraud cases has stayed in the single digits per district in recent cycles. Continuation depends on how many additional referrals Homeland Security Investigations and local election offices send forward. Next visible step is either plea agreements or the first trial docketed in this batch.

This pattern runs at least through the current administration’s term unless referral volume drops sharply.
