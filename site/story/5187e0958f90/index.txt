The pattern holds: Iran tests the blockade with small-boat or missile moves, the US answers with direct fire on the vessels or launch sites, and both sides keep the strait partially open for approved traffic while blocking the rest.

This specific clash is likely to stay at the current tempo for weeks rather than months. Each side has shown it can absorb losses without widening the fight to new theaters or full naval fleets. The next rung will come when either Iran sends a larger wave of boats and drones to force a sustained US response or when one side signals a fresh diplomatic channel that both treat as real.

Observable indicators are the volume of IRGC small-boat traffic near the strait each week and whether CENTCOM reports shift from single-boat intercepts to multi-day air and surface campaigns. If boat attempts drop below one every ten days or if a third party offers a verified mediation schedule both governments accept, the exchange rate changes. Right now those indicators point to continuation at the same narrow level.
