SHABWA
October 20, 2026 · 14:09 UTC

Houthi missiles hit a training camp in Shabwa Yemen and killed 53 security recruits on Monday

Three ballistic missiles struck a training camp for internal security forces and police recruits in government-held Shabwa province on 19 October 2026. The camp sat near Ataq city inside territory controlled by the Saudi-backed Yemeni government. Yemen's civil war, which began in 2014, had escalated again in early October when pro-government forces opened a major offensive to retake Houthi-held ground along the Red Sea coast overlooking the Bab al-Mandab Strait.

The missiles hit the Martyr Ahmed al-Sulaimani Training Centre where recruits from several provinces were undergoing instruction. Local authorities reported 53 killed and more than 200 wounded in the strike, with hospitals overwhelmed and the most seriously injured transferred to Hadramout and Aden. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Sarea stated the missiles targeted a gathering of Saudi enemy forces at al-Alam camp run by Saudi officers. The attack came amid ongoing clashes in western Yemen, around the besieged city of Taiz, and in Marib and al-Jawf provinces, as well as Houthi missile and drone launches toward Saudi targets.

The strike ranks among the deadliest reported in the renewed escalation. Yemen's Presidential Leadership Council head Rashad al-Alimi condemned the action and pledged a firm response. Information Minister Moammar al-Eryani described the training centre as a non-military site.

Thinking ahead
Houthi forces fired three ballistic missiles into the al-Alam training camp near Ataq in government-held Shabwa province on 19 October. The strike killed 53 security recruits and wounded more than 200 at the Martyr Ahmed al-Sulaimani Training Centre, according to the provincial media office and army spokesman. Hospitals moved the worst cases to Hadramout and Aden. Houthi spokesman Yahya Sarea said the camp held Saudi officers and enemy concentrations; government officials called it a non-military target. This sits inside the wider Yemen civil war that sharpened in early October when pro-government units, backed by Saudi air power, began pushing to retake Red Sea coast ground and areas around Taiz, Marib and al-Jawf. The Houthis answered with missile and drone hits on Saudi bases and facilities. The Shabwa attack ranks among the highest-casualty single strikes since that flare-up began. Without a shift in ground control or a new ceasefire channel, tit-for-tat strikes on training sites, airfields and border posts are likely to continue at the current pace.
