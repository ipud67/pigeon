The Declaration lists “the pursuit of Happiness” among unalienable rights, and the family was understood to be the natural place where that pursuit is first protected and shaped. The Tenth Amendment reserves to the states and the people all powers not given to the federal government; nothing in the Constitution grants Congress or the Department of Justice authority to dictate how local school boards handle parental notice on matters of a child’s identity, health, or moral formation.

The Justice Department’s suit against the Kansas district claims the school’s internal guidance on “secret transitioning” violates federal law. This is the first such case of its kind. The suit rests on the Biden-era interpretation of Title IX and the Equal Protection Clause, not on any power enumerated at the founding.

Kansas parents and the elected school board hold the primary duty here. The founding generation trusted families and local communities far more than distant bureaucrats. When government inserts itself between parent and child without clear constitutional warrant, it violates the consent-of-the-governed principle that justified the Revolution itself. The DOJ should stand down.
