Atak was identified in 90 minutes of aerial footage as the pilot wearing a distinctive jacket before he tried to blend in with passengers. Canterbury Crown Court sentenced him to two years and three months in prison on the new endangerment-at-sea charge created this year to target small-boat pilots. The judge noted the offence required an immediate custodial term and that Atak faces automatic deportation as a foreign national once the sentence ends.

This case sits on the ladder of stepped-up UK enforcement against Channel crossings that began with the 2022 Nationality and Borders Act and continued through the 2024 Safety of Rwanda Act and the 2026 creation of the specific endangerment offence. Prosecutors used video evidence, passenger statements, and National Crime Agency assessment of the vessel’s condition to establish recklessness without proof of prior navigation training.

The sentence runs its course with standard UK prison progression and possible early release on licence after half the term. Deportation follows completion unless Atak mounts a successful legal challenge on protection grounds tied to his South Sudanese nationality. The pattern of record dinghy loads continues, with the 165 figure already overtaken by a 230-person crossing in August.

Next visible rung is either Atak’s appeal filing within 28 days or Home Office deportation paperwork once the custodial period ends. Without a successful appeal or grant of leave to remain the removal happens on schedule.
