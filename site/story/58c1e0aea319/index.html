Israeli AI startup founder pleads guilty to $27 million fraud scheme in New York

Ilit Razt built Joonko as an Israeli artificial intelligence startup that matched large companies with talent from smaller tech firms. The company operated for years inside Israel's crowded startup scene, where founders routinely pitch numbers and customer lists to raise venture capital from American funds. Razt served as chief executive and controlled those claims until investors began to question the figures in 2025.

On September 12, 2026, Razt pleaded guilty in federal court in New York to a fraud scheme that used false statements about Joonko's customers and revenue to secure roughly 27 million dollars in funding. Prosecutors laid out how the inflated numbers convinced backers to wire the money over several rounds while the actual business never reached the scale she described.

The plea ends the criminal case against Razt in the Southern District of New York and requires her to assets and pay . Sentencing remains scheduled for early next year, and the investors who lost the money have begun separate civil recovery actions.
