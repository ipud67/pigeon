Recognition of Organizations and Individuals To Assist Veterans, Family Members, and Caregivers Navigating Programs and Services of Veterans Health Administration

The Department of Veterans Affairs has long run a formal accreditation system under 38 U.S.C. 5901-5904 and related rules in 38 CFR 14.628, 14.629. That system lets the VA approve veterans service organizations, their representatives, attorneys, and claims agents to prepare, present, and prosecute veterans' benefits claims before the agency. The rules require training, oversight, and in most cases limit what fees those representatives can charge.

On September 15, 2026, the VA published a notice in the Federal Register asking for public comments on a new recognition process required by section 129 of the Senator Elizabeth Dole 21st Century Veterans Healthcare and Benefits Improvement Act. The law directs the VA to set up a way to recognize organizations and individuals who help veterans, family members, and caregivers find their way through Veterans Health programs and services. The also bars the VA from granting that recognition unless the organization or individual certifies in writing that it will charge no fee or of any kind to the people it assists.

The notice lays out a proposed certification method and invites feedback on how the process should work, including details on the no-fee requirement. Comments can be submitted through regulations.gov under Docket VA-2026-VACO-0001. The VA has not yet adopted final rules or begun recognizing helpers under the new authority.
