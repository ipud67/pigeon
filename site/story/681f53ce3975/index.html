WASHINGTON
September 15, 2026

Recognition of Organizations and Individuals To Assist Veterans, Family Members, and Caregivers Navigating Programs and Services of Veterans Health Administration

Notice. The Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) is requesting comments and information to assist VA in implementing the requirements of section 129 of the Senator Elizabeth Dole 21st Century Veterans Healthcare and Benefits Improvement Act (Dole Act). Section 129 of the Dole Act requires VA to establish a process to recognize organizations and individuals to assist veterans, their family members, or their caregivers in navigating Veterans Health Administration (VHA) programs and services. We invite comments concerning the proposed process outlined herein for VA to recognize organizations and individuals, including how such organizations and individuals can certify to VA that no fee or compensation will be charged to any individual for services rendered in providing assistance. (Veterans Affairs Department)

Constitutional analysis
The federal government exists first to provide for the common defense, and the founders saw veterans of the Revolutionary War as men who had earned the nation’s lasting care. Washington himself led the fight for pensions and land grants in the Continental Congress and later as President, signing the first service-pension law in 1818 that built on colonial bounties and state promises made before the Constitution. Article I, Section 8 gives Congress power to raise armies and to make rules for the government and regulation of the land and naval forces, while the Preamble names provision for the common defense as a core purpose. The Veterans Health Administration carries out that duty by delivering care earned in uniform. Section 129 of the Senator Elizabeth Dole 21st Century Veterans Healthcare and Benefits Improvement Act directs the Department of Veterans Affairs to set up a recognition process for organizations and individuals who help veterans, family members, and caregivers find their way through VHA programs. The law requires those groups and people to certify they will charge no fee or compensation of any kind for the navigation help. VA published a notice on September 15, 2026, asking for public comments on how to build that process and how to handle the no-fee certification. This builds on existing law under 38 U.S.C. 5901-5904, which already lets VA recognize and oversee groups that prepare and prosecute benefits claims. The new step keeps the same principle that assistance tied to earned veteran benefits stays free of private charges. The founders distrusted standing armies yet insisted on keeping faith with those who served; they wrote no unlimited federal bureaucracy into the document, but they did expect Congress to fulfill specific promises made to the men who won independence. VA’s move stays inside that enumerated power to care for those who bore the battle. Comments can be sent through regulations.gov under Docket VA-2026-VACO-0001.
Predictive model
The VA put out a notice on September 15 2026 asking for public comments on how to set up the recognition process required by section 129 of the Dole Act. That section spells out that organizations and individuals must certify they will not charge any fee before VA can list them as helpers for veterans, family members, and caregivers moving through VHA programs. The notice points to the existing accreditation system under 38 U.S.C. 5901-5904 for claims representatives as a possible model while making clear this new list is separate and limited to navigation help only. The comment period runs for 60 days from publication, after which VA will review submissions and decide on final rules. The next concrete step is the close of that window and any adjustments VA makes based on what it hears. The situation changes only when VA publishes the completed recognition process in the Federal Register. This runs another two months at minimum. It moves next to final rulemaking once comments are tallied. Horizon: early 2027.
