Over R42m disbursed to E Cape ex-mineworkers and beneficiaries

South African mineworkers from the Eastern Cape spent decades in underground labour on contracts that often left unpaid claims and unprocessed benefits behind when they returned home. The Department of Employment and Labour and the government set up Project Ithubalethu to locate those workers and surviving family members, register their claims, and move money through the Unemployment Insurance Fund and the Compensation Fund.

On 13 July 2026 the 41-day outreach programme began in Port St Johns. Mobile units travelled the province, offering on-site claim processing, health screenings, identity-document help, and work-seeker registration. By 14 September the programme had paid out more than R42 million directly to former mineworkers and beneficiaries in the Eastern Cape.

The roundtable held at Mthatha Health Resource Centre inside Nelson Mandela Academic Hospital brought together the two ministers, the South African Ex-Mineworkers Council, Rand Mutual Assurance, the Department of Health and a from eSwatini under the SADC banner. Officials met face-to-face, reviewed service delivery on the ground, and recorded the latest progress of the payments. The situation now turns on how quickly remaining claims can be verified and added to the next wave of disbursements.
