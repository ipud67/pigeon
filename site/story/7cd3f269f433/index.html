Occupied part of Kherson Oblast remains without power for over a day after drone strikes

The Russian occupation of Kherson Oblast began in March 2022 when Russian forces seized the region during the full-scale invasion of Ukraine. Ukrainian troops retook the city of Kherson in November 2022 but Russian forces kept control of the eastern part of the oblast on the left bank of the Dnipro River. Since then both sides have struck energy facilities and in the area with missiles, artillery, and drones. On August 28 2026 Ukrainian drone strikes hit energy infrastructure in the occupied part, cutting power to the area. Local authorities reported the outage lasted more than 24 hours.

Thinking ahead
Power has been out in the Russian-held part of Kherson Oblast for more than 24 hours after Ukrainian drones hit energy targets. Local occupation officials reported the strikes and the blackout on August 29. Russian repair crews have not restored supply yet. This fits the pattern of repeated Ukrainian drone attacks on occupied infrastructure that began in 2022 and continued through 2025. Each past strike led to outages lasting one to several days while crews patched lines or switched to backups. No new Russian air-defense moves or Ukrainian follow-on strikes have been reported in the last day. The outage will likely drag on another 12 to 48 hours unless Russian crews finish repairs or Ukrainian forces hit the same sites again. Next visible step is either power coming back in patches or fresh reports of secondary strikes. If blackouts stretch past three days without repairs, it would signal deeper damage or repeated attacks. Horizon: power likely returns in patches within 72 hours or outage widens with new strikes.
