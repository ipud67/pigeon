Former DHS chiefs: New public safety agency needed

The Department of Homeland Security opened in 2003. Congress created it to pull 22 separate federal offices and agencies under one cabinet secretary after the Sept. 11 attacks. The new department took charge of border security, immigration enforcement, transportation screening, cyber defense, and disaster response.

On the 25th anniversary of those attacks, former secretaries Michael Chertoff and Jeh Johnson sat for a joint interview with NBC News. They said the department no longer fits the original mission and called for Congress to stand up a separate public safety agency.

The proposal leaves the current structure in place while shifting some domestic preparedness and response duties to the new body. The secretaries argued that the split would let each agency focus on its core work without the overlap that has grown over two decades.

Predictive model
Former DHS chiefs Michael Chertoff and Jeh Johnson said in an NBC interview on the 25th anniversary of the Sept. 11 attacks that the department needs a new public safety agency. The call comes after two decades of the department handling both counterterrorism and a widening list of domestic duties that include border security, cyber threats, and disaster response. No bill has been introduced in Congress yet and no administration has signaled support for carving out a separate agency. The idea has surfaced before in reviews after major events but gained no traction. Concrete movement would require a lawmaker to file legislation, committee hearings to begin, and at least one current DHS leader or White House official to echo the proposal in public. Without those steps the discussion stays in the opinion column. The proposal is likely to stay at the level of public comment for the next 12 to 18 months. It advances only if a specific bill gains bipartisan sponsors by early 2027. Horizon: late 2027 at earliest for any floor vote.
