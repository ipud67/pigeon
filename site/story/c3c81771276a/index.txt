21:T478,The FDA put cardiovascular machine learning-based notification software into class II with special controls on September 11 2026. This follows the standard review path that moves software devices from higher-risk categories once performance data and mitigation steps meet the agency's checklist. The codified controls now sit in the federal register as the legal baseline for any similar product.

The classification stands until new performance data or adverse event reports trigger a re-review. Next step is manufacturers filing 510(k) notices that show their devices meet those exact controls. If the FDA starts seeing higher false-positive rates in real-world use the agency can tighten the controls or move the device back to class III.

This sits on the ladder of earlier FDA software-as-medical-device rules that began with the 2018 precertification pilot and the 2021 AI/ML action plan. Those steps set the pattern of risk-based tiers that led to this specific cardiovascular notice.

The rule takes effect in 30 days unless a formal objection is filed.
