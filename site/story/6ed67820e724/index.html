New Hampshire Chief Supreme Court Justice resigns over professional misconduct

New Hampshire's supreme court has operated under a judicial conduct committee that reviews complaints against judges and can reach binding agreements with them on violations of the code of conduct. Gordon MacDonald took office as chief justice in 2021 and oversaw the office that sets employment rules for court staff across the state system.

On September 15 2026 MacDonald sent his resignation letter to Governor Kelly Ayotte. The letter accepted a and agreement he had already signed with the conduct committee in which he admitted that his handling of longtime aide Dianne Martin violated the code. In 2025 he had laid Martin off from her post as director of the Administrative Office of the Courts, triggering a payout of nearly $50,000 in unused leave and benefits, then rehired her days later as general counsel for the Office of Bar Admission. The agreement stated that this sequence amounted to favoritism and that it caused him to fall short on requirements to avoid the appearance of impropriety, to promote public confidence in impartiality, and to carry out administrative duties competently.

The resignation took effect immediately and ended his five-year term. The judicial branch now turns on how the conduct committee's agreement will shape any further review of the payout and on who will fill the chief justice post.

Nothing in the public record points to criminal referral or extended litigation. The story ends with the appointment of the next chief justice.
