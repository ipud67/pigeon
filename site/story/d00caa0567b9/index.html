LITHUANIA
September 15, 2026 · 20:26 UTC

NATO jets down drone over Lithuania as Russian ship fires flares at Danish helicopter

NATO fighter jets shot down a drone that entered Lithuanian airspace and Denmark accused a Russian frigate of firing flares at a Danish military helicopter on Tuesday, in incidents that heightened tensions along the alliance’s eastern flank.

Constitutional analysis
NATO jets shot down a drone that crossed into Lithuanian airspace on September 15 while a Russian frigate fired flares at a Danish military helicopter in the Baltic Sea the same day. The incidents took place along NATO’s eastern flank where the alliance guards member states against direct attack. The Constitution gives Congress the power to provide for the common defense and to raise and support armies and a navy under Article I Section 8. The founders built a system that protects the lives and liberty of citizens first so government can secure the unalienable right to life named in the Declaration. George Washington led the militia in person during the Whiskey Rebellion of 1794 to show that the federal government would enforce law and order at home and stand ready to meet threats from abroad. The Militia Acts of 1792 placed those citizen forces under federal command when called to repel invasion. Federalism and enumerated powers limit how far Washington can stretch its reach yet they do not bar the United States from honoring treaty obligations to defend allies when American soil or forces come under fire. These events test whether the alliance structure serves the founding goal of domestic tranquility secured by strength rather than endless entanglement.
Thinking ahead
NATO jets intercepted and downed a drone that crossed into Lithuanian airspace Tuesday while a Russian frigate fired flares at a Danish helicopter operating nearby in the Baltic. The moves sit on a ladder of repeated airspace violations and close encounters between Russian vessels and NATO aircraft that have picked up since the start of sustained fighting in Ukraine. Next steps will likely stay inside standard alliance procedures: NATO members file formal protests through diplomatic channels, Russia denies intent or blames navigation error, and both sides keep aircraft and ships in the same patrol patterns. The pattern shifts only if one side alters rules of engagement, deploys more assets, or a court records an official finding of deliberate provocation. Without those changes the incidents stay at this level. This runs another 6 to 12 months at current tempo.
