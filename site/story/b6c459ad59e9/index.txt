1f:T53d,The FMC just put out a rule that spells out two ways to file a charge complaint over demurrage and detention fees. Shippers can still use the quick interim procedure, or they can go the traditional route with an administrative law judge or small claims officer. In the traditional track the carrier has to prove the charges are reasonable, exactly as the commission’s earlier interpretive rule requires.

This setup has been in the works since the 2022 Shipping Act changes. The new language takes effect September 1 2026. After that date, anyone who files through the ALJ or small claims path will trigger the full burden-shift process.

The rule itself does not add new requirements or change enforcement priorities. It simply makes the alternative path official so filers and carriers know what to expect. No court action or congressional move is on the table right now.

How much longer this continues depends on whether carriers or shippers challenge the rule in court or whether Congress amends the underlying statute. Absent either of those, the dual-track system stays in place. Next observable step is the September 1 2026 effective date, after which the commission will start routing and deciding traditional complaints under the clarified burden rule.
