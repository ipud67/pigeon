MADRID
October 8, 2026 · 00:09 UTC

Spanish pensioner Maricarmen Abascal, 87, whose eviction in Madrid sparked nationwide protests, died in Spain

Maricarmen Abascal lived for more than 70 years in the same rent-controlled apartment in Madrid's Retiro district after her father first signed the lease there in 1956. Rent stayed capped at 500 euros a month until the building changed hands in 2018 and the new owner, Urbagestión, raised it to 2,650 euros, more than her entire pension. Three earlier eviction orders were thrown out by courts, but on 23 September this year bailiffs carried the 87-year-old out on a stretcher after the latest order took effect.

She was taken to Gregorio Marañón Hospital in fragile health. The owner later offered to cut the rent to 1,650 euros, yet she could not sign because she remained in the hospital. On 8 October the Madrid Tenants' Union announced that Abascal had died.

The news reached the camp of protesters who had occupied Puerta del Sol square since the eviction. They fell silent for a minute, lit candles and laid notes at an altar outside the regional government headquarters. The eviction had already triggered weeks of demonstrations across Spain over rising rents, which have more than doubled in Madrid in the past ten years. Those protests led the Socialist-led government to issue two emergency housing decrees that Congress rejected last week, after which Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez called snap elections for 29 November.

Thinking ahead
Maricarmen Abascal, 87, died after her forced removal on a stretcher from a Madrid apartment her family had rented since 1956. Three prior court blocks on eviction had failed to stop the September action by the owner, Urbagestión, which had raised the rent from 500 euros to 2,650 euros a month against her pension. The Madrid Tenants Union announced the death on 8 October, and protesters in Puerta del Sol observed a minute of silence, lit candles, and placed messages calling it a killing. The case sits on a ladder of blocked evictions, rent caps that ended, and repeated protests that already forced two government emergency decrees on housing. Those decrees were rejected in Congress last week, which led directly to Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez calling snap elections for 29 November. Protests in the square have continued daily since the September eviction and show no sign of breaking up after the death. Union statements tie the next moves to whether the 29 November vote produces a government that passes rent controls or eviction limits. The procedural trigger for change is a new parliament seated after that vote and its first legislative session. The situation will likely run at least through the election cycle and the formation of a new government. It moves next to whether the incoming parliament takes up housing legislation or faces fresh rounds of street action if it does not. Horizon estimate: through Q1 2027 or until new rent law passes.
