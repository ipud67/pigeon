Three prior court blocks on eviction had failed to stop the September action by the owner, Urbagestión, which had raised the rent from 500 euros to 2,650 euros a month against her pension. The Madrid Tenants Union announced the death on 8 October, and protesters in Puerta del Sol observed a minute of silence, lit candles, and placed messages calling it a killing.

The case sits on a ladder of blocked evictions, rent caps that ended, and repeated protests that already forced two government emergency decrees on housing. Those decrees were rejected in Congress last week, which led directly to Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez calling snap elections for 29 November.

Protests in the square have continued daily since the September eviction and show no sign of breaking up after the death. Union statements tie the next moves to whether the 29 November vote produces a government that passes rent controls or eviction limits. The procedural trigger for change is a new parliament seated after that vote and its first legislative session.

The situation will likely run at least through the election cycle and the formation of a new government. It moves next to whether the incoming parliament takes up housing legislation or faces fresh rounds of street action if it does not.
