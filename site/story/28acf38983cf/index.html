September 20, 2026 · 15:24 UTC

To Lam left Hanoi on Sunday for the high-level debate at the 81st UN General Assembly in New York

Vietnam has held a seat at the United Nations since 1977 and sends its top leader to the annual high-level general debate each September. The country has moved from receiving aid and technical help in the 1990s to co-sponsoring resolutions and supplying peacekeeping troops since 2014. Its current five-year term on the UN Human Rights Council began in 2023.

On September 20, 2026, General Secretary and President To Lam left Hanoi for New York to speak in the opening week of the 81st session’s general debate. He travels with the ministers of defense, public security, foreign affairs, finance, industry and trade, science and technology, agriculture, environment, and health, plus the permanent representative to the United Nations and other senior party officials.

The Vietnamese delegation now sits inside the week-long debate where every member state lays out its positions on peace, development, and climate. To Lam’s speech will mark Vietnam’s latest statement on the record before the session turns to negotiations on specific resolutions and the election of new council members.

Thinking ahead
To Lam left Hanoi Sunday morning with a 15-member delegation that includes the ministers of defense, public security, foreign affairs, finance, industry and trade, plus Vietnam’s ambassadors to the US and UN. He is scheduled to speak in the general debate at the 81st UN General Assembly in New York. Vietnam has sent its top leader to this annual session every year since gaining full UN membership in 1977; the pattern shows Hanoi uses the platform to list its positions on climate, trade rules, and peacekeeping without tabling new resolutions. No procedural deadline or vote is attached to this debate, so the trip ends with the speech and bilateral meetings on the sidelines. The next rung would be any joint statement or initiative announced from those meetings. This trip is likely to run its standard four-to-five-day course and conclude without follow-on action unless a specific bilateral document is signed in New York.
