1b:T46a,The Advisory Council on Historic Preservation adopted a categorical exclusion from the Department of the Interior that covers policies, directives, regulations, and guidelines. This lets the council skip full environmental reviews for those actions if no extraordinary circumstances show up. The notice lists the exact actions covered, spells out how the council will check for those circumstances, and records the consultation between the two agencies.

This step sits on top of years of federal agencies borrowing each other's exclusions under NEPA section 109 to cut paperwork on routine work. The council now joins a list of agencies that have done the same with DOI's list.

The process will keep running as the council applies the exclusion case by case. It ends only if the council drops the adoption, DOI changes its own list, or a court finds the exclusion was used wrong in a specific project. Next move is the council using the shortcut on its next batch of internal rules.
