September 20, 2026 · 12:00 UTC

ICE-led Florida operation nets 500 — including illegal aliens accused of murder, child abuse, cartel ties

U.S. ICE agents partnered with the Lee County Sheriff and Florida agencies in a weeklong sweep that netted 500 arrests in Southwest Florida.

Constitutional analysis
ICE agents working with the Lee County Sheriff and Florida state agencies carried out a weeklong operation in Southwest Florida that ended with 500 arrests. Among those taken were people in the country illegally who faced charges of murder, child abuse, and ties to drug cartels. The sweep rested on the power Congress gave the executive branch in Article I, Section 8, Clause 4 to establish a uniform rule of naturalization and in the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1952 as amended, which assigns removal of aliens who violate federal immigration law to the Department of Homeland Security. The Constitution assigns the federal government the duty to secure the nation’s borders and enforce its laws so that citizens and lawful residents may live in safety, the very purpose the Preamble lists as providing for the common defence and insuring domestic Tranquility. In 1794 President Washington called out the militia under the Militia Acts of 1792 to put down the Whiskey Rebellion, showing that the new government would use force to uphold federal statutes and keep the peace. Federal officers hold this enforcement power directly from statute and from the executive’s Article II duty to take care that the laws be faithfully executed; states may assist but cannot obstruct it. The arrests turned on specific criminal accusations and immigration violations already on the books, not on new rules written by an agency. A final count from ICE of how many of the 500 now face removal proceedings would settle exactly how the operation lines up with the statutes Congress passed.
Thinking ahead
ICE agents and Lee County Sheriff deputies ran a weeklong sweep across Southwest Florida that ended with 500 arrests. The group included people already wanted on state charges for murder, child abuse, and links to cartels. Federal officers took custody of those without legal status for deportation proceedings. The operation follows routine ICE-federal task force patterns used in past years. Next step is standard: federal hearings to decide removal, with some cases likely triggering criminal prosecution first in Florida courts. Volume stays steady as long as local police keep feeding tips and ICE maintains the same manpower on the ground. If arrests drop it will show up first in the weekly booking logs from Lee County or in ICE’s own detention intake numbers. No sign yet of either. Horizon: another 12 to 18 months at current pace unless Congress changes removal rules or funding.
