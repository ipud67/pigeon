Trump threatens to ‘hit Iran hard’ as strikes resume after month-long lull

The US–Iran armed conflict began on 28 February 2026 when the US and Israel opened strikes on Iran. A June 17 lifted the US naval of Iran and Iran’s closure of the Strait of Hormuz, then opened a 60-day negotiating window. That window collapsed on July 8; Trump declared the over and the US resumed sustained nightly strikes on Iranian targets. Iran struck three commercial tankers on July 6 that had bypassed its approved transit rules. On July 12 the IRGC moved to shut the strait and Tehran suspended the memorandum. The fight narrowed to control of the strait. After a month-long lull, Iranian attacks on US targets resumed; Trump said Monday the US would hit back.

Predictive model
Trump’s latest warning comes after Iran hit US targets in the Gulf, which itself answered a US strike on an island in the Strait of Hormuz. That island strike was the first big move in a month-long lull that followed nightly US bombing runs in July. The running fight has settled into tit-for-tat blows over who controls the 21-mile choke point for oil tankers. Both sides still have the same tools they used in July and August: US naval escorts and air power against Iranian missiles, drones, and fast boats. Iran keeps testing tanker traffic; the US keeps hitting launch sites and support bases. No new talks are on the table and neither side has shown signs of backing down. The pattern points to more flare-ups every few weeks rather than a steady ramp-up or sudden end. It stays narrow unless one side lands a blow that knocks out the other’s ability to operate in the strait or forces a new ceasefire deal. Next visible rung will likely be another Iranian strike on shipping or a US counter-strike on IRGC assets. Horizon: another 4–8 weeks of this cycle before any shift.
