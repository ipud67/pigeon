Free Matter for the Blind and Other Physically Handicapped Persons

Overview
Rule. The Postal Service is revising Mailing Standards of the United States Postal Service, Domestic Mail Manual (DMM[supreg]) to clarify the applicability of the Hazmat Handling Fee, and to require a parcel barcode, on parcels mailed as Free Matter for the Blind and Other Physically Handicapped Persons (Free Matter). (Postal Service)
Short history
The Free Matter for the Blind and Other Physically Handicapped Persons rule began in 1904 when Congress first let the Postal Service mail books, magazines, and other reading matter for blind people free of postage. Over the next decades the rule expanded to cover records, tapes, and certain materials for people with other physical handicaps who cannot read normal print. The Postal Service has required specific packaging and labeling on these parcels for decades. Effective September 1, 2026, the rule now requires a parcel barcode on every Free Matter item and applies the Hazmat Handling Fee to any qualifying parcels that contain hazardous materials.
Predictive model
The Postal Service is updating its Domestic Mail Manual rules to add a Hazmat Handling Fee clarification and a mandatory parcel barcode for anything sent as Free Matter for the Blind. This is a straight administrative change that takes effect September 1 2026. Once the rule is published in final form the only procedural steps left are routine internal rollout and mailer training. The change will stay in place until the Postal Service issues a new revision or Congress changes the underlying free-matter law. No court cases or stakeholder pushback are on record so far. If volume of free-matter parcels drops sharply or if blind-advocacy groups file formal complaints that trigger a formal review, the rule could be walked back or modified. Horizon: at least through 2028 barring new legislation or formal complaint.
