WASHINGTON
August 27, 2026

Schedules of Controlled Substances: Temporary Placement of 5,6-Dichloro Brorphine, 5,6-Dichloro Desmethylchlorphine, N-Propionitrile Chlorphine, and Spirochlorphine in Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act

Overview
Rule. The Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) issues this temporary order to schedule 1-(1-(1-(4-bromophenyl)ethyl)piperidin-4- yl)-5,6-dichloro-1,3-dihydro-2H-benzo[d]imidazol-2-one (commonly known as 5,6-dichloro brorphine or SR-14968); 5,6-dichloro-1-(1-(4- chlorobenzyl)piperidin-4-yl)-1,3-dihydro-2H-benzo[d]imidazol-2-one (commonly known as 5,6-dichloro desmethylchlorphine or SR-17018); 3-(3- (1-(1-(4-chlorophenyl)ethyl)piperidin-4-yl)-2-oxo-2,3-dihydro-1H- benzo[d]imidazol-1-yl)propanenitrile (commonly known as N-propionitrile chlorphine or cychlorphine); and 8-(1-(4-chlorophenyl)ethyl)-1-phenyl- 1,3,8-triazaspiro[4.5]decan-4-one (commonly known as spirochlorphine or R-6890), including their isomers, esters, ethers, salts, and salts of isomers, esters, and ethers, whenever the existence of such isomers, esters, ethers, and salts is possible, to schedule I under the Controlled Substances Act. DEA bases this action on a finding that placing 5,6-dichloro brorphine, 5,6-dichloro desmethylchlorphine, N- propionitrile chlorphine, and spirochlorphine in schedule I is necessary to avoid an imminent hazard to public safety. This order imposes the controls and , civil, and criminal applicable to schedule I controlled substances on persons who handle (manufacture, distribute, reverse distribute, import, export, engage in research, conduct instructional activities or chemical analysis, or possess) or propose to handle these substances. (Justice Department, Drug Enforcement Administration)
Short history
The DEA placed four synthetic opioids — 5,6-dichloro brorphine (SR-14968), 5,6-dichloro desmethylchlorphine (SR-17018), N-propionitrile chlorphine (cychlorphine), and spirochlorphine (R-6890) — into Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act on August 27, 2026. This temporary order covers the substances, their isomers, esters, ethers, salts, and salts of those whenever they exist. It gives the DEA full control over manufacture, distribution, import, export, research, and possession. The action follows the same emergency scheduling process the agency has used since the 1980s for other fentanyl analogs and nitazenes when it determines they pose an imminent hazard to public safety. The order triggers all Schedule I regulatory controls and sanctions on anyone who handles them.
Constitutional analysis
The federal government exists first to secure the right to life and maintain domestic tranquility so citizens can live in peace. The Declaration of Independence names life as the first unalienable right, and the Preamble lists “domestic Tranquility” as a core reason to form the Union. These four new synthetic compounds—5,6-dichloro brorphine, 5,6-dichloro desmethylchlorphine, N-propionitrile chlorphine, and spirochlorphine—act like dangerous opioids. Placing them in Schedule I stops their spread before they kill more Americans. The DEA’s temporary order is exactly the kind of police power the founding generation expected the national government to use against clear threats to public safety. Article I, Section 8 gives Congress power to regulate commerce with foreign nations and among the states. The Controlled Substances Act flows from that power. The first Congress passed the Tariff Act of 1789 and other laws to protect citizens from imported harms. Washington himself led troops in the Whiskey Rebellion of 1794 to enforce federal law when it threatened order. Temporary scheduling here is no different: it is swift federal action against an imminent hazard, just as the Militia Acts of 1792 let the government move fast to protect lives. This is not overreach. It is the minimum duty of government—to stop substances that destroy life and wreck families. The founders did not build a system that stands by while poison flows freely. They built one that uses clear, limited federal power to guard the public from immediate danger. The DEA followed the statute and acted. That protects the right to life first, then keeps the peace.
Predictive model
DEA just put four synthetic opioids — 5,6-dichloro brorphine, 5,6-dichloro desmethylchlorphine, N-propionitrile chlorphine, and spirochlorphine — into Schedule I on a temporary basis. The move follows the usual DEA playbook for new nitazene-type compounds that show up in the illicit supply and are linked to overdoses. This temporary order locks in manufacturing, distribution, and possession controls for two years while the agency gathers data for a permanent rule. The order runs 24 months from the August 27, 2026 effective date unless DEA publishes a final scheduling decision sooner or Congress steps in. Expect the next public step to be either an extension notice or a formal permanent rule once lab and forensic data packages are complete. The only things that would change the trajectory are a successful court challenge on the “imminent hazard” finding or new legislation that alters the emergency scheduling process. This is the latest rung in the DEA’s rolling temporary scheduling of novel opioids that keep appearing faster than permanent rules can be written. Two-year temporary hold, next move is permanent rule or extension notice.
