17:T8fd,A UN fact-finding mission claims reasonable grounds that two U.S. airstrikes in Iran on February 28 amounted to war crimes because they hit civilian sites and killed non-combatants. The first strike destroyed a primary school in Minab and killed more than 150 people including about 120 schoolchildren according to Iranian officials. The second hit a sports center in Lamerd damaged nearby homes and a school and left 22 civilians dead or wounded. The mission says both attacks were indiscriminate with no evidence the targets served military purposes.

The right to life stands first among the unalienable rights named in the Declaration of Independence. Government exists to secure that right above all as the document states when it lists life before liberty or the pursuit of happiness and declares that is why men form governments. The founders wrote the Constitution to give Congress power to declare war and raise armies while limiting the executive to command those forces once called up Article I Section 8 and Article II Section 2. They drew from English common law and colonial practice that held rulers accountable for needless loss of innocent life in war.

Washington led the militia in person to put down the Whiskey Rebellion in 1794 under the Militia Acts of 1792 showing the early republic expected force to protect life and order not to endanger civilians without clear cause. The Tariff Act of 1789 and the First Bank debate in The Federalist No. 36 and No. 44 kept federal power narrow and tied to enumerated ends. No founder argued the United States could lawfully strike civilian schools or sports centers in another country without proof those places sheltered active fighters.

The UN report also faults the Iranian regime for crimes against humanity in its crackdown on unrest that began in late December but that finding does not change the standard the founders set for American arms. The Pentagon says its investigation continues and Centcom earlier denied striking Lamerd that day. The mission lacks binding power yet its claims add to records that international bodies may use later in courts. 1a:T56f,The UN Independent International Fact-Finding Mission on Iran, set up by the Human Rights Council in 2022, released its latest report Thursday. It says it found reasonable grounds that two US airstrikes on February 28 hit a school in Minab and a sports center in Lamerd, both in Iran, and that the strikes were indiscriminate. Iranian officials put the Minab school toll at over 150 dead, including about 120 children; the Lamerd strike killed and wounded 22 civilians. The mission also listed crimes against humanity by the Iranian government in its crackdown on unrest that started in late December.

The report goes to the 47-member Human Rights Council on Monday. It carries no legal force but adds to records that could feed future cases at international courts. The Pentagon said its own probe continues and has no comment yet. The White House called the findings unserious and pointed to Iranian actions instead.

This round of UN documentation on the war that began in February will likely run for months more as the mission keeps collecting evidence and the council debates it. Next step is the Monday presentation, then possible referral to other UN bodies or courts if enough member states push it. The file grows only if fresh witness statements or satellite data come in; without that the report sits as one more entry in the stack.
