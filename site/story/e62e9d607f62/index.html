Queensland Rail network to return to normal after pay agreement reached

Queensland Rail ran a reduced timetable on its south-east network for months because seven unions and the operator could not agree on new pay and conditions for most of its nearly 6,000 workers. Negotiations moved to the Fair Work Commission in May. Protected industrial action by the unions and a growing maintenance backlog cut services and produced frequent delays for commuters in the state's main population corridor.

An in-principle agreement on wages and conditions was reached on 18 September. The deal gives an 8.5 per cent pay rise over three years plus an inflation-linked component. Impacted staff will now vote on the enterprise agreements. The operator will clear the maintenance backlog and restore services in stages.

Ninety-one per cent of the timetable returns on 12 October with 417 extra weekly services, or about 83 more trains each day. The full timetable is scheduled to run again by the end of November.
