SAUDI ARABIA
September 16, 2026 · 08:36 UTC

Saudi Arabia says it shot down Houthi drone south of Mecca

Saudi Arabia has led a of states against the Houthi armed group in Yemen since 2015. The group controls much of western Yemen and has traded air and missile strikes with the coalition for years. That pattern sits inside the larger US-Iran armed conflict that began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran in late February 2026 and has narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz. The conflict closed the strait in July, rerouted Saudi oil exports through the Red Sea, and prompted Houthi advances along that coast.

On 16 September 2026 Saudi Arabia announced it had intercepted and destroyed a Houthi drone south of Mecca the previous evening. The Saudi-led coalition called the attempted strike on the city a disgraceful act. The Houthis denied they had targeted Mecca or any holy site and said their operations hit only Saudi oil facilities and military bases. They stated the latest barrage came in direct response to more than 450 Saudi air strikes on their territory in the past week and claimed they had shot down a Saudi fighter jet over Marib province.

The exchange occurred after the Houthis seized large areas of Yemen’s Red Sea coast, including the port city of Mokha and Perim Island in the Bab al-Mandab Strait. Saudi Arabia’s East-West Pipeline, used to move crude from the Gulf to the Red Sea port of Yanbu, remained closed after damage from a strike the kingdom attributed to Iran-backed militias in Iraq. Saudi forces said they would take necessary and deterrent measures against the Houthis. The US embassy in Riyadh issued a reconsider-travel advisory citing Houthi attacks on cities, , airports, military bases, diplomatic facilities and energy sites.

Thinking ahead
Saudi Arabia reported shooting down a Houthi drone south of Mecca on Tuesday evening. The Saudi-led coalition called the attempted strike on the city a disgraceful act and said it would take deterrent measures against the group. Houthis denied aiming at the holy site, said their attacks hit only Saudi oil facilities and military bases, and claimed the incident was fabricated to justify more Saudi air strikes on their positions in Yemen. This fits the pattern of tit-for-tat exchanges that escalated after the Houthis seized stretches of Yemen’s Red Sea coast, including Mokha and Perim Island last week. Saudi air strikes on Houthi areas have run more than 450 in the past week alone, prompting Houthi drone and missile launches in return. The coalition has kept air and ground operations active along the Yemen-Saudi border and in Marib, where the Houthis say they downed a Saudi jet. The exchange is likely to continue at its current pace for at least the next several weeks unless one side alters its force posture or supply lines. Next step turns on whether Saudi air sorties increase in number or shift targets deeper into Houthi-held western Yemen, or whether Houthis launch another visible strike on Saudi territory that forces a visible response. Without a clear change in those operational patterns the back-and-forth stays limited to border areas and Red Sea shipping lanes. Horizon: 3–6 weeks of continued cross-border strikes at similar intensity.
