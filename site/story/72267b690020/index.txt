Trump announced the package in the Oval Office on September 30. The deal sits on top of years of growing South Korean stakes in American LNG projects and reactor builds.

The money will flow over multiple years as projects clear permits and hit construction milestones. Treasury yields on September 30 showed a 2s10s spread of 41 basis points, with the 10-year note at 5.29 percent and the 30-year at 5.64 percent. Those levels point to steady demand for long-dated paper that infrastructure bonds would compete for.

Next steps hinge on how fast the pipeline route clears environmental reviews and how many of the eight reactors reach final investment decision. If financing closes and steel hits the ground in Alaska and Texas within 18 months, the full commitment stays on track. If reviews drag past two years on any major piece, the $200 billion ceiling will likely get trimmed.

This runs another three to five years before the bulk of the capital is committed.
