17:T65c,Israel destroyed a large underground Hezbollah complex under the Ali Taher Ridge in southern Lebanon on 11 September using over 1,000 tonnes of explosives. The blast registered as a 4.1 magnitude earthquake on USGS monitors. Israeli forces had seized the ridge the week before after months of ground fighting that began when the broader US-Iran conflict pulled Lebanon in on 2 March. The IDF described the site as a two-decade Iranian-funded command center for Hezbollah’s Badr unit that held dozens of rockets, missiles, UAVs, and anti-tank weapons. It said the strike finished a mission to clear eight tunnels and lock down a security zone along the border.

This action fits the pattern of Israeli incremental advances in southern Lebanon that continued even after the June ceasefire between Iran, the US, and Lebanon. Hezbollah has not issued a public statement on the loss. Lebanese media reported additional Israeli strikes the same day in nearby Nabatieh al-Fawqa and Mansouri, while the Lebanese army stepped up deployments in areas still outside Israeli control.

The operation is likely to continue at the current pace of targeted strikes and ground holding actions for at least the next several weeks. Next steps hinge on whether Hezbollah attempts to retake positions or launch cross-border attacks, which would trigger further Israeli responses, or whether Lebanese army movements expand enough to create a buffer that reduces direct clashes. Without a visible shift in Hezbollah activity or a new diplomatic push from Beirut, the security zone stays in place.
