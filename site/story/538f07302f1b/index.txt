The blast registered 4.1 on the USGS scale and was felt 40 kilometers away in Saida. Israeli troops had seized the ridge the week before after months of ground fighting that started when the wider US-Iran conflict pulled Lebanon in on March 2.

This fits the pattern of Israeli forces clearing tunnels and setting up a security zone along the border. The IDF called the site a command center for Hezbollah’s Badr unit stocked with rockets, missiles, drones, and mines built over twenty years. Hezbollah and the Lebanese government have stayed quiet so far while the Lebanese army moves more troops into nearby towns to steady things.

The action completes Israel’s stated goal of destroying eight tunnels in the area. Fresh strikes hit other spots in the south the same day. How much longer this goes depends on whether Hezbollah tries to retake lost ground or launch bigger rocket attacks from farther north. Without a clear shift in those moves the border fighting stays at this level.

Next step looks like continued Israeli patrols inside the zone and more Lebanese army deployments to fill gaps. A drop in Hezbollah rocket fire or an actual ceasefire talk between the two sides would mark a change.
