PM Shehbaz, Kundi stress enhanced efforts for 'harmony' between Centre, KP

Pakistan's federal government and the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa have operated under a power-sharing arrangement set by the 18th constitutional amendment in 2010. That change gave provinces control over most local affairs while the centre kept authority over defence, foreign policy, currency and inter-provincial coordination. Since the current took office in 2022, meetings between the prime minister and the KP governor have served as one channel for settling disputes over funds, security operations and administrative appointments.

On 15 September 2026 Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif met Governor Faisal Karim Kundi in Islamabad. The two men, joined by Kashmir Affairs Minister Amir Muqam and the prime minister’s adviser on political affairs, reviewed the overall political situation in KP, current law-and-order conditions and other shared concerns. A statement from the Prime Minister’s Office said both leaders called for stepped-up work to maintain operational harmony between the centre and the province and to address public complaints more directly.

The discussion produced no new policy decisions or funding announcements. It left the existing federal-provincial coordination mechanisms in place, with the next test of that coordination expected in routine budget reviews and security briefings already scheduled for the coming weeks.
