This came from a case started by the Western Cape Premier and the City of Cape Town against the Speaker of the National Assembly, the Chairperson of the National Council of Provinces, the Finance Minister and others. Finance Minister Enoch Godongwana has said he respects the ruling and pointed out that the old system still carries problems like irregular spending and weak oversight.

The Act now has no legal force. Parliament must go back and pass a new version that meets the constitutional steps laid out in the judgment. Until that happens the existing patchwork of procurement rules stays in place.

This situation will drag on until both houses of Parliament complete the full legislative process again. The next clear step is for the executive to table fresh draft legislation that fixes the procedural flaws. If the new bill clears the required public hearings, provincial input and voting thresholds without challenge it could reach signature within 12 to 18 months.
