RIYADH
September 19, 2026 · 20:32 UTC

Houthis claim attacks on ‘sensitive sites’ in Saudi Arabian capital

The war between the United States and Iran that began with strikes in late February 2026 has narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz. Iran struck three commercial tankers in July after the Islamabad collapsed. The United Arab Emirates cut trade with Iran in August after two missiles headed toward its waters.

On September 19 the Houthis said they hit sensitive sites in the Saudi capital. Video recorded a large plume of black smoke near Riyadh’s international airport.

The attack lands inside the pattern of Houthi operations that have drawn Gulf states closer to the US side since April. Saudi Arabia has stayed out of direct combat with Iran but maintains air defenses around its capital and oil facilities. The strike tests whether Riyadh will treat it as an Iranian proxy move or as an isolated Houthi action.

Thinking ahead
Houthi forces fired at targets near Riyadh airport on 19 September, with video showing a smoke plume close to the runway area. Saudi air defenses engaged the incoming objects. This fits the pattern of Houthi strikes on Gulf states that began after the US-Israel campaign against Iran widened in late February 2026 and after the naval blockade and Hormuz closure phases collapsed in July. The attacks come after the UAE cut trade with Iran in August over missiles fired toward its waters. Riyadh has stayed out of direct combat with Iran but keeps Patriot batteries and fighter patrols around its capital and oil sites. Houthi launch teams operate from northern Yemen with Iranian-supplied missiles and drones that have reached Saudi territory before. No casualties reported from this specific strike. Saudi statements called the objects drones and said all were intercepted. Houthi claims named “sensitive sites” but released no targeting data. Next moves hinge on whether Riyadh orders cross-border strikes into Yemen or tightens its airspace rules further. The fight stays limited unless a Houthi round hits an airport runway or oil facility and forces a Saudi ground response. This round likely lasts days to weeks until one side changes its launch or intercept posture.
