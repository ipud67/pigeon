Taiwan 7th worldwide for citizen input, slips in judicial freedom

Taiwan has measured citizen input through public consultations, and direct tools since the end of law in 1987. Those mechanisms grew after the first full elections in 1992 and the direct presidential election that began in 1996. By the early 2000s the island had built a dense network of referendums, recall votes and open-government platforms that placed it near the top of global rankings for participation.

On September 15 the International Institute for Democracy and Electoral Assistance released its Global State of Democracy 2026 report covering performance through 2025. The report placed Taiwan seventh worldwide in citizen participation, level with Finland, the same rank it held the year before. Taiwan also recorded gains in the effectiveness of legislative oversight, one of only 13 countries to do so. At the same time its score for judicial independence fell enough to move the island from the top-performing group into the middle tier alongside the United States, Argentina, Belgium, India and Nigeria.

The new figures leave Taiwan’s overall democratic standing anchored by strong public voice and legislative scrutiny while its courts sit at the same level as several larger democracies. The report measured 173 countries across representation, rights, rule of law and participation using indicators that include judicial independence, electoral credibility, freedom of expression and access to justice.
