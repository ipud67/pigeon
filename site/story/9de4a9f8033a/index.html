Ukrainian military tests locally made quad bikes with remote-control capability – video

Ukrainian forces have used quad bikes for frontline supply runs since at least the spring of 2022. In April 2026 the government cut the red tape on buying and delivering light vehicles, including pickup trucks, motorcycles and quad bikes, so units could get them faster. Foreign models already in service cost more and lack remote-control functions.

On 18 September 2026 soldiers from the 33 Group inside Defence Intelligence of Ukraine’s Tymur Special Operations Unit ran closed tests on home-built quad bikes. They drove the vehicles themselves, measured them against the exact conditions that matter for winter logistics, and gave the makers immediate, detailed notes on what needed fixing. The trials focused on models that can switch between manned and remote operation.

The Defence Intelligence cluster Brave1 has now added quad bikes with unmanned-control capability to its grant program for Ukrainian manufacturers. Units will use the feedback to refine the vehicles before winter sets in.

Thinking ahead

The tests focused on logistics use ahead of winter, when mud and snow limit tracked and wheeled supply movement. The April 2026 rule change that eased procurement of pickup trucks, motorcycles and quad bikes opened the door for these domestic models to move faster into service. Foreign quad bikes already in use cost more and lack remote-control features. Brave1 added the remote-capable versions to its grant program for Ukrainian builders right after the test. This step sits on the ladder of incremental gear tweaks that started with the procurement simplification. Next comes manufacturer revisions based on the soldier notes, then field trials under actual winter conditions. The situation changes only if the revised bikes clear those trials and reach unit-level distribution before sustained freeze sets in. The remote-control feature lets operators stay under cover while the bike hauls supplies across open ground that draws fire. Without winter-hardened logistics, units lose mobility once the ground turns to slurry or ice. The test itself shows the army is pushing local production to fill that gap rather than rely on imported stock alone. Likely to run through the first snowfalls as feedback loops tighten; next rung is revised prototypes in troop hands by late October. Horizon: 4-6 weeks to first winter deployment or clear failure signal.
