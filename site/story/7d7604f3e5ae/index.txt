1c:T557,The UK delivered its statement at the UN Security Council on the 25th anniversary of the 9/11 attacks, marking continued support for the counter-terrorism framework set by Resolution 1373 in 2001. The minister listed UK efforts in Iraq, the Sahel, and domestic programs as proof that the existing obligations still drive results against groups like Da’esh and Al-Qaida. He pointed to the groups’ shift toward new technology for propaganda, recruitment, and funding as the main change in the threat picture. The statement called for stricter adherence to rules on terrorist financing, including under Resolution 2462, and for wider ties between governments, the UN, and civil society. This fits the pattern of annual Council meetings that keep the same institutions in place without major new mandates.

This approach will likely hold steady for at least the next 12 to 18 months unless a large-scale attack forces a new resolution. Next steps stay inside the current framework: routine updates on financing controls, intelligence sharing, and capacity building in unstable regions. A shift would require either a major breach of the Strait of Hormuz supply lines that pulls counter-terror work into the direct US-Iran fight or a Council vote on fresh sanctions language that gains unanimous backing.
