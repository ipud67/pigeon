GOP official in California apologizes for election fraud claims that turned out to be untrue

California law lets county registrars of voters check voter rolls when someone flags possible problems with names or addresses. Marin County Registrar Natalie Adona received such a flag in February from the chairman of the local Republican Party. John Turnacliff told county supervisors that dozens of dead people had voted on Proposition 50, a 2025 redistricting measure, and he asked for an official review.

Adona said state law did not require her office to open an investigation, yet she ordered one anyway. The review found no ballots cast in the names of deceased voters. At a supervisors meeting last week Turnacliff stood at the public comment microphone and took back his earlier statement. He told the board, the registrar and her staff that he had not located the names he once claimed were on the rolls and that he was sorry for the trouble the caused.

The registrar’s office completed its check and reported the result. Turnacliff’s public retraction closed the matter inside county government. The episode now sits as one more entry in the county’s record of how election complaints are handled and resolved.

Thinking ahead
John Turnacliff, chairman of the Marin County Republicans, made claims in February that dozens of dead people voted on Proposition 50 in 2025. He asked the registrar of voters to look into it. Last week he stood up at a county meeting, said he found no such voters, and apologized to the board, the registrar Natalie Adona, and her office for the trouble it caused. Adona ran the check even though state law did not require it. She did it to keep local trust in the vote count. The review turned up nothing. This kind of back-and-forth over voter rolls has happened in the county before whenever someone flags a name match. The next step is the registrar closing the file and the board moving on to regular business. For the situation to shift, new records would have to surface that show actual mismatched ballots or a formal complaint filed with the state elections office. It probably ends here unless fresh paperwork appears. Horizon: one to two weeks.
