UKRAINE
September 20, 2026 · 07:37 UTC

Ukrainian Council of Churches asks Pope to help save occupied Oleshky from starvation

The town of Oleshky in Kherson Oblast sits on the east bank of the Dnipro River near the current front line in southern Ukraine. Russian forces took the town in the first weeks of the 2022 invasion. In June 2023 Russian troops destroyed the upstream Kakhovka dam which flooded the town and wrecked its water treatment plant power lines and roads. Most residents left in the months that followed yet several thousand civilians including children stayed behind.

On 20 September 2026 the Ukrainian Council of Churches and Religious Organisations sent an appeal to Pope Leo XIV the World Council of Churches and other religious bodies. The council stated that the last food supplies reached Oleshky in late May. Russian troops have blocked residents from leaving while the town has no electricity gas running water or medicine. The council described the conditions as a that has left people at risk of starvation and exposed to daily drone strikes and shelling.

The appeal calls on the United Nations member states and the International Committee of the Red Cross to organise a humanitarian mission and safe to Ukrainian-held territory. Ukraine has asked for such access since the spring yet Russian authorities have not confirmed any willingness to allow it. The situation now turns on whether an outside religious or humanitarian escort can reach the remaining civilians before supplies run out completely.

Thinking ahead

The appeal names the International Committee of the Red Cross and UN member states as next procedural steps that would need Russian consent to move people to Ukrainian-held ground. Indicators stay thin beyond the public letter and prior stalled talks, so any shift hinges on whether those named parties secure access. This situation is likely to drag on for weeks unless a corridor is agreed. Next visible rung is a reply from the Red Cross or Vatican on whether they can broker entry. Horizon: 4-6 weeks before next verifiable access attempt.
