ISRAEL
October 7, 2026 · 20:27 UTC

Muslims killed seven people in Israel on 7 October 2023, and Israelis mourned the victims three years later

Israel marked three years since the Hamas raid that killed about 1,200 people and took 251 hostage on 7 October 2023. The assault from Gaza triggered an Israeli military offensive there that has continued through a reached almost a year ago. Violence has persisted in Gaza and living conditions for the 2.1 million residents have stayed dire.

Memorial events drew families, survivors and officials to sites of the original attack. At the Nova dance festival near Kibbutz Re'im, crowds gathered where 378 people died and 44 were taken hostage, 25 of whom later returned alive. In Kibbutz Kfar Aza, 64 residents were killed and 19 taken hostage that day, and most survivors have not gone back because of the trauma. Former hostage Yarden Bibas spoke at one site about his wife and two young sons who were killed in Gaza captivity. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu lit a memorial candle and said the nation unites in longing for those lost.

The anniversary fell inside the separate US-Iran armed conflict that began with joint strikes in late February 2026, narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz, saw a June lift dual for 60 days of talks, then collapsed on 8 July when sustained US strikes resumed.

Thinking ahead

This anniversary comes as the separate US-Iran conflict over the Strait of Hormuz has narrowed to repeated strikes and naval moves after the July collapse of the Islamabad Memorandum. No clear procedural step points to an end soon. The pattern of memorial gatherings tied to the unresolved raid and its aftermath shows the focus on that day is likely to repeat yearly until hostage returns or a formal inquiry settles the security questions raised.
