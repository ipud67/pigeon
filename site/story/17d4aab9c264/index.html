US Iranian crypto exchange for bitcoin transfers to Revolutionary Guards

The US and Iran have fought over control of the Strait of Hormuz since late February 2026. That month the US and Israel began strikes on Iranian targets and Iran answered with missile and drone attacks on Israel and US bases in the Gulf. The pattern settled into weekly exchanges that included naval clashes in the strait. In June the two presidents signed a that lifted the US naval and Iran's closure of the strait and opened a 60-day negotiating window. The window closed on 8 July when the US resumed sustained strikes and Iran moved to shut the passage again.

On 17 September the US Treasury placed sanctions on BitBank, a cryptocurrency exchange controlled by Iranian financier Babak Zanjani. The action also hit the software developer behind the exchange and three of Zanjani's close associates. Treasury said BitBank had routed hundreds of millions of dollars in payments to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and to Iran's new Hormuz Safe Marine Services Authority, the body that collects fees for safe transit through the strait.

The sanctions form the latest step in the Treasury's Operation Economic Outcast, which has already targeted Zanjani's other digital-asset platforms in January and additional firms tied to him in July. The designation adds BitBank and its supporting company to the list of Iranian financial and logistical nodes the US has moved against while the fight over the strait continues.

Constitutional analysis
The United States has a clear duty under the Constitution to protect the country from foreign threats. Article I, Section 8 gives Congress power to regulate commerce with foreign nations, raise armies and navies, and punish offenses against the law of nations. The founders wrote that language because they knew peace at home depends on strength abroad. Washington himself led troops to put down the Whiskey Rebellion in 1794 and backed the Militia Acts of 1792 so the federal government could secure borders and shipping lanes. The Treasury’s action against Babak Zanjani’s BitBank exchange follows that line. Zanjani’s network moved hundreds of millions of dollars for the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and collected fees for safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz. That money directly supports a force that has seized ships, attacked U.S. allies, and killed Americans. The sanctions target the exchange, its software developer Pishtaz Simorgh, and three of Zanjani’s close associates. They build on earlier moves against Zanjani’s Zedcex, Zedxion, and Dot One companies. Such steps keep federal power inside its enumerated lane while denying enemies the cash they need to threaten American lives and commerce. The founding generation did not wait for attacks before they acted; they built a government that could meet danger before it reached our shores.
Thinking ahead
The US Treasury sanctioned BitBank, a crypto exchange run by Babak Zanjani, along with its software developer Pishtaz Simorgh and three of his associates. Treasury said the platform routed hundreds of millions in payments to the IRGC and to the new Hormuz Safe Marine Services Authority that charges ships for safe passage through the strait. This marks the latest step in the US “Operation Economic Outcast” campaign that has already hit Zanjani’s Zedcex, Zedxion, Dot One conglomerate and related firms in Turkey and the UAE since January. Zanjani’s network has operated as both open commercial projects and hidden channels for sanctions evasion and IRGC support since he re-emerged in 2025 after his death sentence was commuted. The sanctions tighten the financial side of the narrow fight over Strait of Hormuz control that resumed after the July collapse of the Islamabad Memorandum. The move adds one more layer of pressure on the payment infrastructure Iran uses to monetize tanker transits. Further designations of similar digital-asset nodes or front companies remain the procedural next step unless Iran and the US reach a new agreement that reopens verified, unblocked shipping lanes through the strait. No such talks are on record. This sanctions effort is likely to continue for months. Next it will probably expand to any new exchanges or wallets that replace BitBank in the Hormuz payment chain. Horizon: through at least Q1 2027 unless a fresh memorandum restores open strait traffic.
