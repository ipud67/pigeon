Pakistan to formulate new national occupational safety, health policy

Pakistan has operated without a unified national occupational safety and health policy since the country’s creation in 1947. governments handled their own labor rules while the federal level set minimum standards that reached only the formal economy. Most workers stayed outside those protections in the informal sector that employs the majority of the labor force.

On September 10 federal and provincial officials met with employers and worker representatives in Islamabad. The Ministry of Overseas Pakistanis and Human Resource Development called the session with technical help from the International Labour Organisation. They reviewed a prepared draft policy and agreed on the main elements of an implementation framework that would extend coverage to both formal and informal workplaces.

The draft now moves forward for final adjustments before cabinet approval. The agreed framework sets out how federal and provincial authorities will coordinate inspections, training, and enforcement once the policy takes effect.

Pakistan held a national consultation in Islamabad on September 10 to review a draft occupational safety and health policy and map out its rollout. Federal and provincial officials sat with employers, workers, and ILO staff to tighten the text and set timelines for formal adoption. The process sits on top of earlier reviews that flagged gaps in both the formal sector and the much larger informal economy. Next visible steps are final edits by the Ministry of Overseas Pakistanis and Human Resource Development, then cabinet clearance and provincial buy-in. Momentum stays on track as long as the tripartite group keeps meeting and the ILO keeps supplying technical drafts; it stalls if any province withholds endorsement or funding drops off. Horizon: policy locked in within 12 months if the current consultation schedule holds.
