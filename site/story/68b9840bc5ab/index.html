LITHUANIA
September 16, 2026 · 10:03 UTC

Nausėda: drone shot down over Lithuania was Russian Gerbera headed for Ukraine

Lithuania has belonged to NATO since 2004 and keeps fighter jets from member states on rotation at the Šiauliai air base to police the skies over the Baltic states. Russian forces have flown thousands of drones toward Ukraine from launch sites inside Belarus since the full-scale invasion began in February 2022. On several occasions in the past year those drones have crossed into Latvian or Estonian airspace before turning back or being intercepted.

Early on September 16 a Russian-made Gerbera drone crossed the border from Belarus into central Lithuania. It was brought down by Italian jets operating from the NATO base near the Kruonis pumped-storage hydropower plant in the Kaišiadorys district. President Gitanas Nausėda said on September 17 that the drone had been headed for Ukraine but had been knocked off course by Ukrainian electronic warfare systems.

The shoot-down triggered air-threat alerts across the Vilnius and Kaunas regions. It marks the first time Lithuanian forces have downed a drone over their own territory during the current war. Officials say the country now needs faster ways to identify and destroy such objects without tying up fighter jets every time one appears.

Constitutional analysis
A Russian-made Gerbera drone crossed into Lithuania from Belarus and was shot down over the Kaišiadorys district near the Kruonis pumped-storage hydropower plant by Italian NATO fighter jets based in Šiauliai. Lithuanian President Gitanas Nausėda said the drone was likely headed for Ukraine but veered off course after Ukrainian electronic warfare affected it. Residents in the Vilnius and Kaunas regions received air-threat alerts as it flew over. The right to domestic tranquility stands first among the reasons free governments exist, as the Preamble to the Constitution lists it ahead of the common defense and general welfare. The founding generation treated border security and the ability to repel sudden incursions as core duties of the sovereign: the Militia Acts of 1792 required the states to keep ready forces that could be called out to repel invasion, and Washington himself led militia in 1794 to put down the Whiskey Rebellion when federal law was defied inside U.S. territory. Article IV, Section 4 of the Constitution charges the United States to protect every state against invasion. Similar drone crossings have occurred in Latvia and Estonia, and Lithuania has issued repeated air-threat alerts in recent months, including one Sunday that turned out to be a flock of birds. The incident shows that a nation must keep the practical means to identify and destroy aerial threats at the border if it is to secure the peace its people expect.
Thinking ahead
A Russian Gerbera drone crossed from Belarus into Lithuania on Tuesday, got shot down by NATO jets near the Kruonis plant after air alerts went out in Vilnius and Kaunas. President Nausėda said Wednesday it was headed for Ukraine, knocked off course by Ukrainian electronic warfare, and not aimed at Lithuania. This marks the first drone shoot-down on Lithuanian soil since the Ukraine war started, following earlier air alerts for objects from Belarus and a false bird flock detection on Sunday. Similar stray drone events have already hit Latvia and Estonia. Nausėda called for better drone ID tools and cheaper intercept methods beyond fighter jets, warning repeats are likely near the border. The pattern of objects drifting from Belarus will keep producing alerts and occasional intercepts as long as Ukrainian jamming stays heavy and Russian launches continue. Lithuania will test new ground-based options in the next few months; without them, reliance on NATO jets stays the default. If Belarus-origin flights drop or jamming eases, the frequency falls. Otherwise the cycle holds steady. Next rung likely more alerts and one or two additional shoot-downs before year end.
