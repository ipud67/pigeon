18:T6f5,Israel added its sixth Dolphin II-class diesel-electric attack submarine to the fleet at Haifa Naval Base on 25 September 2026. The boat, built by ThyssenKrupp in Kiel, Germany, carries air-independent propulsion that lets it stay submerged longer than earlier models and extends patrol range beyond the Mediterranean and Red Sea. IDF Chief of Staff Lt. Gen. Eyal Zamir spoke at the handover and listed Iran and its proxies as a standing threat that has taken hits but kept the same goals. The navy now holds six submarines, two more than it operated before the current round of fighting began in late February. This raises the number of hulls available for covert missions in the Strait of Hormuz or farther afield while the fight there stays locked on control of tanker routes.

The induction marks one more step in a naval buildup that has run alongside the air and ground phases since the US and Israel opened strikes on 28 February. Weekly strike-and-counterstrike settled in by early March, the Islamabad Memorandum opened and then collapsed in July, and US nightly strikes resumed with focus on Iranian maritime sites and logistics. The new submarine does not change the immediate tactical picture in the strait but adds one more platform that can operate unseen for weeks.

The situation will continue at current intensity until one side alters the naval posture in the strait or a new diplomatic opening appears. Next observable steps are either further Israeli or US strikes on Iranian naval infrastructure or an Iranian attempt to interdict more commercial traffic. Without a visible shift in mine-laying, patrol density, or tanker routing data, the narrow contest over Hormuz holds steady.
