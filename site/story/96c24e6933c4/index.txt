Vehicle imports through the southern port dropped 75 percent from the 70,000 units offloaded in late 2022. Only one cargo ship a month has arrived since the US–Iran strikes widened in February 2026, bringing in about 4,700 vehicles and failing to cover operating costs for its 120 staff.

The operator has cut some fees by up to 90 percent and is asking to extend its concession past 2028. Traffic has shifted to Haifa and Ashdod because shipping lines call the Red Sea route too risky. Houthis seized Perim island and Mocha last week, then claimed to have taken 4,500 square miles and driven Saudi-allied forces from six districts in Taiz and Hodeidah.

The port stays unprofitable as long as Houthi attacks keep insurance rates and transit times high. Traffic returns only when the Red Sea route is cleared of regular attacks or naval escorts make it safe again. Saudi air raids on Houthi positions in Mocha signal one possible pressure point, but no ceasefire or security guarantee has been announced.
