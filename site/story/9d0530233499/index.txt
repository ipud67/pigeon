19:T577,Heavy rain from September 24 to 27 pushed the Gandak River to 11.94 meters at the Devghat gauge in Nepal, the highest reading since the 1960s. The same runoff sent new record highs at Indian gauges in Bagaha, Dumariaghat, Rewaghat, and Lalganj. Water topped embankments, flooded diara farmland inside the river channel, and spilled into settled pockets at Bagaha, Areraj, and Fulia Khand, hitting hundreds of villages and moving thousands of people.

Satellite shots from September 29 show blue floodwater still covering ground that was dry on September 20. Runoff from the late-monsoon storms keeps feeding the braided channels even after the rain stopped. Local crews are now measuring breach points and checking how much silt settled on fields and roads.

The water level will drop once upstream gauges show steady decline and repair crews close the worst embankment gaps. Until then, downstream districts stay under evacuation orders and aid crews move in by boat. The event stays active until the river returns inside its banks at the key gauges and displaced families can go back to their homes.

Next move is full damage surveys by Nepal’s Department of Hydrology and Meteorology and India’s local administrations, followed by decisions on whether to raise or reinforce the embankments before next year’s monsoon.
