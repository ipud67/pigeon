Confesercenti: 'In 4 years lost 46 billion in purchasing power, +77 billion in taxes'

Translated from Italian · read the original →
The original headlineConfesercenti: 'In 4 anni persi 46 miliardi di potere d'acquisto, +77 miliardi di tasse'

Italian households entered the current period with wages and benefits that had not kept pace with price rises since the inflation surge that began in 2022.

On September 25 2026 the retailers' association Confesercenti reported that over the past four years those households lost 46 billion euros in purchasing power while the state collected an additional 77 billion euros in tax revenue from the same income base.

The association's president, Gronchi, added that the gap between nominal growth and real spending power had burned 30 billion euros in consumption and 52 billion euros in real GDP.

The figures mark the latest measurement of how the post-pandemic price shock and subsequent tax brackets have redistributed resources from household spending to government coffers.

Thinking ahead
Italian households lost 46 billion euros in purchasing power over the last four years while taxes rose 77 billion euros according to business group Confesercenti. The group’s president added that resilience carried a 30 billion euro hit to consumption and a 52 billion euro reduction in real GDP. These figures mark the latest step in a stretch of flat or falling real incomes paired with higher fiscal pressure. The pattern shows no clear reversal in the latest quarterly data. Without a pickup in wage growth above inflation or a drop in the tax take the erosion in household spending power is likely to run at least another 12 to 18 months. Next visible move is probably another round of official tallies in early 2027 that either confirm continued losses or register the first narrow gain if nominal wages finally outpace prices and levies. Horizon: into 2027 at current rates.
