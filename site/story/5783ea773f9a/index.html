Appeals court rules officers can search phones at border without warrant or probable cause

U.S. border officers have searched electronic devices of people entering the country for decades under federal rules that treat the border as a zone where Fourth protections are narrower than inside the country. Those rules let Customs and Border Protection agents inspect phones, laptops and other digital storage without a . The practice drew repeated court challenges after agents began copying entire device contents during secondary inspections at ports of entry and airports.

On September 18 a federal appeals court ruled that such searches require no warrant, no probable cause and no reasonable suspicion. The decision came in a case brought by a traveler whose phone was examined at the border; he was later convicted of bank fraud and money laundering. The court held that the government’s interest in controlling what crosses the border outweighs the privacy intrusion at the point of entry.

The ruling now sets binding precedent in its circuit for device searches at all ports of entry. Customs and Border Protection said the decision matches long-standing operational authority used to examine goods, documents and now digital media. Further is expected in other circuits where similar suits remain pending.
