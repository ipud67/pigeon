40,000 liters of sulfuric acid leaked at Örtofta sugar factory

Translated from Swedish · read the original →
The original headline40 000 liter svavelsyra läckte ut på Örtofta sockerbruk

Sulfuric acid has been used for decades at the Örtofta sugar factory in Eslöv to clean equipment and adjust the pH level during beet processing. The factory stores the acid in large tanks on site. Staff check the tanks at the start of each shift.

A tank began leaking during the night of September 18. Workers found the spill before dawn and shut off the flow. The leak released 40,000 liters of the acid onto the factory grounds.

Crews contained the spill inside the plant perimeter. No acid reached nearby streams or public roads. Local emergency services and environmental inspectors arrived at first light to measure the ground and decide on cleanup steps.

Thinking ahead
Staff at the Örtofta sugar factory in Eslöv found the leak of 40,000 liters of sulfuric acid from a tank during the night shift leading into Friday. The factory sits inside the municipality and handles large volumes of the acid for sugar processing. Local emergency crews and environmental inspectors arrived on site to contain the spill and test nearby water and soil. Cleanup crews have started pumping and neutralizing the acid. Swedish environmental authorities will measure how far it spread into drains or ground. The factory must file a report on tank maintenance and alarm systems before it can restart full operations. The situation stays active until the full volume is accounted for and test results come back. If soil or water samples show heavy contamination, the site enters a longer remediation process with outside contractors. If tests clear the area fast, operations resume inside weeks. Next step turns on the lab results from the ground and runoff. Horizon: 4 to 8 weeks.
