Justice Alito’s financial disclosure for 2025 is released

Justice Samuel Alito filed his 2025 financial disclosure on August 11. The Office of the U.S. Courts released it this week. He was the last of the nine justices to file; the other eight met the May 15 deadline and had their forms published in late June. Alito received a 90-day extension.

The form lists $33,333 in income from Hachette Book Group for his book So Ordered. It reports three paid speaking trips: Scottsdale, Arizona, in January for the Sandra Day O’Connor Prize dinner; Rome in September for the EU-US Economic Affairs Forum, funded by the Mentor Group; and London and Oxford in October for a rule-of-law forum hosted by the Association of Marshall Scholars and the American College of Trial Lawyers.

Alito continued to hold individual stocks in chemical and energy companies including 3M, Dow, ConocoPhillips, and Phillips 66. He and Chief Justice John Roberts are the only justices who own stocks in individual companies.

Constitutional analysis
Justice Samuel Alito filed his 2025 financial disclosure on August 11 after a 90-day extension, the last of the nine justices to do so. The form showed $33,333 in book income from Hachette’s Basic Liberty imprint for his forthcoming originalist work on the Constitution, the Court, and the country. He reported three funded trips for speaking events: one to Arizona in January for the Sandra Day O’Connor Prize dinner, one to Rome in September for an EU-US economic forum paid by the Mentor Group, and one to London and Oxford in October for a rule-of-law program covered by the Association of Marshall Scholars and the American College of Trial Lawyers. Alito kept individual stock holdings in chemical firms such as 3M, Abbott Laboratories, and Dow, and in energy companies including ConocoPhillips, Phillips 66, and OGE Energy. The disclosures exist to flag possible conflicts so justices can comply with ethical standards set by statute and by the Court’s own rules. The Constitution vests the judicial power in one supreme Court whose members hold office during good Behaviour, Article III, Section 1. The framers designed an independent judiciary free from the salary and emoluments games that corrupted English courts, as Hamilton explained in Federalist 78 and 79. Early statutes such as the Judiciary Act of 1789 required circuit riding and public service while guarding against personal influence through fixed compensation that Congress could not diminish. Travel for speaking on the rule of law and receipt of book royalties fall within the tradition of justices explaining the Constitution to the public, much as Washington and the first Congress expected officers to model republican virtue without becoming wards of the state. Stock ownership traces back to the founders’ acceptance of private property and commerce as the ordinary condition of free men, subject only to recusal statutes Congress passed under its necessary-and-proper authority. The right to life, the first unalienable right named in the Declaration, is not at stake in the filing itself; structural limits on judicial power and the consent of the governed through elected branches remain the proper check on any perceived conflict.
