18:T8b0,Justice Samuel Alito filed his 2025 financial disclosure on August 11 after a 90-day extension, the last of the nine justices to do so. The form showed $33,333 in book income from Hachette’s Basic Liberty imprint for his forthcoming originalist work on the Constitution, the Court, and the country. He reported three funded trips for speaking events: one to Arizona in January for the Sandra Day O’Connor Prize dinner, one to Rome in September for an EU-US economic forum paid by the Mentor Group, and one to London and Oxford in October for a rule-of-law program covered by the Association of Marshall Scholars and the American College of Trial Lawyers. Alito kept individual stock holdings in chemical firms such as 3M, Abbott Laboratories, and Dow, and in energy companies including ConocoPhillips, Phillips 66, and OGE Energy. The disclosures exist to flag possible conflicts so justices can comply with ethical standards set by statute and by the Court’s own rules.

The Constitution vests the judicial power in one supreme Court whose members hold office during good Behaviour, Article III, Section 1. The framers designed an independent judiciary free from the salary and emoluments games that corrupted English courts, as Hamilton explained in Federalist 78 and 79. Early statutes such as the Judiciary Act of 1789 required circuit riding and public service while guarding against personal influence through fixed compensation that Congress could not diminish. Travel for speaking on the rule of law and receipt of book royalties fall within the tradition of justices explaining the Constitution to the public, much as Washington and the first Congress expected officers to model republican virtue without becoming wards of the state. Stock ownership traces back to the founders’ acceptance of private property and commerce as the ordinary condition of free men, subject only to recusal statutes Congress passed under its necessary-and-proper authority. 1b:T490,Alito filed his 2025 financial disclosure on August 11 after getting a 90-day extension that put him last among the nine justices. The form lists three paid speaking trips, including one to Rome in September 2025 funded by the Mentor Group, plus $33,333 in book income from Hachette for a title set to drop October 6. It also shows continued holdings in chemical and energy stocks that have drawn calls for him to step aside from climate-related cases such as Suncor Energy v. County Commissioners of Boulder County, scheduled for argument the day before the book comes out.

The pattern of staggered filings with extensions has held for years, so this release closes the current cycle without new procedural deadlines. Watchdog pressure on the stock ownership has been steady but produced no recusal so far. The book launch and the Suncor argument sit one day apart next month; any shift would require either a formal recusal motion granted by the court or a change in the stock portfolio before then.

This runs until the next annual filings drop in mid-2026. Next rung comes at oral argument on October 5 or at the book release on October 6.
