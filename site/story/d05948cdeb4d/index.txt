22:T4a6,The latest rung in the Hormuz control fight is the indefinite postponement of Monday's planned meeting in Salalah. Omani Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi cited the need for consensus after Gulf states submitted amendments over language that could lock in a new status quo on fees and routes. The Omani framework under discussion allowed for voluntary payments tied to navigational safety and environmental protection rather than outright Iranian tolls.

This delay adds at least several weeks and probably months to the current pattern of depressed tanker traffic and US military escorts. Without agreement on maps and a legal setup acceptable to Saudi Arabia and the other GCC states, the talks have no near-term path forward. The US position that it prefers the existing control and wants any future Iran talks focused on the nuclear file removes the one outside player whose buy-in the regional actors say is required for a deal.

The situation stays frozen on the water until either Iran accepts modified voluntary contribution terms or the Gulf states and Oman secure explicit US support for a framework. No such signals have appeared.
