17:T472,Legal cannabis has been taxed in California since voters passed Proposition 64 in 2016. The state has now collected just under $8.4 billion in tax revenue from those sales, a figure Governor Newsom released on September 4, 2026. That total comes from excise taxes, sales taxes, and cultivation fees tracked by the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration.

The revenue figure is the latest data point on a program that has run for nearly ten years. State lawmakers set the tax rates and licensing rules, and the governor’s office releases periodic updates. No new ballot measure or legislative vote is scheduled right now.

The program continues as long as lawmakers keep the current tax structure and the Department of Cannabis Control keeps issuing licenses. It would slow or stop only if legislators cut the tax rates, voters repeal parts of Proposition 64, or federal law changes enforcement enough to alter supply. Those steps have not been taken.

The revenue total will keep rising at roughly the current pace until a clear policy shift occurs.
