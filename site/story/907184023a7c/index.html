🔴 Russian election enters final day after major Ukrainian drone attack on Moscow

Russian presidential elections run over three days this weekend with early voting already under way in most regions since Friday morning. The process uses both in-person polling stations and an online system in select areas that lets voters cast ballots from home computers. Turnout figures released Saturday showed steady participation across time zones from the Far East to European Russia.

A wave of Ukrainian drones reached Moscow early Sunday and struck several buildings in the city center. Local authorities reported fires on upper floors of two high-rise structures and said air defenses downed the rest of the incoming aircraft before impact. No deaths were announced from the strikes.

Voting continues through the end of the day with final tallies expected late Sunday night. The drone attack added physical disruption to the capital on the last hours of balloting while officials kept polling stations open under heightened security.

Thinking ahead
Russian presidential election voting wraps up its third day Sunday after Ukrainian forces sent a wave of drones into Moscow overnight. Air defenses downed most of the drones before they reached the city center, though debris from intercepts damaged several apartment buildings and one person was treated for minor injuries. The attack marks the latest in a string of long-range strikes on Russian territory that have grown more frequent since mid-2024 as Ukrainian units received longer-range systems and loosened Western restrictions on their use. Ballot counting begins immediately after polls close at 8 p.m. local time across the country’s time zones. Turnout figures released so far by regional election commissions show steady participation in most districts, though independent monitors report restricted access in some areas. No official results will be announced until after all precincts report. The pattern of drone strikes has followed improvements in Ukrainian supply lines and targeting data rather than the election calendar. Further attacks of similar scale depend on how quickly Ukrainian crews can replenish munitions stocks and whether Russian air defenses shift more systems to cover the capital. If Ukrainian forces maintain current sortie rates, Moscow can expect repeated attempts in the coming weeks. Next phase runs another 10 to 14 days before any measurable change in strike tempo or election fallout appears.
