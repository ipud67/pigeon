1a:T512,The Pentagon inspector general put the price tag for Operation Epic Fury at $33.4 billion through June 30, drawn from training and maintenance accounts because Congress has not passed dedicated funding. A CBO estimate released this week put the cost at $38.1 billion through August 1 and running $2-3 billion a month. The White House sent a $67.1 billion supplemental request on June 24 that sits with Congress, which has taken no action on it.

This pattern of reprogramming base-budget money has already led to deferred maintenance, delayed depot repairs, and slower weapons modernization. The inspector general report marks the first formal accounting of those shifts. The next procedural step is congressional action on the supplemental or on reprogramming requests already submitted by the services.

The fight stays centered on control of the Strait of Hormuz, with the same weekly strike-and-counterstrike rhythm that has held since late February. No new cease-fire talks or blockade changes have appeared.

The funding standoff continues at least until Congress passes a full-year appropriations package or the supplemental. Without one or the other, the services keep pulling from readiness accounts.
