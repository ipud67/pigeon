The case rests on the 1792 and 1795 Militia Acts and the 1789 Tariff Act’s early postal rules, which treated the mail as an instrument of ordered liberty, not a cloak for intimidation. Investigators matched a latent fingerprint on one envelope to Charles and found his name and return address on others.

This is the latest rung on a ladder that already includes Charles pleading guilty in 2018 in New Mexico to five counts of the same offense, serving 542 days in prison, then violating supervised release multiple times.

The case now sits at the initial-appearance stage in federal court. Next observable steps are a detention hearing, arraignment, and either a plea or trial date. The situation changes only if prosecutors drop the charge, a judge rules the evidence inadmissible, or Charles is convicted and sentenced. No indicators yet point to either outcome.

The arrest itself is likely to close this specific mailing campaign. Without new letters or new targets identified, the visible threat line ends here.
