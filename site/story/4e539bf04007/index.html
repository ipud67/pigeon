Justice Ministry eyes budget boost for policies on foreign nationals

Overview
The ministry is set to request a total of some ¥985.6 billion for the year from next April, up 15.7% from the fiscal 2026 initial budget level.
Short history
The Justice Ministry began handling immigration control and residency policies for foreign nationals under the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act of 1951. In 2009 the ministry took over alien registration from local governments and started issuing residence cards that track status, addresses, and employment. By 2019 it created a dedicated Immigration Services Agency to manage entry screening, , and integration programs for growing numbers of foreign workers. On August 26 2026 the ministry said it will ask for ¥985.6 billion in the fiscal 2027 budget, a 15.7 percent rise from the initial fiscal 2026 figure, to expand those operations.
Predictive model
The Justice Ministry is asking for ¥985.6 billion in next year’s budget, a 15.7% jump from the fiscal 2026 starting level, to cover policies on foreign nationals. This sits inside the normal annual budget request season that runs every summer ahead of the December cabinet decision. No court rulings, legislative votes, or sudden spikes in foreign-national incidents are listed as triggers for this specific increase. The request follows years of steady growth in foreign residents and workers in Japan, but the exact size of the jump this time is larger than recent averages. Nothing in the public indicators yet shows whether other ministries will push back during the screening process or whether the final approved number will match the ask. The step-up is likely to clear the first review hurdles and appear in the draft budget by year-end. From there it moves to Diet debate in early 2027. If past patterns hold, the final foreign-national policy pot lands within 5-10% of the request. Horizon: Through at least March 2027 budget passage; next clear marker is the December 2026 cabinet outline.
