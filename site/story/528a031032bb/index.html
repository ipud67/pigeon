NORTHEAST US
September 27, 2026 · 03:33 UTC

A nor'easter brought flooding, power cuts and flight cancellations to the northeast US on Thursday

Nor'easters are cyclones that form off the east coast of the United States in the western North Atlantic and bring heavy rain or snow plus strong winds. They occur in any season but hit most often from fall through early spring. One such storm reached the Northeast on September 27 2026 and struck at the same time as high tide along the coast.

The National Weather Service issued a forecast that warned of major coastal flooding over multiple high-tide cycles through the weekend. New York Governor Kathy Hochul and New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani told residents to stay away from the water and avoid travel. Poweroutage.com reported 95,000 customers without electricity across New York, New Jersey, Connecticut, Massachusetts, Rhode Island and Pennsylvania by 3 p.m. Saturday. FlightAware recorded 439 flight cancellations and 2,459 delays at US airports, with 253 cancellations at Boston's Logan alone. JetBlue, United and American issued travel alerts that let passengers change flights without extra fees. The Global Citizen Festival in Central Park, the All Things Go Festival in New York, the Oceans Calling Festival in Maryland and Ed Sheeran's weekend shows near Boston were all called off.

The storm left coastal roads underwater, cut power to tens of thousands of homes and businesses, and forced the cancellation of large public events. Authorities continued to track the high-tide cycles and the extent of inland flooding as the system moved through the region.

Thinking ahead
A nor'easter is a large cyclone that forms off the US east coast and pulls in northeasterly winds, heavy rain, and strong gusts. This one hit the Northeast on September 27 with flooding at high tide, winds that knocked out power to 95,000 customers across six states, and enough rain to equal a full month's average in parts of Massachusetts. Airlines canceled 439 flights and delayed another 2,459 at US airports that day, while three music festivals in New York and Maryland plus Ed Sheeran's Boston-area shows were scrubbed. The storm system is moving northeast along the coast. Rain and wind typically ease within 24 to 48 hours once the center passes, but coastal flooding can linger through two or three more high-tide cycles. Power crews restore lines block by block once winds drop below safety thresholds; full service usually returns in one to four days depending on tree damage and road access. The next visible change will come when the National Weather Service drops its coastal flood warnings and when flight cancellations fall below 100 per day. This nor'easter is likely to wind down over the next two days as the low-pressure center pulls away. Horizon: clear skies and open runways by Tuesday.
