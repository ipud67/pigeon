YEMEN
September 18, 2026 · 17:17 UTC

At least 112,000 people displaced in Yemen as Houthi fighting intensifies

Yemen's civil war has run since 2014 between the Saudi-backed government and the Houthi movement that seized the capital Sanaa. The Houthis joined the regional fighting in 2023 by firing missiles at Red Sea shipping and at Israel, which drew US and UK airstrikes on their positions through early 2025. Those exchanges died down after the US-Iran in June 2026 but never fully stopped.

On 18 September 2026 the UN migration agency reported that Houthi clashes with local rivals in at least three northern provinces had displaced another 112000 people since early August. The new wave of ground fighting came after Houthi forces moved to secure supply routes near the Saudi border that also feed their Red Sea operations.

The latest displacements leave more than four million Yemenis uprooted inside the country while the US-Iran conflict still centers on the Strait of Hormuz. Houthi commanders have kept missile crews active along the coast, and the fresh internal fighting has cut roads used by aid groups.

Thinking ahead
Houthi forces stepped up ground attacks and cross-border strikes in northern Yemen this week. That pushed another 112000 people from their homes according to UN displacement trackers on 18 September. The latest wave fits the pattern that started after the July collapse of the US-Iran memorandum when Houthi missile teams resumed regular launches toward Red Sea shipping and Saudi border posts. The group still holds the capital Sanaa and most of the northwest highlands. Yemeni government troops backed by Saudi money hold the south and parts of the east. Each new round of Houthi advances empties villages along the front lines faster than aid groups can register the numbers. No ceasefire talks are active. The next likely step is further Houthi pressure on Marib and the Saudi border to stretch government supply lines while US naval forces stay focused on the Strait of Hormuz. Displacement will keep rising until one side loses ground or outside sponsors cut support. This phase will run at least through the end of the year unless a major ground reversal or renewed big-power talks lock in a halt.
