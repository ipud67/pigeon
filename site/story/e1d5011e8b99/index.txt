17:T575,The Supreme Court agreed to hear Missouri’s congressional redistricting case for the third time after Justice Brett Kavanaugh granted an emergency appeal on the 2025 map. Article I, Section 4 of the Constitution gives state legislatures primary authority to prescribe the times, places, and manner of holding elections for senators and representatives. Congress may alter those regulations by law, but the clause assigns the initial power to the state legislature elected by the people of Missouri. The framers placed this authority in the state legislatures to keep election rules close to the consent of the governed, as James Madison explained in Federalist 43 that the state legislatures would guard against federal overreach in the electoral process. Missouri’s voters approved a 2018 ballot measure that created an independent redistricting commission, yet the state legislature later passed a new map and the governor signed it. The case now tests whether a state constitution can override the legislature’s direct constitutional role under the Elections Clause or whether that clause limits such transfers of power. A ruling on the scope of the Elections Clause would settle the boundary between state legislative authority and state constitutional limits.
