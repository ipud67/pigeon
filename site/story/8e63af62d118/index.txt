21:T5b4,The Justice Department filed a complaint in federal court on October 1 against Maryland, its attorney general, the state police training commission, Prince George’s County, and Montgomery County. The suit targets state statutes and county codes that bar federal officers from wearing masks and require specific forms of identification during operations. This is the latest in a string of similar federal actions against mask and ID rules in New York, Virginia, Connecticut, New Jersey, California, Philadelphia, and Milwaukee, where courts that have ruled granted preliminary relief to the government each time.

The case sits at the motion-to-dismiss stage. Federal judges have sided with the government on Supremacy Clause grounds in the prior suits, blocking enforcement of the local rules. Maryland and the counties will file responses, likely arguing their laws protect public trust and officer accountability without directly regulating federal functions.

The dispute will continue at least through briefing and a district court decision, which typically lands within six to nine months on these fast-track constitutional claims. It moves next to arguments over whether the state measures stand or get enjoined, then possibly to the Fourth Circuit on appeal. The situation changes only if the government loses at trial level and that loss holds on appeal, or if Maryland repeals the provisions outright.
