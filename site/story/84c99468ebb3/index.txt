The state high court ruled Thursday that the 2022 map remains in force because a voter petition with over 300,000 signatures qualifies for a 2026 referendum under the state constitution. Hoskins filed under the federal elections clause, arguing the legislature alone sets the rules for congressional districts and that switching maps now would disrupt election officials and voters who already picked candidates in the August primary.

The case sits with Justice Kavanaugh, who ordered a response from the petition challenger by noon Monday. That short deadline points to an administrative stay decision within days. The Missouri Supreme Court already rejected claims of chaos as self-created by the delay in certifying the petition.

The fight stays inside state and federal court filings until the justices act. No change in map use occurs until the Supreme Court rules on the stay or the full appeal. If the justices deny the stay the 2022 map locks in for November; if they grant it the 2025 map stays in place while the case continues.
