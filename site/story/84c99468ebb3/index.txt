18:T5ed,Missouri’s fight over its congressional map puts the Elections Clause of the U.S. Constitution at stake. Article I, Section 4, Clause 1 gives state legislatures the power to prescribe the times, places, and manner of federal elections. The framers put that authority in the hands of the people’s elected representatives, not in petitions signed by a fraction of voters. The Federalist No. 59 warned that leaving election rules to scattered local bodies would invite chaos and undermine the national government the Constitution created. Missouri Secretary of State Denny Hoskins told the U.S. Supreme Court that the state supreme court’s order forces use of the 2022 map and blocks the 2025 map passed by the legislature, creating exactly the kind of last-minute election disorder the framers sought to prevent. The Missouri Supreme Court ruled that the state constitution lets voters challenge “any act of the general assembly,” including congressional redistricting, and that the 2022 map remains the only one in effect until the referendum. Hoskins countered that this reading hands a small group of petitioners the power to suspend a map the legislature adopted, something no court has done before. The case now sits with Justice Kavanaugh for emergency review, with a response due Monday. The founders designed legislatures to draw election lines so that representatives chosen by the people would control the rules under which they stand for office. 1b:T5aa,Missouri Secretary of State Denny Hoskins asked the US Supreme Court late Friday to pause a Missouri Supreme Court order that blocks the 2025 congressional map passed by the legislature. The state high court ruled Thursday that the 2022 map remains in force because a voter petition with over 300,000 signatures qualifies for a 2026 referendum under the state constitution. Hoskins filed under the federal elections clause, arguing the legislature alone sets the rules for congressional districts and that switching maps now would disrupt election officials and voters who already picked candidates in the August primary.

The case sits with Justice Kavanaugh, who ordered a response from the petition challenger by noon Monday. That short deadline points to an administrative stay decision within days. The Missouri Supreme Court already rejected claims of chaos as self-created by the delay in certifying the petition.

The fight stays inside state and federal court filings until the justices act. No change in map use occurs until the Supreme Court rules on the stay or the full appeal. If the justices deny the stay the 2022 map locks in for November; if they grant it the 2025 map stays in place while the case continues.

This runs until the Supreme Court issues a clear order on the stay. Next step is either an administrative pause or denial, followed by full briefing if the court takes the elections-clause question.
