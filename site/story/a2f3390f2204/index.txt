More than 1,000 troops finished their tour this summer and a new rotation has not yet arrived. Lithuania’s foreign minister said Thursday in Washington that Secretary of State Marco Rubio told him the gap will close in days or weeks, with decisions now moving through the Pentagon.

The minister’s statement is the latest public signal that the review of US force posture in Europe has not produced a permanent cut in the Baltic state. Lithuania has prepared facilities for up to 1,500 American personnel and continues to host the remaining US troops from the last rotation.

How long the gap lasts now depends on when the Pentagon finishes its coordination and issues the deployment order. The next observable step is that announcement. If no order comes by late October the assurance will be tested; until then the timeline the minister repeated sets the working expectation.

This episode will likely end within weeks once the next rotation arrives. The posture then returns to the pattern in place since 2022 unless the broader European review produces a new decision.
