Donald Trump hosts Xi Jinping for state dinner with tech titans.

Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping first met as leaders in 2017, when trade disputes between the United States and China had already begun to escalate over tariffs, technology transfers and market access. Those tensions carried forward through subsequent summits, a global pandemic, and renewed friction over Taiwan, artificial intelligence development, and supply chains for semiconductors and rare earth minerals. By 2025 the two governments had settled into a pattern of high-level talks that produced no major agreements but kept direct communication open.

On September 24, 2026, after a closed-door meeting at the White House that covered the Middle East, Ukraine and the Korean Peninsula, Trump and Xi sat down for a state dinner in Washington. The guest list placed the presidents and their spouses alongside American technology executives including Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, Amazon founder Jeff Bezos, Tesla CEO Elon Musk, Google CEO Sundar Pichai, OpenAI President Greg Brockman, Dell CEO Michael Dell and others. Trump called the gathering the hottest ticket in town; Xi toasted the idea that the two nations’ separate ambitions could reinforce each other.

The dinner ended the first full day of Xi’s visit, the first state trip to Washington in more than a decade. Protests took place in Lafayette Park and outside the Chinese embassy, while counter-rallies supported the Chinese leader. Xi is scheduled to tour the National Archives on September 25, where the of , Constitution and Bill of Rights are displayed. The two governments have released no joint statement on new trade or technology measures.

Trump hosted Xi Jinping at a White House state dinner in Washington on September 24 after their summit. The two leaders toasted each other with tech executives from Meta, OpenAI, Amazon, Tesla, Google and Dell in attendance. Beijing listed the talks as covering the Middle East, Ukraine and the Korean Peninsula while the US released no details. The pattern of high-level meetings between the two has run since Trump's first term, with a similar tech lineup at the 2017 Mar-a-Lago summit and the 2019 Osaka G20. No trade deal or joint statement came out of this round, matching the 2018-2019 pattern where summits produced pauses but not resolutions on tariffs or technology limits. The next visible step is Xi's Friday visit to the National Archives followed by his return to Beijing. Further movement would require one side to table concrete steps on Taiwan patrols, export controls or investment screening before the next working-level contact. Without those the cycle of talks and dinners repeats on the same issues. This round of direct leader contact is likely to run another 12 to 18 months before either side shifts its public posture on the core files.
