23:T51f,The US State Department stated on September 28 that American law bars arms sales to China and that no such offer or plan exists. This followed remarks by Ambassador David Perdue on Fox News that President Trump had asked President Xi Jinping during last week's White House visit whether China wanted to buy US weapons, in reply to Beijing's complaints about sales to Taiwan. The White House and State Department both confirmed no sales pipeline to China.

Perdue's account fits the pattern set by earlier Trump-Xi meetings this year, including Trump's May trip to Beijing and the December approval of an $11.1 billion arms package to Taiwan that Perdue called the largest since 1979. The May pause on a separate $14 billion Taiwan purchase, described by Trump as a negotiating chip, showed the same linkage between Taiwan arms and broader US-China talks.

The situation stays frozen until Congress or the executive branch changes the law or policy that currently blocks sales to China. Next visible step would be any formal Chinese response beyond the foreign ministry's general statement on self-reliant defense, or a new US notification to Congress on Taiwan packages that Beijing then protests.

This holds steady for the next several months of scheduled Trump-Xi meetings.
