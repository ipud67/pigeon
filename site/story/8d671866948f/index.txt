17:T441,The presidential commission on a U.S. Space Academy held its first meeting at NASA headquarters in Washington with Secretary of War Pete Hegseth in attendance. This marks the start of formal work after the commission's creation by executive order earlier this year. Next steps include regular meetings to gather input from Pentagon branches, NASA, and industry on academy structure, curriculum, and location.

The process runs on a fixed government timeline. Commission members must deliver recommendations to the president within six months of the first meeting. That clock now runs from September 10. If the report lands on schedule, Congress would take it up in early 2027 budget talks. Any delay in the report would push hearings into the following session.

The commission holds closed sessions until it releases its findings. Public signs of progress will show up in meeting minutes posted to the Federal Register and in follow-on testimony before House and Senate defense panels.
