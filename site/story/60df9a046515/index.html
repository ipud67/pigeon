Fee for Certain H-1B Petitions

The United States has run the H-1B visa program since 1990 to let U.S. companies hire university-educated foreign workers for jobs that need specialized knowledge. Employers file a petition with U.S. and Immigration Services before they can bring in a worker or extend an existing one. The agency sets fees for each type of petition and has raised them several times over the years to cover processing costs.

On September 10 2026 the Department of Homeland Security published a proposed rule that would add a new fee on certain H-1B petitions. The rule would require employers to pay the extra amount when they file for workers already in the country or for extensions beyond the initial three-year period. The proposal lists the exact dollar figure and the petition categories it would reach and gives the public 60 days to submit comments before any change takes effect.

The proposed fee sits inside the existing petition process and would increase the cost for those specific filings. The situation now turns on the comment period and the final language the department adopts after it reviews what employers universities and trade groups send in.

Thinking ahead
The Homeland Security Department put out a proposed rule on September 10 2026 that sets a new fee for certain H-1B petitions. This is the latest step in a string of adjustments to visa processing costs that agencies have rolled out since the early 2000s. The rule now sits in the standard notice-and-comment period where companies, lawyers, and trade groups file written feedback. How long it runs depends on how many comments come in and whether the department decides to extend the window. After comments close the agency reviews them, makes any changes it chooses, then publishes a final version. The fee would take effect 30 to 60 days after that final rule appears unless a court blocks it or Congress passes legislation that stops the change. Next move is the end of the comment period followed by the final rule. If the volume of pushback stays low the final rule drops in early 2027. If lawsuits hit right after the final rule the fee could get tied up in district court for months or years. Horizon: final rule expected first half of 2027 unless comment volume forces a delay.
