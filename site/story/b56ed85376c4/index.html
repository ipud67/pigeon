VANUATU
September 15, 2026 · 22:03 UTC

Vanuatu PM praises China's 'generous' NZ$155,000 donation for dual-disaster recovery

Vanuatu has faced two major emergencies in quick succession. A volcano on the island of Ambae began renewed activity early in 2026 and produced a large eruption at the end of August that displaced communities and cut off clean water. Days later a ferry named MV Matui suffered a serious accident that killed at least two people and left dozens missing. The disasters stretched local response capacity and kept Prime Minister Jotham Napat from traveling to the United Nations General Assembly.

On 15 September 2026 the Chinese government transferred the equivalent of NZ$155,000 to the Vanuatu government. Beijing’s senior diplomat in the country, Li Minggang, handed the funds directly to Napat in Port Vila. The money is earmarked to buy a drilling rig for new wells on Ambae, to purchase relief supplies for the same communities, and to cover immediate costs tied to the ferry incident.

The transfer adds external resources to Vanuatu’s ongoing recovery operations. Napat’s office stated that coordination with national agencies on the ground continues and that the government intends to work with all available partners to address the needs created by the twin events.

Thinking ahead
The Vanuatu government took delivery of the Chinese cash grant on 15 September for water wells on Ambae, relief goods there, and immediate help after the MV Matui sinking that left two confirmed dead. Prime Minister Napat publicly called the money a sign of true friendship and asked the Chinese ambassador to relay thanks to President Xi. The grant slots into an existing pattern of Beijing stepping in after Pacific island disasters with earmarked project money rather than loose cash. The money is already assigned to specific purchases and field operations, so spending will start inside weeks once procurement clears. Napat’s office says it is coordinating with line agencies on the ground, which points to the next visible step being arrival of the drilling rig and first relief shipments on Ambae plus updated tallies from the ferry search. Further grants or joint projects would require another formal handover and public statement from either side. This round of aid runs until the listed items are bought and delivered. It ends when the drilling machine is on site, the relief kits are distributed, and the ferry response account closes. No new disaster or fresh request would keep the current grant alive beyond those concrete deliverables. Horizon: 4–8 weeks until equipment lands and first results are reported.
