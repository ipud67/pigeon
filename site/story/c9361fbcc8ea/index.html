Jamaica to hand slavery reparations petition to King Charles

Jamaica has remained a constitutional with the British sovereign as its head of state since in 1962. The country’s highest court of appeal is still the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council in London, a link that dates back to colonial rule. Slavery operated on the island under English law from the 17th century until the British Parliament abolished it across most of the empire in 1833. At that time the government paid £20 million in to slave owners; the loans taken to raise that sum were repaid by British taxpayers in 2015.

On 8 September 2026 the Jamaican government will hand King Charles a petition that asks him to refer three specific questions to the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council. The questions are whether the enslavement of Africans in Jamaica was legal under English law at the time, whether it broke international law, and whether Britain now holds a legal duty to provide redress. The petition itself makes no demand for payment. The King has no personal power to decide whether the referral happens; Buckingham Palace has already coordinated with Jamaica’s Governor General to lodge the document correctly.

The referral, if it occurs, will produce answers that the Jamaican government says will shape its next steps. Culture Minister Olivia Grange is also scheduled to meet officials at the British Museum to discuss the return of artefacts removed from Jamaica in colonial times. The move sits inside a long-running dispute over the legacy of slavery that has seen no formal British reparations since David Cameron ruled them out during a 2015 visit to the island.
