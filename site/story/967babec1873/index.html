NHRC, Accountability Lab push for coordinated framework to tackle digital ecosystem complaints

The National Human Rights Commission holds a mandate under the 1999 Constitution of Nigeria and the NHRC Act as to receive complaints of rights violations, investigate them, secure remedies for victims, review laws and policies against human rights standards, and work with other government bodies and nongovernmental groups. Those rights to dignity, privacy, expression, assembly and association have applied to online conduct since digital platforms reached Nigerian users more than two decades ago. The commission has long operated alongside civil society groups that document cases, journalists who report patterns, and lawyers who file suits, yet the pathways between them stayed loose.

On September 16 2026 the NHRC and Lab a stakeholders meeting in Abuja where the commission’s executive secretary Tony Ojukwu laid out the gaps that still block swift coordinated action. Referral routes remain unclear, no standard rules govern preservation of digital evidence, and major cases often drag through procedural bottlenecks. He proposed a standing framework that would link law enforcement, courts, regulators, civil society, technology companies and telecom operators into one operational network anchored by the NHRC.

The meeting ended with participants agreeing to shape that network before the 2027 election cycle. The next concrete step turns on whether the commission can translate the existing Cybercrimes Amendment Act of 2024 and its revised Section 24 into joint procedures that keep state powers inside constitutional limits on speech, information access and civic activity.
