18:T5b1,President Trump issued a proclamation on September 8, 2026, adjusting the list of Canadian goods hit with extra tariffs first set in Proclamation 11048. The original action rested on section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930, which lets the president raise duties when a foreign country discriminates against American commerce in a way not applied equally to others. The founders gave Congress power over tariffs in Article I, Section 8, Clause 1 so the national government could protect domestic industry and bargaining position with other nations, as seen in the Tariff Act of 1789 passed by the First Congress. Washington and the early administrations used such tools to secure fair treatment for American ships and goods without yielding sovereign control of trade policy. The new order keeps the duties in place to counter the Canadian motor-vehicle tariff scheme but narrows their reach after executive officials reported the change would still offset the burden while serving the public interest. This follows the pattern of the Jay Treaty of 1794 and early reciprocity statutes, where presidents and Congress adjusted duties in response to foreign barriers rather than granting permanent advantages. The adjustment shows the executive enforcing the original finding of discrimination while retaining flexibility under the statute. No change was made to the core legal basis or the fact that Canada resumed the unequal treatment after a brief
