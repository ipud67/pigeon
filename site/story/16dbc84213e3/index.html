LAHORE
September 20, 2026 · 19:04 UTC

Pakistani police arrested Imran Khan's three sisters in Punjab and sent them to a jail in Lahore on Monday

Pakistan's former prime minister Imran Khan has sat in prison since his 2023 conviction for failing to declare income from gifts received in office. He has since collected sentences in more than 100 cases that range from corruption to leaking state secrets. His party, Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf, continues to organize country-wide demonstrations demanding his release. The next round of marches is set for 27 September, with participants planning to converge on the capital, Islamabad.

On 20 September authorities in Lahore arrested Khan's sister Aleema Khanum. The deputy commissioner signed an order that accused her of using social media and public meetings to rally Khan's supporters for the coming protests. The order directed that she be held for 30 days at Kot Lakhpat jail in the same city. Police confirmed the detention to reporters. Her son reported losing all contact with her that afternoon and demanded the government reveal her location and return her.

The arrest leaves the PTI without one of its most visible organizers four days before the planned marches. Shipping containers already block streets around government buildings in Islamabad. The interior minister has stated that the protests damage the country and that the government will take all steps to prevent them.

Thinking ahead
Aleema Khanum, sister of jailed former prime minister Imran Khan, was arrested in Lahore on Sunday and ordered held for 30 days at Kot Lakhpat jail. The deputy commissioner’s order cites her use of social media and public meetings to rally PTI supporters ahead of nationwide marches set for 27 September that plan to converge on Islamabad. Police have already placed shipping containers around government buildings in the capital, a step used in past protests to block access. Khan has been in custody since 2023 on multiple convictions including a three-year sentence for undeclared gifts and a 14-year term in a land case, with PTI calling each one politically driven. Her son has demanded her immediate release on X, and the party labeled the detention illegal. This pattern of pre-protest detentions has run for three years each time Khan or his circle call for street action. The 30-day hold keeps her off the street through the 27 September march and its immediate aftermath. Next visible rung is either her release after the protest window closes or fresh charges if the marches turn violent. The only clear off-ramp is a court order quashing the detention or a government decision to drop the preventive hold. The detention is likely to run the full 30 days barring a court reversal.
