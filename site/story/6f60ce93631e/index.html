US appeals court rejects Trump policy allowing swift to third countries

The Immigration and Nationality Act gives the executive branch power to deport noncitizens who lack legal status. Since the 1990s the government has used that authority to return people directly to the country they came from or, in limited cases, to a third country that agrees to accept them. Presidents have issued detailed rules spelling out when and how those transfers can happen without full hearings.

On September 18 2026 a three-judge panel of the US Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit ruled that a Trump administration expanding third-country removals violated the act’s procedural requirements. The panel held that the rule allowed swift deportation to nations with no prior connection to the migrant and without adequate opportunity to show harm there.

The decision blocks the expanded policy while litigation continues in district court. The government can still deport people under the narrower rules that existed before the regulation. The case now turns on whether the administration can rewrite the rule to satisfy the or whether Congress must change the underlying law.
