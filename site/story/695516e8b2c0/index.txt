17:T537,Israel has opposed the Durban process since the 2001 conference in South Africa drew walkouts from the US and others over language equating Zionism with racism. The pattern held at follow-on reviews in 2009 and the 2021 twentieth-anniversary session, when 36 countries plus Israel stayed away. Danny Danon’s call for a boycott of the September 28 high-level meeting in New York fits that same track: Israel labels the declaration’s legacy toxic and asks states that share that view to skip the event.

The procedural next step is the General Assembly’s scheduled six-hour session on the 28th with plenary, panel, and debate. Turnout will be the clearest indicator. If the number of participating countries drops from the 2021 level or matches the earlier boycotts, the meeting proceeds with reduced weight. If major Western states join the no-show list again, the Durban framework stays on the margins inside the UN calendar.

Barring a sharp shift in voting patterns at the General Assembly or a public reversal by key capitals, the cycle of Israeli-led boycott appeals and partial non-participation repeats at the next milestone. No mechanism now on the table alters the underlying text or removes the item from the agenda.
