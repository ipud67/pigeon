This new FAA airworthiness directive for certain Pilatus PC-12/47E airplanes directly serves that purpose: it orders a fix to passenger service unit trim panels so the emergency exit door can be opened from inside, addressing a reported failure that could trap people during a crash or fire.

The Constitution grants Congress power under Article I, Section 8 to regulate commerce among the states and with foreign nations, which the founders understood to include uniform rules for safe interstate and international air travel. The first Congress quickly used similar authority in the Tariff Act of 1789 and the Militia Acts of 1792 to protect public safety through concrete, targeted measures rather than vague grants of power. Here the FAA acts on a specific unsafe condition reported on these Swiss-made airplanes, requiring modification if dual lock fastener tapes are missing and banning installation of affected parts.

This is a classic use of enumerated federal authority to prevent loss of life in the air, not an overreach into areas reserved to the states. The founders who suppressed the Whiskey Rebellion in 1794 under Washington’s direct command showed that government must act decisively when safety and order are at stake.
