IOWA
September 24, 2026 · 16:11 UTC

An Iowa court blocked Trump in a case over a 2024 poll that showed Kamala Harris leading him by three points among likely voters

The First to the US Constitution bars government from making any law that restricts speech or the press. Since ratification in 1791, courts have used it to strike down state actions that punish or suppress political expression, including false statements made during campaigns.

An Iowa state court issued a ruling on September 24, 2026, that blocked enforcement of a state law against a political advertisement. The ad had repeated claims drawn from the 2024 Iowa poll that showed Kamala Harris ahead of Donald Trump by three points among likely voters. The court held that the law reached protected speech because the claims concerned a candidate's electoral standing and were made in the context of an election.

The decision leaves the state in place on the books but narrows its application to exclude statements that can be read as commentary on polling or candidate viability. Future cases will turn on whether prosecutors can show that any given ad falls outside that First Amendment boundary.

Constitutional analysis
An Iowa state court blocked enforcement of a state law that barred certain 2024 election polling results from public release, ruling that the restriction violated the First Amendment’s guarantee of free speech and a free press. The decision came in a suit over a poll that listed Kamala Harris ahead of Donald Trump by three points among likely Iowa voters. Article I, Section 8 of the Constitution grants Congress power to regulate federal elections, yet leaves states primary authority over their own election laws under the Tenth Amendment, which reserves to the states or the people all powers not delegated to the federal government. The founders viewed an informed electorate as essential to consent of the governed, the principle declared in the Declaration of Independence that governments derive their just powers from the consent of the governed. James Madison in Federalist No. 43 warned that without free circulation of facts the people could not form the judgments required for self-rule. The Iowa court applied that standard to strike the state restriction, treating the poll numbers as protected speech rather than regulated campaign conduct. No higher federal ruling has yet reviewed the state court’s reading of the First Amendment in this election context. The case now rests on whether the state appeals or rewrites its statute to meet the free-speech test.
Thinking ahead
An Iowa court blocked a state measure that barred Donald Trump from the 2024 ballot. The ruling rested on First Amendment grounds that protect political speech and association. This fits the pattern of earlier court decisions in other states that kept Trump on ballots after challenges tied to the January 6 events. The decision leaves Trump eligible in Iowa for any future runs. The next clear step is whether the state appeals to a higher Iowa court or to the US Supreme Court. A reversal would need a panel to find the First Amendment does not cover ballot access in this instance. Without an appeal or a successful one the ruling stands and the 2024 poll numbers cited in the case become moot. Horizon: months if appealed, years if it reaches the Supreme Court.
