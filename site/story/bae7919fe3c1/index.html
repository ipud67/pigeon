Kansai Electric manually shuts Mihama nuclear reactor in central Japan

Japan operates 33 nuclear reactors across the country. The Mihama plant in Fukui Prefecture on the Sea of Japan coast has run three pressurized-water units since the 1970s. Unit 3, a 780-megawatt reactor, entered commercial service in 1976 and received approval to operate past 40 years under post-Fukushima safety rules that let utilities apply for extensions if they met new seismic, cooling and containment standards.

On September 17, 2026, Kansai Electric Power Co. started the manual shutdown sequence for Mihama Unit 3. The company cited the need to carry out planned inspections and maintenance that cannot occur while the reactor produces power. Control rods began inserting into the core at the direction of operators in the control room, cutting the fission rate and lowering output toward zero over the next several hours.

The reactor is now offline and will remain so for the duration of the scheduled work. Kansai Electric has not set a date for restart, and the other two units at Mihama stay unaffected by the move.

Thinking ahead
Kansai Electric started the manual shutdown of Mihama No. 3 on September 17 after an alert showed a problem in the reactor's cooling system. The company has run similar manual stops at its plants before when sensors flag an issue that needs checking. Next step is a full inspection of the affected parts, then a decision on when or if the reactor returns to service. The shutdown will last until the inspection finishes and any fixes pass regulator review. It ends only when Kansai confirms the cooling system meets operating specs and gets the green light to restart. No fixed restart date exists yet. This stays contained to one reactor at one site. Horizon: at least several weeks.
