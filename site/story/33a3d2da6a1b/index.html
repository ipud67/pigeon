DEARBORN
November 18, 2026

Michigan pharmacy owner Ali Naserdean got 82 months in prison for a 5.6 million dollar health care fraud scheme and illegal oxycodone distribution

Ali Naserdean owned a pharmacy in Dearborn Heights and worked as a technician at three others in the metro Detroit area. They used prescriptions to cover the scheme. In the same years Naserdean also sold oxycodone to traffickers for cash without checking whether any physician had prescribed the pills or whether they were dispensed in good faith.

Yesterday a judge in the Eastern District of Michigan sentenced him to 82 months in prison, followed by three years of supervised release. The court ordered immediate of money and property worth about 1.2 million dollars, entered a personal money judgment against him for 5,636,721.89 dollars, and required of 3,654,895.65 dollars to the Department of Health and Human Services plus 21,428.93 dollars to Blue Cross Blue Shield.

Naserdean has remained in custody since October 7 after agents arrested him for violating his bond conditions. The case was investigated by the FBI Detroit Field Office, HHS Office of Inspector General, and Dearborn police. A trial attorney from the Justice Department’s Fraud Division and an assistant U.S. attorney prosecuted it.
