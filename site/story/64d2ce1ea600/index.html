House sends impeachment bid against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee

The House of Representatives holds the power to officers for misconduct under Article II of the Constitution. That process starts when a member introduces articles of and the full chamber votes on whether to refer them to the Committee for review. The current Congress ends in January when Democrats take control of both chambers after the midterms.

On Tuesday the House voted 211 to 204 to send eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee. They charged that Hegseth had conducted military operations in Iran for more than 90 days without congressional authorization and had overseen extrajudicial killings during campaigns against drug boats in the Pacific and Caribbean. The referral motion killed the effort for this Congress because little time remains before the new Democratic majority arrives. Massie cast the lone Republican vote against the referral while more than a dozen members including 11 Democrats were absent.

The articles now sit with the committee where they are expected to remain until the new Congress convenes. Impeachment resolutions carry past the end of a term so Democrats can reintroduce them in January. President Trump posted on Truth Social that the vote amounted to exoneration of a Great American Patriot and cited recent military recruitment gains. Hegseth called the entire bid a farce from the Pentagon. Massie said he was disappointed but not surprised that colleagues knew what to do yet could not bring themselves to act.
