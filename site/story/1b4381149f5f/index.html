MOSCOW
September 20, 2026 · 10:24 UTC

Ukraine hits Russia with major drone attack, targeting refinery

Russian refineries and airfields have faced repeated Ukrainian drone strikes since the fall of 2022 when Kyiv began receiving longer-range systems from Western allies. Moscow has expanded its own long-range strikes on Ukrainian energy sites in the same period. Russian forces hold ground across four eastern Ukrainian regions annexed in 2022 and continue daily advances measured in hundreds of meters.

On September 20 2026 Ukraine launched more than one thousand drones across Russian territory in a single night. Moscow officials called it the largest such attack to date. Two people died in the Moscow region and one oil refinery sustained damage.

The strikes occurred on the final day of Russia's first wartime parliamentary election. Russian authorities reported intercepting the majority of the drones while emergency crews worked at the damaged refinery site. The exchange leaves both capitals pressing air defenses and energy against the next wave of strikes.

Thinking ahead
Ukraine launched more than 1,000 drones across Russia on the final day of voting in the wartime parliamentary election. Moscow called it the largest drone attack yet. Two people died in the Moscow region and one oil refinery took damage. The strike fits a pattern that began with early cross-border raids and has grown into regular deep strikes on energy sites and airfields. Russian air defenses have intercepted most drones in recent waves but gaps keep appearing around refineries and near the capital. This round shows Ukrainian forces still hold the capacity to mass-produce and coordinate long-range strikes even while Russian ground units press in the east. Next moves hinge on whether Russia shifts more air-defense batteries to cover energy infrastructure or whether Ukraine can sustain the launch rate once stocks or launch sites come under heavier pressure. The exchange is likely to run at this pace for weeks unless one side loses the ability to manufacture drones at current volume or air defenses improve enough to cut hit rates below 10 percent. Horizon: through October barring a major shift in supply lines.
