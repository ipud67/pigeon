18:T468,Ukraine launched more than 1,000 drones across Russia on the final day of voting in the wartime parliamentary election. Moscow called it the largest drone attack yet. Two people died in the Moscow region and one oil refinery took damage.

The strike fits a pattern that began with early cross-border raids and has grown into regular deep strikes on energy sites and airfields. Russian air defenses have intercepted most drones in recent waves but gaps keep appearing around refineries and near the capital.

This round shows Ukrainian forces still hold the capacity to mass-produce and coordinate long-range strikes even while Russian ground units press in the east. Next moves hinge on whether Russia shifts more air-defense batteries to cover energy infrastructure or whether Ukraine can sustain the launch rate once stocks or launch sites come under heavier pressure.

The exchange is likely to run at this pace for weeks unless one side loses the ability to manufacture drones at current volume or air defenses improve enough to cut hit rates below 10 percent.
