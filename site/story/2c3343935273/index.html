Rebuilding the United States Navy and America’s Shipbuilding Industrial Base

The United States Navy operated 290 battle force ships at the start of 2025. That number had held roughly steady for two decades after the post-Cold War drawdown that cut the fleet from 600 ships in 1987. Shipyards that once turned out a new submarine or surface combatant every few months now delivered them years late and billions over budget. The industrial base that supplied steel, engines, and skilled welders had shrunk to a handful of prime contractors and a thin supplier chain.

On August 13, 2026, President Trump signed a directing the Secretary of Defense, the Director of the Office of Management and Budget, and the Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs to produce a plan that rebuilds the Navy fleet and the shipbuilding industrial base. The order requires them to identify specific force-structure goals, accelerate procurement timelines, expand domestic production capacity, and restore the workforce and supplier network that support sustained ship construction.

The memorandum sets in motion a government-wide review that must report back with concrete actions and funding requirements. The Navy’s size, the health of its shipyards, and the capacity of the American industrial base now turn on what that review delivers and how quickly Congress and the executive branch act on it.

Constitutional analysis
The Constitution charges the federal government first with providing for the common defence. Article I, Section 8 gives Congress power to raise and support armies, maintain a navy, and make rules for the government of the land and naval forces. The framers knew sea power protected American commerce and kept enemies from our shores. In 1794 Congress passed the Naval Act to build six frigates after Barbary pirates seized U.S. ships. Washington signed the bill and later sent those frigates against the French in the Quasi-War. The new presidential memorandum directs the Secretary of War, the Office of Management and Budget, and the national security adviser to rebuild the Navy and the shipyards that supply it. That order rests on the same enumerated power and the same founding purpose: secure the nation so citizens can live in peace. The founding generation did not trust standing armies on land but accepted a strong navy as the first line of defense. They funded it through regular appropriations and kept shipbuilding inside American yards under American law. This memorandum follows that line.
