Gaza: IDF slays Nukhba Force platoon commander, additional Hamas terrorist

The Israel Defense Forces have controlled 54 percent of the Gaza Strip since the fighting resumed after the collapse of earlier ceasefires. That control began with the ground operation that followed the Hamas attack on Israel on October 7, 2023, which killed civilians in the northwestern Negev and took hostages into Gaza.

On September 26 the IDF struck and killed Ibrahim Fawzi Ismail Mohsen, a platoon commander in Hamas’s Nukhba Force, in the Nuseirat area of Gaza. The same day it killed Mohammed Kamel Suleiman Mohsen in the Khan Yunis area. Both men had taken part in attacks on IDF soldiers and Israeli civilians and had worked to rebuild Hamas capabilities in violation of ceasefire terms. Earlier that Friday the military announced it had killed Hassan Mahmoud Siam, a commander in Hamas’s military wing, in Gaza City on September 24; he had taken part in the October 7 attack and in holding hostages Dafna Elyakim, Keith Siegel, Aviva Siegel, and Amit Soussana during the war. The day before that strike the IDF killed Adham Sabhi Salim Ziara, another military-wing operative in the same area; both men had carried out shooting attacks on IDF troops and planted explosive devices along the Yellow Line in violation of the ceasefire. The military said all four were eliminated from the air after steps to reduce civilian harm, including precise munitions and aerial surveillance.

The IDF also killed Hamas commander Jabr Moqbel in Deir al-Balah on September 25; he ran a money-exchange network that moved millions of shekels in foreign currency to fund wages, military infrastructure repair, and operations. In a separate strike the same day it killed sniper Hasan al-Batta in Khan Yunis. Troops continue to clear infrastructure in the controlled area, including a two-kilometer underground tunnel dismantled along the Yellow Line. As of September 25 the IDF and Israeli Security Agency had eliminated 16 key Hamas figures involved in financing that totaled more than $1 billion.

Thinking ahead

These targeted air strikes follow a pattern of weekly eliminations of mid-level Hamas commanders and financiers who violate the truce by rebuilding tunnels and attack networks inside the 54 percent of Gaza held by Israeli troops. The IDF continues ground clearance of infrastructure, including the recent discovery and dismantling of a two-kilometer underground tunnel in the Yellow Line sector. The operational tempo points to continued pinpoint strikes on identified Hamas field commanders and money handlers for at least the next several weeks. Next moves will likely stay inside current Israeli-held zones unless Hamas steps up attacks enough to force expansion of the cleared area. The situation stays stable as long as ceasefire violations remain at the present level. Three-to-four weeks of similar targeted killings.
