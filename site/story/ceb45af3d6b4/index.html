US judge rules that Trump plan to halve FEMA workforce violated law

The Federal Emergency Management Agency has operated inside the Department of Homeland Security since Congress created the department in 2002 and moved most disaster-response duties there the next year. Presidents from both parties have since adjusted its size and structure through executive orders and budget requests, but any permanent cut to its workforce has required approval under the that set federal personnel floors for the agency.

On September 12 a federal judge in Washington ruled that an executive plan to cut the FEMA workforce by half violated those statutes. The order at issue had directed the Department of Homeland Security to reassign or eliminate roughly 3,000 positions without first obtaining new from Congress.

The decision blocks the reduction and returns the agency to the staffing levels set by the most recent appropriations law. The government has not yet said whether it will appeal.
