STRAIT OF HORMUZ
September 8, 2026 · 20:34 UTC
This story is from September 8, 2026 and is no longer on the front page.

Iran says it captured a U.S. sea drone in the Strait of Hormuz

The US and Israel began strikes on Iran in late February 2026. The fight narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz after a June that lifted the dual and opened a 60-day negotiating window collapsed on 8 July. Iran struck three commercial tankers on 6 July that had bypassed its approved transit routes. The IRGC moved to shut the strait on 12 July and Tehran suspended its commitments under the memorandum. Sustained US strikes resumed that same week and have continued since.

On 8 September Iran announced it had seized a US sea drone in the strait. US officials confirmed the loss but said the drone was defective and carried no classified systems.

The seizure leaves both sides pressing their claims over passage through the 21-mile channel. The US continues nightly strikes on Iranian maritime targets while Iran maintains its threat to block tanker traffic.

Thinking ahead
Iran grabbed a US sea drone in the Strait of Hormuz on September 8. US officials called it a defective unit with nothing sensitive on board. The fight has stayed locked on who controls the narrow shipping lane since the Islamabad Memorandum fell apart in July, with Iran hitting tankers that skip its rules and the US hitting Iranian ports and storage sites most nights. Both sides keep trading small moves in the same patch of water. Iran has grabbed gear before to show it can reach out and touch traffic; the US has answered with strikes on launch sites and fuel lines. Nothing in the last six weeks points to either side backing off the daily pattern. The next rung looks like another US strike on Iranian naval assets or a fresh Iranian claim of another interdiction. The pattern holds until one side loses enough boats or faces a wider coalition escort system that makes the strait routine again. This episode will run at least another month.
