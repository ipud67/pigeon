FRANCE
September 10, 2026 · 17:52 UTC

French govt warns right-wing TV channel about rebroadcasting expelled pro-Putin pundit

France has required foreign nationals to leave the country when officials determine they pose a security risk or spread state-directed messages. The rule has applied for decades to people the interior ministry lists as threats. In August the ministry ordered Russian commentator Xenia Federova out after it classified her as a Kremlin agent who had used French airwaves to repeat Russian government lines.

On September 10 the government publicly warned the conservative channel CNews against bringing Federova back on air. The interior ministry stated that any rebroadcast would violate the terms of her expulsion order and could trigger legal action against the network for giving platform to a banned foreign national.

The warning places CNews on notice that continued appearances by Federova will be treated as defiance of the expulsion. The channel must now decide whether to cancel the bookings or risk that the ministry can impose on licensed broadcasters.

Thinking ahead
The French government issued a warning to CNews on September 10 after the channel brought back Xenia Federova, who French authorities expelled in August for alleged Kremlin links and propaganda activity. This marks the latest step in a sequence that began with her removal from French airwaves and territory earlier this summer. The warning signals regulators are watching for further rebroadcasts and could trigger formal sanctions or license reviews if the channel continues. How much longer this runs depends on whether CNews keeps her on air and whether the government follows through with enforcement. Observable indicators are the channel's scheduling decisions and any written follow-up from broadcasting authorities. If the government files an official complaint or opens an investigation, the dispute escalates into regulatory proceedings. If the channel drops her again, the warning likely fades without further action. The situation stays contained to French media regulators and one outlet unless Federova secures legal residency elsewhere in Europe or CNews challenges the warning in court. Horizon: 4-8 weeks until next procedural step or quiet drop-off.
