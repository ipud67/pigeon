The system is one of the older early-warning sets Russia has kept in the field since the first weeks of the war. Confirmation from the Ukrainian side marks the latest in a string of targeted strikes on Russian air-defense gear that started with similar hits on S-400 components in 2022 and continued through 2024-25 with decoy and jamming tactics.

How much longer this goes depends on whether Russia keeps the remaining Kasta units in forward positions or pulls them back. The next observable step is whether Moscow replaces the lost radar with newer Nebo or Protivnik sets or simply shifts existing ones from other sectors. A measurable change would show up if Ukrainian drone footage drops off or if Russian air-defense coverage gaps widen enough for more Ukrainian aircraft to operate near the line.

The pattern has run for three years with incremental losses on the Russian side and no sign either side has run out of the systems or the drones.
