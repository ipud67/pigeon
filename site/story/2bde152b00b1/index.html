MIAMI
September 16, 2026

Russian National Sentenced for Synthetic Identity Theft Fraud Scheme and Conspiring to Submit Fraudulent Voter Registrations

Dmitry Shushlebin arrived in the United States and set up sham companies that he used to commit identity theft over multiple years. He created synthetic identities by combining real and fake personal information to apply for credit cards and loans from financial institutions. The scheme ran for years and reached hundreds of victims.

On July 24 2025 federal prosecutors in Florida filed a four-count superseding information against him that charged conspiracy to give false information when registering to vote, submit fraudulent voter registrations, wire fraud, making false statements and aggravated identity theft. Shushlebin pleaded guilty to all counts the next day. Court records show he had hired Sanjar Jamilov and others to file more than 100 fake voter registration applications with the Pinellas County Supervisor of Elections in February and March 2023 so the resulting identities could be used in the financial fraud.

On September 16 2026 a federal judge in Miami sentenced the 45-year-old Russian national to six years in prison followed by three years of supervised release and ordered him to pay $458,839.69 in restitution. The case was investigated by the FBI with help from the Florida Department of Law Enforcement and the U.S. Postal Inspection Service and prosecuted by the Justice Department’s Public Integrity Section and the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Middle District of Florida.

Thinking ahead
Dmitry Shushlebin got six years in prison on September 16 2026 after he pleaded guilty a year earlier to conspiracy to file false voter registrations, wire fraud, false statements, and aggravated identity theft. He paid people to send over 100 fake registration forms to Pinellas County elections officials in early 2023 so he could build synthetic identities, then used those to open credit cards and loans against banks hundreds of times. The case started with an FBI probe that included the Florida Department of Law Enforcement and postal inspectors; prosecutors from the Justice Department Public Integrity Section and the Middle District of Florida handled it from the July 2025 charging papers onward. The sentence marks the end of this specific federal case. Shushlebin now moves into the Bureau of Prisons system to serve the term. No further court dates are set unless he files an appeal or the restitution collection hits a snag. This pattern of using voter rolls to seed fake identities for bank fraud has shown up in other federal cases over the past decade. Without new co-conspirators coming forward or fresh fraud tied to the same ring, the matter stays closed after he finishes the prison stretch and the three years of supervised release. Horizon: case closes in 2026 with no visible next rung.
