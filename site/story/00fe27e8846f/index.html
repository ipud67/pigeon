TEHRAN
October 7, 2026 · 01:07 UTC

Strikes on a site near Tehran in Iran leave buildings destroyed in March

The United States and Israel began strikes on Iran at the end of February 2026. The campaign opened with Israeli air and cyber operations against roughly 500 targets, followed by weekly exchanges of strikes that narrowed over the months to control of the Strait of Hormuz. In March Israeli forces hit a compound northeast of Tehran identified as Minzadehei, a site the Israeli military said housed buildings and hillside structures used by Iranian nuclear scientists to restore and advance technology required for nuclear weapons development.

Satellite images recorded on Thursday and reviewed by the Institute for Science and International Security now show regular vehicle traffic at the same site together with fresh earthmoving and cleanup work around the buildings and underground areas damaged in the March strike.

The renewed activity at Minzadehei follows similar construction reported in September at Pickaxe Mountain, another mountainous facility south of Tehran that Israeli linked to restoration of nuclear program elements. The fighting between the US, Israel and Iran continues to center on the Strait of Hormuz, where naval clashes and tanker incidents have kept oil flows restricted since the Islamabad collapsed on 8 July.

Thinking ahead
Satellite images from VANTOR Technologies dated last Thursday show vehicle traffic and earthmoving at the Minzadehei site northeast of Tehran. The facility took Israeli strikes in March that wrecked surface buildings and likely damaged underground sections. Israeli forces had tracked nuclear scientists moving in and out of the compound before the March hit and described it as a site for restoring technology tied to weapons development. The US-Israel campaign against Iran that began in late February has settled into repeated strikes on military and nuclear-linked targets while the fight centers on the Strait of Hormuz. Iran has responded with missile and naval actions and resumed work at other suspected nuclear sites such as Pickaxe Mountain. Renewed ground activity at a bombed site points to repair or concealment efforts. That pattern has held at other struck locations since the summer wave of nightly US strikes. Next visible steps would be further satellite or intelligence confirmation of what is being rebuilt underground, followed by any Israeli decision on follow-up targeting. This activity level has continued at similar pace for the seven months since the March strike. Horizon: at least another 30 days.
