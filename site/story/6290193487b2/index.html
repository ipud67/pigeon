Venezuelan engineer detained during immigration appointment in US

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlineIngeniera venezolana fue detenida durante una cita de inmigración en EEUU

Patricia Valdez arrived in the United States from Venezuela in 2023 and applied for . She received permission to remain in the country while her case moved through the immigration courts. She held a work permit, took a job as an engineer, and checked in regularly with federal immigration officers as required.

On September 26, 2026, Valdez appeared for one of those scheduled appointments at an immigration office. Officers took her into custody on the spot and moved her to a detention center for people facing removal proceedings.

She remains in federal custody. Her asylum claim is still pending before the immigration court, and no hearing date has been set since the arrest.

Thinking ahead
Patricia Valdez, a 37-year-old Venezuelan engineer, was taken into custody by US immigration officers during a scheduled appointment and moved to another facility. The case sits inside the standard removal process for people whose legal status has been flagged for review. Next steps turn on whether her lawyers file for bond, asylum relief, or administrative closure; without one of those, the government can move her toward deportation within weeks. The situation shifts only if a judge grants relief or immigration officials change the charge. This pattern of detentions at check-in meetings has run steady since early 2025. Likely to run another 4 to 12 weeks per case until a hearing or transfer decision. Next visible rung is either a bond hearing or removal flight.
