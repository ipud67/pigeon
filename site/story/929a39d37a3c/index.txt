17:T41a,The governors of Hokkaido and Osaka sat down and agreed to work together on a backup-capital plan. The idea is that both prefectures would step in with key government functions if Tokyo gets hit by a big disaster. This is the latest move in a long-running discussion about spreading out national functions that has picked up pace after past quake warnings and pandemic stress tests.

Next concrete steps are joint working groups between the two prefectures to spell out which agencies and systems would relocate where. That work has to produce a written framework before it can go to the national government for review or funding. Until those groups deliver something on paper and Tokyo shows interest in budgeting for it, the plan stays at the talking stage.

No court action or election deadline is visible right now to force faster movement. If either governor loses re-election or the national government shelves the idea, the cooperation could stall.

This stays on the discussion track for at least the next 12 to 18 months.
