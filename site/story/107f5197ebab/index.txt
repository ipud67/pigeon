19:T475,The 2021 Dutch law that registers anyone without recorded wishes as a no-objection donor has produced only a small rise in actual organ donations according to the Dutch Transplantation Foundation data released this week. Prior to the law most adults had left their preferences blank so the switch created a large registered pool yet hospitals and families still face low consent rates in practice. The foundation report shows the gap between registered no-objection status and recovered organs has stayed wide since the change took effect.

This pattern is likely to hold for at least the next two to three years unless procurement teams change how they approach families at the bedside or launch a fresh public campaign that directly lifts conversion rates. The next visible rung will be the ministry’s scheduled review of the law’s outcomes later in 2027 which could trigger adjustments to registration wording or training for transplant coordinators. Without measurable movement in family consent numbers the donation totals will remain near current levels.
