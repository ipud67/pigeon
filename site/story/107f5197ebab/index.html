Donor law change yields few extra organ donations despite 'no objection'

Translated from Dutch · read the original →
The original headlineToch bezwaar bij 'geen bezwaar': nieuwe wet levert weinig extra orgaandonaties op

Until 2021 most people in the Netherlands had left no record of their wishes on organ donation after death. Doctors and families then followed the default that organs would not be taken. On July 1 2021 the government put a new law into force that registers every adult who has done nothing as having no objection to donation.

The Dutch Transplantation Foundation has now counted the actual donations in the five years since the register changed. Its figures released this month show the number of extra organs recovered from donors registered as no objection has stayed very low.

The law therefore left the overall supply of organs for transplant almost unchanged. The gap between people who need transplants and the organs available still turns on how many families agree when asked at the bedside and on how many citizens actively record their own decision.

Thinking ahead
The 2021 Dutch law that registers anyone without recorded wishes as a no-objection donor has produced only a small rise in actual organ donations according to the Dutch Transplantation Foundation data released this week. Prior to the law most adults had left their preferences blank so the switch created a large registered pool yet hospitals and families still face low consent rates in practice. The foundation report shows the gap between registered no-objection status and recovered organs has stayed wide since the change took effect. This pattern is likely to hold for at least the next two to three years unless procurement teams change how they approach families at the bedside or launch a fresh public campaign that directly lifts conversion rates. The next visible rung will be the ministry’s scheduled review of the law’s outcomes later in 2027 which could trigger adjustments to registration wording or training for transplant coordinators. Without measurable movement in family consent numbers the donation totals will remain near current levels. Horizon: flat donation counts through 2028 unless consent procedures shift.
