1a:T495,The US has pressed NATO allies to pull out of the Rome Statute and cut all ties to the ICC, according to three diplomats who heard the request. This fits a pattern of US moves against the court that go back years, including earlier sanctions on ICC officials. The next visible steps would be formal statements from individual NATO governments on whether they will start withdrawal procedures, which normally take one year under the treaty once a country notifies the UN. For the push to gain traction, at least a few member states would need to file those notices and begin domestic votes to repeal supporting laws. Right now the diplomats report only a verbal call, with no public NATO document or confirmed government reply on record.

The situation is likely to drag on for months as capitals review their legal options and weigh relations with the US. It could stall if most allies treat the request as non-binding talk, or it could spread if one or two smaller states move first and set a procedural example. Without a visible withdrawal notice from at least one NATO member the forecast stays flat.
