23:T4b8,Scientists built a new map of the rock under Greenland’s ice sheet by tracking faint bumps on the ice surface from satellites and running them through Ice Flow Perturbation Analysis. The map connects 1,943 valleys, adds 650 that earlier maps missed, and shows that half the valleys already in the BedMachine Greenland dataset reach hundreds of kilometers farther inland than recorded before. Many of those valleys start in the southern and eastern highlands where the ice first formed and keep the wide branching angles typical of rivers that cut the land long before the ice arrived.

The work sits inside an ongoing effort to update BedMachine Greenland with better bedrock data. Next steps are further satellite passes to check the new valley outlines and plug the sharper map into ice-sheet models that run forward in time. Refinement will stall until the team can match the straight, aligned valleys in the west-central zone against known fault lines or other rock records; without that match the map stays partly unexplained.

This mapping effort will run for years as each new satellite dataset and ground check either sharpens or forces changes to the current lines.
