1d:T6cc,The Trump administration has again blocked visas for Mahmoud Abbas and other Palestinian Authority officials to attend the UN General Assembly in New York. This marks the second year in a row the State Department has cited the PA and PLO for continued payments to people who carried out attacks, public praise of those attacks, and use of textbooks that name them as models. The department also listed the PA's filings at the International Criminal Court and International Court of Justice plus pushes for unilateral state recognition as breaches of past agreements to settle the conflict through direct talks. Waivers were issued only for staff already posted at the Palestinian observer mission. The General Assembly is set to vote Thursday on letting the PA deliver video or recorded remarks next week instead, with Abbas listed to appear on September 24 alongside the Israeli prime minister. 

The visa denials have held steady since the first Trump term and repeated in the current one, tied to the same list of PA actions. No procedural shift in US policy appears unless the PA halts the stipends, revises the textbooks, and drops the court cases. The UN vote offers a workaround for remote participation but does not restore physical entry or change the US conditions. Abbas has kept calling for a role in Gaza but the administration has shown no move toward including the PA in those arrangements.

This pattern runs at least through the current General Assembly session. It stays in place until the listed PA practices measurably stop or a US administration lifts the criteria.
