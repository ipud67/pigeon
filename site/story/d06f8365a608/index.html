September 19, 2026

Conservative legal group files judicial conduct compaint against two federal judges

The federal judiciary in Minnesota has operated under the standard rules that govern all U.S. district courts since the Judiciary Act of 1789 first created them. Those rules require judges to avoid public comment on pending cases or controversies that could come before them and to maintain impartiality in all proceedings.

A conservative legal group filed a formal judicial conduct complaint on September 19 against two of those judges, U.S. District Judges Patrick J. Schiltz and John R. Tunheim. The complaint cites statements the judges made to the New York Times and published on September 17 in which they described operational difficulties the Minnesota federal courts encountered while handling a specific matter.

The complaint now sits with the Eighth Circuit judicial council, which holds the authority under the Judicial Conduct and Disability Act to review such filings, appoint investigators if needed, and decide whether any discipline short of removal is warranted.

Thinking ahead

Tunheim over their reported comments in a September 17 New York Times article about operational pressures on the state's federal courts. Judicial conduct complaints go first to the circuit's judicial council for review under fixed procedural rules that set timelines for initial screening and possible dismissal or deeper inquiry. These complaints rarely lead to public discipline or removal. Most get screened out early with no further action. The next observable step is the Eighth Circuit judicial council's response, which must occur within set deadlines or trigger escalation to a higher review panel. Change would require the council to find a clear violation of conduct rules and vote for sanctions, a sequence that has happened in only a handful of cases in recent years. This marks another round in the steady flow of formal complaints lodged against federal judges over public statements. The council's first action will show whether this one advances past intake. Horizon: 30 to 90 days until first council decision.
