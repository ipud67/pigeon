WP: US senators urge Trump to withdraw Putin's G20 invitation

A group of 14 US senators sent a letter to President Donald Trump on September 26. The letter asks him to revoke the invitation he extended to Russian President Vladimir Putin for the G20 leaders' summit scheduled in Miami in December. The senators include members from both parties and point to the United States placed on Putin, Russian Finance Minister Anton Siluanov and other Russian officials after Russia began its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022.

The senators wrote that Putin's attendance would reduce the pressure on him to negotiate a in Ukraine. Secretary of State Marco Rubio had said he hoped Putin would come. Bloomberg reported that Putin is likely to skip the summit. Putin recently turned down a proposal from Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to meet on the sidelines of the event. Trump has said he does not know whether Putin would join a three-way meeting with Zelenskyy there.

The letter now sits with the White House as the December summit date approaches. The decision on whether to withdraw the invitation rests with Trump.

Constitutional analysis
Fourteen US senators sent a letter to President Trump asking him to revoke the invitation he extended to Vladimir Putin for the G20 leaders summit set for Miami in December. The Constitution places the power to receive foreign ambassadors and other public ministers in the president under Article II, Section 3. That same article and the powers it draws from the law of nations gave George Washington authority in 1793 to decide which envoys from revolutionary France the United States would meet. The senators cited Putin’s responsibility for the war in Ukraine and the fact that he and members of his delegation remain under US sanctions imposed for actions judged to threaten American national security. The letter argued that allowing Putin to attend could lessen the pressure on him to accept a ceasefire. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has said he hopes Putin will come. Bloomberg has reported that Putin is expected to skip the summit. A decision by the president on whether to withdraw the invitation would settle the matter.
Thinking ahead
Fourteen senators from both parties sent a letter to Trump on September 26 asking him to pull the December G20 invitation to Putin. The letter points to US sanctions already in place on Putin, Finance Minister Siluanov, and other Russian officials, and argues that letting Putin attend would cut his reason to stop the daily strikes on Ukrainian civilian sites. Secretary of State Rubio had said he hoped Putin would come, yet Bloomberg reports Putin is expected to skip the Miami meeting and has turned down a side talk with Zelenskyy there. Trump has said he does not know if Putin would join a three-way sit-down. The letter is the latest step in a running pattern of congressional pushes to keep Russia boxed out of big forums while the Ukraine fighting continues. This pressure is likely to stay inside Congress and the administration for the next few weeks while staff draft a reply and prep the summit guest list. The situation changes only if Trump publicly cancels the invite or if Putin confirms he is not coming. Without either move the back-and-forth drags into November. Next move likely stays at the letter-and-statement level until the White House answers in writing or Trump speaks on it. Three-to-four week horizon before the next clear procedural step.
