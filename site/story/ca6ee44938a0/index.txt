1c:T816,The federal government listed the Pacific Link pipeline as a project of national interest under the Building Canada Act on October 1 2026. This follows three months of consultations by the Major Projects Office with more than 130 Indigenous communities in Alberta and British Columbia plus federal departments and the BC government. The project builds on the completed Trans Mountain expansion as the next rung in west coast crude export capacity with Canada and Alberta holding equal ownership Trans Mountain leading development Pembina providing private investment and Indigenous groups offered at least 10 percent equity backed by loan guarantees. A southern route was chosen to skirt the Great Bear Sea and other sensitive northern ecosystems. The Major Projects Office and Canada Energy Regulator now run a single integrated federal review that sets binding conditions on ownership benefits Indigenous rights environmental rules local hiring and oversight with a deadline of September 1 2027 before construction can start.

This step gives project backers one year of regulatory certainty while they finish route mapping ecological surveys cost estimates and workforce plans. The review will produce one conditions document that folds in all federal permits instead of separate approvals. Past Canadian pipeline projects show that once a project clears the national interest gate and conditions document the main remaining variables are final Indigenous agreements detailed engineering and any court actions on consultation adequacy. Those steps have taken 12 to 36 months on comparable files.

The timeline points to possible shovels in the ground in late 2028 or 2029 if the conditions document lands on schedule and no major legal blocks arise. Next observable rung is the release of the draft conditions document and associated public hearings by the Canada Energy Regulator sometime before the September 2027 cutoff.
