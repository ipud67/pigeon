This step sits on the ladder of synagogue protests that picked up after Oct. 7, when some demonstrators targeted events tied to emigration to Israel.

Passage stays tied to how many House and Senate members see the 100-foot buffer and the commerce hook as a workable split between speech and worship. Courts will test the exact line on what counts as a protected religious service once the measure clears both chambers and reaches the president. The bill's own sponsors already flagged that litigation over events like aliyah gatherings is baked in.

Without floor votes scheduled yet, the push stays at the urging stage. Observable indicators are the number of co-sponsors added in the coming weeks and whether the House Judiciary Committee marks it up before the end of the session.
