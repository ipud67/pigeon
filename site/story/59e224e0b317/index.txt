Hunt, a former Army helicopter pilot who completed two deployments in the region and served as a diplomatic liaison there, cited his firsthand knowledge of the stakes for American interests.

The founders designed diplomatic posts to secure the young republic’s peace and commerce abroad so that life and property could be protected at home. Federalist 75 explains that the treaty and appointment powers demand men of “integrity” and “knowledge of the world” to guard against foreign dangers without entangling the nation in permanent alliances. Washington himself navigated early relations with Muslim powers, dispatching consuls to the Barbary states after Congress authorized funds under the 1794 Naval Act to end tribute payments and protect American shipping.

Hunt’s record includes co-chairing the Congressional Caucus on Black-Jewish Relations and steady support for Israel. The nomination fills a post left vacant since Trump’s second term began.
