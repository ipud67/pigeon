Valditara: I will reward the student who stopped the 13-year-old in Rome

Translated from Italian · read the original →
The original headlineValditara, premierò lo studente che ha bloccato il 13enne a Roma

Italian schools have operated under a national code that sets rules for student conduct and discipline since the early 2000s. Teachers and principals hold authority to enforce those rules inside buildings and on grounds. The code gives the education minister power to recognize individual students for actions that uphold school order.

On September 19 the minister, Giuseppe Valditara, announced he will invite one high-school student to the ministry in Rome. The student had stepped in and stopped a 13-year-old who attacked others inside a school that day. Valditara described the invitation itself as the reward.

The ministry has not yet set the date for the visit or spelled out any further recognition. The student remains unnamed in the public statement. The case now rests on when the invitation is issued and what, if any, additional steps the minister takes under the existing code.

Thinking ahead
The education minister invited the student who stopped the 13-year-old attacker to the ministry in Rome. Valditara called the action very nice and said he will reward the student. This follows the incident on a Rome school bus earlier this week. The reward is a procedural next step that requires only the minister's decision and no further approval. The situation stays at this level unless the student declines the invitation or a court files charges that change the public handling of the case. Next move stays inside the ministry.
