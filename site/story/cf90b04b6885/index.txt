1a:T517,Djibouti signed the Artemis Accords on Monday at NASA headquarters, becoming the 72nd country and the eighth from Africa. The move follows its launch of two satellites on U.S. Falcon 9 rockets in 2023 and 2024 after starting a national space program in 2020. NASA Deputy Administrator Matt Anderson tied the signing to current U.S. direction to return to the Moon and prepare for Mars, with the Accords now listing practical steps for hardware, payloads, and training that new members can supply.

This step sits on a ladder that began with the Accords launch in 2020 by eight founding nations and has added members at a steady clip since. Djibouti’s satellites already used American launch services, so the signature lines up with that pattern rather than starting a new one. The next observable rung will be any specific agreement on a CubeSat, research project, or student exchange that NASA or the State Department announces with Djibouti.

The pace of new signings has held at roughly six to ten nations per year. Nothing in the ceremony points to a faster or slower rate ahead. The situation stays on track until a member state walks away from the Accords or a major lunar mission partner declines to sign.
