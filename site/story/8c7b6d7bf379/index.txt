1a:T408,ICE hit almost 50,000 detentions in July, the highest monthly number seen so far in Trump’s second term. That figure comes straight from the Deportation Data Project run by UC Berkeley and UCLA. The count sits on top of a steady climb that started when the administration ramped up interior enforcement after the 2024 election.

The pace is likely to hold or push higher through the rest of 2026 as long as Congress keeps funding at current levels and federal courts do not issue broad blocks on the expanded arrest priorities. Next visible rung will be the August and September numbers when they drop in October; any sustained drop below 45,000 would signal either resource strain or a policy shift. Continued high numbers will feed straight into larger deportation totals by year-end.

If detention capacity hits a hard ceiling or lawsuits start tying up field offices, the monthly totals flatten. Right now the indicators point to more of the same.
