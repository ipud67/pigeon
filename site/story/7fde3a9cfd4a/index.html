Iranian officials proposed reopening the Strait of Hormuz to end hostilities but US President Donald Trump rejected it on Saturday

The United States and Israel began strikes on Iran in late February 2026. The fight narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz, the 21-mile waterway that carries much of the world's seaborne oil. A June memorandum lifted the dual blockades on Iranian ports and the strait and opened a 60-day negotiating window, but that deal collapsed on 8 July and sustained US strikes resumed.

On 26 September 2026, President Trump told reporters at the White House that he had rejected an Iranian proposal delivered through Qatari . The plan, developed in coordination with Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei and presented at the UN General Assembly, called for a full cessation of hostilities across the Middle East, including Lebanon, with the strait reopened on the seventh day of a truce. It required the release of frozen Iranian assets, the lifting of sanctions on Iranian oil, and an end to the US naval —the same terms as the collapsed Islamabad Memorandum.

The strait remains largely closed to international shipping. Iranian ports stay under US blockade. Trump said he would like a deal but found the Iranian terms unacceptable, and he posted on social media calling the waterway the Trump Strait. The conflict now turns on whether either side will alter the terms that ended the last negotiating window.
