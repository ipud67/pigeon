Three-quarters of religion-based hate crimes in Hawaii last year targeted Jews, who are less than 1% of the state

Hawaii has tracked hate crimes by category since 2021. The state attorney general releases annual figures that break out offenses by the that drove them. Religion-based incidents have formed a small slice of the total each year. In 2022 Jews were the target of one of four such cases. In 2023 they were the target of three of seven. In 2024 all four religion-based cases singled out Jews.

On September 17 2026 the attorney general posted the 2025 numbers. Of four religion-based hate crimes that year three were directed at Jews who make up less than 1 percent of the population. One of the three offenders was arrested. The fourth case targeted a member of a different faith. Overall hate crimes in the state fell to 44 from 92 the year before. One of the three 2025 cases against Jews involved a Montana man charged in June with leaving antisemitic voicemails at the office of Jewish Governor Josh Green.

The figures show Jews accounted for 75 percent of religion-based hate crimes in 2025 and roughly 67 percent of them since 2023. No anti-Muslim cases appear in the 2025 or 2024 data. The count now stands at those four 2025 incidents inside a four-year pattern in which Jews have been the target in the clear majority of religion-based offenses the state records.
