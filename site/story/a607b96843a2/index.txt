The right to life and the security of the person come first in the founding order. Government exists to secure unalienable rights against violence and threat, as the Declaration states. The framers drew from colonial experience with religious persecution and wrote the First Amendment to guard free exercise while expecting the republic to punish crimes that disturb domestic tranquility.

James Madison in Federalist 43 listed “domestic Tranquility” as a core purpose of the new Constitution. The Militia Acts of 1792 placed responsibility on states to keep order and protect inhabitants from private violence. Washington called out militia in 1794 to put down the Whiskey Rebellion when local courts could not enforce law. The founding generation treated targeted intimidation and threats as direct attacks on the peace the government was formed to maintain.

Hawaii’s figures show Jews, at 0.69 percent of the population, accounted for 75 percent of religion-based hate crimes and about 6.8 percent of all hate crimes. One case involved antisemitic threats to the governor’s office. No anti-Muslim or anti-Arab hate crimes appeared in the religious category.

The data fit a pattern since 2023. Jews faced roughly two-thirds of religion-based hate crimes in that period while forming a tiny share of the population.
