Constitution must remain supreme, every exercise of public authority must be within its limits: FCC CJ

The Federal Court has operated inside Pakistan’s written constitution since its creation in 1956. That document sets out the powers of every public office, from the president down to local administrators, and requires all of them to stay inside the lines it draws. Judges, civil servants and elected officials swear oaths to uphold it before they take their posts.

On 15 September 2026, Chief Justice Aminuddin Khan repeated the same rule while speaking at an event in Islamabad. He said the constitution must remain the highest law and that every use of public power must stay inside the limits written in its text. He described the document as the recorded decision of the people as a whole.

The court now sits with that statement on record as the latest public reminder of the boundary that has governed Pakistani public life for seventy years. All future exercises of authority will be measured against the same written lines the chief justice cited.

Constitutional analysis
The Constitution stands as the supreme law of the land, a written charter that limits every branch and officer of government to enumerated powers drawn from the consent of the governed. The Federal Constitutional Court chief justice spoke plainly in Islamabad: the Constitution must remain supreme and every exercise of public authority must stay inside its boundaries. That principle echoes the American founding. The Declaration grounded government itself in securing unalienable rights, beginning with the right to life. The Preamble lists domestic Tranquility and the general Welfare as objects that government may pursue only by laws that follow the enumerated powers in Article I, Section 8. Federalist 78 explains that the courts serve as an intermediate body between the people and the legislature to keep the latter within the limits of the Constitution. The First Congress passed the Judiciary Act of 1789 on that understanding, and Washington enforced federal law directly when he led the militia to put down the Whiskey Rebellion in 1794 rather than allow state or private actors to nullify acts of Congress. A constitution that is the supreme expression of the collective will therefore binds judges, executives, and legislatures alike; any public act outside its text and original meaning steps beyond the charter the people ratified.
Thinking ahead
The Federal Constitutional Court chief justice repeated the standard line on constitutional limits during a Tuesday speech in Islamabad. This matches what FCC judges have said in prior rulings on public authority cases. No new case or procedural step was announced. The statement keeps the existing pattern going. Courts continue to cite the same supremacy language when they hear challenges to government actions. Change would need a specific petition that tests those boundaries and draws a written order. It runs until the next bench decision or constitutional amendment proposal surfaces. Next rung likely another routine citation in an ongoing petition.
