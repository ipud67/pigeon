1c:T510,The US put sanctions on the whole International Criminal Court on October 9. This extends an executive order from the start of the current administration that first hit individual ICC judges and prosecutors. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the aim is to stop the court from trying US citizens or allies and to cut its ability to function until it ends those efforts.

The sanctions sit apart from the running US-Iran armed conflict that began with strikes in late February 2026 and has since narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz. Israel, whose leader faces an ICC arrest warrant from 2024, welcomed the move. Eight US allies including Canada, France, Germany and the UK issued a joint statement regretting the sanctions and pledging to keep backing the court.

The ICC rejected the sanctions and said it will keep working on cases based only on law and evidence. No immediate procedural next step has been named by either side. The situation stays frozen until the US sees concrete ICC steps to drop its probes or until allies press the court to change course.

This could run for months or longer while the US keeps pressure on funding and operations. Next likely move is further US measures to isolate the court if no rollback happens.
