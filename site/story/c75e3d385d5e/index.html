US sanctions entire International Criminal Court in The Hague

The International Criminal Court has operated in The Hague since the 1998 Rome created it as an independent judicial body to try individuals for war crimes, genocide, crimes against humanity and the crime of . The United States never joined the Rome Statute and has long rejected the court's claim to over American citizens or over nationals of non-member allies such as Israel.

On October 9 the Trump extended those sanctions to the entire court. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the step would cripple its ability to operate and warned that the United States would continue to dismantle the court until it no longer threatened American citizens.

The court rejected the sanctions the same day and said it would continue its work on the basis of law and evidence alone. Canada, Denmark, Germany, France, Italy, Japan, the Netherlands and the United Kingdom issued a joint statement regretting the move and pledging to support the court's operations. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who faces an ICC arrest warrant issued in 2024, welcomed the American decision. The sanctions now sit inside the running US-Iran conflict that has narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz.
