1c:T403,The Supreme Court turned down the Justice Department's push to let the Postal Service roll out tighter rules on mail ballots for the November midterms. North Carolina and Alabama already started sending ballots this week, with more states set to follow in the next few days. The ruling keeps a Boston federal judge's block in place, so the new restrictions stay on hold through the congressional elections.

This setup holds until at least November unless the administration files a fresh request after the midterms or a different court case reaches the justices with new facts. The next clear procedural step is the election itself, where mail ballots go out under the old rules. Any shift would need either a post-election appeal that the Court accepts or states changing their own plans on their own.

The order stands as the latest move in the string of last-minute legal fights over ballot deadlines and postal handling that have marked every cycle since 2020.
