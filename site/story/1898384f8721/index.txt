Officers fired shots into the air after some in the crowd broke the cordon on the 2-kilometer route. More than 170 police took part, including drone teams, and the agency Efe said the situation came under control within hours. The transfer is the latest step after the July border breach that left an estimated 13,000 migrants still in the enclave according to local authorities, though the central government puts the number lower. Most of the original 72,000 who crossed have already been sent back.

This phase of beach clearances will likely run several more days until the camp fills or the remaining migrants are moved. Next moves hinge on how fast officials finish individual identification checks inside the camp and decide each case. If the pace of returns slows or new crossings pick up from Morocco, the camp could overflow and push authorities back to open-air holding. The gap between local and central headcounts on total migrants still in Ceuta is the clearest indicator that the operational picture remains unsettled.
