Shots heard as police start moving migrants from Ceuta beaches

Spanish authorities in Ceuta have housed migrants who crossed the border from Morocco in July on the territory's beaches for the past two months. The enclave covers 18.5 square kilometers and sits on the north African coast. Local officials put the number still present at 13,000 while the central government in Madrid gives a lower figure. Most of the original 72,000 who entered in that single breach have already been returned.

On Friday morning at about 7 a.m. local time police began moving groups along a two-kilometer route from the beaches to a new tented camp in the port that holds 1,700 people. More than 3,000 reached the site in the first hours of the operation. Officers deployed 170 personnel including drone teams to keep order. When crowds broke through the cordon and jostled for places shots were fired into the air. Video recorded young men running in the dark before the line was restored and transfers continued.

The camp now holds several hundred migrants while identification and status checks proceed. Ceuta's mayor told the European Parliament this month that the had altered daily life in the city. The Spanish government describes the situation as both a humanitarian and matter inside the enclave.

More than 170 police took part, including drone teams, and the agency Efe said the situation came under control within hours. The transfer is the latest step after the July border breach that left an estimated 13,000 migrants still in the enclave according to local authorities, though the central government puts the number lower. Most of the original 72,000 who crossed have already been sent back. This phase of beach clearances will likely run several more days until the camp fills or the remaining migrants are moved. Next moves hinge on how fast officials finish individual identification checks inside the camp and decide each case. If the pace of returns slows or new crossings pick up from Morocco, the camp could overflow and push authorities back to open-air holding. The gap between local and central headcounts on total migrants still in Ceuta is the clearest indicator that the operational picture remains unsettled. Horizon: 7-14 days until camp capacity forces the next procedural shift.
