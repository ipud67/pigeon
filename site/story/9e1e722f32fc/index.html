Russia causes new power outages in five Ukrainian oblasts

Overview
New power outages were recorded in Kyiv, Donetsk, Sumy, Odesa and Kharkiv oblasts on the morning of 1 September as a result of Russian missile and drone strikes.
Short history
Russian missile and drone strikes hit energy facilities in Ukraine on 1 September 2026. The attacks caused new power outages in five oblasts: Kyiv, Donetsk, Sumy, Odesa and Kharkiv. This is the latest in a series of Russian strikes on Ukrainian power that began in October 2022 and have continued through repeated waves in 2023, 2024 and 2025.
Predictive model
Russia hit Ukraine’s power grid again with missiles and drones early on 1 September, knocking out electricity in five oblasts: Kyiv, Donetsk, Sumy, Odesa and Kharkiv. This fits the pattern of repeated strikes on energy sites that has run since the first big wave in late 2022. Repair crews are already moving in, but full restoration usually takes days to weeks depending on how deep the damage is. The outages will likely drag on through the coming weeks as winter nears and demand rises. Next moves depend on whether Russia keeps the pace of strikes or Ukraine manages to shoot down a higher share of incoming drones and missiles. A clear drop in attack frequency would show only if Russian launch numbers fall or Ukrainian air defenses visibly improve their intercept rate. No single indicator yet points to a lasting change in either side’s targeting or protection tactics. Horizon estimate: outages in these five oblasts continue at least through mid-September, possibly longer if fresh strikes hit repair work.
