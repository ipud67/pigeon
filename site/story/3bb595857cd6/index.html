PAKISTAN
September 13, 2026 · 16:28 UTC

PM Shehbaz announces 'relief scheme' for bikes, autos, 800cc vehicles amid rising fuel prices

Pakistan imports nearly all its fuel and sets retail prices every two weeks according to a formula that passes global oil costs straight to drivers. The country has run this system since the last major price spiral in 2022. Motorcycles and small autos make up more than two-thirds of road vehicles on Pakistani roads.

On 13 September 2026 Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif announced a relief scheme that cuts the effective cost of fuel for motorcycles, rickshaws and cars up to 800cc. The measure came after global oil prices climbed for two straight months once renewed fighting narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz.

The scheme hands those vehicle owners a direct offset against the latest round of pump-price increases. It sits inside a supply route already strained by Houthi attacks on the Bab al-Mandab passage that Saudi oil now uses more heavily. The government has not said how long the relief will last or how it will be funded.

Predictive model
Pakistan's PM Shehbaz announced a relief scheme for motorcycle, auto, and 800cc vehicle users on September 13 to ease the hit from higher fuel costs. The move comes after two months of climbing prices tied to renewed fighting in the US-Iran conflict that has narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz. That fight picked up in late February with US and Israeli strikes, saw a brief pause under the June Islamabad Memorandum that collapsed on July 8, and has since featured Iranian moves to shut the strait, tanker strikes, and Gulf rerouting that cut Hormuz crossings. The scheme is the latest in a string of government steps in Pakistan to blunt the domestic fallout from the Hormuz disruption that began with the July 12 IRGC closure attempt and the August 19 UAE cutoff of trade with Iran. Observable indicators now are the sustained weekly strike-counterstrike rhythm since early March, the pattern of oil-route threats pulling in Bab al-Mandab and Houthi activity, and the absence of any new ceasefire talks since the July collapse. This relief measure will likely stay in place at least through the current quarterly fuel pricing cycle and could extend if Hormuz crossings remain below normal levels. Next steps hinge on whether US-Gulf escort operations restore steady tanker flow or if further tanker incidents trigger another price spike that forces Islamabad to widen the subsidy. Without fresh indicators on tanker volumes or a negotiating window, the duration is hard to pin tighter. Horizon: 3-6 months, tied to any shift in Hormuz transit numbers.
