19:T5b2,Thailand will cut the visa-free stay for Israeli passport holders from 60 days to 30 days starting September 15. The move keeps Israel on the list of 60 countries that still get visa-free entry but shortens the clock. Israeli tour operators and airlines have already flagged the change in booking systems; Thai immigration posts at airports will begin stamping the new 30-day limit on arrival.

The policy sits on top of repeated earlier tweaks: Thailand first extended visa-free stays for many nationalities in 2023-2024 to pull back tourism numbers after COVID, then began trimming durations again in 2025 as overstays and local complaints rose. Israeli arrivals grew sharply in that window, so Bangkok picked them for the next cut.

No court case or bilateral treaty blocks the change; it is a straightforward cabinet decision that takes effect in ten days. The only visible off-ramp would be a fresh Thai government order reversing the 30-day cap before it beds in. Absent that reversal, the shorter stay becomes standard procedure at every Thai entry point.

It runs at least through the high season that starts in November. Look for Thai immigration stats in October to show whether Israeli bookings drop or visitors simply compress their trips. Next likely move is either quiet extension of the cut to more countries or a quiet reversal if hotel occupancy in Phuket and Bangkok falls hard.
