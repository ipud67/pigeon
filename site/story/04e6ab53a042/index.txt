The Constitution gives Congress the power to regulate commerce with foreign nations under Article I, Section 8, Clause 3, the same clause the First Congress used to pass the Tariff Act of 1789 and set trade policy from the start. Founders saw sanctions and trade restrictions as tools for the political branches to protect American interests without keeping a large standing army, a worry spelled out in Federalist 8 and the Second Amendment’s militia clauses. The bill reaches the president’s desk for his judgment, matching the Decision of 1789 that placed foreign-affairs execution in the executive. Life comes first: any measure that shortens a war and reduces the killing of civilians in Ukraine serves the core purpose of government stated in the Declaration — to secure the right to life. The founding generation backed this approach when Washington called out the militia to enforce federal law in the Whiskey Rebellion of 1794, showing that Congress and the president together could act firmly abroad and at home to keep the peace. The measure now moves to the full House for a vote.19:T492,The House Rules Committee cleared the bipartisan Russia sanctions bill on September 15. This is the latest step in legislation first pushed by the late Sen. Lindsey Graham that targets anyone working for the Russian government who blocks or drags out peace talks with Ukraine. Next it heads to the full House floor for a vote.

The bill moves forward because the committee approved the text and set the rules for debate. Passage there is a procedural gate that bills must clear before the chamber takes them up. If the House passes it, the Senate would need to act on its own version or take this one up to send it to the president.

How long this stays alive depends on floor scheduling and whether enough members in both chambers back the expanded penalties. The measure gains traction only if leadership puts it on the calendar and members vote yes. Without those votes or if talks with Russia advance faster than lawmakers can act, the bill stalls.

It could reach the House floor in the coming weeks if the majority schedules it. Or it sits if other priorities push it back.
