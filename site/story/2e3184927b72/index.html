Metropolitan Nashville Airport Authority approves plan to rename airport after Dolly Parton

The Metropolitan Nashville Airport Authority has run the city's main airport since the 1960s under the name Nashville International. Dolly Parton, born in Sevier County in 1946, built a career in country music and business that made her one of the state's most recognized figures. She had turned down earlier proposals to attach her name to the airport while she was still living.

Late last month, Parton died at age 80 after a short battle with cancer. On September 11 the airport authority board voted to rename the facility in her honor.

The decision sets in motion the legal and signage changes required to put her name on the airport. The authority has not yet released a timeline for when the new name takes effect or how much the project will cost.

Thinking ahead
The Metropolitan Nashville Airport Authority voted to rename the facility after Dolly Parton. She had turned down the idea while alive but died at 80 from cancer late last month. The vote sets up the next procedural step: formal paperwork with the FAA to change the airport code and signage. The rename clears the main local hurdle. FAA approval usually takes months once the authority files. If the FAA signs off the code switches; if it flags costs or confusion the board would have to revisit the plan. No other bodies hold a veto at this stage. The change is likely to stick and roll out over the next year. The authority has already lined up the vote and the FAA process is standard once started. Horizon: rename takes effect in 9 to 14 months barring FAA hold-up.
