Florida carries out its 15th execution of the year for the 1996 murder of a young man

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlineFlorida ejecuta a su condenado número quince del año por el asesinato de un joven en 1996

Florida has carried out the death penalty by lethal injection since the state supreme court reinstated capital punishment in 1976 after the U.S. Supreme Court allowed it again. The process requires a governor to sign a death warrant once all direct appeals and clemency reviews end. Republican Gov. Ron DeSantis has signed the warrants that produced a steady pace of executions in recent years.

On September 10 Daniel Owen Conahan Jr., a 72-year-old former nurse convicted in the 1996 murder of a young man, received a lethal injection at Florida State Prison in Raiford. The execution began at 6:12 p.m. local time under the warrant DeSantis signed.

The state has now carried out 15 executions in 2026, the highest annual total in Florida in more than two decades. Conahan’s death closes the legal case that began with his 1999 conviction and sentence.

Thinking ahead
Florida carried out its 15th execution of 2026 on September 10 when Daniel Owen Conahan Jr. received a lethal injection at Florida State Prison. The state has now completed one more death sentence this year than it did in all of 2025. This pace points to the current lineup of death warrants and appeals. Florida has 14 more inmates with active warrants or scheduled dates through the end of the year. Unless a federal court grants a stay or the governor halts a warrant, the count will keep climbing at roughly one every three weeks. The next visible change would come from a successful last-minute appeal in one of those pending cases or a shift in the governor’s warrant schedule. Execution count likely holds steady or ticks up through December.
