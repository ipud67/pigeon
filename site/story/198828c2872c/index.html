Trump admin’s fraud crackdown targets foreigners accused of infiltrating US federal programs

The United States has run federal programs that buy equipment and services for the military and other agencies since the middle of the last century. Those programs require suppliers to meet exact standards on parts, testing, and origin. Over the past decade inspectors have found growing numbers of and substandard components reaching government warehouses, many traced to overseas sellers who used false paperwork to win contracts.

On September 11, 2026, the Trump added Turkish national Onur Simsek to the FBI's Most Wanted list. Simsek stands accused of running a scheme that delivered fake parts for U.S. military gear through defense contracts. The government says the parts failed required specifications and that Simsek used false statements to get past federal vetting.

The addition of Simsek to the list marks the latest public step in a broader effort to bar foreign suppliers accused of similar fraud from federal programs. Investigators now hold an active for his arrest, and the case sits inside an ongoing review of how overseas vendors enter and move through the procurement system.
