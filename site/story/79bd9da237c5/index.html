NORTH KOREA
September 15, 2026 · 01:22 UTC

N. Korea's ex-defense official linked to nuclear, missile programs dies

North Korea has run a nuclear weapons and ballistic missile program since the 1950s under three generations of the Kim family. The program expanded sharply after Kim Jong-un took power in late 2011, with frequent public tests of rockets and warheads that drew international and military alerts from South Korea and the United States. Senior officers in the munitions industry and the armed forces oversaw the technical work and appeared beside Kim at launch sites.

On Sept. 14, 2026, former Vice Defense Minister Yun Tong-hyon died. State media reported the death the next day and said Kim sent a wreath and expressed condolences. The Korean Central News Agency described Yun as having spent years in responsible posts in the munitions sector and credited him with strengthening the military’s technical capabilities. Yun had stood next to Kim during multiple rocket and missile tests in the mid-2010s, held the rank of colonel general since 2015, and in 2016 publicly warned of preemptive nuclear strikes if the regime’s authority was threatened. He had stepped back from public view after 2018.

The death removes one of the last regular faces from the early years of Kim’s nuclear buildup. The program itself continues without reported change in pace or direction.
