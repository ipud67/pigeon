Amnesty says Iran committed 'crimes against humanity' in protests crackdown

Iranian security forces have put down street protests since at least 2019. The largest wave began in September 2022 after the death of Mahsa Amini in morality-police and continued for months with demonstrations in cities across the country. Authorities responded with mass arrests, live ammunition, and lethal force that killed hundreds.

On 16 September 2026 International released a report that classified the 2022 crackdown as crimes against humanity. The organization said the same pattern of lethal force, , and torture had appeared again in demonstrations that broke out this year, only on a larger scale. The report called for an international criminal-justice process to hold those responsible to account.

The current US-Iran armed conflict, now centered on control of the Strait of Hormuz, continues alongside these internal security operations. The latest Amnesty findings sit inside that wider war, which began with US and Israeli strikes in late February 2026 and has seen repeated cycles of bombardment, tanker attacks, and blockades through the summer.
