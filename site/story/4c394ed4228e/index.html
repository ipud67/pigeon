Liberia: 29 Liberians Seek Urgent Repatriation From South Africa

Liberia has run a formal program for years that lets citizens abroad apply for voluntary return when they face legal or economic trouble in their host country. The process requires the applicant to notify Liberian authorities, who then coordinate travel documents and transport with the foreign government. South Africa has hosted one of the larger Liberian communities in Africa since the end of Liberia’s civil wars in 2003, with many entering on temporary visas that later expired.

On September 10, 2026, twenty-nine Liberians in South Africa submitted a joint request for immediate repatriation under that program. The group cited expiring permits, lack of work rights, and fears of arrest or deportation as the reasons they wanted to leave at once. Liberian officials received the and began the paperwork to bring them back.

The request now sits with Liberia’s foreign ministry and repatriation committee, which must arrange flights, clear any outstanding South African fines or charges, and prepare reception in Monrovia. Until those steps finish, the twenty-nine remain in legal limbo in South Africa.
