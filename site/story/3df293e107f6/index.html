First National Dialogue for a Culture of Peace Held; Interior Ministry Follows Up on 1968 and 1971 Events

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlineCelebran Primer Diálogo Nacional por una Cultura de Paz; Segob da seguimiento a hechos de 1968 y 1971

Mexico has seen organized crime groups control territory and run rackets in parts of the country since the mid-2000s. Federal forces deployed against the largest cartels in 2006. That campaign produced splinter groups that fought both the state and one another. Over the next two decades successive alternated between high-intensity military operations and negotiated local truces. Violence persisted in states such as Guerrero, Michoacán, and Zacatecas.

On September 19 2026 the Interior Ministry convened the first National Dialogue for a Culture of Peace. Officials from the ministry met with groups, local leaders, and academics to record past experiences and collect written proposals. The ministry stated it would examine records of the 1968 student massacre and the 1971 Halconazo killings as part of the same effort to address long-standing patterns of state and non-state force.

The dialogue now sits inside the ministry’s standing mandate to coordinate security policy across federal, state, and municipal lines. Its working groups are scheduled to produce recommendations that the ministry must route into existing inter-agency councils. Those councils retain final authority over deployment of the National Guard and allocation of federal anti-crime funds.
