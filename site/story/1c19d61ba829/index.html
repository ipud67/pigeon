Putin and Iranian president arrive in India for summit focusing on war

Russian and Iranian leaders have met at summits in India for years as part of the BRICS group of nations that expanded to 11 members this year. The group formed its current shape after several countries joined in 2024 and 2025, giving Iran and Russia formal seats alongside Brazil, China, India, and others at annual gatherings that cover trade, energy routes, and security issues.

On September 11 2026 Vladimir Putin and the Iranian president landed in India for talks that will lead into the full BRICS summit. The schedule now centers on the fighting between the United States, Israel, and Iran that began with strikes in late February 2026, narrowed to repeated clashes over the Strait of Hormuz after the Islamabad Memorandum collapsed on July 8, and produced fresh naval moves and oil price swings through August.

The summit stands as the first time the full 11-member group gathers while sustained US strikes continue against Iranian and storage sites and while Gulf states have cut trade links with Tehran over missile incidents near their waters. Discussions will turn on how the members handle the narrowed contest for control of the strait that carries much of the world's seaborne oil.

Putin and the Iranian president landed in India on 11 September for a BRICS summit where the main items are the fighting in Ukraine and the US-Iran exchanges over the Strait of Hormuz. The summit follows the July collapse of the Islamabad Memorandum, the renewed US strikes, Iran's attempt to shut the strait, and the August UAE cutoff of trade with Tehran. Russia has kept its ties with Iran intact through the war and supplies components that appear in Iranian strike packages. Talks at the summit will likely produce joint statements that repeat calls for ceasefires in both conflicts and back Iran's right to transit in the strait. No side has shown signs of changing its red lines on Hormuz control or on sanctions relief. The procedural next step after the summit is a fresh round of back-channel contacts between Washington and Tehran, possibly routed through Gulf or Indian intermediaries. The war narrows or widens according to whether Hormuz crossings stay open and whether either side hits new land targets. Right now the pattern of weekly strikes and tanker incidents holds steady. This round of diplomacy runs another two to three weeks before the next visible move on the ground or at the table.
