UN delegates open talks on nuclear risks and illegal guns

The First Committee of the United Nations General Assembly has run the annual disarmament and international security session since the early days of the Cold War. It sets the agenda for treaty compliance talks, reviews nuclear non-proliferation measures, and hears positions from regional groups on arms control. The Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons entered into force in 1970 and requires non-nuclear states to forgo weapons while nuclear states pursue disarmament. Separate treaties have since established nuclear-weapon-free zones that ban the stationing, testing, or use of nuclear arms inside defined territories and require verification.

On 7 October 2026 the committee approved its programme of work and began the general debate. Delegates from CARICOM, the Central Asian Nuclear-Weapon-Free Zone, the New Agenda , and the Russian Federation addressed the floor in sequence. Jamaica, speaking for CARICOM, pointed to ongoing illegal firearms flows that feed gang violence and organized crime in the Caribbean. Kazakhstan noted that its zone remains the only one located entirely in the Northern Hemisphere. Mexico, for the New Agenda Coalition, recorded that the New START treaty between the United States and Russia had expired with no successor, leaving the two largest nuclear arsenals without binding numerical limits, inspections, or monitoring. Russia, also speaking for Belarus, stated its continued support for the Non-Proliferation Treaty while citing NATO activities inside the Ukraine crisis as a source of direct security threats to both countries.

The debate now turns on whether member states will table concrete resolutions on new verification measures, renewed talks between the largest nuclear powers, and expansion of nuclear-weapon-free zones before the committee closes its session.

Thinking ahead
The First Committee opened its general debate on October 7 after approving its work schedule. Delegates from CARICOM, the Central Asian Nuclear-Weapon-Free Zone, the New Agenda Coalition, and Russia each repeated long-standing positions on nuclear non-proliferation treaties, regional weapon-free zones, the lapse of New START limits between the US and Russia, and NATO actions tied to the Ukraine conflict. These statements follow years of annual sessions where the same blocs have tabled resolutions on exactly these points without producing new binding agreements. The pattern of repeated calls without follow-through has held for more than a decade. No new treaty text or verification mechanism has advanced in the committee since the last major zone treaty entered into force. The procedural calendar now moves to specific resolution drafts over the next several weeks, where the same language is expected to reappear in near-identical form. This round is likely to run its full scheduled course through November with parallel debates on nuclear risk, conventional arms including illegal firearms, and outer space security. Any shift would require one of the nuclear-armed states to table a verifiable successor to New START or a new zone protocol that gains consensus support from the opposing bloc. Those indicators remain absent. Horizon: Committee wraps its session in six weeks with resolutions passed but no new treaties signed.
