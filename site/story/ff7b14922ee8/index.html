Arequipa adds new protected heritage house with UNESCO Blue Shield

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlineArequipa suma nueva casona protegida con el Escudo Azul de la UNESCO

The historic center of Arequipa has held protected status since UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage site in 2000. That listing covers a grid of colonial buildings built from white volcanic stone, many of them private homes that face daily wear from tourism, traffic, and seismic risk. The city government and the national Ministry of Culture have run a program for years that marks selected structures with signs and digital markers to track their condition and limit changes.

On September 10 the Municipality of Arequipa placed a Blue Shield emblem on the house at 311 San Francisco Street. The shield is the international marker created by UNESCO to identify cultural property that must stay off-limits during armed conflict or disaster. Workers also installed a QR code beside the door so anyone with a phone can pull up the building’s official record.

The new markers give the house an added layer of formal recognition inside the existing heritage rules. Local inspectors now have a visible record they can check during routine patrols or after any earthquake, and the QR code feeds real-time updates back to municipal and ministry databases.
