Netanyahu visits school in Ramle hit by missile during Iran war as Israel classes start nationwide

Overview
The prime minister, alongside Education Minister Yoav Kisch, visited the school and talked with teaching and staff, as well as the students on their first day of class.
Short history
Netanyahu visited a school in Ramle hit by an Iranian missile as Israeli schools reopened nationwide on 1 September 2026. The US and Israel began strikes on Iran on 28 February 2026. Iran replied the same day with Operation True Promise IV, firing missiles and drones at Israel and US bases in the Gulf. The conflict settled into weekly exchanges that included attacks on Israeli civilian sites. A June 17 between the US and Iran lifted the naval and Iran's closure of the Strait of Hormuz and set a 60-day negotiation period. That deal collapsed on 8 July after Iran struck commercial tankers; the US resumed sustained nightly strikes and the fight narrowed to control of Hormuz. As of 1 September, classes resumed across Israel while the war continued. Netanyahu and Education Minister Yoav Kisch met staff and students at the damaged Ramle school.
Predictive model
Netanyahu showed up at a damaged school in Ramle on the first day of classes while the rest of Israel opened schools nationwide. The visit puts the prime minister in front of teachers, staff, and kids right after a missile hit the building during the ongoing Iran war. This is the latest sign that the conflict, now narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz, still reaches deep inside Israel even after nine months of strikes and counter-strikes. The war has run on a pattern of weekly exchanges, failed talks, broken ceasefires, and repeated Iranian attempts to shut the strait since the Islamabad Memorandum collapsed in July. Israeli leaders have kept public appearances tied to visible damage from those attacks to show the fight continues at home. No new ceasefire talks or major ground moves have surfaced in the last six weeks. How much longer this runs depends on whether Iran can keep trying to close Hormuz or whether the US and Israel decide to widen strikes again. Right now the daily rhythm of missile launches, air defense intercepts, and limited naval clashes shows no sign of stopping. Netanyahu’s school stop does not change the military track but keeps the domestic front visible. Next step looks like more of the same: another round of strikes, another Iranian response, and Israeli officials continuing to tour hit sites while schools stay open under alert. Horizon: at least another 4–6 weeks unless a new diplomatic opening appears.
