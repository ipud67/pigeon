The visit puts the prime minister in front of teachers, staff, and kids right after a missile hit the building during the ongoing Iran war. This is the latest sign that the conflict, now narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz, still reaches deep inside Israel even after nine months of strikes and counter-strikes.

The war has run on a pattern of weekly exchanges, failed talks, broken ceasefires, and repeated Iranian attempts to shut the strait since the Islamabad Memorandum collapsed in July. Israeli leaders have kept public appearances tied to visible damage from those attacks to show the fight continues at home. No new ceasefire talks or major ground moves have surfaced in the last six weeks.

How much longer this runs depends on whether Iran can keep trying to close Hormuz or whether the US and Israel decide to widen strikes again. Right now the daily rhythm of missile launches, air defense intercepts, and limited naval clashes shows no sign of stopping. Netanyahu’s school stop does not change the military track but keeps the domestic front visible.

Next step looks like more of the same: another round of strikes, another Iranian response, and Israeli officials continuing to tour hit sites while schools stay open under alert.
