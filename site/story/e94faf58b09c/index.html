Minister flies to Pilbara after report of up to 15 kids living unsupervised

Child protection in remote Western Australia has operated for years through a state department that receives reports from residents, police and agencies about children at risk in towns across the Pilbara and Kimberley. The department holds the power to investigate those reports, remove children from homes where their safety is at stake, and place them under supervision or in care. That system came under renewed pressure after the 2022 murders of children by their mother in Port Hedland, when staff were later accused of ignoring earlier warnings about the family.

On 14 September 2026 the Child Protection Minister Jessica Stojkovski flew from Perth to Port Hedland after The Australian reported that up to 15 children, some as young as five, had been living unsupervised in a South Hedland house for some time. The newspaper said the Department of Child Protection had received multiple complaints of neglect and separate of sexual abuse in the East Kimberley yet had not acted on them. The minister left the airport without speaking to reporters and issued a written statement calling for urgent answers from her department.

The visit placed the department’s handling of the specific South Hedland case and its broader operations in the region under direct ministerial . Local elders described children left to fend for themselves as a long-standing problem and said the system needed to keep children in their homes where possible while giving families a place to seek help. The department has not released details of its response to the latest reports.
