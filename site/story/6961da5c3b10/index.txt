1d:T5c6,The US announced sanctions on the International Criminal Court on October 9 2026 cutting off transactions and resources as part of a campaign that began in the first Trump term with opposition to probes into actions in Afghanistan. Secretary of State Marco Rubio stated the measures will block any prosecution of Americans and target the court's operations while ICC President Tomoko Akane who already faces US sanctions responded that the court will keep working on cases under the Rome Statute treaty with 125 member states. The move followed the awarding of the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize to former ICC judge Navi Pillay and a statement from the Nobel Committee that referenced judges and institutions under sanction.

The sanctions add to existing US non-recognition of the court's jurisdiction which has not stopped it from issuing warrants on referrals from member states against officials from non-members such as Russia and Israel. Next steps depend on whether the court secures alternative funding from its 125 parties or faces further transaction bans that limit staff travel and case work in The Hague. Change would require either a US policy reversal after a future election or successful legal challenges in member-state courts that test the sanctions reach.

This round of measures is likely to hold through the current administration with the court continuing limited operations on existing dockets.
