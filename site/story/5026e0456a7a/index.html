Judge blocks Trump’s latest attempt to restrict birthright

A federal judge blocked President Trump’s latest executive order on birthright citizenship on September 3, 2026. The order directed federal agencies to stop recognizing citizenship for certain children born in the US to non-citizen parents. The judge cited the Supreme Court’s June 2026 ruling that struck down Trump’s first such order on the same grounds. The 14th has granted citizenship to everyone born on US soil since 1868, except for children of foreign diplomats.

Constitutional analysis
A federal judge blocked President Trump’s latest executive order aimed at restricting birthright citizenship for children of non-citizens. The ruling follows the Supreme Court’s June decision that struck down Trump’s earlier order on the same subject. The Constitution’s 14th Amendment states that all persons born or naturalized in the United States and subject to its jurisdiction are citizens. The founders built the nation on the principle that government’s first duty is to secure the right to life and the equal protection of laws for those who live under them. James Madison in Federalist 43 described the Constitution as a charter that protects the fundamental rights of the people within its borders. The order tested the reach of executive power under Article II against the clear text of the amendment ratified in 1868. Federal judges hold lifetime appointments under Article III to check such actions, yet the measure returns the question to the elected branches and the text the framers left. The case now heads toward further review.

The fight stays in the courts. No change until an appellate court or the Supreme Court alters the June precedent. Horizon: at least 12-18 months.
