Mozambique: Senior Malawian Officials Arrested Over Fraudulent Transfer of Over 400,000 Dollars to Mozambique

Overview
[AIM] Maputo -- The Malawian Police Service (MPS) has arrested two senior officials from the National Oil Company of Malawi (NOCMA) over the alleged fraudulent transfer of over 400,000 US dollars to Mozambique, which was aimed at covering loading and clearing charges for fuel shipments at the central port of Beira and northern port of Nacala.
Short history
Malawi’s National Oil Company (NOCMA) began importing fuel through Mozambique’s ports of Beira and Nacala in the early 2010s. The company paid loading and clearing fees directly to Mozambican handlers for each shipment. On 1 September 2026 Malawian police arrested two senior NOCMA officials after investigators found that more than 400,000 US dollars had been wired to accounts in Mozambique without proper documentation or approval. The transfers were recorded as payments for port services but are now under investigation as fraudulent. The case remains with the Malawian Police Service.
Predictive model
Malawian police have arrested two senior NOCMA officials for sending more than 400,000 US dollars to Mozambique to pay fake loading and clearing fees at Beira and Nacala ports. The transfers fit a pattern of fuel-shipment fraud that has surfaced before in Malawi’s oil-import chain. Next step is formal charges and a court appearance where prosecutors must show evidence of the fake invoices and the money trail. If the money is recovered and the Mozambican partners are identified, the case could wrap up inside six months. If the officials fight the charges or point higher up the ladder, it stretches into 2027 with more arrests. Right now the indicators point to a contained prosecution rather than a wide collapse of the fuel-import system. Horizon: charges filed and first court date by end of October 2026.
