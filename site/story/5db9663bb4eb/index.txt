1c:T62e,Greg Lui, 38, owner of Earthmade Computer Inc. in City of Industry, California, got arrested on a three-count federal indictment for smuggling over $300 million in high-end servers packed with U.S.-made GPUs used for super intelligence computing. The scheme ran from 2023 into 2024: his company bought the restricted gear from American makers, shipped it through Malaysia and Singapore where licenses were not needed, then rerouted it to end users in China while filing false paperwork that hid the real destination. This marks the latest step in U.S. enforcement of export rules on advanced chips that took effect after 2022 restrictions tightened controls on items that could boost other nations' military computing power.

The case now sits with federal prosecutors in the Central District of California. Lui faces an initial court appearance and arraignment this afternoon. From here the process runs on standard federal timelines: discovery of evidence, possible plea negotiations, and trial if no deal is reached.

How long this runs depends on whether Lui pleads or fights the charges, and whether co-conspirators or overseas partners get named in follow-on actions. The situation changes only if the Department of Commerce updates the export control list or if courts rule on what counts as sufficient proof of knowledge and false statements. No indicators yet point to either shift.

Next likely step is pretrial motions and document unsealing that lay out the full chain of payments and emails already referenced in the indictment.
