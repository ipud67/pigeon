TAIWAN
November 6, 2026 · 07:01 UTC

Taiwan central bank sold dollars in October and cut forex reserves to 596.4 billion dollars

The bank also holds U.S. Treasury securities that pay interest through swap deals.

At the end of October the reserves dropped to 596.437 billion dollars, a decline of 4.308 billion from the month before. The central bank sold U.S. dollars and bought Taiwan dollars on several trading days after foreign institutional investors pulled a net 28 billion dollars out of local stocks and bonds, including repatriation. That net selling reached the equivalent of 9.09 billion dollars in a single session and pushed the Taiwan dollar down 0.626 against the U.S. dollar, or 1.93 percent, while the Taiex index lost 5.67 percent.

The reserves now sit below the 600 billion dollar line for the second month in a row. Foreign investor holdings of Taiwan stocks, bonds and deposits fell to 1.743 trillion dollars, equal to 292 percent of the reserves. The central bank stated it will keep to limit volatility and maintain enough reserves to handle further outflows.

Taiwan's central bank sold dollars and bought Taiwan dollars in October to steady the local currency after foreign institutional investors pulled a record net $28 billion. That intervention plus exchange rate moves on other currencies pushed forex reserves to $596.437 billion at month-end, down $4.308 billion from September and below the $600 billion line for the first time in a while. Foreign holdings of Taiwan stocks, bonds, and deposits dropped to $1.743 trillion from $1.927 trillion the prior month, now equal to 292 percent of reserves versus 321 percent before. The Taiwan dollar weakened 1.93 percent against the greenback while the Taiex index fell 5.67 percent. The central bank says it will keep enough reserves on hand to handle further outflows. This pressure has run for two straight months. It is likely to keep going as long as foreign investors keep selling local assets and the Taiwan dollar stays volatile. Next moves will show in the size of any fresh intervention and whether reserves drop further or stabilize around current levels.
