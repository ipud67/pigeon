France leads 11 other nations calling for against 'settlement' goods

European governments have regulated trade with the West Bank and Gaza for decades under rules that treat those territories as distinct from Israel proper. Those rules date back to the 1990s Oslo framework and the 2001 EU-Israel trade agreement that excluded settlement products from preferential .

On September 8 France joined Canada, Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Ireland, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Spain, Sweden and the United Kingdom in a joint call for fresh sanctions that would block or tax goods produced in Israeli settlements. The twelve foreign ministers issued the statement after months of internal EU debate over enforcement gaps that let some settlement merchandise enter markets under Israeli labels.

The joint letter now sits with the European Commission, which holds the power to propose updated import controls. Member states must still agree on the exact list of products and the legal mechanism before any new restrictions take effect.

Thinking ahead
France and 11 other governments issued a joint call for sanctions on goods from settlements. The group lists Canada, Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Ireland, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Spain, Sweden and the UK. Next procedural step sits with the EU Council and national legislatures that must vote on any formal import bans or labeling rules. The call follows years of separate national measures on labeling and import checks. It lands as EU member states debate updates to trade rules with Israel. Momentum depends on how many of the 12 governments follow through with draft legislation and whether the full EU adopts a common position. Without concrete votes or commission proposals the effort stays at the statement stage. A shift would show in filed bills in at least half the listed capitals or an EU working group schedule. This round likely runs 4 to 8 months before any policy change or quiet drop-off. Horizon: early 2027.
