WESTERN AUSTRALIA
September 16, 2026 · 21:43 UTC

One Nation volunteer previously promoted neo-Nazi ideologies

One Nation has run candidates and taken volunteers in Western Australian state elections for more than a decade. The party has faced repeated public questions about who it allows to represent it after Senator Pauline Hanson announced in June that four branches had been closed because of infiltration by people she described as extremists.

On 27 August a man named David Donis handed out how-to-vote cards for One Nation at a polling booth in the Secret Harbour by-election and later stood near the stage at the party's victory event. The same man had been identified three years earlier in a 2023 Appeals Tribunal decision that reviewed an army reservist's security clearance. The tribunal accepted evidence that Donis, as founding member and president of the now-deregistered Society of Western Australian Nationalists, had promoted racist and neo-Nazi ideologies.

One Nation stated that Donis was never a member, that the party does not condone such views, and that it would not permit him to continue any association. WA One Nation leader Rod Caddies said any extremist found inside the party would be expelled. A senior Liberal source said the party had received warnings for years about its candidate and volunteer screening.

Thinking ahead

Pauline Hanson shut four branches in June after saying they had been infiltrated by extremists, and senior Liberals have said the party has had years of warnings on vetting. This episode will likely stay contained to internal party discipline and public statements. Next steps hinge on whether One Nation releases details on its vetting changes or whether another public link to Donis surfaces that forces a formal expulsion statement. Without new tribunal documents, fresh incidents at polling booths, or a candidate tie-in, the story has few procedural legs left. The matter probably fades inside two weeks.
