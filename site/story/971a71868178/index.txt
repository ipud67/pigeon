The notice told David Streever to stop his criticism or face prosecution. The court granted part of his request for a preliminary injunction against the agency relying on that notice, issuing similar threats, or acting on them.

The First Amendment protects the right of the people to speak against government officials and their actions. The founders wrote it to guard exactly this kind of sharp dissent. They had lived under British officials who used threats of prosecution to quiet opposition before the Revolution.

Government exists first to secure the unalienable rights listed in the Declaration of Independence, beginning with the right to life. The killing of Alex Pretti in Minneapolis that angered Streever is the kind of event that makes plain why the people must be free to criticize how officials handle law, order, and public safety. The Preamble names domestic tranquility as a core purpose of the new Constitution so citizens can live in peace under equal laws.

The founding generation knew that vague warnings from federal agents could chill speech as surely as direct censorship. James Madison in Federalist 43 warned against any construction of federal power that would let the government stretch its reach into areas reserved to the people. The First Amendment stands as one of those clear limits.

The judge’s order blocks further use of the notice but does not reach the separate question of whether the agents had authority under any statute to investigate the email as a threat. 1b:T51f,A federal judge in New York ruled on September 16 that a warning notice delivered by DHS agents to David Streever after his critical email to a former ICE director violated the First Amendment. The notice told Streever to stop his criticism or face possible prosecution. The court issued a preliminary injunction that blocks DHS from using that notice, sending similar warnings, or acting on its threats.

This case sits on a ladder of prior incidents where federal agents delivered comparable notices to individuals voicing criticism of immigration enforcement, including one to poll worker Paigelynne Gonyea in Syracuse earlier this year.

The injunction lasts until the full lawsuit reaches final judgment. Streever’s legal team from the Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression will push for permanent relief and fees. DHS can appeal the ruling to the D.C. Circuit or adjust its internal procedures for handling complaints against employees. The situation changes only if an appeals court reverses the injunction or if DHS drops further enforcement attempts tied to the original notice.

Next step likely stays inside the district court for discovery on the agents’ conduct and any pattern of similar notices.
