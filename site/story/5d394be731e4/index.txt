The Constitution grants Congress power to lay and collect duties in Article I, Section 8, Clause 1, to pay debts, provide for the common defense, and promote the general welfare of the United States. The first Congress exercised that power right away with the Tariff Act of 1789, which imposed duties on imported goods to generate revenue and shield early American industry while the new nation secured its independence. Founders such as Alexander Hamilton in Federalist No. 12 argued that tariffs on foreign imports would strengthen the union by funding government without direct taxes on the people and by encouraging domestic manufacturing so the country did not depend on rivals for essential goods. This solar decision follows that tradition of using enumerated trade powers to guard American labor and self-reliance instead of leaving the field open to subsidized overseas output.
