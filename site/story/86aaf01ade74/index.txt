23:T505,A federal judge in California ruled Friday that DHS broke the 2006 Post-Katrina law by directing FEMA to slash its CORE on-call staff from about 23,000 to 11,500. The cuts started as term renewals under a hiring freeze even though DHS itself was exempt from the freeze. The judge called the staffing target pulled from thin air with no record of reasoned steps and said both DHS and FEMA acted unlawfully by overriding FEMA’s own personnel authority.

This dispute has run since unions sued in 2025 over the planned reductions. The immediate next step is the two sides must meet on the scope of relief. If they fail to agree, they file a joint statement by October 9 and the judge will then order specific fixes.

The ruling blocks the cuts for now but leaves room for DHS to rebuild a documented case that meets the law’s test for reasoned decision-making. How long it lasts hinges on whether the administration files new paperwork that survives another court check or whether Congress steps in to clarify the 2006 limits. Without one of those two moves the standoff continues at least until the October filing and likely into early 2027.

Next rung likely lands in settlement talks or a revised DHS plan by late fall.
