22:T463,The D.C. Police Union filed a class grievance and misconduct complaints after internal affairs used Flock license plate reader cameras to track officers suspected of misconduct in July. The department denied the grievance, called the use appropriate for the investigation, and issued a new directive that limits department-wide access to the system. The union says the full capabilities of the cameras remain unknown and that guardrails have not been negotiated or implemented. The grievance now heads to arbitration between the union and the department. The misconduct complaints against managers remain open.

This dispute is likely to run at least until an arbitrator rules, which typically takes months in D.C. police contract cases. It could end there if the arbitrator upholds the department's position, or it could widen if the union wins limits on camera access and forces public disclosure of the system's rules. The next clear procedural step is the arbitration hearing itself; without a union win or a separate court order, internal affairs keeps the tool for officer cases.
