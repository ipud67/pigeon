ICE agent charged in Minneapolis shooting taken into custody after Texas extradition standoff

The confrontation happened on January 14 during Operation Metro Surge, the federal immigration enforcement push ordered under President Trump that sent ICE teams into Minneapolis and set off weeks of street protests and clashes with agents. In one apartment raid that day, agent Christian Castro shot Venezuelan national Julio Cesar Sosa-Celis in the leg through a closed door while children were inside the unit. Castro then told Homeland Security superiors that the man and others had come at him with a snow shovel and broom; body-camera video showed otherwise.

Minnesota prosecutors filed state assault and false-report charges. When Castro returned to his home state of Texas after the shooting, officials there held him for ninety days on a governor’s . Texas Governor Greg Abbott declined to sign extradition papers sent by Minnesota Governor Tim Walz, citing questions over whether Castro counted as a fugitive once the Department of Homeland Security had reassigned him home. A federal judge in Texas ordered his release on August 27 after he posted bond on separate federal charges of lying to investigators.

On September 17 Castro drove north under an agreement with Minnesota authorities to surrender at his lawyer’s office in downtown Minneapolis. Agents from the Minnesota Bureau of Criminal Apprehension took him into minutes before the scheduled handoff and booked him into Hennepin County jail on the outstanding state warrant. The case now rests on the state charges moving forward in Minnesota court.
