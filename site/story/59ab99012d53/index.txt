21:T56d,A suicide car bomb hit a police compound in Kohat on September 18, killing at least 16 people — five police, ten civilians and the attacker — and wounding more than 30. The blast damaged the mosque where officers, relatives and civilians were inside for prayers, followed by a gunfight when two more attackers tried to enter the site. President Asif Ali Zardari pointed to the TTP as the group behind it, using the term Fitna-al-Khawarij.

This sits on the ladder of rising TTP attacks in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa since the Afghan Taliban took power in 2021, with Pakistan blaming cross-border safe havens and carrying out airstrikes into Afghanistan as recently as June. Casualty counts shifted during rescue work but settled on the provincial police chief’s figure of 16 dead. No group has claimed the bombing yet.

The pattern of TTP strikes on police and security posts in the northwest has run at this pace for four years with no sustained drop. Next steps likely stay inside Pakistan’s current playbook of local sweeps, faster checkpoints in the province and more pressure on Kabul to cut TTP movement. Change would need either a measurable fall in cross-border infiltration or a shift in how Afghan authorities handle TTP logistics on their side of the line.
