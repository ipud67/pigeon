1b:T4f8,The Supreme Court stepped in on Sept. 10 and blocked Missouri from using the new congressional map passed last year. That map came after the legislature acted on a push from Trump to redraw lines and add a Republican seat. Organizers had gathered signatures for a 2026 referendum to send the map back to voters. The state supreme court ruled the petition valid and ordered the 2022 map used instead. Secretary Hoskins called it election chaos and asked the U.S. Supreme Court to pause that order. The justices granted the stay while the full case plays out.

This fight sits on top of the 2025 bill signing, the December signature drive, Hoskins’ last-day rejection in August, and the state court’s unanimous decision days before the general election. The next clear rung is the lower-court litigation on the referendum’s legality. If that court sides with the organizers the map stays frozen. If it sides with the state the new lines could return. The U.S. Supreme Court could also take the case on the merits later.

The stay holds at least until the district court rules and any appeals reach the justices again. Look for filings and hearings in the next few weeks to set the timetable.
