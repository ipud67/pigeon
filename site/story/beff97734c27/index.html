Feds paid $9.5 billion in leave costs in 2025 due to DOGE buyout

Federal agencies tracked paid administrative leave as a standard category in personnel costs for years before 2023. That year the total spent on it stood at a baseline figure that covered employees between assignments, during investigations, or in other holding periods required by civil service rules.

In 2025 the Department of Government Efficiency buyout program offered cash incentives to workers who agreed to separate. Many who accepted the offers remained on payroll in paid leave status until their exit dates cleared. A Government Office report released on September 16, 2026, shows the leave bill for that year reached $9.5 billion, a 435 percent jump from the 2023 amount.

The new total now sits on agency budgets as a one-time charge tied to the buyout wave. Congress and the executive branch must decide how to fund similar future without repeating the same leave-cost spike.

Thinking ahead
The GAO report tallied $9.5 billion spent on paid administrative leave across federal agencies in 2025, a jump tied to the buyout offers rolled out under the Department of Government Efficiency. Paid leave usage had already climbed 435 percent between 2023 and 2025 as agencies shifted workers to administrative status instead of immediate separation. Congress set the buyout rules in statute years earlier; the report simply records the cost once the program ran. Next moves sit with the appropriations committees. Lawmakers review the GAO figures during the next budget cycle and can tighten leave caps or rewrite buyout payout formulas in the fiscal 2027 spending bills. Without those changes the pattern holds: agencies keep the same authority, costs stay high, and future GAO updates will log similar or larger totals. The situation shifts only when statute changes. Until then the $9.5 billion line item repeats each year the buyouts continue at current scale. Horizon: at least through end of fiscal 2027 unless appropriations bills alter the rules.
