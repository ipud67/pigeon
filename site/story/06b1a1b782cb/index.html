KYIV OBLAST
August 28, 2026 · 20:24 UTC

Detonations continue at warehouse site in Kyiv Oblast after Russian strike, PM Koretskyi says

Overview
A fire broke out which set off detonations at warehouses in the Bucha district in Kyiv Oblast following a Russian strike, Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi said.
Short history
Russian forces struck warehouses in Bucha district, Kyiv Oblast, on 28 August 2026. A fire broke out at the site immediately after the strike. Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi stated that detonations have continued at the warehouse since then. The incident is the latest strike in the war that began with Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022.
Predictive model
Detonations are still going off at the struck warehouses in Bucha district, Kyiv Oblast, more than two hours after the Russian hit reported by Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi. Fire crews and local officials are keeping people back while the stored munitions cook off. This pattern has repeated on both sides since early 2022: strike, secondary explosions, cordon, then slow clearance. The site will keep popping until the last shells or rockets burn out or get soaked down. Next observable step is when the blasts taper enough for emergency teams to move in and assess damage. That only shifts if Russian strikes hit the same area again or if Ukrainian forces push the front line far enough to put this depot out of range. Horizon estimate: Ongoing detonations likely taper within 12-48 hours; clearance and any follow-on strikes stretch weeks.
