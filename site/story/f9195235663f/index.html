UK announces trade on Israeli settlements in occupied West Bank

The three countries have stood with the US and other allies in the armed conflict that began when the US and Israel opened strikes on Iran on 28 February 2026. That fighting settled into weekly exchanges, naval clashes in the Strait of Hormuz, a short-lived in June that lifted for 60 days of talks, and resumed US strikes after the window collapsed on 8 July. Oil prices climbed, tanker traffic slowed, and the contest narrowed to control of the strait itself.

On 8 September Britain, France and Canada announced they will ban imports of goods produced in Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank. The UK minister who made the statement accused Israel of turning a blind eye to the ethnic cleansing of Palestinians in the territory.

The new policy adds economic pressure on settlement activity at a moment when the wider conflict has already redrawn trade routes through the Gulf and drawn in statements from European and North American governments about conduct on the ground in the West Bank. The measure now sits alongside the ongoing US-led strikes and the fight for the Strait of Hormuz.

Thinking ahead
UK, France and Canada announced a joint ban on goods from Israeli settlements in the West Bank. The move follows years of EU and allied statements against settlement expansion and comes as the US–Iran armed conflict has narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz. The three governments cited continued settlement activity and related violence in the West Bank as the trigger. The ban will take effect through updated import rules in each country. Next steps hinge on whether the EU adopts a similar bloc-wide measure and on any Israeli or US legal challenges at the WTO. Observable indicators are the volume of settlement produce reaching European ports and the pace of new court filings. Without a visible drop in settlement output or a coordinated reversal by the three governments, the restrictions stay in place. A shift would require either a new trade deal that carves out exemptions or a sustained halt in settlement construction verified by satellite and customs data. This runs at least through 2027 unless one of those indicators changes.
