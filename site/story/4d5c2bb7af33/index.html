AIVD: Iran hacks into dissidents online to steal sensitive information

Translated from Dutch · read the original →
The original headlineAIVD: Iran breekt online in bij dissidenten om gevoelige informatie te stelen

Iran has tracked and surveilled its critics abroad for years through digital means. The effort reached Western soil where many opponents of the Tehran government live and work. Dutch tracked Iranian operators who used spyware to pull data from phones and computers of those targets.

On 15 September 2026 the Dutch AIVD published a joint alert with the US FBI and the British NCSC. The notice detailed how Iranian services planted spyware on devices of Iranians they consider critical, then extracted sensitive files, contact lists and location records.

The disclosure lands inside the running US-Iran armed conflict that began with American and Israeli strikes in late February 2026, narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz, and now includes nightly US attacks on Iranian . The new warning shows the digital side of that fight has expanded to private citizens in the Netherlands, the United States and Britain.

Thinking ahead
Dutch intelligence AIVD, the FBI, and Britain's NCSC issued a joint warning that Iran is using spyware to break into accounts of Iranian dissidents and exiles in the West and pull out names, contacts, and locations. This fits the pattern of Tehran gathering intelligence on opponents while the US-Iran fight stays locked on the Strait of Hormuz. The alert marks the latest rung after months of naval clashes, tanker strikes, and broken ceasefires that began with the February strikes and the collapsed June memorandum. The campaign is likely to run as long as the Hormuz standoff continues because the same IRGC units handling maritime disruption also run overseas cyber and espionage teams. It will probably expand to more phishing waves and custom malware updates aimed at diaspora activists and journalists. The only clear off-ramp would be a new Hormuz transit deal that pulls US and Iranian forces back from direct confrontation. Next observable step is either fresh AIVD or FBI takedowns of the command servers or a wave of arrests inside Europe based on the stolen data. Horizon: 4-6 months unless a Hormuz agreement is signed.
