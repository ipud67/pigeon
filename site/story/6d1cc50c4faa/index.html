US Supreme Court rejects Christa Pike's last appeal to stop her execution in Tennessee

Christa Pike has sat on Tennessee's death row since a jury convicted her of first-degree murder and sentenced her to death in 1996. At 18 she and her boyfriend killed 19-year-old Colleen Slemmer at a Knoxville job-training camp for troubled teens. Witnesses said Pike later showed them a piece of the victim's skull and bragged about the crime.

On September 29 the US Supreme Court denied Pike's final petition seeking to block the lethal-injection execution scheduled for the next morning. Tennessee Governor Bill Lee had already refused clemency the day before. The denial cleared the final legal barrier after years of appeals that centered on Pike's age at the time of the offense and claims about adolescent brain development.

Pike is set to become the first woman put to death in Tennessee in more than two centuries. She remains scheduled for execution at 11 a.m. Eastern at Riverbend Maximum Security Institution in Nashville.
