19:T558,Six Flags Magic Mountain closed the X2 rollercoaster in July after more than 100 riders reported brain injuries from its 360-degree spinning seats. The ride first opened in 2002 as X, shut down months later for design fixes, closed again in 2007 for lighter trains, then reopened in 2008 as X2 and carried over 16 million riders. A law firm says it heard from about 400 people claiming injuries, some requiring emergency brain surgery. One 22-year-old rider died in 2022 after riding it, and his family settled a wrongful death suit in August. The park says it passed safety tests but chose to retire the ride permanently because guest confidence dropped.

The next steps sit in the civil courts. Multiple lawsuits from riders and the deceased rider's family are already filed. Those cases will turn on medical records, ride maintenance logs, and expert testimony about the spinning seats and g-forces. No regulatory shutdown order has appeared yet. The park could replace the ride with a new model once any court orders or settlements clear.

This legal pressure and the permanent closure are likely to run for at least 18 to 36 months while the suits work through discovery and possible trials. The situation changes only if the courts dismiss the bulk of claims or the parties reach broad settlements that end further filings.
