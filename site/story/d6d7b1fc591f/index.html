Center-left opposition in Sweden narrowly larger than right-wing bloc

Translated from Dutch · read the original →
The original headlineCentrumlinkse oppositie in Zweden nipt groter dan rechts blok

Sweden held a general election on September 11 2026 after the right-wing government formed in 2022 lost its majority in parliament. That four-year term followed the 2018-2022 period when no single bloc held a clear edge and governments relied on shifting support deals. The 349-seat Riksdag splits into two main blocs that compete for 175 seats needed to control the chamber.

On September 16 public broadcaster SVT tallied the last and reported the center-left opposition led by Social Democratic leader Magdalena Andersson had taken 175 seats. At 5 p.m. that day the gap between the blocs stood at 26 169 votes with 99 percent of ballots counted. The right-wing parties therefore fell short of the majority they defended.

The opposition now holds the narrowest possible edge in the new Riksdag. Parliament must to elect a speaker and confirm a prime minister under the same rules that produced the 2022 outcome. The final results will set the exact seat distribution the next government works inside.
