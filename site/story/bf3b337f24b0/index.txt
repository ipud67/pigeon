17:T53c,The Micron Taiwan union turned down the company's one-time reward package on September 15 and stuck to its demand for a fixed 15 percent share of operating profits on an ongoing basis. This came after Micron rolled out the package last week in response to strike threats, offering the equivalent of 35-68 months' pay for fiscal 2026 with cash minimums of NT$1.7 million and average total pay around NT$3.4 million. Union head Lin Che-jui said the package was put forward without union agreement and that two more negotiation rounds are set for Friday and September 21; failure there would trigger a strike vote.

The standoff sits on top of years of rising AI-driven chip demand that lifted Micron's sales and profits along with those of Samsung and SK hynix. Taiwan accounts for roughly 60 percent of Micron's memory output, mostly DRAM and high-bandwidth chips, so any walkout would tighten supply further. No court filings or regulatory rulings have surfaced as indicators yet.

Talks could drag another two to four weeks if the sides keep missing each other's terms. A strike vote looks likely if the September 21 session ends without a profit-sharing formula both sides accept. Absent that, production stays on schedule and the one-time payout stands.

Next visible rung is the outcome of the September 21 meeting.
