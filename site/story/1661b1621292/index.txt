18:T4c2,The National Park Service just put out a proposed rule to ease the paperwork needed before opening more park roads and trails to bikes. This is the latest step in a long push to cut red tape that started years ago when Congress told agencies to line up their bike rules with federal law. Right now the rule sits in the standard comment period that runs through fall 2025. After that NPS will read the comments, make any tweaks, and decide whether to issue a final rule.

How long this drags on depends on how many groups file comments and whether they push for hearings or lawsuits. If the comments stay light and friendly the final rule could drop by late 2026 and bike trails could open faster in some parks. If big environmental or safety groups file detailed objections the timeline stretches into 2027 or later while NPS rewrites sections or defends the changes in court.

Next rung is the end of the public-comment window, followed by the publication of the final rule in the Federal Register. The only thing that clearly changes the path is a wave of formal opposition that forces NPS to reopen the record or scrap the proposal.
