Repeat Felon Arrested and Charged in $4.75M Medicare Fraud Scheme

Medicare bars people convicted of health care fraud from billing the program. Emory Matthews received that exclusion after his earlier for soliciting and receiving illegal kickbacks at an adult day care center he ran in Detroit. His wife owned the center, called New Beginnings Adult Center Inc.

A federal grand jury in the Eastern District of Michigan returned an on September 15 charging Matthews, 62, of Farmington Hills, with one count of to commit health care fraud and three counts of health care fraud. Prosecutors say that from January 2020 through June 2025 he worked as the center's administrator and submitted more than $4.75 million in claims for psychotherapy services that were never provided. He was arrested the same day.

Yolanda Matthews pleaded guilty to one count of the same conspiracy on July 27. Matthews now faces a maximum of 10 years in prison on each count if convicted. The case was investigated by the FBI Detroit Field Office and the Health and Human Services Office of Inspector General. An indictment is an ; defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty.

Emory Matthews, 62, got arrested Tuesday on a federal indictment out of the Eastern District of Michigan. The charge says he ran an adult day care center in Detroit with his wife, sent Medicare more than $4.75 million in bills for group psychotherapy sessions that never happened, and did it after he had already been barred from Medicare for a prior kickback conviction on the same operation. His wife pleaded guilty to the conspiracy count back in July. The case now sits at the point where prosecutors file motions and the defense answers the indictment. This kind of health-care fraud indictment usually takes 12 to 24 months from arrest to trial or plea in the Eastern District. The next visible step is an arraignment within the next two weeks, then a schedule for discovery and motions to dismiss. The situation changes only if Matthews strikes a plea deal, which happens in most fraud cases once the evidence list gets turned over, or if a judge throws out counts on legal grounds before trial starts. The ladder shows this is the latest rung on a repeat-offender pattern that began with his earlier conviction and exclusion from Medicare, followed by the new scheme that ran from 2020 through mid-2025.
