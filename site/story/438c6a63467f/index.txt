This year’s edition drew 3,000 people and listed more than 1,000 openings from TSMC, MediaTek, Delta Electronics, and others, plus research posts at Academia Sinica and ITRI. The National Development Council handed out its Employment Gold Card on the spot to at least one U.S. software engineer, part of a program started in 2018 that fast-tracks work permits and residency for skilled foreigners in tech fields.

The event sits on a steady ladder: the Taiwan Tech Summit began in 2022 and has hosted some version of this career day every year since. Taiwan’s chip and AI sectors keep expanding while global competition for the same narrow pool of specialists stays tight. No new policy shift or sudden labor shortage triggered this round; it is the latest rung in the same recruitment push.

With Treasury yields at 4.28 percent on the three-month bill, 4.92 percent on the two-year note, 5.24 percent on the ten-year, 5.56 percent on the thirty-year, and a 2s10s spread of 32 basis points, borrowing costs remain elevated but show no abrupt move that would directly change hiring plans for these firms.

The fair will keep running once a year at the summit as long as Taiwan’s semiconductor and AI investment stays on its current pace and the talent shortage does not ease. Next year’s version is already on the calendar for the 2027 summit; the main variable is how many Gold Cards get issued and whether U.S. visa rules tighten enough to push more engineers toward the Taiwan option.
