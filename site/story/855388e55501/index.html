US cautions Americans in UAE regarding Jewish- and Israeli-associated sites

The United Arab Emirates has stood with the United States since the opening strikes on Iran in late February 2026. After the Islamabad collapsed in July the UAE cut off trade with Iran on August 19 following an accusation that Tehran fired two missiles toward its waters. The fighting has continued with weekly exchanges focused on the Strait of Hormuz and on targets inside Iran.

On September 11 the US Mission to the United Arab Emirates sent a security alert to American citizens. The message listed locations associated with Jewish and Israeli communities including places of worship and told citizens to remain vigilant maintain heightened situational awareness and practice good personal security measures. It also reminded them they could enroll in the Smart Traveler Enrollment Program to receive updates and to help authorities find them in an emergency.

The alert sits inside the ongoing armed conflict between the US and Iran and the heightened regional tensions that followed the UAE’s trade cutoff. It directs US citizens already in the country to change their daily conduct around specific sites. The situation now turns on whether further incidents occur at those locations or whether the alert produces no new events before the next round of strikes or talks.

Constitutional analysis
The U.S. Embassy in Abu Dhabi warned American citizens on September 11, 2026, to stay vigilant at sites in the UAE tied to Jewish and Israeli communities, including places of worship. Government exists first to secure the unalienable right to life named first in the Declaration of Independence, the very purpose for which men institute government. The founders treated protection of citizens abroad as a core duty under the law of nations they inherited from Grotius, Vattel, and Blackstone, and they backed it with concrete acts such as the 1794 Neutrality Act and President Washington’s personal enforcement of treaty obligations during the Whiskey Rebellion. Article I, Section 8 gives Congress power to define and punish offenses against the law of nations, while Article II, Section 3 charges the president to see that the laws are faithfully executed, including consular warnings that keep Americans out of harm’s way. Federalist 3 and 8 underscore that a chief reason for union was to shield citizens from foreign violence so they could live in safety. This alert flows from that duty: enumerate the threat, advise practical steps, and let citizens decide how to order their own affairs. The measure remains the same one the founders applied: government must protect life first, then leave the rest to the people.
