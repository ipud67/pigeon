US House also approves large package against Russia

Translated from Dutch · read the original →
The original headlineOok Amerikaans lagerhuis stemt in met omvangrijk sanctiepakket tegen Rusland

Russia has fought in Ukraine since it sent troops across the border in February 2022. The United States has answered with repeated rounds of sanctions that block Russian banks from dollar markets, freeze assets of officials and companies, and limit sales of oil and technology. Those measures have stood in place through two full Congresses while lawmakers looked for ways to tighten the restrictions further.

On September 16, 2026, the House of Representatives passed a fresh package of sanctions that adds new targets and harsher limits on Russian revenue streams. The Senate had cleared the same bill the previous month. The two chambers sent the legislation to the president’s desk for signature.

The bill now sits with President Trump. Once signed it becomes law and the new restrictions take effect according to the timelines written into the text.

Constitutional analysis
The Constitution grants Congress power to regulate commerce with foreign nations under Article I, Section 8, Clause 3. The founders used this power early and often to protect American interests. The Tariff Act of 1789 set duties on imports to fund the new government and shield domestic industry. Washington signed the law and enforced it without hesitation. The first Congress also passed the Neutrality Act of 1794 to keep the young republic out of European wars while still allowing targeted measures against hostile powers. Sanctions that cut off funds for aggression fall within that tradition of using commerce power to secure peace at home. Domestic Tranquility, listed first in the Preamble, remains the reason government exists. This package now awaits the president's signature under the same Article I framework that has guided foreign economic policy since the founding.
