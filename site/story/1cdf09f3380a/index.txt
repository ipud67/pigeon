1a:T51c,The Treasury Department and IRS put out a proposed rule on September 3 that sets a standard for stripping tax-exempt status from private schools found to give preferential treatment to minority students. This is the latest step in a string of Trump administration moves since January 2025 to tighten federal rules on nonprofit tax benefits. The regulation still needs public comments and final approval before it takes effect.

How long it runs depends on two clear markers: the volume of comments filed in the next 60 days and whether federal courts accept challenges under existing civil-rights precedents. If the comment period draws heavy pushback from schools and advocacy groups, the final rule could slip into 2027. Court filings in the next six months would show whether the policy gets tied up in litigation the way past IRS enforcement actions did in the 1980s and 2010s.

Bond yields give no direct read on this rule. The 10-year Treasury yield sits at 4.77 percent and the 2s10s spread at 43 basis points as of September 3, numbers that reflect broad fiscal and inflation views rather than any shift in education policy.

Next visible rung is the close of the public-comment window and any early lawsuits filed in federal district court.
