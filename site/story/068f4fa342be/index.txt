1b:T81d,CIA Director John Ratcliffe declassified 71 daily presidential briefings on the 25th anniversary of the Sept. 11 attacks. One December 4, 1998, briefing to President Clinton carried the title “Bin Ladin Preparing To Hijack US Aircraft and Other Attacks.” It reported that al-Qaida and allied groups had completed planning for an operation inside the United States on Bin Laden’s behalf, that a leader planned to hijack a U.S. airliner, and that network members had received hijack training. Later briefings in December 1998, April 2001, and August 2001 repeated the pattern of aircraft-hijack warnings, including surveillance of federal buildings in New York. The attacks on September 11, 2001, killed 2,753 people in New York, 184 at the Pentagon, and 40 in Pennsylvania.

The Constitution begins with the Preamble’s promise to “insure domestic Tranquility” and “provide for the common defence.” The founders saw protection of American lives from foreign attack as the first duty of the national government. Federalist 8 warned that weakness invites invasion and that safety must come before every other object of government. Washington himself took the field with the militia in 1794 to put down the Whiskey Rebellion and enforce federal law. The Militia Acts of 1792 placed the able-bodied men under federal call to repel invasion or suppress insurrection. Intelligence that names specific threats to American soil and American aircraft falls squarely inside that core duty.

The briefings show the executive branch possessed concrete, repeated notice of planned hijackings years before the attacks. Article II, Section 2 charges the President to “preserve, protect and defend the Constitution” and to act as Commander in Chief.
