Thirteen of those cases surfaced between 13 August and 17 September, pointing to faster local spread. One recent case involves a man who returned from Spain on 14 September after testing there; authorities are still cross-checking results with Spanish labs. All patients are adults aged 23 to 50, 18 of them male. The department has secured a small supply of Tecovirimat vaccine doses and says it will target them to break chains of transmission while pushing for symptom checks, hand hygiene, and isolation.

The cluster has grown in a matter of weeks inside one city, so the rise will likely keep going for at least another month unless testing and contact tracing scale up fast enough to catch every new link. Next step is deployment of the limited vaccine doses plus expanded lab work on the imported case to decide whether it seeded the current wave or arrived after local spread had already started. The situation changes only when weekly new-case counts drop for two consecutive weeks and the proportion of cases with no known travel or contact history falls.
