US and China discuss AI safety plan ahead of Trump-Xi summit

US and Chinese officials have managed trade and technology ties since the two countries agreed a truce in 2025. That truce is set to end on 10 November. The United States and China have raced each other to build the largest AI systems since at least 2023, when both governments began publishing national strategies that treat compute power, data centers and model weights as strategic assets.

Top US and Chinese officials met in New York on 21 September 2026 and agreed to create a notification mechanism that requires each side to alert the other when it detects an AI incident that could affect national security. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent described the talks with Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng as successful and said the new channel would move both countries from opaque to more transparent practices on the two leading AI powers. The same meeting also produced plans to operationalize a Board of Trade process that could identify tariff cuts on specific goods.

The notification mechanism now forms part of the agenda for the Trump-Xi summit scheduled in Washington later this week. The tariff truce remains in force until 10 November while the new AI channel and the Board of Trade process move forward.

Thinking ahead
US and Chinese officials met in New York on Sunday and agreed to set up a notification system for AI events that touch national security. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called the talks successful and said the step moves both sides from no visibility to basic shared alerts between the top two AI developers. The meeting also touched plans for a Board of Trade that would flag possible tariff cuts, with the current tariff truce set to end November 10. This sits on the ladder of earlier talks: the May Beijing session between Trump and Xi already listed AI cooperation as one agenda item, and both governments have kept the same working-level channel open since then. The procedural next rung is the Trump-Xi summit in Washington later this week, where the notification mechanism and the Board of Trade process are expected to be put on paper or expanded. The situation stays on track as long as both sides keep sending the same mid-level economic officials to prep the summits and neither walks away from the November truce deadline. If the summit produces a signed text on the AI channel, the mechanism would move from talk to first test cases within months; if the summit yields only a vague statement, the channel stays on the drawing board. Next rung looks to be the Washington summit text.
