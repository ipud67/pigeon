Tennessee governor declines to halt execution of state's lone woman on death row

Tennessee has carried out the death penalty by lethal injection since the U.S. Supreme Court allowed states to resume capital punishment in 1976. The state sets execution dates through its department of correction after all direct appeals and state post-conviction reviews conclude. Governors hold the power under the state constitution to grant and commute a sentence to life in prison at any time before the scheduled hour.

On September 29 Governor Bill Lee rejected Christa Pike’s clemency . Pike, convicted in 1996 of the first-degree murder of Colleen Slemmer during a 1995 attack at a Knoxville job-training camp when Pike was 18, is scheduled for lethal injection on September 30. Her lawyers had asked the governor to change the sentence to life imprisonment, citing her age at the time of the offense, history of childhood trauma, and current remorse.

Pike now has a petition pending before the U.S. Supreme Court that seeks a last-minute stay. If the execution occurs she would become the first woman put to death in Tennessee in more than 200 years and the first person the state has executed in the modern era for a crime committed at age 18.
