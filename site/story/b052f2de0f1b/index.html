N. Sumatra tourism sites closed as Mount Sinabung volcanic activity increases

Mount Sinabung in North Sumatra began erupting in 2010 after more than 400 years of dormancy. It has gone through repeated phases of lava dome growth, ash explosions, and pyroclastic flows since then. Indonesian authorities raised the alert level in late August 2026 as seismic activity and dome growth increased. On 31 August 2026 officials closed all tourism sites around the volcano and banned all activity inside a 3 km radius of the summit. The order gave local police and disaster agencies power to enforce the exclusion zone.

Thinking ahead
Mount Sinabung has been rumbling harder lately, so North Sumatra officials shut the nearby tourism spots and told everyone to stay at least 3 km from the summit. This fits the pattern of past blow-ups where the mountain spits ash and the alert level gets raised. Next moves depend on what the monitoring instruments show: if quake counts and gas readings keep climbing, the exclusion zone could widen and closures stretch for weeks or months. If the signals drop off and stay low for a stretch, they start letting people back in bit by bit. The situation is likely to run at least several weeks and could drag into months if fresh activity spikes show up. It ends when instruments flatten out long enough for the alert to drop and local government lifts the ban. Horizon: 4-12 weeks.
