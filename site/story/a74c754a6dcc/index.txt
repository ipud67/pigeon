This comes after years of local pushback over noise, power draw, and land use in places where big tech has tried to build. The statement is the latest rung in a running fight between federal push for AI growth and town-level resistance that has already slowed projects in Virginia, Georgia, and the Midwest.

How much longer this runs depends on whether more governors and state legislatures keep fast-tracking data-center approvals or if local zoning boards keep voting them down. Next step is likely more state-level bills in 2027 sessions that override local objections, plus fresh local hearings where Trump’s wording gets quoted by both sides. The situation only shifts if enough towns start seeing direct tax revenue or jobs that outweigh the complaints, or if a court case sets a clear national rule on what counts as “backwards.”

No solid indicators yet on which way the next dozen local votes will break.
