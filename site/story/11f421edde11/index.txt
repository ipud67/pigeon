19:T516,The Hōlei Sea Arch stood at the end of Chain of Craters Road in Hawaii Volcanoes National Park until sometime between 23 and 27 September. National Park Service records show the surrounding cliffs had cracked and shifted after the 2018 Kīlauea eruption and again in January 2020, when the main viewing spot was closed and moved farther back. A chunk broke off the base during a tropical storm later that year, and the arch finally dropped into the ocean last weekend while the park was shut for Tropical Storm Nolo.

Park crews have already noted the new gap in the coastline. The NPS statement lists this as one more step in the steady inland migration of the cliff edge, a process that has left other arches and sea stacks in the same area over the past decades. No new closure or rerouting of the road is required because the viewing area was already set at a safe distance in 2020.

The formation will stay gone. Similar arches have formed and fallen along this stretch for centuries as waves and gravity work on the lava rock. The next change will come when erosion cuts a fresh opening in the cliff face at a different spot, though no timeline or location for that has been measured yet.
