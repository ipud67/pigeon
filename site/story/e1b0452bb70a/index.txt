These drone strikes on the capital have run at a steady pace since the summer wave began in June. Russian forces keep launching from inside their own territory or from occupied areas in the south and east, using cheap one-way drones that take hours to reach the city. Ukrainian air defense units in the Kyiv region have downed most of them this year, but a few get through each month when the launch numbers spike or the interceptors run low on missiles.

The pattern holds as long as Russian production lines keep turning out the drones and Ukrainian crews stay short on Western-supplied interceptors. Next moves depend on whether Moscow ramps up the nightly launches again before winter or whether Ukraine receives more air-defense batteries that can cover the capital. Without a clear shift in either supply line the strikes stay at the same low but regular rate.
