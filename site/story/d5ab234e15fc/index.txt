21:T43f,The Trump administration filed an emergency request with the Supreme Court on September 8 to let states keep using a federal citizenship database for checking voter rolls. Lower courts had blocked parts of the system after lawsuits claimed it overstepped federal authority. The filing sits on top of years of back-and-forth between the executive branch and states over access to that database, with each new administration tweaking the rules and courts stepping in to draw lines.

The justices now face a narrow procedural call on whether to freeze the lower-court order while the full case plays out. How long this round lasts turns on how fast the Court schedules argument and whether it treats the request as urgent enough to rule before the next election cycle. The situation shifts only if the Court grants the stay, sends the case back down for more hearings, or if Congress passes clearer statutory language on database use.

Next step is a response from the states and voting groups that sued, followed by the justices deciding on the stay.
