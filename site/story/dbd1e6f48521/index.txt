1f:T512,Six cities led by New York filed suit in federal court Tuesday against the Department of Homeland Security over a Trump-era rule that ties legal immigrants' use of certain public benefits to their immigration status. The suit marks the latest step in a string of local-government challenges to federal immigration enforcement that date back to the first Trump term and continued through Biden years. Courts have blocked or narrowed similar rules before when plaintiffs showed likely harm to mixed-status families and local budgets.

The case now sits with a district judge who will set a briefing schedule on a preliminary injunction. DHS has 60 days to respond under standard procedure. The rule takes effect in phases over the next year unless the court halts it.

Duration hinges on how fast the judge rules and whether the government appeals. If the injunction is granted and upheld on appeal the rule stays frozen for the rest of the administration. If the suit fails the rule rolls out city by city as enforcement begins.

Next visible rung is the government's written answer due by mid-November and any request for an expedited hearing. Without those documents the timeline stays unclear.
