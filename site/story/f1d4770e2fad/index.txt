1e:T4e8,The Trump administration filed an emergency request with the Supreme Court on September 28 to pause a federal judge's August 26 order that stopped the Bureau of Prisons from rolling out its February 2026 policy. That policy bars gender-transition surgeries, hormone therapy, and items like makeup or wigs for inmates, after the agency cited new scientific reviews, safety risks inside facilities, and fairness issues among the prison population. The request follows the D.C. Circuit's refusal to stay the order and builds on the February 2025 executive direction to halt federal spending on such procedures.

The justices now hold the next clear step: grant or deny the stay. A grant would let the 2026 policy take effect while the underlying lawsuit moves forward. Denial would keep the old access rules in place pending full appeals. The record the Bureau submitted, the judge's use of outside evidence, and the level of deference to prison operators form the main points the Court would weigh.

This round of emergency litigation will likely play out in weeks, not months, given the pace of prior stays in similar prison-policy cases. The situation changes only if the Court acts or the full merits case reaches it.
