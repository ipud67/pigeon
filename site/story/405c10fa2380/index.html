Iraqi butcher sentenced to 10 years in prison for selling pork

Translated from Italian · read the original →
The original headlineMacellaio iracheno condannato a 10 anni di carcere per aver venduto carne di maiale

Iraq maintains a legal prohibition on the sale of pork that dates to the years after the 2003 fall of the Baathist government, when the new constitution and subsequent aligned commercial rules with the religious practices observed by the Muslim majority. Courts in the holy cities have enforced that prohibition through regular prosecutions of butchers and food vendors caught violating it.

On September 14 a criminal court in one such city sentenced a local butcher to ten years in prison after evidence showed he had sold pork to customers while labeling and pricing it as beef. The rested on testimony from buyers, laboratory tests of seized meat samples, and the butcher’s own records that tracked the substitution over a period of months.

The sentence now stands as the latest application of the prohibition in a city that receives millions of religious visitors each year. The case file remains open for any appeal the butcher may file within the window.
