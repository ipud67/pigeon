HAITI
August 26, 2026 · 01:09 UTC

Gangs killed 47 people and kidnapped more than 50 near Port-au-Prince in Haiti

Overview
The UN Secretary General has condemned a violent attack that left at least 47 dead and saw over 50 more kidnapped.
Short history
A gang attack hit a crowded area in Haiti’s capital on August 26, 2026. Gunmen killed at least 47 people and took more than 50 others hostage. UN Secretary-General António Guterres publicly condemned the killings and abductions. Haiti’s gang violence has grown since 2021 when President Jovenel Moïse was assassinated. Gangs now control large parts of Port-au-Prince and have blocked fuel, food, and medicine from reaching neighborhoods. The attack is the latest in a series of mass killings that have displaced hundreds of thousands since 2022.
Predictive model
The attack that killed 47 and took more than 50 hostages fits the pattern of gang raids on towns and roads that have grown since 2021 when the president was assassinated. UN statements have come after every big massacre but have not slowed the gangs who now control most of the capital and large stretches of the countryside. Next measurable steps are whether Haitian police or the small Kenyan-led force mount any visible counter-operation in the attacked area and whether the number of reported kidnappings in the following weeks drops, stays level, or climbs. Without a big shift in security control on the ground the cycle of raid, condemn, repeat is likely to keep running. This pattern is likely to continue for months.
