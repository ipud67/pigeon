UKRAINE
September 13, 2026 · 14:14 UTC

Moscow: 'We struck railways carrying military goods from Europe'

Translated from Italian · read the original →
The original headlineMosca, 'colpite ferrovie che trasportano merci militari dall'Europa'

Russia invaded Ukraine in February 2022. Since then both sides have struck each other's supply lines and energy . Moscow has repeatedly targeted rail routes that carry weapons and fuel from European countries into Ukraine.

On September 13 Russian forces hit railway sections in southern Ukraine that were moving military cargo from Europe, the defense ministry said. The same day its units also bombed logistics centers and energy sites across the region.

The strikes cut another segment of the western supply network that Ukraine relies on for sustained operations. The rail lines and storage points now stand as the next targets both sides will fight to control or defend in the south.

Predictive model
Russia hit rail lines that move weapons and supplies from Europe into Ukraine plus logistics hubs and power plants in the south. Moscow said the targets carried military cargo. Ukrainian officials reported the strikes knocked out electricity in parts of the region and damaged rail tracks. This fits a pattern of repeated Russian attacks on supply routes and energy infrastructure that has run since early in the war. Ukrainian repairs usually take days to weeks before trains roll again at reduced capacity. Power crews restore lines on a similar timeline unless the damage is heavy. The pace holds steady as long as European shipments continue by rail and road. Next moves depend on whether Ukraine shifts more cargo to trucks or western routes and whether Russia keeps the same target list or widens it. A clear drop in strikes would show only if rail traffic visibly falls or a ceasefire takes hold. The situation stays on this track for months unless one side loses the ability to move or hit the cargo.
