17:T6f3,The International Press Institute marked the second anniversary of Ukrainian journalist Viktoriia Roshchyna's death in Russian captivity by calling for Russia to free 27 Ukrainian journalists still held there and to identify those responsible for her case. Roshchyna went missing in occupied Enerhodar in August 2023 while reporting on Russian torture sites, was confirmed detained by Russian authorities in May 2024, and died on 19 September 2024 just as she was added to a prisoner exchange list. Her body, returned in February 2025, showed signs of torture and missing internal organs. Russia has released few civilians in swaps compared with captured soldiers, and most of the remaining journalists face charges of terrorism or espionage or are held without any public charge.

This call sits on a ladder of repeated demands from press groups and Ukrainian officials that have produced no visible change in Russian handling of these cases. The pattern since 2022 shows 16 journalists killed on duty in Ukraine, with Roshchyna the first to die in formal Russian custody, yet prisoner exchanges continue to favor military over civilian lists. No new exchange talks that include these names have been announced.

The situation is likely to continue for months at the current pace because Russia has not altered its detention or disclosure practices for these civilians. Next steps would require either a bilateral swap agreement that adds named journalists to the list or international pressure that forces Russia to acknowledge and transfer them. Without one of those two shifts, the numbers held stay flat.

The call adds another public marker but does not change the exchange mechanics now in place.
