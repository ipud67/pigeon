1f:T4e4,Tinubu directed a full probe into the deaths of suspected illegal miners held by the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps in Minna, Niger state. The men died in custody after an enforcement sweep around the Lt.-Gen. Mohammed Inuwa Wushishi Estate. The order names specific checks on arrest records, detention numbers, medical logs, and conditions at the site. It also suspends involved officers as an interim step while requiring the interior ministry and NSCDC to hand over all files without interference. Prosecution follows only if evidence shows negligence or abuse by named officials.

This kind of presidential directive on custody deaths has usually run three to six months before a public report lands. The next visible rung is the appointment of the investigative panel and its first witness schedule, which normally drops within two weeks. If the panel delivers a report that pins blame on specific ranks, expect arrests or internal sackings; if it stays vague on medical and overcrowding details, the file closes with warnings only. The pace hinges on whether families or state officials push for open hearings or let the federal team work quietly.
