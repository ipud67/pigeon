US pressures NATO in campaign against International Criminal Court

Translated from Dutch · read the original →
The original headlineAmerika zet NAVO onder druk in hetze tegen Internationaal Strafhof

The United States has opposed the International Criminal Court since its creation in 2002 under the Rome . Washington never joined the court and in 2002 Congress passed the American Service-Members' Protection Act which cut military aid to countries that the treaty without a bilateral immunity agreement and authorised the president to use force to free any US personnel detained by the court. Successive have refused to recognise ICC over American citizens.

On September 10 sources told NOS that the Trump administration is now pressing NATO allies to support an effort to dismantle the court piece by piece and to withdraw their own membership. The campaign follows a Politico report that detailed American demands inside closed-door alliance meetings.

The pressure places European governments that remain parties to the Rome Statute in direct tension with Washington at a time when NATO unity is already strained by other disputes. The sources said the US wants its allies to join in restricting the court's funding, limiting its investigative powers, and ultimately exiting the treaty.

Thinking ahead
The US has pushed NATO allies to pull support from the International Criminal Court and join efforts to weaken it step by step. Sources told NOS the pressure follows a Politico report on the campaign. NATO members now face repeated US requests to review their ties to the court. This pressure track has run since the court's early years when the US first opposed its reach over American personnel. The latest rung is the direct ask to allies to help dismantle it piece by piece. How much longer it runs depends on how many NATO governments comply and whether the court opens new cases that touch US interests. Next steps would show in public withdrawals by one or two allies or in fresh US sanctions tied to court staff. The situation shifts only if a clear majority of NATO states refuse the requests in writing or if the court itself folds key investigations. It stays active for months unless several allies issue joint rebuffs.
