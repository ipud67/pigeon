S. Korea, U.S. vow to strengthen cooperation to accelerate wartime OPCON transition in defense talks

South Korea and the United States have shared wartime operational control of combined forces since the end of the Korean War in 1953. In October 2014 the two governments agreed to a conditions-based plan to shift that control to Seoul. The plan requires South Korea to build certain lead-command capabilities, improve its strike and air-defense systems, and wait for a regional security environment that supports the handoff. Presidents Lee Jae-myung and Donald Trump issued a joint fact sheet in November 2025 that listed follow-on defense steps.

The 29th Korea-U.S. Integrated Defense Dialogue ran in Busan from September 16 to 18, 2026. At its close the two released a statement that said they had reviewed progress on the 2014 transition plan and agreed to increase joint work so the transfer could move faster.

The talks also checked implementation of last year’s ministerial agreements on broader defense cooperation. The South Korean minister for national defense policy and the U.S. deputy assistant secretary of defense for East Asia led the sessions. The delegations toured shipbuilding and aviation maintenance sites to deepen industry ties. Both sides said they would keep consulting on alliance security matters.

South Korea and the US wrapped up three days of senior defense talks in Busan this week and put out a joint statement Friday saying they will step up work to speed the handover of wartime operational control from US to South Korean command. The 29th Korea-US Integrated Defense Dialogue reviewed the conditions-based plan first set in 2014, which requires Seoul to build lead command ability, strike and air defense capacity, and a stable regional security setup before the switch. This round fits the current Seoul administration’s stated goal of completing the transfer by 2030. The process has moved in fits and starts since the 2014 agreement, with repeated joint assessments and follow-on steps tied to each year’s Security Consultative Meeting. The latest meeting also included site visits to shipbuilding and aviation maintenance facilities to tighten industrial ties that support the military transition. How much longer it runs will track against the three standing conditions and the pace of South Korean capability upgrades already under way. Next visible steps are further consultations laid out in the joint statement plus the next scheduled defense ministerial meeting where progress gets measured again. If capability benchmarks stay on the current schedule and the regional security environment holds, the formal OPCON shift could reach a decision point inside the current presidential term. Horizon: measurable movement at the 2027 defense ministerial talks.
