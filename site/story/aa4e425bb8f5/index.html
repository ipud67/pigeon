Nelson donors raised 30000 dollars in four days for Nepalese woman Samira Basnet's cancer surgery

Samira Basnet moved from Nepal to Nelson two years ago to study for a master's degree in management studies at NMIT. A month ago she went for a medical check as part of her post-study work visa application. The doctor found a lump on her neck that tests showed was advanced thyroid cancer spread to neck muscles and lymph nodes.

The diagnosis left her ineligible for publicly funded healthcare because she is not a New Zealand resident and does not hold the required work visa. Surgeon Dr Ros Pochin told her the cancer would keep spreading without surgery that costs about 26 thousand dollars plus another 15 thousand for radioactive iodine therapy in Christchurch. Pochin and anaesthetist Dr Katie Ben offered to work without charge and set up a Givealittle page on September 17.

The page raised 30 thousand dollars in four days. The money now covers the full cost of the operation that can remove the cancer and the follow-up treatment that gives a 90 percent survival rate at ten years. Basnet remains in Nelson while the surgery date is set.

Thinking ahead

The situation changes only if the full treatment cost is paid or if her visa and residency status shifts to qualify her for public coverage. No court action or policy change has been filed so far. The story will likely run until the surgery date is set and the remaining funds are confirmed. Horizon: 4-6 weeks until surgery.
