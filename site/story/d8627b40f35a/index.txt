1d:T4ef,The Commerce Department issued a notice on September 23 that spells out which specialty drugs and ingredients get zero tariffs under the April 2 Proclamation 11020. The list covers orphan drugs, nuclear medicines, plasma therapies, fertility drugs, cell and gene therapies, antibody drug conjugates, medical countermeasures for chemical biological radiological and nuclear threats, and animal health products. Zero rate applies if the goods come from a country with a current or pending trade and security framework agreement or if Commerce approves them for urgent U.S. health need; the notice sets up the public submission process for that approval and adds six technical fixes to the proclamation annexes.

This notice is the first detailed step after the April proclamation, so the zero-tariff list and approval pipeline now sit in place. How long the setup runs depends on how many companies file for urgent-need rulings and how fast Commerce processes them. Next likely move is the first batch of approvals or denials once submissions come in, plus any adjustments if trade deals get signed with new partners.
