Thirty-seven men died in custody of Nigeria’s NSCDC after an arrest sweep in Wushishi and Lukoto Village on September 18, 2026. Official counts say the men were suspected of illegal artisanal mining and were held in a facility not built or run by the Nigeria Police. The Renevlyn Development Initiative and Niger Governor Umar Bago both called for full probes, including autopsies, by state, police, DSS, and NSCDC panels.

The founders placed law enforcement and the keeping of the peace squarely with civil authority under the consent of the governed. Federalist 69 shows Hamilton expected the executive to execute laws through officers accountable to elected legislatures, not through paramilitary bodies holding men in makeshift jails. The Constitution’s Article I, Section 8 limits federal power to enumerated objects and leaves most policing to the states. When arrests lead to mass death in custody, the first question is whether the state protected the lives of those it seized, exactly as the Declaration says governments are instituted to do.

Niger state’s committee and the federal agency reviews now under way test whether officials will answer to the people whose lives were lost. The record of the founding generation offers a plain measure: Washington marched the militia to suppress the Whiskey Rebellion in 1794 only after lawful process, and the Militia Acts of 1792 kept command under civil officers. No founder left room for unexplained deaths inside government walls.
