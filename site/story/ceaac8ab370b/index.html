Cuba received 778,000 barrels of fuel from the US in June

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlineCuba recibió 778.000 barriles de combustible de EE UU en junio

Cuba has faced chronic fuel shortages for years. Power plants run on diesel and the island imports most of its supply because domestic production covers only a fraction of demand. The government rationed fuel for transport and industry through 2024 and 2025 while it sought shipments from any available supplier.

On June 30 the island took delivery of 778000 barrels of US-origin fuel. That volume marked a 250 percent increase in diesel imports from the May figure. The cargo arrived under a general license that allows limited sales of petroleum products to non-government Cuban buyers for private use.

The June shipment brought the first notable rise in US fuel reaching Cuba in months. It eased immediate pressure on generators that supply Havana and other cities but left the overall import total still well below the volume required to run the national grid without blackouts. Cuban officials have not released figures for July or August purchases.

Thinking ahead
Cuba got 778,000 barrels of fuel from the US in June. That marked a 250 percent jump in diesel imports from May. The shipments fit a pattern of occasional US fuel sales to the island that started after the 2021 energy crunch. The volume points to continued spot purchases rather than a steady contract. Next shipments will depend on Cuban cash on hand and any fresh US licensing tweaks. The flow stays alive only as long as Washington keeps the export window open and Havana lines up payment. This setup runs another three to six months at current pace unless US rules tighten or Cuban reserves dry up. Horizon: steady purchases into early 2027 if licenses hold.
