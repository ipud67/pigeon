UNITED ARAB EMIRATES
September 24, 2026 · 18:46 UTC

UAE suspends Iranian airlines' flights until further notice, decision based on US

The United Arab Emirates has tightened its economic posture against Iran inside a war that began when the US and Israel struck Iranian targets on 28 February 2026. That conflict settled into repeated strikes and counter-strikes, with naval clashes in the Strait of Hormuz, the 21-mile waterway that carries much of the world's seaborne oil. A June briefly lifted the US naval and Iran's closure of the strait and opened a 60-day negotiating window, but that collapsed on 8 July and nightly US strikes resumed. Iran struck commercial tankers that bypassed its approved routes, the Revolutionary Guard moved to shut the strait again, and on 19 August the UAE cut off all trade with Iran after two missiles were fired toward its waters.

On 24 September the UAE's General Civil Aviation Authority suspended all flights by Iranian airlines to and from the country until further notice. The authority stated that the step followed US sanctions placed on those airlines for their use of airports worldwide.

The decision adds another layer of isolation around Iran's air links at a time when Gulf states have moved closer to the US alliance and the fight has narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz.

Thinking ahead
UAE's General Civil Aviation Authority halted all Iranian airline flights to and from the country on 24 September 2026 citing US sanctions on those carriers for their use of global airports. This follows the UAE's trade cutoff with Iran on 19 August after two missiles were fired toward its waters and fits the pattern of Gulf states tightening measures as the US-Iran fight stays locked on control of the Strait of Hormuz. The suspension blocks a remaining civilian link that had survived the earlier trade break. The step adds one more procedural layer to the realignment already visible in Gulf posture since the August trade cutoff. It does not alter naval or strike mechanics in the strait itself. Next observable change would require either a new US-Iran procedural agreement that eases sanctions or a direct UAE-Iran deal on overflight and airline status. This measure will hold at least until one side alters its sanctions or aviation compliance stance.
