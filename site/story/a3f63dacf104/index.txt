Under federal law, candidates for federal office can purchase advertising time at favorable rates during the 45 days before a primary election and 60 days before a general election. The dispute before the court arose this spring, after the Media Bureau of the Federal Communications Commission issued a “public notice” informing broadcasters that political parties and joint fundraising committees are also entitled to the preferential rates. The Democratic candidates asked the full FCC to review the public notice. Approximately six weeks later, before the FCC had acted on that request, they went to federal court, where they argued that the public notice conflicted with federal election laws. As a practical matter, they contended that the rule favors Republicans, whose fundraising committees have more to spend, while Democratic candidates rely more on small-money donors. A divided panel of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 4th Circuit agreed with the candidates. 1f:T43c,Democratic candidates including Ossoff filed a brief Thursday urging the Supreme Court to keep the 4th Circuit ruling that blocks the FCC public notice on expanded political ad rates. The 4th Circuit panel split on whether the law limits the lower rates to candidates only or whether the notice could stand while the full FCC still reviews it. The Republican committees asked the justices this week to pause the appeals court order and hear the case on both timing and the meaning of the statute.

This back-and-forth stays in the Supreme Court briefing schedule for the next several weeks. The justices will either call for a response from the FCC or simply schedule the pause request for a private conference. A grant of the pause would reopen the lower rates for party committees until the case ends; denial would keep the 4th Circuit block in force through the fall cycle.

The situation changes only if the court takes the full case and rules on the statutory text or if the FCC itself issues a final order that moots the notice.
