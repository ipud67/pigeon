17:T607,Turkish police used tear gas Monday to break up a protest in Izmir against weekend raids that hit offices of six LGBTQ groups across 12 provinces. Authorities arrested 26 people per official count, seized drugs, smuggled alcohol and one firearm, and blocked dozens of related websites. The operations sit inside Erdogan’s “Decade of the Family” push that began earlier this year and frames the moves as protection of children and traditional order. Kaos GL puts the arrest total at 50 and says more than ten were picked up at home; the group also reports fresh probes into donors. Human rights group IHD called the actions state discrimination on social media. 

The crackdown has run in waves since 2015 when public gatherings like Istanbul Pride were first banned. Police have broken up repeated attempts to march since then, producing arrests each time. The weekend raids mark the first coordinated multi-province sweep tied directly to the Decade of the Family label.

This round looks set to run at least through the current probes into donations and any follow-up raids the justice minister has already promised will continue. Next visible steps are court appearances for those charged with facilitating prostitution or related offenses, plus possible new website blocks. The situation shifts only if courts block further operations or the government eases the family campaign, neither of which current statements indicate.
