19:T430,Curiosity rover hit one kilometer of elevation gain above the Gale Crater floor on Sol 4,996. Nine days later it shot a 95-frame panorama from that new spot looking back across the crater floor to the rim. The rover has been working its way up the lower slopes of Mount Sharp since 2014, drilling samples and taking images along the way.

The mission runs on a fixed schedule of drives, drills, and data returns set by the operations team at Jet Propulsion Laboratory. Next steps stay on that track: continue the climb, examine the sand ridge in front of it for layering and grain clues, then pick the next drill target higher up. The only thing that changes the pace is a mechanical fault, a command delay from Earth, or a decision by mission managers to shift the route.

This pattern of steady elevation gain and periodic look-back shots has held for the last twelve years. Without a major hardware failure or budget cut, it keeps going the same way.
