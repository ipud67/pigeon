18:T504,Saudi Arabia says an Iranian attack on the tanker Sidr in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday killed two Filipino sailors. The kingdom condemned the strike on the vessel that was hit by unknown projectiles. This fits the pattern that has held since July when Iran hit three commercial tankers bypassing its approved routes and then moved to shut the strait after the Islamabad Memorandum collapsed.

The fight for control of the strait has run on tit-for-tat tanker strikes and naval moves for the last seven weeks. Each new hit draws statements from Gulf states and keeps traffic low without forcing a wider naval clash or a new ceasefire. Observable indicators are the frequency of these attacks, Saudi and Emirati public condemnations, and continued US escort operations in the same waters.

This round is likely to continue at the current pace of one or two strikes per month until either side alters its rules of engagement or a new diplomatic channel opens. Next step is probably another Iranian claim or denial paired with a Saudi call for international patrols. The situation changes only if tanker traffic drops sharply for two straight weeks or if CENTCOM announces expanded escort convoys that stay in the strait full time.
