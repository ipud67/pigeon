19:T760,The Federal Reserve raised its key interest rate by 25 basis points to a range of 3.75 to 4 percent on September 16 2026. The move followed the plain text of its statutory mandate under the Federal Reserve Act of 1913 to pursue stable prices. Inflation had stayed above the 2 percent target for years, eroding the purchasing power of wages and savings.

Founders built the republic on sound money and limited federal power. Hamilton in Federalist 30 warned that unstable public credit and currency would destroy the ability of government to secure life liberty and property. The Coinage Act of 1792 set clear metallic standards so citizens could trust the value of their earnings. Price stability protects the right to life because families cannot feed clothe or house themselves when the dollar loses value year after year.

The Constitution grants Congress power to coin money and regulate its value under Article I Section 8 Clause 5. It does not hand that authority to an independent board free from direct accountability to elected representatives. Presidents from Washington onward expected the executive and legislative branches to stay within their lanes while still answering to the people at election time. The Fed’s unanimous decision shows officials acting on data rather than political pressure yet the structural question remains whether an agency with such reach over every household loan and business investment fits the enumerated-powers design.

Higher rates raise borrowing costs for homes cars and credit cards while rewarding savers with better returns on deposits. The founding generation saw domestic tranquility as the first purpose of government listed in the Preamble. When inflation steals from paychecks and forces families deeper into debt the peace of ordered liberty frays.
