Horrific moment sports car crosses California highway and flips massive truck and trailer

A California highway intersection had carried steady traffic for years between the farm fields of Fresno County and the oil fields farther west. State Route 33 runs north and south while Coalinga-Mendota Road cuts east and west; drivers on the smaller road must yield before crossing the divided four-lane highway.

On Sept. 9 at about 11:30 a.m. a sports car traveling east on Coalinga-Mendota Road entered the intersection without stopping. It struck the right front of a tractor-trailer rig that had the right of way on Route 33. The car’s momentum lifted the trailer’s front wheels, flipped the entire rig onto its side, and sent both vehicles sliding across the pavement.

The truck driver and the car’s occupants were taken to local hospitals with injuries that were not life-threatening. The California Highway Patrol closed the intersection for several hours while crews cleared diesel fuel and wreckage. The agency is still examining video from the scene and has not yet released the names of those involved.

Thinking ahead
A red sports car ran the stop sign at State Route 33 and Coalinga-Mendota Road on Sept. 9 at 11:30 a.m. and slammed into the side of a semi-truck pulling a trailer. The impact flipped the entire rig onto its side. The truck driver and the sports-car driver both walked away with minor injuries; no one else was in either vehicle. California Highway Patrol is still measuring the exact speeds and angles from the skid marks and vehicle damage. The sports-car driver has not been charged yet. Next step is the full crash report, which usually takes weeks, followed by any citation or civil claim from the trucking company. The pattern at this rural intersection has held steady for years with stop-sign violations as the main trigger. Nothing in the physical layout or traffic volume has changed since the wreck. Unless the county adds signals or rumble strips, the same sequence stays possible for the next driver who misses the sign. This stays a single-vehicle property-damage case until the report lands. Horizon: weeks.
