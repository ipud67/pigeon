Russian attacks in Sumy region leaves one civilian killed, 13 injured over past day

Russian strikes on the Ukrainian border region of Sumy have run daily since the full-scale invasion began in February 2022.

On September 26 the Sumy Regional Military reported that a 69-year-old man died in the Shalyhyne community from a drone strike the previous day. In the Sumy community a guided aerial bomb wounded nine civilians, among them a one-and-a-half-month-old baby, a 12-year-old girl and a 15-year-old boy; a separate UAV strike there injured a 14-year-old girl. An FPV drone hit the Seredyna-Buda community and wounded a 75-year-old man. Two more residents of the Sumy community sought treatment for wounds from strikes on September 23 and 24. In all, 18 settlements across 12 communities came under fire from aerial bombs, UAVs, FPV drones, mortars and artillery. The attacks damaged or destroyed apartment buildings, private houses, civilian infrastructure, outbuildings and vehicles. The regional air-raid alert sounded for more than 22 hours.

The prior day, September 24, Russian forces carried out more than 70 attacks on the same border districts and wounded four civilians.

Russian strikes hit 18 settlements in 12 communities of Sumy region on September 25-26, killing one 69-year-old man in Shalyhyne from a drone and wounding 13 civilians in Sumy and Seredyna-Buda communities from guided aerial bombs, FPV drones and other UAVs. The strikes damaged apartment blocks, private houses, civilian infrastructure, outbuildings and vehicles. Air raid alerts ran more than 22 hours in the past day, following over 70 attacks the day before that left four civilians injured. This pattern of daily cross-border strikes from Russian positions using cheap drones, mortars and artillery has run for months along the shared frontier. The attacks continue because Russian forces hold the launch areas within range and Ukrainian air defense cannot cover every low-level threat in open terrain. No shift in force posture or supply lines has appeared on either side of the border in recent weeks. The same level of strikes is likely to hold steady until one side alters its ground posture or air defense coverage along the Sumy line. Next step points to continued routine bombardment of border villages unless Ukrainian units push Russian firing positions back or Moscow redirects those units elsewhere. Horizon: weeks at current intensity.
