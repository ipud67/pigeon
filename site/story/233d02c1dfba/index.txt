1d:T510,The Trump administration issued new Treasury rules on October 1 interpreting the 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act to let married couples filing jointly claim a $3,400 education tax credit for donations to scholarship granting organizations. That doubles the per-couple amount from the original $1,700 per taxpayer figure. Orthodox Union and Agudath Israel of America, which pushed for the underlying law and for state participation, called the clarification a direct boost to the math on funding Jewish day schools.

Thirty states have already opted into the program that starts in 2027. The remaining holdouts sit mainly in Democratic-led states in the Northeast, Midwest, and Pacific coast. Agudath Israel officials said the doubled credit gives donors in non-participating states a reason to route money elsewhere, which in turn pressures governors to join so their residents keep the local benefit.

The next procedural step is for more states to decide on opt-in before the 2027 launch. Momentum depends on whether governors in the holdout states calculate that the lost donations outweigh the political cost of entry. If enough join, the credit flows wider; if not, the program stays patchy.
