20:T70f,The right to keep and bear arms stands at the center of the cases the Supreme Court will hear on December 2. The Second Amendment declares that this right shall not be infringed. The founders wrote it after living through British attempts to disarm the colonists. They understood an armed people as the final check on government power and the surest guardian of life, liberty, and property. Cook County, Illinois, and Connecticut banned AR-15s and other semiautomatic rifles after the 2012 Sandy Hook shooting. Lower courts upheld those bans. Gun owners and rights groups say the laws violate the plain text of the Amendment and the tradition of the founding generation, which placed no such categorical limits on common arms. The justices will now measure those bans against that original understanding.

The December calendar also includes a parental-rights case from Washington state. Parents claim they must have standing to challenge laws that let runaway teens obtain mental-health treatment, including gender transitions, without notice or consent. The founders viewed the family as the first society, with parents holding primary authority over the care and upbringing of children. The case tests whether courts will recognize that ancient right against state encroachment.

Arizona election rules face review as well. The state requires proof of U.S. citizenship on its voter-registration form and wants to remove noncitizens from the rolls. The National Voter Registration Act and the Constitution’s delegation of election authority to the states form the core questions. The founders designed a republic where only citizens choose the government.
