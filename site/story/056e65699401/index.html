KRAMATORSK
September 20, 2026 · 12:44 UTC

Russians attack on point in Kramatorsk with two aerial bombs: two injured

Russian forces have struck Kramatorsk in eastern Ukraine with aerial bombs for months as they push to control the Donetsk region that they first entered in 2014. The city sits behind the current front line and serves as a hub for civilians leaving areas under daily shelling and for the aid groups that move them.

On the morning of 20 September two Russian aerial bombs hit the city shortly after 11:00 local time. One struck an evacuation point where civilians and rescue workers from the Proliska mission were gathered; the second damaged a preschool, an educational building and four apartment blocks.

Two people were reported injured in the attack on the evacuation point. Local officials said emergency crews were still checking the full extent of damage across the sites.

Thinking ahead
Russian forces dropped two aerial bombs on Kramatorsk at 11:19 local time on 20 September. One struck an evacuation point where civilians and aid workers were gathered. Two people were injured in that strike, per the city military administration head. A preschool, one other school building, and four apartment blocks also took damage. This fits the pattern of repeated strikes on rear-area logistics and movement hubs in Donetsk oblast that has run since the front stabilized near the city in 2022. Local officials report the hits, count the wounded, and move to clear debris and reroute evacuations. No change in that cycle has appeared. The procedural next step is continued bomb-damage assessment by Ukrainian civil-defense teams, followed by adjusted evacuation routes and timing to reduce exposure at fixed points. The situation stays the same until one side alters either the frequency of these rear-area strikes or the ground posture that keeps Kramatorsk inside glide-bomb range. Horizon: another 10–14 days of similar strikes at current rates unless front-line movement shifts the bomb-release line.
