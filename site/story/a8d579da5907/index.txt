Eight of nine justices found the law unconstitutional. This marks the latest step in a long-running dispute over exemptions that dates back decades and has repeatedly returned to the court when temporary laws expired or faced challenges.

The immediate effect is that the exemption framework no longer holds, so military summons and potential arrests for non-compliant students can proceed under existing rules. Government officials now face pressure to pass a new law that meets the court's constitutional standards or to enforce the draft directly. Political parties tied to haredi voters have already signaled they will push for fresh legislation or coalition adjustments to restore some form of exemption.

How long this lasts depends on whether the Knesset can draft and pass replacement legislation that survives the next court review. Without that, enforcement actions against draft evaders will pick up. The pattern from prior cycles shows repeated short-term fixes followed by fresh lawsuits.

Next visible rung: either a government bill introduced in the coming weeks or reports of stepped-up IDF summons to haredi yeshivas.
