At least 16 villages emptied of inhabitants after M23-Wazalendo clashes in Masisi

Translated from French · read the original →
The original headlineAu moins 16 villages vidés de leurs habitants après des affrontements M23-Wazalendo à Masisi

Fighting between the M23 and Wazalendo groups has run for years across North Kivu in eastern Congo. The latest round of clashes between the AFC/M23 and the Wazalendo coalition began four weeks ago in parts of Masisi territory.

On September 10 at least 16 villages in Masisi stood empty after residents fled the recent exchanges. Local officials reported the inhabitants left their homes in the wake of direct combat between the two armed groups.

The security picture in those Masisi areas has now tightened around the displaced populations who left the 16 villages. Aid groups face blocked roads and contested ground as they try to reach the people who moved out.

Thinking ahead
Fighting between the AFC/M23 and Wazalendo groups has emptied at least 16 villages in Masisi territory of North Kivu. This marks the latest step in a four-week flare-up of clashes that has steadily pushed more people out of their homes in the area. Local security reports show the displacements tied directly to the recent rounds of combat rather than to any single attack count. The pattern of village emptying has held steady as the fighting continues without a clear pause. No official indicators yet point to either side gaining lasting ground or to talks starting. That leaves the immediate trend as more of the same. This is likely to run at least another four weeks on the current pace of clashes. It may spread to neighboring pockets of Masisi if the coalitions keep pressing each other. Horizon: another month of displacement at this rate.
