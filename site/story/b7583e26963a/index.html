South Africa’s government says President Ramaphosa addressed an international access to justice conference in South Africa on Thursday

For generations before that the law had racial oppression by setting where people lived and worked whom they married what schooling they received and whether they could vote. The new text replaced that system with one built on human dignity equality and the rule of law. It created courts and institutions that hold public power and made clear that no person no body and no government stands above the law.

On 12 November 2026 President Cyril Ramaphosa told at an international conference on access to justice in Sun City that the gap between constitutional promises and daily experience still undermines public trust. He said justice must be measured by whether an ordinary person can secure protection obtain redress and live with dignity even when poverty limits choices or economic power shields the wealthy from accountability. The gathering under the theme Strengthening People-Centred Justice Systems Deepening Democracy heard him call for reduced barriers to legal aid stronger community services use of technology that does not widen divides and measurable tests of whether courts deliver fair timely outcomes that people can enforce.

The conference now turns on practical steps that governments judicature bodies and international partners can take to move from declared rights to remedies people actually reach. South Africa continues to operate Legal Aid South Africa Equality Courts and community advice offices while drawing on support from bodies such as the International Criminal Court and the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime.
