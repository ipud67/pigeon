Deregistration Under Section 8(f) of the Investment Company Act of 1940

A closed-end investment company registered under the Investment Company Act of 1940 filed an application on July 29, 2026, under Section 8(f) asking the SEC to declare that it has ceased to be an investment company.

Section 8(f) lets a registered investment company apply for an order canceling its registration once it has stopped holding itself out as an investment company, sold or transferred all its assets, and made final distributions to shareholders.

The company transferred all its assets to First Trust Active Global Quality Income ETF, paid $509,897.68 in reorganization expenses itself, and made a final distribution to its shareholders on November 11, 2023, based on net asset value. Its address is in Wheaton, Illinois.

The SEC has not yet issued the order as of September 1, 2026.

Thinking ahead
The closed-end fund finished moving all its assets into the First Trust Active Global Quality Income ETF and paid its last cash to shareholders on November 11, 2023. It then filed the formal Section 8(f) deregistration application on July 29, 2026, asking the SEC to declare it is no longer an investment company. The only remaining procedural step is for the SEC to issue the order; once that order is entered the fund’s registration ends and its legal life as a registered investment company is over. No public comment period or hearing is required under the standard 8(f) track, so the timeline now sits with SEC staff review. The situation changes only if the SEC finds a missing document, an unpaid claim, or some other defect and sends the application back for more information. Horizon: SEC order likely within 60–90 days of the September 1, 2026 notice.
