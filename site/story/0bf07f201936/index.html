Houthi rebels in Yemen seize strategic Bab el Mandeb strait

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlineRebeldes hutíes de Yemen se apoderan del estratégico estrecho de Bab el Mandeb

The US-Iran war that began with American and Israeli strikes in late February 2026 has narrowed to a contest over the Strait of Hormuz and its oil traffic. Iran has used naval and missile power to threaten that passage since the Islamabad collapsed on 8 July and nightly US strikes resumed. The Red Sea route that links Europe and Asia to the same oil markets has stayed open under Saudi-led patrols since the earlier phase of fighting.

On 12 September Houthi forces in Yemen took the entire length of the Yemeni Red Sea coast including the Bab el Mandeb strait. They declared that commercial navigation would continue except for Saudi-flagged vessels.

The strait that now sits under Houthi control feeds directly into the Gulf of Aden and the Indian Ocean approaches used by tankers that have rerouted around the Hormuz fighting. Saudi Arabia has not commented on the new coastal positions or the exception carved out for its own shipping.

Thinking ahead
Houthi rebels took the full Yemeni Red Sea coast on 12 September 2026 and now hold both shores of the Bab el Mandeb strait. They stated they will allow safe passage for all shipping except Saudi vessels. This move sits outside the main US-Iran fight over the Strait of Hormuz that has run since the June memorandum collapsed in July. The Houthis have used Red Sea control as leverage against Saudi Arabia for years. Their latest advance follows the pattern of incremental coastal gains without direct clash with US or Israeli forces. No immediate change in Hormuz tanker traffic or US strike tempo has been reported. The situation will likely hold steady as long as the Houthis face no coordinated naval push from Saudi or Western fleets. Next steps hinge on whether Riyadh responds with its own blockade or whether the US widens its Hormuz campaign to include Red Sea escorts. A shift would require either a Saudi-Houthi ceasefire deal or direct US strikes on Houthi coastal positions. Horizon: 4-6 weeks barring a Saudi naval move.
