19:T510,Cotton arrivals in Sindh hit 1,139,354 bales by Aug 31 this year, up from 870,062 bales at the same point last season, according to the PCGA report. That 31 percent jump follows moderate rains that avoided heavy damage to the crop and early March sowing that delivered 28-30 maunds per acre in places like Matiari and Tando Allahyar. Growers now get Rs 9,500 to Rs 10,000 per 40 kg, better than the Rs 11,000-12,000 offered in June but still enough to keep them putting fertilizer and pesticide on the plants for extra pickings through December.

The same PCGA data shows nationwide arrivals at 1,696,451 bales versus 1,335,632 last year, with Sindh outpacing Punjab’s 557,097 bales. Last season Pakistan finished at 5.607 million bales, 45 percent below the government target. Pink bollworm reports continue and ginners still push to drop the 18 percent tax on binola oilseed and the layered taxes on oilcake that push some lint production into undocumented channels.

This season’s higher arrivals mark the latest step after years of acreage loss to other crops in Punjab and steady weather sensitivity in Sindh. The October PCGA report will show whether August momentum holds once full September data arrives.
