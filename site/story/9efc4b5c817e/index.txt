1e:T4d2,The Supreme Court hears arguments Monday in Johnson v. United States Congress on whether district courts can take constitutional claims against a 1970s-era law that caps VA disability pay for veterans locked up more than 60 days. Floyd Johnson got his PTSD rating cut from 80 percent to 10 percent after his 2013 Florida felony conviction and 40-year sentence. The 11th Circuit ruled last August that the Veterans’ Judicial Review Act routes all such disputes through the VA board, then the Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims, then the Federal Circuit, and that this setup bars district-court review.

This marks the latest step in a line that runs from the 1974 Johnson v. Robison decision through the 1988 act that set the current review channel. The justices will test how far that channel reaches when a veteran claims the cap itself violates the bill of attainder clause and the Fifth Amendment.

The case will run at least through the current term. A ruling would set the next procedural fork: either the district court gets the case back or Johnson must restart inside the VA-Federal Circuit track. Observable signals are the oral-argument transcript in October and the opinion release, likely by June.
