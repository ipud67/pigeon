17:T577,Guterres told the BRICS leaders in New Delhi that inclusive global growth needs a strong multilateral system. The summit wrapped up its two days of talks on resilience, innovation, cooperation and security with the New Delhi Declaration. This sits on the ladder of Gulf states tightening ties with the US after Iran’s moves in the Hormuz clashes and the August trade cutoff by the UAE.

The session on inclusive global governance drew in the expanded BRICS group plus partner countries. Leaders pushed for UN and financial institution reform while the fighting in the strait keeps oil routes tight. South Africa used the platform to line up more intra-BRICS trade and African infrastructure deals tied to the continental free trade area.

How long this runs depends on whether the next BRICS chair keeps the reform push alive and whether the Hormuz naval standoff eases enough for trade lanes to open wider. The procedural next step is the handover to the next chair and follow-up working groups on finance and governance. The situation changes only if a Hormuz ceasefire holds or if a fresh round of direct talks between the US and Iran produces a signed deal.

Next rung likely comes at the foreign ministers meeting scheduled for early 2027 where they set the agenda for the 2027 summit.
