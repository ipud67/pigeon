President Trump proclaimed the Oval Office a national monument

The West Wing of the White House holds the working offices of the president and staff. Its central room took its present oval shape and location under Franklin D. Roosevelt after the 1930s renovation. That space has remained the place where every subsequent president has sat to conduct the daily business of the executive branch and meet foreign leaders. The entire White House complex and grounds already operate as a national historic landmark under the National Park Service.

The new designation places the rooms under a separate layer of protection while they remain inside the working White House used by the president.
