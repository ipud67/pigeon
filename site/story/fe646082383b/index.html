Hundreds of Nigerians flee to Cameroon after fatal violence in Mambilla Plateau

Clashes between cattle herders and crop farmers on the Mambilla Plateau in Taraba State date back at least to 2017 when coordinated attacks hit multiple settlements including Nguroje Mayo Ndaga and Gembu and displaced thousands into Cameroon. Further rounds of retaliatory killings and cattle rustling continued into early 2018. The same pattern of land and resource competition has produced repeated cycles of machete attacks house burnings and livestock deaths in the border districts of Sardauna Local Government Area.

On 13 September a dispute between one herder and one farmer in the Mbamnga area escalated into widespread violence. Machete-wielding groups struck homes in the herder settlement of Wuro Geroji killed an elderly woman named Hajja Mbamnga and her son Shuaibu Bammi along with several security personnel and destroyed houses and animals. The next days saw roughly 502 residents mostly women and children cross into Cameroonian villages at Ntong Fa’am Mfe Nwa and Yang where local officials registered them as displaced.

Taraba authorities placed a 24-hour curfew on Mbamnga and sent additional security forces to contain further attacks. The state assembly member for the constituency Abel Diah publicly demanded immediate deployment to protect remaining residents and a formal investigation to identify those responsible. The displaced remain across the border while forces attempt to restore movement inside the affected communities.
