Officials presented the plan on September 17 as the next concrete step after earlier corridor agreements.

Work is scheduled to start in the coming months once final permits clear. Completion depends on contractors finishing the underground route on time and hooking the new cable into cross-border lines already in place. Any delay in land access or supply of fiber would push the timeline out.

The project runs on state budget and private operator commitments already signed. No new federal approval is required. If the state keeps the current pace, the cable should be live by late 2027 or early 2028.
