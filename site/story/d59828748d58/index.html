Senator blocks bill meant to lower electricity prices linked to data centers

The federal government has regulated electricity markets for decades through the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission, which oversees wholesale power sales and transmission across state lines. Data centers have expanded rapidly in recent years, drawing large blocks of steady power that utilities must supply around the clock, often from the same grid that serves homes and factories. Congress has considered changes to those rules to adjust how costs get divided between different types of customers.

On September 17, Senator Martin Heinrich of New Mexico stopped the from taking up a bill that had cleared the House by a wide margin. The measure would have required regulators to change the way they allocate transmission and generation costs when large new loads such as data centers come online, shifting more of the expense onto those facilities instead of spreading it across all ratepayers.

The bill now sits blocked in the Senate. Its fate turns on whether supporters can find sixty votes to overcome the hold or whether the House and Senate will take up narrower changes to the cost-allocation rules before the current session ends.
