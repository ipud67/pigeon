NIGERIA
September 19, 2026 · 16:38 UTC

NiHSA alerts 15 states to high flood risk

Nigeria’s river systems have carried seasonal flood risk for decades. The Nigeria Hydrological Services Agency tracks water levels at stations along major rivers and issues numbered alerts when forecasts show those levels will reach dangerous heights.

On 19 September 2026 the agency released alert NFA-2026-262. It named 15 states — Imo, Cross River, Ebonyi, Benue, Anambra, Akwa Ibom, Lagos, Rivers, Edo, Kogi, Taraba, Delta, Bayelsa, Enugu and Abia — where riverine flooding is expected between 19 and 25 September. Director-General Umar Mohammed listed nine river stations that now require close monitoring, among them Obubra, Itigidi and Epento on the Cross River. The assessment recorded 2,471 communities, 703 schools, 344 health facilities, 145 markets, 453 religious centres and 854 hectares of farmland inside the flood-exposure zones in Cross River state alone.

State governments, local councils, emergency agencies and community leaders now hold the task of moving people, livestock and goods to higher ground, clearing blocked channels, positioning relief supplies and preparing shelters. NiHSA continues to monitor the same river stations and will release further updates as levels change.

Thinking ahead
NiHSA put out alert NFA-2026-262 on Sept. 19 naming 15 states at high risk of riverine flooding through Sept. 25. The list runs Imo, Cross River, Ebonyi, Benue, Anambra, Akwa Ibom, Lagos, Rivers, Edo, Kogi, Taraba, Delta, Bayelsa, Enugu and Abia, with nine river stations flagged for close watch, including Obubra, Itigidi and Epento on the Cross River. In Cross River the count shows 2,471 communities, 703 schools, 344 health facilities, 145 markets, 453 religious centres and 854 hectares of farmland sitting inside the exposure zones. The agency told state and local officials, emergency teams and community leaders to clear drains, prep shelters, stock water and first-aid kits, and move people, livestock and goods to higher ground where needed. This kind of weekly alert usually runs as long as the river gauges stay high, which in past Nigerian flood seasons has stretched three to six weeks once the rains peak. Next step is daily river-level updates from NiHSA; if any station crosses its red threshold the alert upgrades to evacuation orders for the exact communities downstream. The situation changes only when measured water levels drop below the high-risk line or the rainy season ends. Horizon estimate: through early October unless gauges fall sooner.
