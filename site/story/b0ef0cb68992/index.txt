Eight of the cases are in Texas, including a Mexican national who admitted registering and voting in 2024 and another who faces counts for wire fraud, false passport statements, and illegal gun possession after voting twice. Three cases sit in Georgia with defendants who voted in 2022 or 2024 while holding lawful permanent resident status, one of whom also submitted false naturalization paperwork.

This is the latest step in routine federal election-crime enforcement that has run at a steady pace for years. Each case now moves to arraignment, plea talks, or trial in the district where the voter lived. The number of new filings will stay flat or tick up only if field offices keep routing tips from poll workers and database cross-checks at the same rate.

The situation changes only when prosecutors finish the current batch or when a court ruling narrows what counts as knowing false citizenship on a registration form.
