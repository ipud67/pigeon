House votes to curb AI data center costs

Electricity demand from new data centers built for artificial intelligence training and has driven up power rates for homes and businesses in several states since 2022. Utilities have proposed rate hikes and asked regulators to approve billions in new transmission lines and generating plants to meet the added load. Congress has held hearings on the pace of those approvals but passed no until this week.

On September 16 the House passed the Ratepayer Protection Act by a bipartisan vote. The bill requires federal agencies that review large energy projects to consider the impact on residential electricity bills before granting permits or rate adjustments tied to data-center construction. It also directs the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission to give state consumer a formal role in proceedings that involve AI-related infrastructure.

The measure now goes to the . Its passage sets the first nationwide procedural check on how quickly and at what cost utilities can recover AI-driven capital spending from households.
