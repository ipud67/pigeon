Perelló laments 'disqualifications' of judges from politics and demands respect: they decide 'without yielding to

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlinePerelló lamenta las "descalificaciones" a jueces desde la política y exige respeto: deciden "sin ceder a influencias"

Spain's Supreme Court and its General Council of the Judiciary have operated for decades as the top bodies that name, promote and discipline judges across the country. The council's membership has stood vacant in key seats since 2018 because the two main political parties have repeatedly failed to reach the required three-fifths majority in parliament to renew it. Judges have repeatedly warned that the prolonged deadlock leaves the institution exposed to charges of political influence.

On September 10 2026 Isabel Perelló, who serves as both president of the Supreme Court and of the council, spoke out against what she called repeated disqualifications aimed at judges who hold institutional roles. She stated that judicial decisions are reached without yielding to influences, pressures or interference of any kind and demanded respect for those rulings.

The remarks come as the council continues to function with expired terms and as political debate over judicial appointments shows no sign of resolution. Perelló's position places the next move on the parties that must still agree on a renewal slate before the council can regain its full legal composition.
