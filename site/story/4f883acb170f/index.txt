25:T517,The FMCS just put out an interim final rule that spells out when its Office of Arbitration can turn down a request for a panel, make a direct appointment, or skip the service altogether. The change wipes out old wording that looked like it forced the agency to hand over a panel every time somebody asked, even when the law or the agency’s own rules said no. Because of a paperwork glitch they are republishing the rule and giving the public 30 more days to send in comments.

This is the latest tweak in how the agency runs its arbitration roster after years of handling union and employer requests under the same basic setup. The rule takes effect right away but stays open for feedback.

How much longer this plays out depends on how many comments roll in and whether anyone challenges the rule in court. If comments are light and no lawsuit lands, the clarified language stands and the OA keeps declining panels it thinks are out of bounds. If a federal court blocks the rule or Congress steps in with new legislation, the old “honor every request” reading could come back.

Next rung: comment period closes, FMCS reviews what it got, then decides whether to tweak the final version or let the interim rule ride.
