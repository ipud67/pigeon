Requests for Arbitration Panels

The Federal and Service has run an Office of since the 1940s. Under its rules, any party to a collective bargaining agreement could ask FMCS to supply a list of arbitrators or appoint one directly. The agency treated these requests as largely mandatory.

On August 28, 2026, FMCS published an interim final rule that changes the arbitration services regulation. The new text gives the Office of Arbitration clear power to refuse to issue a panel, make a direct appointment, or supply any related service when the request falls outside FMCS legal authority or other legal constraints. It removes earlier language that could be read to require FMCS to honor every unilateral request.

The rule takes effect immediately but carries a reopened 30-day comment period because of a technical posting error.

Thinking ahead
The FMCS just put out an interim final rule that spells out when its Office of Arbitration can turn down a request for a panel, make a direct appointment, or skip the service altogether. The change wipes out old wording that looked like it forced the agency to hand over a panel every time somebody asked, even when the law or the agency’s own rules said no. Because of a paperwork glitch they are republishing the rule and giving the public 30 more days to send in comments. This is the latest tweak in how the agency runs its arbitration roster after years of handling union and employer requests under the same basic setup. The rule takes effect right away but stays open for feedback. How much longer this plays out depends on how many comments roll in and whether anyone challenges the rule in court. If comments are light and no lawsuit lands, the clarified language stands and the OA keeps declining panels it thinks are out of bounds. If a federal court blocks the rule or Congress steps in with new legislation, the old “honor every request” reading could come back. Next rung: comment period closes, FMCS reviews what it got, then decides whether to tweak the final version or let the interim rule ride. Horizon: 4–8 months until any court filing or final-rule adjustment shows up.
