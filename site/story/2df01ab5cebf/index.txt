19:T561,The right to keep and bear arms is a core founding freedom, secured so free citizens could defend life, liberty, and property without depending on a standing army the founders feared. The Second Amendment declares it shall not be infringed. Congress added the federal firearms disabilities in 1968 as an exercise of commerce power, but the Constitution never gave the national government open-ended authority to strip that right from whole classes of people forever. Article I, Section 8 lists specific powers; everything else belongs to the states or the people under the Tenth Amendment. The founders trusted juries, judges, and the pardon power far more than they trusted a permanent federal bureaucracy to restore rights. Washington himself led the militia to put down the Whiskey Rebellion in 1794 under the Militia Acts of 1792, showing that the early republic handled threats to order with clear law, local participation, and limited federal reach, not blanket administrative disqualifications. When the Attorney General grants relief from these disabilities, he is simply undoing part of what Congress stretched the commerce clause to create in the first place.
