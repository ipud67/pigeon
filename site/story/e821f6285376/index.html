Court of Appeal upholds verdict against four Lundsberg students

Translated from Swedish · read the original →
The original headlineHovrätten fastställer domen mot fyra Lundsbergselever

The Lundsberg boarding school in Sweden has run a system where older students hold authority over younger ones through assigned roles that include discipline and initiation rites. That structure came under criminal after reports surfaced of repeated physical attacks on first-year students in the fall of 2024.

On 18 September 2026 the Court of Appeal for Western Sweden upheld the district court's of four older students for assault. The appellate panel sustained the findings that the four had beaten four younger students as part of the initiation practice, though it adjusted some of the sentences and damage awards.

The four now face the upheld guilty verdicts and must pay to the victims. The case turns on whether the school's internal hierarchy and the specific acts it permitted crossed the line into criminal assault under Swedish law.

Thinking ahead
The Court of Appeal for Western Sweden upheld most of the lower court verdict against the four Lundsberg students convicted of assault last winter. The four older students had been found guilty of attacking four younger ones at the boarding school. This decision leaves the convictions and sentences largely in place. The next procedural step is a possible appeal to the Supreme Court, which would have to grant leave first. Without that leave the case ends here. A Supreme Court review would require the appellants to show a point of law that needs clarification nationwide. The upheld verdict stands unless the Supreme Court takes the case and overturns it. Horizon estimate: case likely closed within 3-6 months if no Supreme Court leave.
