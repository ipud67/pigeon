18:T487,Oilserv signed an MoU with Gabon on September 18 to set up a framework for developing hydrocarbon reserves in the DEF-3 and D4-5 blocks and to build gas transport, compression and treatment infrastructure. The deal follows years of Nigerian firms pushing into West and Central African energy projects after domestic successes in pipeline and facility work. Gabon’s petroleum minister called for policy changes on skills and capital retention to make such ventures stick.

This kind of MoU usually takes 12 to 24 months to turn into firm contracts while both sides finish technical studies, secure permits and line up financing. Next steps will show in award of front-end engineering contracts or government approval of a joint operating agreement for the blocks. The pace will hinge on how fast Gabon clears local-content rules and whether Oilserv brings in partners for the gas-to-power piece.

If the technical work confirms commercial volumes and funding closes, first gas flow could start by late 2028 or 2029. Without visible contract awards or rig mobilization by mid-2027 the timeline will slip.
