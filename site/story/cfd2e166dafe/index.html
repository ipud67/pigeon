Record jail sentence for Israeli money launderer after Sydney trial

Shabtay Yaacoby built and ran Blue Star Exchange as a licensed digital currency business in Australia that let customers buy and sell cryptocurrencies. The company and other firms tied to him took in money from people who thought they were making legitimate investments such as high-interest term deposits. Those transfers began years earlier and continued through 2023.

On 17 September 2026 Justice Averre sentenced the 56-year-old Israeli to 14 years in prison with parole eligibility after nine years. The NSW District Court found Yaacoby played a leading role in a criminal enterprise that knowingly moved more than $50 million in scam proceeds out of the country. Thirty-nine victims gave evidence at trial including one New South Wales man who lost about $4 million from a shared superannuation fund after wiring it to Blue Star Exchange. The judge ruled the offending was not isolated and persisted over time even though Yaacoby took no part in the initial fraud itself. Yaacoby was arrested in Sydney in November 2023 while trying to unfreeze accounts linked to the businesses. The court heard he had used cocaine during the period of the crimes and later received a PTSD diagnosis but neither condition reduced his planning ability or understanding of the acts. No reparation was offered before sentencing.

The sentence stands as the longest yet given in Australia for money laundering. Justice Averre also ordered reparations to the victims.
