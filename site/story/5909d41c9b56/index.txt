The fight for the strait has run on alternating US strikes against Iranian naval and logistics targets and Iranian attempts to harass or block tanker traffic since the Islamabad Memorandum collapsed on 8 July.

Both sides keep enough forces in place to keep trading blows without widening the theater. Iran still fields anti-ship missiles and small boat squadrons along the narrow channel. The US maintains escort ships and air patrols that can reach any point in the strait within minutes. Neither side has moved extra divisions or squadrons into the immediate area in the last month.

The pattern holds until one side changes the force count or the rules of engagement. A new US escort package or an Iranian mine-laying run would mark the next shift. Without those moves the exchange of single-ship strikes and missile shots continues at the current low intensity.
