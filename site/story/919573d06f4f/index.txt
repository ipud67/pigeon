18:T4a7,The USTR opened a Section 301 investigation into Germany’s pricing rules for innovative drugs after years of complaints from US drug makers that Berlin’s reference pricing and reimbursement caps keep payments below what those firms say covers their R&D costs. Tomorrow’s public hearing is the next procedural step: interested parties file written comments and then give on-the-record testimony before USTR staff in a room closed to cameras. A transcript follows within days.

How much longer this runs depends on how fast USTR moves from hearing to formal findings. Past Section 301 cases on trading partners’ pharma rules have taken six to eighteen months from hearing to any tariff or negotiation outcome. The case advances only if USTR decides Germany’s system burdens US commerce; if the two governments open talks instead, the docket can stretch years with no deadlines.

Next step after the transcript posts is USTR’s written determination on whether to list Germany as a priority foreign country or propose remedies. Without a change in German law or a bilateral deal that raises Berlin’s payments, the probe stays open.
