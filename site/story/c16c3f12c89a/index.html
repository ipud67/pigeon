ISRAEL
September 20, 2026 · 14:28 UTC

A poll showed 17 percent of Israelis and 21 percent of Palestinians believe peaceful coexistence is possible in the West Bank

Israel and Iran have struck each other since late February 2026. The US and Israel began the campaign with air and cyber attacks on roughly 500 Iranian targets. Iran answered with missile and drone barrages on Israel and US bases in the Gulf. The fighting settled into weekly exchanges that reached Lebanon and the Strait of Hormuz. A June lifted the dual and set a 60-day negotiating window. That window closed on 8 July after Iran hit commercial tankers and tried to shut the strait again. US strikes have continued nightly since then while the fight narrowed to control of the strait.

A Pew Research Center poll released on 20 September 2026 found that 17 percent of Israelis and 21 percent of Palestinians in the West Bank and east Jerusalem believe Israel and an independent Palestinian state can coexist peacefully. Fifty-six percent of Israelis and 68 percent of those Palestinians said coexistence was not possible. The survey did not reach Gaza. Among Jewish Israelis the share saying coexistence was possible stood at 16 percent. Among Arab Israelis it stood at 23 percent. The Israeli figure marked the first time since Pew began asking the question in 2013 that more than half said peaceful coexistence was impossible.

The poll asked those who rejected coexistence to explain their answers in their own words. Israelis who said no most often cited security risks from a Palestinian state that could arm itself or turn hostile. Palestinians who said no most often cited Israeli settlements, checkpoints, and limits on movement that they said had made a viable state impossible. Both sides named disputes over land, repeated violence, and mutual distrust as core barriers. The results sit inside the larger US-Iran war now centered on the Strait of Hormuz.

Thinking ahead
A Pew poll conducted in the West Bank and east Jerusalem this month shows 21 percent of Palestinians there and 17 percent of Israelis overall believe an independent Palestinian state and Israel can coexist peacefully. That Israeli figure marks the first time since the question was first asked in 2013 that more than half said coexistence is impossible. Among Jewish Israelis the possible share sits at 16 percent, while Arab Israelis stand at 23 percent. The survey lands inside a regional war that began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran in late February 2026, narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz, saw a June memorandum lift the dual blockades for a 60-day negotiating window, and returned to sustained US strikes after that window collapsed on 8 July. Palestinian respondents who said coexistence cannot happen most often cited Israeli settlements, checkpoints, and movement restrictions as the barrier that has made a viable state impossible. Israeli respondents who gave the same answer most often pointed to security risks from a hostile Palestinian state or from leaders they believe seek Israel's destruction. No court action or procedural step is scheduled on the two-state question. The next observable change would require either a shift in territorial control on the ground or a new round of direct talks that produces an agreement both sides accept. At present the indicators give no clear signal of either. This low coexistence reading is likely to hold steady for months unless major territorial or diplomatic movement occurs.
