1b:T612,A Pew poll conducted in the West Bank and east Jerusalem this month shows 21 percent of Palestinians there and 17 percent of Israelis overall believe an independent Palestinian state and Israel can coexist peacefully. That Israeli figure marks the first time since the question was first asked in 2013 that more than half said coexistence is impossible. Among Jewish Israelis the possible share sits at 16 percent, while Arab Israelis stand at 23 percent.

The survey lands inside a regional war that began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran in late February 2026, narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz, saw a June memorandum lift the dual blockades for a 60-day negotiating window, and returned to sustained US strikes after that window collapsed on 8 July. Palestinian respondents who said coexistence cannot happen most often cited Israeli settlements, checkpoints, and movement restrictions as the barrier that has made a viable state impossible. Israeli respondents who gave the same answer most often pointed to security risks from a hostile Palestinian state or from leaders they believe seek Israel's destruction.

No court action or procedural step is scheduled on the two-state question. The next observable change would require either a shift in territorial control on the ground or a new round of direct talks that produces an agreement both sides accept. At present the indicators give no clear signal of either.
