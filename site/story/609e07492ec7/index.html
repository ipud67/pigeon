ISRAEL
September 17, 2026 · 06:28 UTC

Israel National Biotech Innovation Center (INBI) launches with Anat Cohen-Dayag as CEO of INBI Core

Israel's biomedical research sector has operated for decades inside a network of universities, hospitals and independent labs that produce discoveries but often struggle to move them into drug development and clinical testing. Fifteen of those institutions — from the Weizmann Institute and Technion to Sheba Medical Center, Tel Aviv University and Clalit Health Services — have worked separately on early-stage science without a single national bridge to industry capital and pathways.

On 17 September 2026 the Israel National Biotech Innovation Center opened operations as a nonprofit central hub to take those discoveries forward. It formed INBI Core Ltd as a wholly owned subsidiary, appointed Anat Cohen-Dayag, longtime executive chairperson of Compugen, as CEO of the subsidiary, and named Samuel Moed, former head of corporate strategy at Bristol Myers Squibb, as chairperson of both INBI and INBI Core. The center supplies a shared team, , funding and commercialisation channels that the individual partners previously lacked.

The new structure now links every major Israeli source of life-sciences research under one translational engine, with 8400 The Health Network continuing to coordinate the global industry contacts that feed capital and partnerships into the portfolio.

Thinking ahead
Israel launched the National Biotech Innovation Center on 17 September 2026 with Anat Cohen-Dayag as CEO of its core operating arm. The nonprofit pulls together 15 universities, hospitals and research outfits to move lab work into drug candidates, funded and steered through a central translation pipeline. Samuel Moed chairs the board. This sits apart from the US-Iran fighting that has held the Strait of Hormuz since July. No visible tie exists between the new center and the conflict. The launch marks one more step in a long Israeli pattern of building applied-research bodies. It will keep running as long as the partner institutions keep supplying projects and the 8400 network keeps routing global partners and capital. Next moves are hiring the drug-development team, picking the first programs and opening formal ties to outside investors. The center stays on course unless budget shortfalls hit or the war pulls key medical-center staff or funding into emergency roles. It runs at least another 24 months before the first clinical-stage hand-offs are due.
