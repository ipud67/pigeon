1b:T504,Trump signed the bill into law on September 18 that sets up new sanctions on Russia. The measure passed both chambers of Congress with veto-proof margins after months of negotiation between the two parties. It requires the executive branch to report back within 90 days on which Russian entities and individuals meet the criteria spelled out in the text.

The law sits on top of earlier rounds of sanctions imposed in 2017, 2019, and 2022. Those earlier measures targeted energy exports, banks, and specific officials; this one adds fresh triggers tied to military aid and cyber activity. Once the first report lands, agencies must start freezing assets and blocking transactions unless Congress votes to lift the requirements.

The clock now runs on that 90-day reporting deadline. Sanctions kick in automatically after the report unless the president certifies a national-security waiver and Congress accepts it. Without a waiver or a new law to repeal the triggers, the restrictions stay in place and can only widen if further Russian actions trip the listed conditions.

The situation stays on this track until either a congressional repeal passes both houses and is signed or a president issues and defends a waiver that survives review.
