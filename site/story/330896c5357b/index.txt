17:T41a,NASA aircraft will fly low over Colorado farms starting in late September 2026 to measure ozone, methane, ammonia, and particulates coming off crop fields and livestock operations. The FarmFlux mission carries more than a dozen sensors that track how these gases and particles move into the lower atmosphere and interact there. The project is run jointly from NASA’s Goddard Space Flight Center and teams based in Colorado.

The flights follow years of ground-based sampling that showed agricultural sources make up a sizable but poorly mapped slice of regional air chemistry. Data collection is scheduled to run for several weeks this fall, with follow-on analysis taking most of 2027. The program ends when the aircraft complete their set number of passes and the instruments are packed up; nothing short of a major mechanical failure or sudden airspace restriction would stop it before then.

Next step is the first set of low-altitude runs over target farms, already cleared with local operators.
