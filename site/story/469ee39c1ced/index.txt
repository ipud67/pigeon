CDU posted its lowest result ever in the state and may fall short of the five-percent threshold needed for seats. This marks the latest in a string of eastern-state gains for AfD and Die Linke that have pushed CDU-led coalitions into tighter spots since the federal shift earlier this year.

The state parliament now has thirty days to convene and elect a minister-president. Any coalition needs at least 35 seats; current numbers leave the Social Democrats short without partners who have ruled out deals with AfD. CDU would need to clear the threshold or form an unlikely cross-party bloc to regain ground.

The pattern holds as long as eastern turnout stays high for AfD and Die Linke while CDU vote share keeps sliding below 20 percent in these states. A change would require either a federal policy shift that lifts CDU numbers in the east or a collapse in AfD support after the next state vote in Saxony-Anhalt.

Next likely step is weeks of talks among the Social Democrats, Die Linke, and smaller parties to test minority or three-way options.
