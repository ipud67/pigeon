Painful loss for CDU in German state: first time it fails to clear electoral threshold

Translated from Dutch · read the original →
The original headlinePijnlijk verlies CDU in Duitse deelstaat: voor het eerst kiesdrempel niet gehaald

Germany's state elections operate under a mixed-member proportional system that awards seats in the Landtag to parties clearing a five-percent threshold. Mecklenburg-Vorpommern held its most recent vote on 20 September 2026 after the previous parliament's term ended.

An ARD exit poll released at 19:57 on election day showed the Alternative für Deutschland taking 37.9 percent of the vote and the Social Democratic Party at 35.7 percent. The Christian Democratic Union led by Chancellor Friedrich Merz recorded its lowest share in the state since reunification and it remained unclear whether the party crossed the five-percent threshold needed to enter the .

The outcome leaves the largest bloc without an obvious partner inside the Landtag while the two parties that finished first and second both sit outside the federal governing coalition in Berlin.

Thinking ahead
AfD took first place in the Mecklenburg-Vorpommern state vote with 37.9 percent, just ahead of the Social Democrats at 35.7 percent, according to the ARD exit poll. CDU posted its lowest result ever in the state and may fall short of the five-percent threshold needed for seats. This marks the latest in a string of eastern-state gains for AfD and Die Linke that have pushed CDU-led coalitions into tighter spots since the federal shift earlier this year. The state parliament now has thirty days to convene and elect a minister-president. Any coalition needs at least 35 seats; current numbers leave the Social Democrats short without partners who have ruled out deals with AfD. CDU would need to clear the threshold or form an unlikely cross-party bloc to regain ground. The pattern holds as long as eastern turnout stays high for AfD and Die Linke while CDU vote share keeps sliding below 20 percent in these states. A change would require either a federal policy shift that lifts CDU numbers in the east or a collapse in AfD support after the next state vote in Saxony-Anhalt. Next likely step is weeks of talks among the Social Democrats, Die Linke, and smaller parties to test minority or three-way options. Horizon: at least six months until the next eastern state election tests whether the CDU floor holds.
