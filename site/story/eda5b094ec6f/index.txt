Under this system, foreign producers may send up to 1,117,195 metric tons raw value into the United States at the lower tariff rate set by statute; any amount above that level faces the higher statutory tariff. The announcement completes the allocation of the full quota amount to which the United States committed in the Uruguay Round Agreements that implemented the World Trade Organization framework. Congress holds the power to lay and collect duties under Article I, Section 8, Clause 1 of the Constitution, and it has long used tariffs both to raise revenue and to protect domestic industry. The first Congress passed the Tariff Act of 1789, which imposed duties on imported goods including sugar to fund the new government and encourage American agriculture and manufacturing. That act and the later protective tariffs of the early republic rested on the understanding that trade policy belongs to elected representatives accountable to the people, not to international bodies. The executive branch here carries out the quota numbers fixed by statute and the WTO minimum commitment.
