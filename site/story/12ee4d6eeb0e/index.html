IRAN
August 31, 2026 · 08:23 UTC

Trump says Iran striking Gulf neighbors was 'very good thing,' Israel would be gone without him

“Everybody was shocked. I was surprised. I thought it was a very good thing because they lost all of the support they had,”
Overview
"Everybody was shocked. I was surprised. I thought it was a very good thing because they lost all of the support they had," Trump said.
Short history
The US and Israel began strikes on Iran in late February 2026. A June lifted the mutual on the Strait of Hormuz and set a 60-day negotiating window; it collapsed on 8 July after Iran hit three commercial tankers, triggering renewed nightly US strikes. The fighting has since narrowed to control of the Strait. On 31 August 2026 Trump said Iran’s recent strikes on Gulf neighbors were a very good thing because they cost Iran the regional support it once had. He added that Israel would be gone without him. The UAE had cut trade with Iran on 19 August after accusing it of firing two missiles toward its waters.
Predictive model
Trump’s remark on 31 August that Iran’s recent strike on Gulf neighbors was “a very good thing” because it cost Tehran regional support lines up with the August 19 UAE decision to cut all trade after two Iranian missiles headed toward its waters. That move is the latest in a string of Arab states pulling away since Iran’s April strategy began backfiring and validating their fears. The war has been stuck on control of the Strait of Hormuz since the July 8 collapse of the 60-day Islamabad Memorandum, with nightly US strikes, Iranian tanker attacks, and attempts to close the strait. The pattern shows Iran’s moves keep handing neighbors fresh reasons to side with the US-led bloc instead of staying neutral. Each new Gulf incident tightens that isolation. Next likely step is either another Iranian strike that costs it one more partner or a US push to widen the coalition now that Arab doors are open again. This phase runs until Iran stops handing out reasons for neighbors to cut ties or until a new negotiation window is forced open. Without one of those two shifts the cycle of strike, isolation, and continued US pressure keeps repeating. Horizon: 4–8 weeks until next major coalition shift or new ceasefire talks.
