An appeals court blocked the swift of immigrants to third countries

The Department of Homeland Security has operated under a policy since 2019 that allowed federal officers to send certain immigrants to countries other than their own for removal proceedings when their home nation would not accept them back. That arrangement expanded in 2024 to cover more nationalities and relied on agreements with third countries that had agreed to accept the arrivals.

On September 19 2026 a three-judge panel of the appeals court issued a unanimous ruling that struck down the policy. The panel held that the procedure violated the specific requirements of the Immigration and Nationality Act for how removal orders must be issued and reviewed before transfer to a third country takes place.

The decision blocks immediate deportation under the struck-down rule and returns the question to the district court for further proceedings on what remedies apply to those already affected. The government can still pursue removal through the standard process that keeps the immigrant’s home country as the first destination.

Thinking ahead
A federal appeals court ruled against a Department of Homeland Security policy that sent some immigrants to third countries for quick removal. The three-judge panel decided the policy violated existing rules on how removals must happen. This blocks the government from using that shortcut until it changes the policy or wins at a higher court. The case sits on a ladder of earlier court checks on removal speed and destination rules. The unanimous opinion sets a clear procedural hurdle: DHS must now either rewrite the policy to match the court's reading or ask the Supreme Court to step in. Lower courts have blocked similar fast-track moves before when paperwork and notice fell short. How long this holds depends on whether DHS rewrites the rule or appeals. The next clear step is either a revised policy from the agency or a petition for Supreme Court review, which usually takes months to decide on hearing. Without one of those moves the block stays in place. This setup likely drags on for several months at minimum. It heads next to either a DHS rewrite or a Supreme Court docket. Horizon estimate: 4-8 months until next decisive filing or policy shift.
