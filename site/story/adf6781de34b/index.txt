17:T5f2,Two lawsuits filed this week in California federal and state courts target paintings held by the Norton Simon Museum in Pasadena and the Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum in Poland. The first suit, brought by the Jewish Federation Los Angeles after receiving assignment of claims from heir Marei von Saher, relies on a 2024 state law that lets California residents pursue items taken during political persecution and on a federal statute that blocks foreign government acts as a defense in Nazi-looted art cases. That claim follows a 2007 federal suit by von Saher that ended in 2018 when the Ninth Circuit ruled courts could not override the Dutch government's postwar transfer of the Lucas Cranach the Elder works. The second suit seeks watercolors made by Auschwitz prisoner Dina Gottliebova Babbitt under orders from Josef Mengele.

Both cases now sit at the motion-to-dismiss stage, where museums will argue statutes of limitations, prior rulings, and foreign sovereign immunity. The Federation has said it will donate most proceeds from any recovery to aid Holocaust survivors. Progress turns on whether the new statutes clear the procedural bars that stopped the 2007 case and on whether Polish museum officials respond in U.S. court.

The suits will likely take 18 to 36 months to reach any trial or settlement, driven by discovery fights over provenance documents and foreign-government defenses. Next step is the museums' answers or dismissal motions, due in coming weeks.
