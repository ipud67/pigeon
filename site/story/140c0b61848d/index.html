FBI temporarily paused cooperation with Canadian counterpart over CBC 9/11 references - report

The FBI and the Royal Canadian Mounted Police have worked together for decades on cross-border cases that include terrorism investigations and cybersecurity. That cooperation began in its modern form after the 2001 attacks on the United States and has continued through changes in both governments.

On August 19 the Canadian Broadcasting sent a memo to its journalists directing them not to use the word terrorism for the September 11 attacks unless they it to an expert. FBI Director Kash Patel saw the memo and ordered a temporary pause in some FBI-RCMP meetings and joint work. The pause lasted only days before the CBC reversed its guidance in late August and said the term could be used without attribution. Canadian officials described the original memo as pedantic. Twenty-four Canadians died in the attacks.

The pause ended without a lasting break in operational ties. The RCMP stated that it still values the partnership on national-security threats. The two agencies now continue their regular exchanges while the broader US-Canadian relationship remains strained by an ongoing tariff dispute that began in early 2025.
