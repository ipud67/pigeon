1a:T522,The UN Secretary-General told the Security Council today that attacks on food systems are feeding a cycle of hunger and fighting with no quick fix in sight. This is the latest call in a string of similar briefings the Council has heard over the past two years as conflicts in places like Sudan, Gaza, and Ukraine keep wrecking farms, silos, and supply routes. Guterres pushed for concrete Council action on humanitarian access, protection of civilian infrastructure, and better early-warning systems.

How much longer this runs depends on whether member states turn the speech into a resolution or working group with teeth. Right now the observable indicators are thin: another high-level meeting, repeated warnings, and no announced follow-up vote or sanctions mechanism. Without a procedural step like a draft resolution tabled or a dedicated monitoring mandate approved, the cycle described today is likely to grind on for years in the active conflict zones.

Next rung would be any Council member putting forward specific language on food-security safeguards or an Arria-formula meeting that actually produces agreed language. If those stay missing, the pattern of speeches without measurable enforcement continues.

Horizon: at least 12-24 months of repeated warnings unless a binding Council product
