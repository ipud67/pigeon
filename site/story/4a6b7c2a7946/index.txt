Similar clusters turned up in Ohio Amish areas and scattered cases reached 44 other states plus D.C., with South Carolina at 370 and Utah at 535 per the CDC's Oct. 2 tally. New York added an executive order declaring a statewide disaster emergency after logging 108 cases.

The outbreak keeps growing because many household contacts show measles-like symptoms but never get tested unless they land in the hospital, which leaves the real number higher than the official line and slows containment. State health updates come out weekly. Hospital records of complications like encephalitis, hepatitis, and severe diarrhea in otherwise healthy patients signal wider community spread.

The count will likely climb for weeks until either vaccination crews reach the hold-out pockets or natural immunity from recovered cases builds a wall. Next visible step is more county-level testing pushes and possible tighter school entry rules where clusters sit. Without those, the map widens into neighboring states that share low-vaccination pockets.
