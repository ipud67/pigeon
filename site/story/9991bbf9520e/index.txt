The Constitution gives the federal government power to regulate commerce among the states, yet the founders drew that power narrowly and expected it to protect honest dealing rather than substitute federal judgment for every private bargain. The Federalist No. 42 explains that the commerce clause was meant to stop states from erecting trade barriers, not to create a national nanny for every sales pitch. Early statutes such as the Tariff Act of 1789 and the first patent and copyright laws show Congress focused on clear rules that let people keep the fruits of their labor and trade without constant federal second-guessing of business models.

When government steps in to police earnings claims and training materials sold between private parties, it risks turning the limited commerce power into a general police power the Tenth Amendment reserves to the states or the people. The founders insisted that free men must be allowed to make their own mistakes and learn from them; the alternative is endless bureaucratic supervision that crowds out the personal responsibility required for self-government. 1a:T58f,The FTC and the state of Washington filed a complaint against Amway Corp., World Wide Group, and Leadership Team Development on September 17, 2026. The complaint lists specific practices: earnings claims that overstated likely income, instructions to IBOs to buy fixed monthly volumes of product regardless of resale or personal use, and directions to log fake sales to make the business look product-driven instead of recruitment-driven. The proposed settlement order requires the three entities to pay $225 million, the largest FTC recovery from any multilevel marketing operator to date, with nearly all of it earmarked for consumer refunds.

This action sits on a ladder of prior FTC cases against other multilevel marketing firms that ended in monetary judgments and conduct restrictions once complaints detailed similar earnings misrepresentations and inventory loading. The current case follows the same pattern: complaint first, then a proposed order that still needs court approval to become final.

The case will likely drag on for months while the court reviews the order for fairness. If approved, the companies pay and operate under the order’s limits on claims and purchase requirements. If the court rejects it or the parties renegotiate, the matter returns to litigation. The next observable step is the court’s decision on the proposed order.
