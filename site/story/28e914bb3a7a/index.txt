22:T5fb,North Korean group WaterPlum hit more than 30,000 devices from December 2025 through July 2026, stole login details for about 7,000 crypto accounts, and moved roughly ¥1.7 billion in crypto to wallets it controlled. The National Police Agency, FBI, and five other agencies from four countries released a joint public attribution document on September 19 naming the group and tying its malware servers to the same IP addresses used by North Korean IT workers living in North Korea, China, and Russia who took remote jobs under fake names. Japanese police broke up the local support network that supplied computers, servers, ID documents, and bank accounts for those workers.

The operation sits inside the 313 General Bureau, the same unit that handles weapons development and overseas IT earnings for the Workers Party. Public attribution documents like this one usually trigger follow-on law enforcement actions such as account freezes, new malware signatures pushed to antivirus firms, and updated takedown requests to hosting providers. The next visible step will be whether other countries issue their own arrest warrants or sanctions packages tied to the named bureau.

How long this specific campaign stays quiet depends on whether WaterPlum rebuilds its infrastructure and changes its recruitment lures. The support network in Japan is dismantled for now, so fresh infections would need new overseas helpers and fresh IP ranges.
