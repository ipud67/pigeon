‘Going to have as many non-Jews on stage as possible,’ Leo Terrell says of upcoming 15-city tour to fight Jew-hatred

The Justice Department maintains a task force that tracks antisemitic incidents in the United States. Leo Terrell chairs that task force. The position was created after a sharp rise in reports of harassment and vandalism directed at Jewish institutions and individuals that began in late 2023.

On September 8 Terrell told an audience at a JNS event in Las Vegas that he will open a 15-city tour later this year. He said the tour will place as many non-Jewish speakers on stage as possible in each city. The stated purpose of the appearances is to demonstrate that opposition to antisemitism crosses lines of background and faith.

Terrell’s remarks position the tour as an extension of the task force’s work. The schedule and exact lineup remain under development, with the first stops still to be announced.

Thinking ahead
Leo Terrell chairs the Justice Department task force on Jew-hatred. He spoke at a JNS event in Las Vegas on September 8 2026 and laid out plans for a 15-city tour. The tour will put non-Jews on stage as often as possible to show hate comes in all shapes and forms. The tour is the next procedural step after the task force appointment and the Las Vegas remarks. It runs as a series of public events aimed at shifting who appears in anti-hatred messaging. Continuation depends on turnout at the first stops, follow-on funding from the task force or private backers, and whether federal priorities stay fixed after any change in administration. No court filings or official tallies mark measurable shifts yet. The schedule itself supplies the only hard indicator right now. This runs at least through the 15-city slate and likely longer if early stops draw repeat invitations. Next visible rung is the first tour date and headcount of non-Jewish speakers.
