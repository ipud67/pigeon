1c:T42c,The US and Denmark reached a security deal for Greenland on September 19 after months of Trump threats to take the territory by force. The agreement gives the US permanent control over security matters and veto power on military presence or sensitive investments by adversaries, with no cost to Washington. It still needs parliamentary approval in Denmark after a planned signing next week at the UN General Assembly.

Denmark and Greenland leaders described the pact as strengthening Arctic and North Atlantic security while preserving Danish sovereignty and Greenland self-determination. NATO called it a step toward regional stability and cooperation. No full text has been released.

This phase of open threats and diplomatic friction is likely over once the deal clears parliament. The next visible step is the formal signing and then any follow-on talks on basing or investment rules. The situation stays stable unless one side walks away from ratification or a new external threat appears in the Arctic.
