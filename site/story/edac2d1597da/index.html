WASHINGTON
October 9, 2026

United States gave emergency tax relief on diesel fuel in Washington

The federal government has collected excise taxes on diesel fuel used in highway vehicles and off-road equipment since the Highway Revenue Act. Those taxes, set at 24.3 cents per gallon for most diesel under 26 U.S.C. 4041, flow into the Highway Trust Fund. Farmers and truckers pay the tax when they buy clear diesel or when they use dyed diesel on public roads, where such use carries a separate penalty under 26 U.S.C. 6715.

On October 6, 2026, President Trump signed an executive order directing the Secretary of the Treasury to determine within five days whether a qualifying event under 26 U.S.C. 7508A has occurred that would allow deferral of the taxes imposed by 26 U.S.C. 4041(a)(1)(A) and 4041(b)(1)(B) for fuel purchased or used between October 5 and December 31, 2026. The order also requires the Secretary to direct the Internal Revenue Service not to impose penalties for selling or using dyed diesel on highways during that same period and to forgo penalties for missed semimonthly deposits of the tax.

The order now requires the Treasury Secretary to issue guidance that lists exactly which taxpayers and liabilities are covered, sets the start and end dates, and names the deadline for eventual payment of any deferred amounts. It further instructs the Secretary to study ways, including new legislation, to cancel the deferred tax obligation entirely and to review how the Internal Revenue Service assigns enforcement resources to diesel-fuel compliance.

Constitutional analysis
The President issued an executive order directing the Secretary of the Treasury to determine whether 26 U.S.C. 7508A authorizes deferral of excise tax payments on diesel fuel under 26 U.S.C. 4041(a)(1)(A) and 26 U.S.C. 4041(b)(1)(B) for the period from October 5, 2026, through December 31, 2026. The order also directs the Secretary to provide penalty relief without interest or additional amounts for those deferred taxes and to instruct the Internal Revenue Service not to impose penalties under 26 U.S.C. 6715(a)(1) or 26 U.S.C. 6715(a)(2) for use of dyed diesel fuel on highways during the same period. The Secretary of Agriculture and the Secretary of Transportation must facilitate implementation, and the Secretary of the Treasury must issue guidance that lists the covered taxpayers, liabilities, deadlines, and the date postponed taxes must be paid. The order claims authority vested in the President by the Constitution and the laws of the United States. Article I, Section 8, Clause 1 of the Constitution grants Congress the power to lay and collect taxes, duties, imposts, and excises. The Federalist No. 30 from 1788 explains that the power of taxation resides in the legislature because only the consent of the governed through their representatives can justify taking property. The order further directs the Secretary to explore legislation that would forgive the deferred tax amounts. No court ruling or statute in the order resolves whether the executive can defer or forgive taxes without new legislation from Congress.
Thinking ahead
The president signed an order directing the Treasury secretary to check within five days if 26 U.S.C. 7508A lets him defer excise taxes on diesel under sections 4041(a)(1)(A) and 4041(b)(1)(B) for fuel used from October 5 through December 31 2026. The same order tells the IRS to skip penalties on dyed diesel used on highways in that window and on missed semimonthly deposits. Treasury must then publish guidance spelling out who qualifies, the exact dates, and when the deferred money has to be paid. This relief sits on top of earlier steps the administration took to hold down fuel costs for farmers and truckers. The next concrete steps are the secretary’s decision inside five days, the IRS announcement, and the guidance that follows. The order also tells the secretary to look at ways to cancel the deferred taxes outright, which would need new legislation or other legal moves. The situation stays in place through the end of 2026 unless the secretary finds the law does not allow the deferral or Congress changes the underlying tax rules. Horizon: through December 31 2026, or until legislation alters the tax obligation.
