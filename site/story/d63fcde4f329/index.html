Dumek Turbay rebuts Gustavo Petro with numbers after ex-president said tourism in Cartagena is over: 'Ideological wars'

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlineDumek Turbay desmintió con cifras a Gustavo Petro tras el expresidente afirmar que en Cartagena se acabó el turismo: “Guerras ideológicas”

Cartagena has operated as a Caribbean port and tourism center for centuries, drawing visitors to its colonial walls, beaches and historic districts. The city recorded steady growth in arrivals until the pandemic halted travel in 2020. Recovery began the following year and continued through successive national administrations, with local officials tracking monthly visitor numbers through airports, cruise terminals and hotels.

On September 13 2026 Mayor Dumek Turbay released figures showing more than four million travelers had arrived in the city through the first nine months of the year. The count covered both domestic and international visitors and stood in direct contrast to a statement by former president Gustavo Petro that tourism in Cartagena had collapsed.

The data placed the annual pace on track to exceed previous records. Turbay framed the exchange as an ideological dispute rather than a reflection of actual arrivals, while the city continued to operate its port, hotels and tour operations under the same that governed the prior decade of measured growth.
