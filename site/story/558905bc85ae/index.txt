That leaves a €45 billion gap staring at European capitals and the Ukrainian finance ministry right now. This sits on top of years of repeated aid rounds that started after the 2022 invasion and have steadily run into donor fatigue and domestic budget fights in several EU states.

The shortfall is already on the table for the next donor coordination meetings scheduled later this fall. Brussels has not put new money on offer yet, and the gap size is now a public benchmark any additional pledges will be measured against. No court action or fresh disbursement trigger has moved since the announcement.

How much longer this runs depends on how fast EU members and other donors step up to close that exact one-third hole. Without visible new commitments by year-end, the funding conversation will simply roll into 2027 talks at the same shortfall level.

Next likely step: EU finance ministers discuss gap-filling options at their October or November meeting.
