Problems at airports in northeastern US due to telecom outage

Translated from Dutch · read the original →
The original headlineProblemen op luchthavens in noordoosten VS door telecomstoring

Air traffic control in the northeastern United States relies on telecom lines that feed radar data, voice communications, and flight plans into tower and TRACON systems. Those systems have operated without major regional failure for years under FAA oversight that requires redundant links between facilities and airlines.

A telecom outage struck on September 21 and cut those links at multiple sites. The FAA responded by halting all departures and arrivals at Philadelphia International Airport and Newark Liberty International Airport in New Jersey. It also stopped traffic for short periods at New York's JFK and LaGuardia airports and at Boston's Logan.

Limited operations resumed at the two primary airports under backup procedures that allow only a fraction of normal traffic. The rest of the affected airports returned to full capacity once the telecom feed was restored, while controllers continued to work with reduced data from the remaining lines. The outage's root cause remains under investigation by the FAA and the affected carriers.

Thinking ahead
A telecom outage hit air traffic control systems and forced the FAA to ground flights at Newark and Philadelphia airports on September 21 2026. Traffic halted for a short time at JFK, LaGuardia, and Boston as well. The outage cut voice and data links that controllers use to sequence arrivals and departures, so only a few planes can move under tight manual rules right now. The FAA has run similar outages before and usually restores full service once the telecom provider fixes the broken circuits or switches. Crews are already testing backup lines. Full operations return when the primary links come back online and controllers confirm they can talk to every plane in their sector without gaps. If the outage drags past a few hours, airlines will start canceling flights for the rest of the day because crews time out and gates fill up. The next visible step is the FAA issuing a notice when the systems test clean. This is likely to wrap up in less than 24 hours once the telecom gear is back.
