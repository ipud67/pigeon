Dozens of House Democrats vote with Republicans to table Trump impeachment attempt

House Democrats have held the minority in the House since Republicans took the majority in the 2024 elections. Since then the chamber has seen repeated attempts by individual Democrats to force impeachment votes against President Trump on subjects ranging from policy disputes to specific agency actions. Those efforts have routinely failed along party lines because articles of impeachment require a House majority to advance.

On September 15 2026 Texas Democrat Al Green brought the latest such resolution to the floor. It charged the president in connection with the deaths of two U.S. citizens at the hands of federal immigration officers earlier in the year. The Republican majority moved to table the measure and end debate.

Sixty-four Democrats joined Republicans on the tabling vote, including 47 who voted present. House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries was among those voting present. The motion passed and the impeachment attempt was set aside without a vote on the merits. The House returned to its regular calendar.

Thinking ahead
House Democrats joined Republicans on Sept. 15 to table Rep. Al Green's impeachment resolution against President Trump over the earlier killing of two U.S. citizens by federal immigration officials. The tally showed 64 Democrats voting to set the measure aside, with 47 of them, including Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, voting present instead of yes or no. Green had pushed for an immediate floor vote, arguing the Constitution sets no waiting period and that further committee review would shield the president. The procedural path now runs through the House calendar and committee gatekeeping. A fresh resolution would need new sponsorship, a discharge petition to force a floor vote, or a rules change to reach the floor again. Leadership statements point to a focus on other legislative priorities and documented investigations before any repeat effort. The current alignment holds as long as Democratic leadership keeps the caucus behind the present-or-table strategy and Green lacks the 218 votes for discharge. A shift would show in a spike of co-sponsors on a new filing or a rules vote that breaks the present bloc. This episode sits on the ladder of earlier House attempts that reached the floor only to be tabled by cross-party majorities when investigative steps were absent. It runs until a new filing or a rules fight forces another tally. Next marker is any discharge petition gaining signatures or a revised resolution with hearings attached. Horizon: months, not weeks.
