Flight carrying Zelenskyy to Norway was delayed in Moldova because of drone incursion

Russian forces have sent waves of Shahed drones into Ukrainian airspace for years, prompting Kyiv to build up interceptors and production lines for systems that can down the jet-powered versions now in use. On September 10, 2026, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy was in Moldova en route to Norway when air defenses there reported a fresh drone incursion.

The presidential flight stayed on the ground until the threat cleared. Zelenskyy posted on Facebook after the delay, noting that briefings had covered ongoing work with manufacturers on new-generation interceptors, testing already under way, and the financial backing now in place to scale output fast. He said 67 Ukrainian companies can already mass-produce such systems, with three to five showing concrete results.

The president also received updates on the front line, where Ukrainian units continue to hold and press positions, especially the 3rd Army Corps, the 66th and 125th Mechanized Brigades, and the 46th Separate Airmobile Brigade in Donetsk region. The incident underscored the reach of the drone campaign into neighboring airspace and the pressure on Ukrainian production to deliver working defenses in volume.

Thinking ahead
Zelensky's plane sat on the ground in Moldova for hours on September 10 after a Russian drone crossed into its airspace. Air traffic controllers held the departure until the threat cleared. This matches a pattern seen in prior trips where flight paths get altered or scrubbed when Russian drones or missiles operate near borders. The delay itself points to tighter real-time coordination between Ukrainian, Moldovan, and NATO air-defense teams. Zelensky's own readout after the stop focused on speeding up interceptor production against jet-powered Shaheds, naming specific brigades holding lines in Donetsk. Those two data points together show the incident is tied to the same supply-and-frontline pressure that has driven Ukrainian procurement talks for months. How much longer this continues depends on whether Russian drone routes keep clipping Moldovan territory and whether the new interceptors reach units in numbers that force a route change. Next step is the scheduled Norway arrival and whatever joint statements come out of those meetings on air-defense cooperation. If drone incursions drop or the intercept tests deliver claimed results, the flight holds shorten; if not, ground delays stay routine for high-profile Ukrainian movements. Horizon: weeks to months until interceptor field numbers are verifiable.
