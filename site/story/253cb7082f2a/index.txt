The complaints say each one got California licenses then registered with the county subsidy programs run by Child Development Associates and the YMCA. They turned in monthly sheets claiming they watched dozens of kids on days when surveillance video showed almost no one coming or going except during one state inspection. All twelve were arrested the same morning by a team of 250 officers who also hit twelve homes listed as the daycare sites. The cases are separate but follow the same paperwork trail that pulled more than ten million dollars in federal funds meant for low-income families.

The next visible steps are initial court appearances followed by motions to dismiss or plead. Prosecutors will need to turn over the surveillance tapes and the exact attendance sheets to back the perjury counts. The defendants can fight the video evidence or argue the counts do not add up to ten million. Until a judge rules on those motions or a jury decides the facts the charges stay active.

This stays in the federal courthouse until pleas or trial dates are set. If enough defendants plead the docket shrinks fast. If they all go to trial the calendar stretches into 2027.
