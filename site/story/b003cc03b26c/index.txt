The Constitution gives Congress power to tax and spend for the general welfare under Article I, Section 8, Clause 1, yet the founders warned against turning that power into a vehicle for plunder. In Federalist 41, Madison explained that spending must stay within the enumerated objects listed in the Constitution; otherwise, the clause becomes “a boundless field of power.” The scheme here took money collected from taxpayers and funneled it through false claims instead of delivering the care those taxes were meant to support.

Founding statutes such as the Tariff Act of 1789 and the early revenue laws show the first Congress expected taxes to fund only legitimate public objects, not private gain. Washington’s personal command of the militia in the Whiskey Rebellion of 1794 enforced the rule that revenue laws must be obeyed so the government can meet its first duty under the Preamble: provide for the common defense and general welfare without allowing fraud to undermine trust in the system.

The sentence upholds the principle that government exists to secure life, liberty, and property. When public funds for health care are stolen, the protection of life that those funds were meant to serve is directly attacked. 1b:T76e,Rita Ntusa Anagho, 54, owner of Tusa Integrated Clinic in Phoenix, got sentenced to 14 years in federal prison on September 17 2026 for running a scheme that billed Arizona’s Medicaid program AHCCCS more than 69 million dollars between May 2022 and March 2023. She targeted Native American patients under the American Indian Health Care Program because it paid higher rates, submitted claims for therapy sessions that never happened or were not delivered as billed, paid kickbacks to sober-home operators for patient referrals, and ordered staff to fake records after a subpoena arrived. The court also ordered her to pay 55 million in restitution and forfeit 9.5 million from seven bank accounts plus 7 million in real estate.

This case sits at the end of a ladder that started with the clinic’s rapid billing spike in 2022, moved to the federal investigation and subpoena in 2023, produced her guilty plea to conspiracy to commit wire fraud and health care fraud in May 2025, and reached sentencing this week.

The sentence is final. Anagho reports to prison within weeks unless her lawyers file a notice of appeal in the next 14 days. Any appeal would turn on whether the restitution amount or the loss calculation holds up, not on the conviction itself. Co-conspirators already sentenced or still pending will finish their cases without her testimony. The Arizona Medicaid program has already clawed back what it paid and tightened AIHP billing rules.

The fraud ring ends here. No new patients or claims will flow through Tusa. Federal prosecutors in Arizona keep the same health-care fraud caseload they carried before this one closed.

Next visible step is her surrender to the Bureau of Prisons and the start of restitution collections from seized assets. The situation changes only if an appeals court throws out the loss figure and sends the case back for resentencing.
