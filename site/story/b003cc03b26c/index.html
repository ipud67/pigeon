Arizona Addiction Treatment Clinic Owner Sentenced to 14 Years in Prison for Leadership Role in $69M Medicaid Fraud Scheme

Arizona’s Medicaid agency, the Arizona Health Care Cost Containment System, has paid for addiction treatment services since the state expanded coverage under the program in 2014. The American Indian Health Care Program within it has paid higher rates for Native American patients than standard plans since the program began in 1988.

On September 17 2026 Rita Ntusa Anagho, 54, of San Tan Valley, was sentenced in Phoenix federal court to 14 years in prison. Anagho owned and operated Tusa Integrated Clinic in the Phoenix area. From May 2022 through March 2023 the clinic billed AHCCCS more than 69 million dollars for addiction treatment that was either not provided or not provided as billed. AHCCCS paid the clinic about 54.9 million dollars on those claims. Anagho targeted patients covered by the American Indian Health Care Program because it paid higher rates. She paid kickbacks to sober-home owners for patient referrals, falsified treatment notes to support the claims, and later directed employees to create fake records after a arrived.

Anagho must pay 54.9 million dollars in restitution. She must also forfeit 9.5 million dollars seized from seven bank accounts she controlled and another 7 million dollars in real estate. The case is one of two Medicaid fraud sentencings in the past week. On September 11 a Brooklyn adult-daycare owner received prison time for a 64-million-dollar scheme.

Constitutional analysis
A federal court in Phoenix sentenced Rita Ntusa Anagho to 14 years in prison for her leadership in a conspiracy to commit wire fraud and health care fraud that billed Arizona’s Medicaid agency more than $69 million in false claims for addiction treatment services from May 2022 through March 2023. The Arizona Health Care Cost Containment System paid out roughly $54.9 million on those claims before the scheme was stopped. Anagho, who owned Tusa Integrated Clinic, targeted patients covered by the American Indian Health Care Program because it paid higher rates than other Medicaid plans. She and her co-conspirators submitted claims for services never provided, paid kickbacks to sober-home operators for patient referrals, falsified treatment records, and obstructed justice by directing employees to fabricate documents after a subpoena arrived. The court ordered her to pay nearly $55 million in restitution and to forfeit about $9.5 million from seven bank accounts plus roughly $7 million in real estate. The Constitution grants Congress power under Article I, Section 8, Clause 1 to lay and collect taxes to provide for the general welfare, which the first Congress exercised in the Tariff Act of 1789 by funding federal operations through duties while leaving most domestic matters to the states. The founders viewed fraud against public funds as an attack on the consent of the governed and the domestic tranquility the Preamble names as a core purpose of government. Washington himself led militia in 1794 to enforce federal revenue laws in the Whiskey Rebellion, showing that protecting the treasury from deliberate deceit was seen as essential to ordered liberty. This case reaches federal court because the fraud used interstate wires and targeted a program created under Congress’s spending power. The sentence, restitution, and forfeiture follow from statutes Congress passed to guard those funds. No further federal authority is required to punish the taking of public money obtained by lies.
Thinking ahead
Rita Ntusa Anagho, 54, owner of Tusa Integrated Clinic in Phoenix, got sentenced to 14 years in federal prison on September 17 2026 for running a scheme that billed Arizona’s Medicaid program AHCCCS more than 69 million dollars between May 2022 and March 2023. She targeted Native American patients under the American Indian Health Care Program because it paid higher rates, submitted claims for therapy sessions that never happened or were not delivered as billed, paid kickbacks to sober-home operators for patient referrals, and ordered staff to fake records after a subpoena arrived. The court also ordered her to pay 55 million in restitution and forfeit 9.5 million from seven bank accounts plus 7 million in real estate. This case sits at the end of a ladder that started with the clinic’s rapid billing spike in 2022, moved to the federal investigation and subpoena in 2023, produced her guilty plea to conspiracy to commit wire fraud and health care fraud in May 2025, and reached sentencing this week. The sentence is final. Anagho reports to prison within weeks unless her lawyers file a notice of appeal in the next 14 days. Any appeal would turn on whether the restitution amount or the loss calculation holds up, not on the conviction itself. Co-conspirators already sentenced or still pending will finish their cases without her testimony. The Arizona Medicaid program has already clawed back what it paid and tightened AIHP billing rules. The fraud ring ends here. No new patients or claims will flow through Tusa. Federal prosecutors in Arizona keep the same health-care fraud caseload they carried before this one closed. Next visible step is her surrender to the Bureau of Prisons and the start of restitution collections from seized assets. The situation changes only if an appeals court throws out the loss figure and sends the case back for resentencing. Horizon: closed.
