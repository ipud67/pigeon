PAKISTAN
September 18, 2026 · 09:02 UTC

Pakistan police re-arrest prominent rights lawyer after top court granted bail

Pakistan has seen repeated arrests of lawyers who take human rights cases against state agencies. Courts have granted bail in some of those cases only for police to pick the lawyers up again on fresh charges. The pattern dates back years and has drawn public attention each time it surfaces.

On September 18 police took the prominent rights lawyer back into custody hours after the Supreme Court ordered his release on bail in an earlier case. Officers acted on a new detention order at the prison gate as soon as the bail papers were processed.

The lawyer now sits in custody under the second order while his legal team prepares to challenge it in court. The Supreme Court’s bail decision remains on record but has not yet produced his release.

Thinking ahead
Police in Pakistan picked up rights lawyer Asad Manzoor Butt again on September 18 right after the Supreme Court ordered his release on bail. Officers cited fresh charges tied to earlier protest cases. The court had ruled the prior detention lacked solid grounds, yet the re-arrest kept him in custody within hours. This pattern has run for months. Earlier steps on the ladder include multiple arrests on protest-related counts, court grants of bail, and immediate police rearrests on new complaints. The sequence shows authorities file additional cases the moment release looks likely. The situation will likely hold steady as long as police keep lodging fresh complaints that reset the clock on detention. Next move sits with lower courts or the Supreme Court reviewing the new charges. Change would need either the top court to block rearrest on identical protest matters or prosecutors to drop the pattern of serial cases. Horizon: weeks, until a court hearing forces a clearer procedural break.
