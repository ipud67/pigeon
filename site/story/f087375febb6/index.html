BROOKLYN
September 24, 2026
This story is from September 24, 2026 and is no longer on the front page.

United States prosecutors charged Uzbek national Davud Ansariy in New York with leading a Brooklyn fundraising network that sent at least three fighters to Syria

Uzbek nationals who became naturalized U.S. citizens lived in Brooklyn and formed groups that collected money from 2013 onward to pay for plane tickets and family support for men who wanted to reach Syria. The collections operated under the name chayxona, the Uzbek term for tea house, and the participants sent at least three travelers that year. All three later died in Syria. One participant left the United States for Turkey on a one-way ticket in May 2016, five days after federal agents arrested a co-conspirator in the same network. A federal grand jury in Brooklyn returned an against that man in November 2021 on charges of and provision of material support to the Islamic State of Iraq and al-Sham and to the Al-Nusrah Front, both designated foreign terrorist organizations during the period covered by the charges.

Davud Ansariy, also known as Kozimjon Gafurov and Kozim, was arrested in Tbilisi, Georgia, in August 2026 and extradited from there to the Eastern District of New York on September 23.

He appeared this afternoon in federal court in Brooklyn before Judge Lara K. Eshkenazi for on the 2021 indictment. The case was investigated by the FBI with assistance from the New York Police Department, New York State Police, U.S. Customs and Border Protection, Port Authority Police Department and U.S. Marshals Service. The Department of Justice Office of International Affairs, the FBI legal attache in Tbilisi and Georgian prosecutors handled the arrest and transfer.
