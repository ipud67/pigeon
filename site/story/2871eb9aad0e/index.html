TARARUA
September 21, 2026 · 07:44 UTC

Six staff resigned from the Pahiatua medical centre in Tararua where almost 5000 patients depend on the clinic and 81 have left in two years

Six staff left the Pahīatua Medical Centre on 21 September 2026. The centre serves almost 5000 patients in the Tararua district. Over the past two years 81 people have resigned from the Tararua Health Group citing heavy workloads and long waits for appointments and tests.

A phlebotomist with 15 years at the centre, an administrator with 21 years, three registered nurses and one nurse practitioner handed in their notices together. Registered nurse Mark Emmerson said the clinic operated with few clinicians and had set up an acute clinic a few months earlier that was being used for work such as ACC claims and income renewals that could not be handled in that setting. He said a nurse practitioner worked four days a week and a full-time nurse prescriber was due to leave in two weeks.

The clinic now faces the loss of staff with long local experience. The Primary Health Organisation that holds the contract said the concerns could be met with a plan but did not detail one. Patient Voice Aotearoa called for a review of every screening test done through the Tararua Health Group and said Health New Zealand should consider ending the contract and returning the service to community control.

Thinking ahead
Six staff quit the Pahīatua Medical Centre today: a phlebotomist with 15 years, an administrator with 21 years, three registered nurses, and one nurse practitioner. The Tararua Health Group has already lost 81 people over the past two years on the same grounds of unsafe workloads and long waits. The clinic serves almost 5000 patients with a shrinking pool of clinicians; one nurse practitioner works four days a week and the full-time nurse prescriber leaves in two weeks. Patient Voice Aotearoa called for a full review of every screening test done through the group and for Health New Zealand to pull the contract so the community can set up a new provider. The Primary Health Organisation chief executive said the problems could be fixed with a plan but gave no details on what that plan would be or when it would start. Staff offered to work with any provider that meets safety standards for the area. This level of staff loss will keep the clinic under pressure until either a new contract holder steps in or enough replacements are hired and trained. The next visible step is whether Health New Zealand or the minister acts on the review call and contract review. Without those moves the pattern of exits seen over the last two years is likely to repeat in Tararua and in places like Kirikiri. Horizon: 3-6 months until next major staffing or contract change.
