17:T55a,Six staff quit the Pahīatua Medical Centre today: a phlebotomist with 15 years, an administrator with 21 years, three registered nurses, and one nurse practitioner. The Tararua Health Group has already lost 81 people over the past two years on the same grounds of unsafe workloads and long waits. The clinic serves almost 5000 patients with a shrinking pool of clinicians; one nurse practitioner works four days a week and the full-time nurse prescriber leaves in two weeks. Patient Voice Aotearoa called for a full review of every screening test done through the group and for Health New Zealand to pull the contract so the community can set up a new provider. The Primary Health Organisation chief executive said the problems could be fixed with a plan but gave no details on what that plan would be or when it would start. Staff offered to work with any provider that meets safety standards for the area.

This level of staff loss will keep the clinic under pressure until either a new contract holder steps in or enough replacements are hired and trained. The next visible step is whether Health New Zealand or the minister acts on the review call and contract review. Without those moves the pattern of exits seen over the last two years is likely to repeat in Tararua and in places like Kirikiri.
