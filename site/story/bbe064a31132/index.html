Yemen conflict displaces over 100,000 people, UN says

Fighting in Yemen between Houthi forces and a Saudi-led began in 2015 after the Houthis seized the capital Sanaa and forced the internationally recognized government to flee. The coalition launched air strikes that month to restore the government, and the war settled into a pattern of front-line clashes, blockades and air campaigns that drove millions from their homes over the next decade.

On September 15, 2026, the United Nations reported that more than 100,000 people had been displaced by renewed fighting in recent weeks.

The new displacements added to the existing population of internally displaced Yemenis and kept the conflict centered on control of key roads, ports and capitals where neither side has been able to force a decisive end.

Thinking ahead
The UN count of over 100,000 newly displaced in Yemen marks the latest spike tied to fighting between Houthi forces and government-aligned units that has dragged on since the 2014 takeover of Sanaa. Ground clashes in northern and central provinces plus Houthi drone and missile strikes have pushed families out of homes and farms in the past month. No peace talks are scheduled, and aid groups report roads remain blocked, so the flow of displaced people will likely stay high through the rest of 2026 unless one side loses ground or outside backers cut supplies. Next visible shift would show up in UN tracking of new displacement figures dropping below 20,000 per month or in confirmed cease-fire talks between Riyadh, the Houthis, and the recognized government. Horizon: another 9 to 18 months of steady or higher displacement at current fighting levels.
