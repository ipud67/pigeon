6 corps members receive N10m agribusiness grants

The National Youth Service Corps requires every Nigerian graduate under 30 to serve one year in a posting away from home. That year includes a skills acquisition and entrepreneurship development component that trains participants in trades including farming and food processing. The British American Tobacco Nigeria Foundation has run an annual grant scheme inside that component since 2017, selecting a handful of corps members each cycle for startup capital after they complete business training and pitch their plans.

On Monday the NYSC Director-General presented cheques totalling N10 million to six corps members who finished the latest round of the Farmers for the Future Programme. Dominic Olufemi collected N3 million, Temitope Adewole and Abdulwaheed Bala received N2 million each, and Olanike Mayungbe, Kingsley Udoeyen and Lorember Lorsue got N1 million apiece. Nearly 3,000 corps members had applied; 20 reached a final boot camp and pitching session before the six were chosen.

The six winners now start their agribusinesses under mentors already operating in the market, while the remaining applicants keep the training they received during the selection process. The programme continues as a standing part of the NYSC entrepreneurship track.

Thinking ahead
Six corps members each got cash grants totaling N10 million under the BATNF-NYSC Farmers for the Future Programme in Abuja on Monday. The money went to six finalists picked from nearly 3,000 applicants after a boot camp and pitching round. Recipients now get mentors tied to established market operators to move their agribusiness products. This kind of grant round has run for years as part of the same NYSC partnership. The latest payout follows the same selection ladder that has produced winners, training for the rest, and public calls to turn corps service into lasting farm businesses. The programme is likely to keep running on the same cycle of open applications, shortlist, boot camp and awards. Next round will probably surface in the 2027 service year once the current cohort finishes mentoring and starts selling. No market indicators here bear on the grant size or selection numbers. Horizon: at least two more annual cycles.
