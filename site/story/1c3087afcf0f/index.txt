1d:T53a,A federal judge in Oklahoma issued a preliminary injunction Thursday that blocks the statewide charter school board from keeping the National Ben Gamla Jewish Charter School Foundation out of the program over its religious character. The foundation had sued in March after the board turned down its application, citing a state supreme court decision that barred religious schools. The judge wrote that a charter school starts as a private outfit with its own unelected board, so letting it join on the same terms as other charters does not violate the Establishment Clause.

This round of the fight has run for six months since the denial and will keep going until the board either drops the nonsectarian rule for this applicant or the case reaches final judgment. The next clear step is either an appeal by the state to the Tenth Circuit or a trial on the permanent injunction. The situation changes only if the Tenth Circuit reverses the injunction, the U.S. Supreme Court takes the case and rules against the school, or the Oklahoma legislature rewrites the charter statute to remove the religious exclusion.

The dispute sits on the ladder of charter-school religion cases that turned on whether the school counts as state actor or private operator.
