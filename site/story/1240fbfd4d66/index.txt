17:T4f8,Health insurance complaints hit the top spot at the Insurance and Financial Services Ombudsman Scheme for the year to June, after jumping 91 percent and passing house and motor vehicle insurance disputes. The scheme closed 833 dispute investigations, a 46 percent rise from the year before, with more than 92 percent of them tied to insurance. Most turned on disagreements over policy exclusions, benefit rules, limits, or term changes that policyholders only spotted when they filed claims.

The pattern sits on a steady climb in complaints that began well before this year, fed by higher healthcare costs and tighter health system capacity. Travel insurance complaints rose 32 percent in the same period, some linked to Middle East conflict disruptions. The ombudsman paid out over $1.39 million to consumers and cleared cases in an average of 46 days.

This volume will likely keep climbing as long as households stay under financial pressure and people keep discovering gaps only after they need to claim. Next step is more investigations into the same exclusion and eligibility fights unless insurers rewrite policy wording or run clearer upfront notices that cut the surprises. Without those changes the ombudsman caseload stays elevated.
