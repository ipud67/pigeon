19:T4ad,UNHCR has stated it will not take part in Malaysia’s planned return of Myanmar refugees. This follows Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim’s announcement that Myanmar agreed to take back 5,000 Rohingya. The plan sits on the long-running ladder of Rohingya arrivals in Malaysia after the 2017 crackdown, repeated pushback attempts by Malaysian authorities, and earlier failed repatriation talks that stalled over safety guarantees.

The UNHCR position removes one procedural check that had slowed past returns. Malaysian officials now need direct bilateral paperwork with Myanmar and on-ground clearance from both sides before any flights can leave. If Myanmar supplies formal acceptance documents and Malaysia lines up transport without court blocks or fresh boat arrivals, the first group could move within months. If UNHCR stays out and no new violence reports surface from Rakhine, the program can continue on a small scale. A surge in new Rohingya boats or a Malaysian court injunction would pause it again.

It runs at least through 2026 at the current pace of talks. Next visible rung is the signing of a written repatriation agreement between Kuala Lumpur and Naypyidaw.
