The embassy responded by stating that freedom of movement, expression and the press are fundamental rights. This fits a pattern of Cuban authorities restricting access to diplomatic events involving critics of the government.

The blocks rely on short-term physical surveillance and detentions that security forces have used before at similar gatherings. Those measures usually last only for the duration of the event. Next steps will likely stay in the same lane: more invitations to independent voices, more on-the-ground barriers by security, and public statements from both sides.

The situation shifts only if the embassy stops hosting such events, security eases its perimeter controls, or higher-level talks between the two governments produce new access rules. Right now the indicators point to continued repetition of the same sequence.
