Fiji proposes scrapping immunity for past coup leaders including current PM

Fiji has operated under a 2013 constitution written by the military government that took power in the 2006 coup. That document gave absolute and irrevocable immunity to anyone involved in the coups of 1987, 2000 and 2006. It also kept in force all issued during previous military rule. The constitution itself was never put to a popular vote, a point the supreme court noted last year when it ruled the text carried a democratic deficit and left room for a formal review.

On 23 September 2026 the Constitution Review Commission tabled its report in parliament in Suva. The 200-page document recommends repeal of the immunity clauses, removal of the saved decrees, and an explicit ban on any future grant of immunity for coup-related acts. The changes would reach the 1987 coups led by current Prime Minister Sitiveni Rabuka. The commission also proposes rewriting the military’s role so that it handles only external defence and can be sent inside the country only with parliamentary approval. The Republic of Fiji Military Forces has backed the immunity repeal.

The report further calls for replacement of the present 55-member unicameral parliament elected from a single national list with a bicameral house of 60 seats chosen by a mix of constituency first-past-the-post and party-list votes. Eight of the nineteen senators would be nominated by the government. Fiji’s next general election is scheduled for February. The prime minister has said it is premature to say whether the proposals will be put into law before the vote or whether debate on them would affect the election timetable.
