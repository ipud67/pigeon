18:T66c,The Constitution Review Commission tabled its report in parliament on 23 September 2026, recommending the removal of absolute immunity clauses that have protected participants in the 1987, 2000 and 2006 coups. That list includes current Prime Minister Sitiveni Rabuka, who led the 1987 actions. The same report proposes shifting back to a two-chamber parliament with 60 seats, changing the electoral map from one national constituency to a mix of local first-past-the-post seats and party-list seats, and rewriting the military’s mandate to limit it to external defence unless parliament approves domestic use.

Fiji’s 2013 constitution was written under the Bainimarama military government that took power in 2006. The Supreme Court ruled last year that the document had a democratic deficit because it was never put to a popular vote, which opened the way for this review. The military itself has backed the proposal to scrap future immunity grants.

How long this runs depends on whether parliament votes to adopt the changes before the February election. The deputy prime minister has already called the timing premature. If the report moves forward it would need a two-thirds majority to amend the constitution; without that the existing immunity stays in place and the election proceeds under current rules.

Next visible step is MPs reading the 200-page document and deciding whether to schedule debate or hold the February vote first. Thin procedural signals beyond the tabling and the deputy PM’s remark make any longer horizon guesswork.
