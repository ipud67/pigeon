19:T60f,The UN Security Council kicks off its October schedule with Greece in the president’s chair for the second time in its current rotation. The month lines up two signature events: a 26 October briefing on children and armed conflict chaired by the Greek foreign minister to mark the mandate’s 30th year, and a 29 October debate on emerging technologies focused on maritime security chaired by the Greek prime minister. Greece also set a joint seminar with the African Union for 21-22 October, a formal AU consultative meeting on the 23rd, plus mandated debates on the Middle East on the 15th and on women, peace and security on the 27th. Straw polls on the next Secretary-General are scheduled through the month as the current term winds down.

These dates sit on the fixed calendar of rotating presidencies and recurring mandates, so the sequence runs on procedural rhythm rather than sudden crisis. The only visible pressure point is the pending drawdown of the UN Interim Force in Lebanon, where the Council now acknowledges a gap will open once the force leaves. How that gap gets filled stays on the agenda but shows no fixed deadline this month.

The programme itself runs its course by 31 October when the gavel passes to the next member. Any extension of a specific item beyond that depends on whether the straw polls produce a clear candidate for Secretary-General or whether the Lebanon gap forces an emergency session. Absent those triggers the calendar simply turns over.
