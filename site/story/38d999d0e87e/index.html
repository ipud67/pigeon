ATHENS
September 26, 2026 · 12:47 UTC

British national among six dead in building explosion close to Acropolis in Athens

A three-storey building that housed holiday rental apartments stood in the Plaka district of central Athens, a narrow street between the Acropolis and the Temple of Olympian Zeus that draws large numbers of tourists each year. The structure also contained the residence of a local couple on the same site.

The building collapsed at midday on Friday after a suspected gas leak triggered an explosion. Rescue teams using excavators and thermal imaging cameras recovered the bodies of six people by Saturday afternoon: four American tourists, one British national, and one Greek national.

All six victims had been inside at the time of the blast. The force damaged neighbouring buildings, scattered debris across nearby streets, and prompted the of several structures in the area. Athens Mayor Haris Doukas described the event as an incredible disaster. Local police noted the American tourists were first reported missing after they failed to board a scheduled flight from Athens airport.

Thinking ahead
Six people died when a three-storey building in Athens Plaka district collapsed in a suspected gas explosion on Friday. The dead include four American tourists, one British national, and one Greek national who lived on site. Rescue crews finished pulling all bodies from the rubble by Saturday afternoon and have moved on to checking neighbouring structures for damage and leaks. Greek authorities treat the event as an accident driven by a gas leak. Police opened an investigation into the building’s gas lines and maintenance records. The UK Foreign Office and US officials are coordinating with local police on victim identification and return of remains. No new casualties have been reported from falling debris. City crews continue to cordon the street between the Acropolis and Temple of Olympian Zeus while engineers inspect adjacent buildings. The procedural next step is the completion of the technical report on the gas system; any shift would require that report to flag criminal negligence or a wider infrastructure fault. This incident is likely to stay contained to the one site and the one investigation. Horizon: another 7–10 days until the final technical report.
