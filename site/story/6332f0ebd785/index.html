Florida man sues Orlando after staff took his Nazi flag at a kickboxing match

Jay Brown attended a combat-sports event at the city-owned Kia Center in Orlando on August 29. The heavyweight kickboxing match pitted Israeli-American fighter Ben Azoulay against Paul Nicholas Miller, who goes by Gypsy Crusader online. An usher seized the Nazi flag Brown waved during the bout, called it hateful, said he should feel ashamed, and escorted him to a coat-check area where staff held the flag while warning that continued display would get him removed.

Brown filed an complaint in U.S. District Court for the Middle District of Florida on September 29. The suit claims the venue allowed other patrons to display large Black Lives Matter flags at the same event but suppressed his expression. It accuses the city of viewpoint discrimination under the First , which protects nearly all speech. Brown seeks compensatory and damages plus an order that bars the city from restricting political expression based on its content or viewpoint.

The Kia Center’s published policies limit signs to 11 by 17 inches and ban those with indecent or comments, images, or political statements. Security may inspect items and bar anything deemed inappropriate for crowd safety. On September 2 the city and Orlando Venues issued a statement that they must follow the First Amendment and cannot deny access based on political ideology while condemning hate speech, antisemitism, and racism. The case now turns on whether the city-owned venue applied its rules in a content-neutral way.
