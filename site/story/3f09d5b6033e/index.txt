1d:T5e2,The Supreme Court added three cases to its argument calendar for early 2027 after the justices met Sept. 28. One grants the Trump administration’s request to review its policy of mandatory detention without bond hearings for noncitizens picked up inside the country. That policy applies a statute written for people at the border to long-term residents and has drawn more than 9,300 district-court rulings against it since last year, with appeals courts split on whether it fits the law. The second case tests how far local zoning rules can go against houses of worship. The third looks at a federal statute meant to shield ex-spouses of service members from loss of certain benefits. The court will hear all three between January and April 2027 unless it changes the schedule.

This docket addition sits on the ladder of the court’s regular September conference after its summer break, the first step that sets the 2026-27 term’s calendar. The split among appeals courts on the detention rule supplied the main signal that moved the case forward.

The cases stay on the calendar until argued and decided. Next public step is additional orders from the same conference on Oct. 5, then the first arguments of the term. The detention policy remains in force while the case moves ahead; any change would require the court to rule against it or Congress to rewrite the statute. The zoning and benefits disputes follow the same track.
