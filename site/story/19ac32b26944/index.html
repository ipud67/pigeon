UK Publishes Legislation for Tougher on Iran Over Nuclear Program

The United Kingdom first imposed sanctions on Iran in 2011 after the country refused to halt uranium enrichment at Natanz and Fordow in violation of UN resolutions. Those measures froze assets, banned arms sales, and restricted oil and banking ties. Britain later joined the 2015 nuclear agreement that lifted some sanctions in exchange for limits on Iran's enrichment levels and inspections by the International Atomic Energy Agency. The US withdrew from that deal in 2018 and reimposed broad sanctions. Iran responded by exceeding the agreed enrichment caps and reducing IAEA access.

On September 9 2026 the UK government published new legislation that expands the legal powers available to ministers. The text adds further Iranian individuals and entities to the sanctions list, widens the scope of prohibited financial services, and lengthens the period during which designated persons face travel bans and asset freezes.

The new rules now sit inside the existing sanctions framework and give UK authorities clearer authority to act without fresh parliamentary votes each time Iran advances its nuclear activities. The situation turns on whether and how those authorities choose to designate additional targets in the coming weeks.

Thinking ahead
The UK government published new legislation on September 9 that sets up fresh sanctions tied to Iran’s nuclear activities. This follows years of stepped-up measures after the collapse of the 2015 nuclear deal and repeated IAEA reports showing Iran enriching uranium beyond civilian levels. The bill now heads to parliament for debate and votes; passage would let ministers add names and freeze assets without fresh primary legislation each time. How long this runs depends on whether parliament clears the bill in the coming weeks and whether the EU and US follow with parallel packages. The situation changes only if Iran verifiably rolls back enrichment to 3.67 percent and lets inspectors back in full, or if a new US or UK government drops the pressure. Right now the procedural next step is parliamentary approval. Horizon estimate: 4 to 8 months until sanctions list expands or stalls.
