USCIS set higher immigration fees for fiscal year 2027 under H.R. 1

U.S. and Immigration Services has administered a set of fixed immigration fees since the One Big Beautiful Bill Act took effect on July 4 2025. That law set minimum fee amounts for year 2025 under the Immigration and Nationality Act, required annual adjustment according to the change in the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, and ordered those adjustments rounded down to the next lowest multiple of ten dollars. USCIS first published the base fees in a notice dated July 22 2025, added the separate one-thousand-dollar parole fee on October 16 2025, and applied the first inflation update for fiscal year 2026 in a notice dated November 21 2025 that took effect January 1 2026.

On September 30 2026 the agency released a new notice that sets the second round of inflation-adjusted fees for fiscal year 2027. The adjustment uses the percentage change in the index from July 2025 to July 2026 and follows the same rounding rule written into the 2025 . The updated amounts apply to every immigration benefit request postmarked on or after October 16 2026; any filing that arrives without the new correct fee will be rejected.

The fees now stand at the levels fixed by the fiscal year 2027 calculation and remain subject to the same annual Consumer Price Index adjustment schedule required by the original law. The next update will be calculated from the July 2026 to July 2027 index period and published later this year.
