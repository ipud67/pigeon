Dar stresses importance of uninterrupted energy supplies in phone call with Iran's FM Araghchi

Pakistan has relied on energy imports routed through the Strait of Hormuz for decades. The waterway carries roughly one-fifth of global oil and liquefied natural gas. Since late February 2026 the United States and Israel have conducted strikes on Iran while Iran has tried to control passage through the strait. A June memorandum lifted the mutual blockades and opened a 60-day negotiating window. That window closed on 8 July and nightly US strikes resumed. Iran struck three commercial tankers in July and moved to shut the strait. Oil prices climbed above 86 dollars a barrel and tanker crossings dropped.

On 19 September Pakistan’s Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar spoke by phone with Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi. Dar told Araghchi that uninterrupted energy supplies and the safe and passage of ships remain essential. The two men reviewed the latest regional developments. Dar pointed to the effect on developing countries and on global supply chains.

The conversation leaves the fight for control of the strait unchanged. Pakistan imports most of its oil and a large share of its LNG through that route. Any sustained closure would cut those flows and raise costs for importers that have no quick alternative. The US strikes continue and Iranian forces still operate along the narrow channel.
