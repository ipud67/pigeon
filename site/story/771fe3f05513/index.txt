19:T57f,The Preamble of the Constitution lists “domestic Tranquility” and the “general Welfare” as reasons we formed government in the first place. That starts with keeping the peace and protecting human life. A new UCLA study shows many inmates in LA County jails die weeks or months after arrest, including from overdoses. These men and women are in the custody of the county. Their lives are literally in the hands of the government.

The founders understood that once the state locks a person up, it assumes total responsibility for his safety. The Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments forbid the government from depriving any person of life without due process of law. That duty does not disappear just because the inmate has a drug problem or a long wait for trial. Washington himself led troops to put down the Whiskey Rebellion in 1794 to restore order and protect life and property under law. The same principle applies inside the jail walls.

Long-term deaths from overdose point to a collapse in basic order. Guards, medical staff, and administrators are not keeping drugs out or getting addicts the help they need to stay alive. This is not a minor management failure. It is a direct breach of the government’s core job: to secure the right to life. Until LA County restores real custody, real screening, and real discipline, it is violating the very purpose for which legitimate government exists.1a:T471,A UCLA study out this week shows a chunk of deaths in LA County jails, including overdoses, happen weeks or months after booking rather than right away. That lines up with years of lawsuits and county reports tracking the same pattern in the nation’s largest jail system. The county has already rolled out more medical screenings, drug treatment beds, and mental health staff under court pressure and its own reform plan.

How much longer this runs depends on whether the death rate drops in the next two quarterly reports the sheriff and health department file with the board of supervisors. If those numbers stay flat or climb, expect fresh federal oversight hearings, more plaintiff suits, and another round of mandated staffing increases. Real change would need the county to hit and hold lower monthly death totals for at least a year while adding enough treatment slots to cut the wait times that keep people in withdrawal or untreated.

Next visible rung is the supervisors’ October review of the latest jail death dashboard numbers.
