1a:T4be,Iran and Oman said Tuesday they are setting up a temporary corridor for commercial ships through the Strait of Hormuz. This comes after Iran struck tankers in July, tried to close the strait on July 12, and kept up attacks that dropped crossings to a three-week low. The move follows the UAE cutting trade with Iran on August 19 over missile fire near its waters.

The corridor is a framework agreement between the two foreign ministers, not a full reopening. It gives Iran a way to keep some oil moving while the fight stays locked on strait control. Oman’s involvement points to Gulf states trying to carve out limited traffic without ending the US-Iran clashes.

How long it lasts depends on whether US or Iranian forces interfere with the new route and whether more tankers actually use it. Next step is likely the first test convoys and whether Iran hits any that skip its approval process. If the corridor draws fire or stays empty, the strait stays mostly shut and the war grinds on.

It could widen if Oman brings in other Gulf states or if the US agrees to stand down on escorts. Or it could collapse if Iran demands veto power over every ship.
