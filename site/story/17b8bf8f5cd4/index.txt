Article I, Section 8 of the Constitution gives Congress power to make laws necessary and proper for carrying out the duties of the executive, yet the First Amendment bars Congress from making any law that abridges the freedom of the press. The founders understood press freedom as a check on federal power, not a guarantee of access to every government building or event. George Washington in 1794 called out the Whiskey Rebellion in western Pennsylvania and led militia under the Militia Acts of 1792 to restore order, all while newspapers printed sharp criticism of his administration without losing physical access to federal spaces. 1b:T495,CNN, Politico, and MS NOW filed suit Monday in US District Court in Washington after the White House barred their reporters from the building Saturday. The outlets had already pulled out of the shared TV pool that covers presidential events, leaving no replacement crew for Trump's trip to the UN General Assembly. Trump said his team will appeal any ruling against the ban.

The case sits at the motion-for-preliminary-injunction stage. A district judge will set a briefing schedule and hearing date in the next few weeks. The First Amendment record shows past administrations lost similar credential fights when exclusion turned on coverage content rather than neutral rules like space or security.

How long it runs depends on how fast the judge moves and whether the White House keeps the ban in place while appeals climb. Next rung is either a temporary order restoring access or an administrative stay that holds the status quo until the DC Circuit weighs in. The ladder from Friday's announcement to Monday's filing and pool suspension points to a short procedural fight measured in weeks, not months.
