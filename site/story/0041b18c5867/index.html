IRAN
November 21, 2026 · 03:20 UTC

Iran foreign minister Abbas Araqchi said diplomacy can end its war with the US

The United States and Israel opened strikes on Iran in late February 2026. A June memorandum lifted the dual blockades and opened a 60-day window; it collapsed on 8 July and US strikes resumed. The fight has narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi said Tehran still hoped diplomacy could end the war with the United States and wanted guarantees that Washington was serious about before proceeding further. Messages continued to be exchanged through . Araqchi told state media Iran sought an agreement that would ensure no further war while keeping its highly enriched uranium inside the country though a fair solution remained possible.

President Donald Trump said Iran wanted a deal but one that was not acceptable to him. Qatari and Iranian officials met to bridge perspectives and narrow the gap between proposals on the ground. The situation now turns on what form confidence-building measures would take.

Iran's foreign minister said Tehran is exchanging messages with Washington through mediators and wants guarantees that the US is serious about talks before going further. He listed keeping highly enriched uranium inside the country as a red line but added that a fair solution on nuclear material could still be found. Trump stated at a news conference that Iran wants a deal yet the terms are not acceptable to him. The comments follow the collapse of the June Islamabad Memorandum that had lifted blockades and opened a 60-day negotiating window, after which US strikes resumed and the fight narrowed to control of the Strait of Hormuz. This round of indirect messaging is likely to drag on for weeks at least because both sides have restated core demands without new concessions or direct talks. The next observable step would be mediators presenting concrete confidence-building measures or one side adjusting its red lines on uranium or strike pauses. If the gap stays this wide the exchanges stay frozen in place.
