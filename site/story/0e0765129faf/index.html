United Kingdom’s government says Funeral of Ratko Mladić: UK statement to the OSCE

The wars that broke up Yugoslavia in the 1990s left the former Yugoslav republics with courts, peace deals and obligations that still bind them. In 1993 the UN Security Council set up the International Criminal for the former Yugoslavia to try the most serious violations. Serbia signed the Dayton Peace Agreement in 1995 and thereby accepted responsibility to support for those violations and to help keep peace inside Bosnia and Herzegovina.

On 17 September 2026 the UK delivered a statement at the Organisation for Security and Co-operation in Europe after a funeral held in Serbia for Ratko Mladić. The statement said that Mladić had commanded forces responsible for some of the worst crimes committed in Europe since the Second World War and that an independent UN tribunal had convicted him of genocide, crimes against humanity and war crimes. It described any denial or glorification of those crimes as an affront to international law that undermines regional stability and trust.

The statement reminded governments that Serbia is bound by the tribunal’s judgments and by the Dayton Agreement. It said the UK stands by international justice, the judgments of the tribunals and the need for accountability to the victims and their families. At a time when the Western Balkans remain tense, the UK called on all political leaders to respect the of judicial processes and to avoid denial or revisionism of the established record.
