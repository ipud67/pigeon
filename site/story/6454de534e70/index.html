MADRID
September 15, 2026 · 12:00 UTC

Pedro Sánchez announces government has approved historic €234 million investment in vocational training

Translated from Spanish · read the original →
The original headlinePedro Sánchez anuncia que el Gobierno ha aprobado una inversión histórica en Formación Profesional de 234 millones de euros

Spain’s government has run active labour market policies since the return to democracy in the late 1970s. These policies have included repeated attempts to expand vocational training so that workers could move between jobs as industries changed. The latest version of that effort began after the 2008 financial crisis when youth unemployment climbed above 50 percent and stayed there for years. Successive cabinets increased spending on apprenticeships and adult retraining but never reached the scale lawmakers set as a target in the 2010s.

On 15 September 2026 Pedro Sánchez told an audience at the School of Industrial Organization in Madrid that the cabinet had just approved a €234 million package for vocational training. The money will go to new training centres, updated curricula and direct grants that link course content to current openings reported by employers.

The decision adds another layer of public money to the existing training system and sets the next test on whether the extra funds reach workers who need new skills before their current jobs disappear.

Predictive model
The Spanish government approved a €234 million vocational training package on 15 September 2026. Pedro Sánchez presented the plan at the School of Industrial Organization in Madrid and tied it to matching fast-changing job demands with worker skills. This is the latest step in a series of training-budget increases that have run for several years. The money now moves to the ministries responsible for rolling it out through new courses and apprenticeships. Implementation depends on regional governments signing off on their shares and on companies offering enough training slots. Any cut in next year’s budget or a shift in coalition priorities would slow the rollout. The package is likely to stay on track for the next 18 to 24 months unless a new election or fiscal squeeze forces a revision. Next visible marker is the first batch of funded courses opening in early 2027. Horizon: through mid-2028 barring early budget reversal.
